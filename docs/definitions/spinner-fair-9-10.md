The study's decisions of 2026-10-23 stand ([record](../archive/DECISIONS-2026-10-23.md#decisions)): these levels sit at `school`'s top with the hidden spinner last, and the level-10 explanation ("the spinner has no memory") ships after the building session's source check, which replaces the educator's read the study first asked for. The study's two levels are three here (why is in the table below); nothing else needs a new decision.

**Fun action and purpose:** hand wedges between two critters until one wins about as often as the pet asked, then watch eighty spins bear it out; spin until one critter comes up five times in a row and say who is more likely next; spin a wheel hidden under a cover and read its share from the record alone. Learning goal: *a share of the wheel can be made to order, and a long run comes out near it; a run of one result changes nothing about the next spin, because the chance is in the wheel; when the wheel cannot be seen, a long run is the evidence for its shares, and it is evidence, not proof.* Standards it serves: CCSS 7.SP.C.6 (long-run relative frequency approximates a probability, and predicts an approximate count), 7.SP.C.7 (a probability model compared with observed frequencies) and the independence of repeated trials that both assume.

## The accounting rule carries over unchanged

The [levels 1–5 rule](spinner-fair.md#the-accounting-rule) binds 9–11 word for word: **a spin is never a miss; a miss comes only from a question the picture decides; every spin is an honest seeded draw, never filtered, retried or authored.** Two of the study's rounds cannot be built as written without breaking it, so this definition changes them (departures from the study, decided here):

- **Level 10's run of five.** The study shows "five reds in a row" on an even spinner. Choosing a seed that opens with five the same authors the run. Here the spins are honest and **the stopping rule makes the streak**: the wheel is spun until one critter has come up five times running, however long that takes (23 spins at the median on an even wheel, measured below). A rule about when to stop looking does not touch any spin, and the next spin is as honest as the rest. It also shows what the study wanted shown: streaks turn up by themselves.
- **Level 11's hidden wheel.** With the wheel covered, the only picture she can see is the record. So **the strip decides the question, never the wedges under the cover**: the right card is the one nearest the share the strip shows. When an honest run misleads (it will, rarely), she is right for reading it, and the uncovered wheel is called *unusual*, not her mistake. Judging by the hidden wedges would make an honest spin a miss.

## The model (`logic.ts`)

**Plans.** `PLANS` gains `{ level: 9, ask: 'rig', name: 'Rig it', sets: 2, spins: 80 }`, `{ level: 10, ask: 'due', name: 'Is it due?', sets: 2, spins: 240 }` (the cap, below) and `{ level: 11, ask: 'hidden', name: 'The hidden spinner', sets: 2, spins: 160 }`. `Ask` gains `'rig' | 'due' | 'hidden'`. `LEVELS.school` becomes `{ min: 3, max: 11 }` once all three are built (3–9 after CS3a, 3–10 after CS3b); levels 1–8 keep their numbers, so saved levels and pins stay valid.

**The eight-wedge wheel.** `Wheel` stays `readonly Kind[]`; its length is now 6 (levels 1–8 and 10) or 8 (levels 9 and 11), `RIG_WEDGES = 8`. `spin(rng, wedges = WEDGES)`, `raceWheel(racers, a, wedges = WEDGES)`, `wedgeAt(rotation, wedges = WEDGES)` and `give` (which reads `wheel.length`) take the size; with the default they draw from `rng` exactly as today, so every level 1–8 seed replays the same (rule-tested). An eight-wedge stop lies at least 9° inside its 45° wedge (the six-wedge 12° of 60°, in proportion).

### Level 9, Rig it

- **A set.** Two critters on an eight-wedge wheel that starts **even, 4–4** (`raceWheel(racers, 4, 8)`), wedge lines showing. The pet names a target for its favourite: set one an **eighths** target, `k ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7}` ("about 3 out of every 8 spins"); set two a **quarters** target, `k ∈ {2, 6}` ("about 1 out of every 4 spins", "about 3 out of every 4"). The favourite is the same critter in both sets; the friend is redrawn. A half is never the target (that is level 5).
- **Making the wheel.** She drags a wedge onto the other critter's box, as at level 5; a tap on a critter's box also hands it one wedge from the other (the same `give`, so a phone never needs to aim at a 45° wedge). Each critter's box shows its count as a numeral with pips ("3" of eight). Every move is free and undoable by the opposite move; a critter keeps at least one wedge.
- **Check.** Right when the favourite has exactly `k` wedges. A wrong Check is a miss: the pet says the count ("Duck has 3 of the 8 wedges") and the reason line. Check judges the wheel, never a spin.
- **The proof run.** After a right Check: "Let's spin 80 times and see." The strip (eight ticks on its ruler, the marker at `k/8`) fills as 80 honest spins fast-forward (about 4 s; the first eight land one by one). The pet names the count: within ten of `10 · k`, "22 of 80: about 1 in every 4!"; outside, the *unusual* line. The proof run is never a miss and is not re-spun.
- **Helpers.** `rigSet(rng, fav, first)` returns the racers, the start wheel and the target `{ k, n, of }` (`n` out of every `of`: `k`/8 in lowest terms); `rigRight(wheel, fav, k)`; `nextRig(wheel, racers, k)` (the critter the next wedge goes to, or null when right); `PROOF_SPINS = 80`.

### Level 10, Is it due?

- **A set.** Two critters on a six-wedge wheel, wedge lines showing: one set **even** (3–3) and one **uneven** (the favourite 4 or 2 of six, seeded), in seeded order.
- **The run.** She taps the spinner (or the fast-forward button) and the wheel spins by itself, each result joining a **ribbon** of small faces (the latest twelve showing, older ones sliding off), until one critter has come up `STREAK = 5` times running. The run in progress is banded together on the ribbon with its length beside it ("3"), so the streak is seen growing and breaking. The first twelve spins take about 0.35 s each, later ones about 0.1 s, the rest after sixty about 0.04 s, so no run lasts more than about 15 s. Every spin is `spin(rng)`; none is skipped or redrawn. **The cap:** if no critter has made five by 240 spins (about 2 runs in 10 000 on an even wheel), the run stops and the longest streak on the ribbon is banded instead; the same lines speak its length. Under reduced motion the wheel does not turn and the ribbon steps.
- **The question.** "{kind} came up five times in a row! Which is more likely on the next spin?" on three cards: the favourite's face, the friend's face, and **the same** (both faces on a level seesaw). Right is `dueRight`: *the same* on the even wheel, the critter with more wedges on the uneven one. The streak never enters the answer.
- **The next spin.** Then she taps the spinner once and the honest spin lands wherever it lands: "Pig! Every spin starts fresh." The line is the same whoever comes up, so a result that happens to match a wrong idea is never offered as support for it.
- **Helpers.** `dueSet(rng, fav, even)`; `streakRun(rng, wheel)` (spins until the last five agree or the cap; returns every spin); `longestStreak(results)` → `{ kind, length, end }` (the latest of the longest); `dueRight(wheel, racers)` → a `Kind` or `'same'`; `STREAK = 5`, `STREAK_CAP = 240`.

### Level 11, The hidden spinner

- **A set.** An eight-wedge, two-critter wheel with the favourite's `k ∈ {2, 4, 6}` wedges, **under a cover**: a dome painted with question marks hides the wedges, and a window at the pointer shows only the wedge the wheel stops on (the landing critter pops up there and is spoken). The round's two sets draw two **different** `k`; the friend is redrawn. She is told both critters are on the wheel.
- **The runs.** Three runs on the level-6 strip, each started by her tap on the spinner or the fast-forward button: spins 1–8 (one by one), 9–40 and 41–160 (fast-forward), 160 in all. The strip's ruler has **four** ticks (quarters) and **no marker**: where the wheel's share lies is the question. Counts are spoken after each run, as at level 6.
- **The question.** "What does the strip say? About how much of the wheel is Duck's?" on three cards, each a small wheel with a quarter, a half or three quarters filled in the favourite's colour and face: *about a quarter*, *about half*, *about three quarters*. Right is `readCards(count, 160)`: the card nearest the strip's share (`count / 160` against 1/4, 1/2, 3/4); a strip exactly halfway between two cards (60 or 100 of 160) accepts **both**.
- **The reveal.** The cover lifts and the wedges are counted aloud. When the wheel matches the card the strip pointed to: "Duck has 2 of the 8 wedges: a quarter." When it does not: "Duck has 4 of the 8 wedges. The run said something else: that's unusual, and you read the strip right." The reveal never judges her.
- **Helpers.** `hiddenSet(rng, fav, used)`; `HIDDEN_K = [2, 4, 6]`; `HIDDEN_RUNS = [8, 40, 160]`; `readCards(count, total)` (one or two of 2, 4, 6); `revealMatches(card, k)`.

- **Touch-down feedback.** As built: the spinner jiggles, cards and buttons lift, a dragged wedge lifts at once; a tapped critter box bounces as its wedge arrives.

## Accounting, help and finishing

| Level | Counts a miss | Counts a hint | Never counted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 9 | a Check on a wheel that is not `k` | the glow after the second miss in a set, or asked-for help; at most one per set | every wedge move (drag or tap), the proof run, its count and whether it is *unusual* |
| 10 | a card that is not `dueRight` | the same, at most one per set | every spin of the run, how long the streak took, the streak's critter, the next spin |
| 11 | a card not in `readCards` | the same, at most one per set | every spin and run, what the cover reveals, whether the wheel matches the strip |

- **Spoken reasons.** Level 9: "Count {kind}'s wedges. {n} out of every {of}: how many of the eight is that?" Level 10: "Look at the wheel, not the run. Who has more wedges?" Level 11: "Look where the strip stops on the ruler."
- **Help.** After the second miss in a set, or on the pause sheet's *Show me* (`askForHint`): level 9 marks the target on the wheel's rim (an arc over `k` wedges) and glows the critter `nextRig` names; level 10 glows the wheel's two sections with their counts and the right card; level 11 glows the strip's end against the ruler and the card or cards it reads to. Asked again while the glow is on, the ghost finger makes the move or taps the card: the supported finish, still one hint. No hint names a spin's outcome as the answer; level 11's hint names the strip, which is the picture.
- **Finishing.** The round ends after the second set (level 9: its proof run; level 10: its next spin; level 11: its reveal) with `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` and one ordinary sticker, help or not. A run, a streak or a reveal never gives, takes or unlocks anything.
- **Adaptation.** As built: two smooth rounds step up, two struggling rounds step down, clamped to school's range. Nothing claims she understands probability or independence; it claims she made a wheel to a stated share, answered from the wheel after a streak, and read a share from a long run.
- **Grown-up wording** (`describeLevel` and the page): "makes a spinner with a share the pet asks for", "says what is likely next from the spinner, not from what just happened", "reads a hidden spinner's share from many spins". "Rig it" is the level's name only; no line calls a spinner cheating, lucky or due.

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Plans and bands:** levels 9–11 exist with the asks above; `school` ends at the built top; levels 1–8 unchanged (their tests stand).
- **Wheel size:** `spin(rng)` and `spin(rng, 6)` draw the same from the same seed, as do `raceWheel` and `wedgeAt` with and without the default; an eight-wedge stop is inside its wedge by at least 9°; over 80 000 eight-wedge spins each wedge is within 1 point of an eighth.
- **Level 9:** each set starts 4–4 on eight wedges with two critters; set one's `k ∈ {1, 3, 5, 7}`, set two's `k ∈ {2, 6}`; the spoken `n` out of `of` equals `k/8` in lowest terms; `rigRight` accepts exactly the wheels with `k` and nothing else; from 4–4, repeated `nextRig` reaches the target in `|k − 4|` moves and `give` never leaves a critter without a wedge; over 2 000 seeds the proof run is `inBand` in at least 97% of sets for each `k` (measured, never enforced; 98% at `k = 3` and `5`, higher elsewhere).
- **Level 10:** a round holds one even and one uneven set; `streakRun` ends at the first spin where the last five agree, or at 240, and draws only through `spin(rng)`; over 20 000 seeds it ends by a streak in at least 99.9% on the even wheel and takes a median of 20–26 spins there; `longestStreak` returns the latest of the longest and equals the trailing five whenever the run ended by a streak; `dueRight` is `'same'` exactly on 3–3 and otherwise the critter with more wedges, for every wheel and **every** streak (the answer never depends on the results); the next spin is drawn after the question and is the stream's next draw.
- **Level 11:** each set's wheel has eight wedges, two critters, `k ∈ {2, 4, 6}`; a round's two `k` differ; `readCards` returns the nearest of a quarter, a half and three quarters, both at exactly 60 or 100 of 160, and never none; over 2 000 seeds `readCards` holds the wheel's own share in at least 99% of sets for each `k` (it documents how often the reveal says *unusual*); the right card is computed from the count alone and never from `k`.
- **Help:** every glow names a wedge arc, a critter box, the wheel's sections, the strip or a card, never a spin's outcome; following it from any state reaches a right answer; at most one hint per set.
- **Layout:** each level's layout at the lands test's four views: every card, button, the spinner and the critter boxes at least 100 units; nothing under the pet or corner buttons; portrait stacks rather than shrinks; the ribbon and the strip never overlap a card.

## Build slices and check tiers

Each is one level, one session, in order:

1. **CS3a, level 9 Rig it** (new levels on an existing interaction, level 5's wedges and level 6's strip, but the wheel functions change for every level): typecheck, all the game's rule tests, one browser play at level 9 with one wrong Check and the proof run, a regression play of `spinnerfair45` (the six-wedge editor it shares), `fingerdemo` at 9, `npm run build` (voice and how-to change).
2. **CS3b, level 10 Is it due?**, after CS3a (taps, cards and a fast-forward: existing interactions; the ribbon is display): typecheck, rule tests, one browser play at 10 with one wrong card on each set, `fingerdemo` at 10, `npm run build`; the source check above.
3. **CS3c, level 11 The hidden spinner**, after CS3b (existing interactions; the cover is new art): typecheck, rule tests, one browser play at 11 with one wrong card, one portrait screenshot of the covered wheel, `fingerdemo` at 11, `npm run build`; the source check above.

Measured for this definition (a simulation of honest spins, 200 000 runs each, not the game's code): on a 3–3 wheel a run of five arrives after a median of 23 spins (mean 31, 95% within 85) and by 240 spins in 99.98% of runs; on a 4–2 wheel the median is 14 (95% within 49). Exact binomial figures: the 80-spin proof run is within ten of `10 · k` in 98.5% of runs at `k = 3` or `5`, 99.4% at `k = 2` or `6` and 99.9% at `k = 1` or `7`. At level 11's 160 spins the strip reads to the wheel's own card (a halfway strip included) in 99.9% of runs at `k = 4` and 99.98% at `k = 2` or `6`; at 80 spins the half wheel would read to another card alone in 1.8% of runs and sit halfway in another 1.5%.

Not run in this docs slice: everything but `node scripts/check-links.mjs` and `git diff --check`. Not established: any child's or educator's reading of the level-10 lines; whether a streak found by waiting reads as a surprise or as a wait; whether a share read from a strip without a marker is clear on a phone.

## Open decisions

Each has a default the builds use; none blocks CS3a.

1. **Three levels, not two.** Default: 9, 10, 11 as above. The alternative keeps the study's numbering by making the hidden spinner the last set of level 10's round, at the cost of a level with two asks.
2. **Level 9's start.** Default: always 4–4, so it opens as level 5's fair wheel. A seeded uneven start is a later variant.
3. **The streak's length.** Default five (the study's). Four arrives sooner (a median of 11 spins) but is less striking.
4. **Level 10's uneven set.** Default: one even, one uneven. Dropping the uneven set makes *the same* the answer every time.
5. **Level 11's spins.** Default 160. Eighty is quicker, but the half wheel then reads as another card, or sits halfway to one, in about 3% of honest runs.
