# Keep House, levels 1–4 (definition)

Defined 2026-10-27 as slice ML1 of the [medieval-life study](../deep-dives/subjects/medieval-life.md#build-slices-in-order) ([packet](../deep-dives/subjects/RECOMMENDATIONS.md#medieval-life-recommendations)), [PR #1017](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1017). The study's planning labels A, 1, 2 and 3 become saved levels **1, 2, 3 and 4** here, so no level is ever added below a saved one. Decisions 1–2 were taken 2026-10-23 on the developer's delegation: the castle is a home and a workplace (walls and a gate are drawn and never used; no weapon, dungeon or siege at any band), Cozy Village if the lands test passes (else Story Grove), and a first build of these four levels only. **Levels 1–4 built 2026-10-27** ([PR #1021](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1021)). ML2 built levels 1–4 as one [batch](../GAME-BATCHES.md#keep-house-1-whos-at-the-door-who-works-where-the-cooks-walk-and-fit-the-household); levels 5–6 (ML3) and Market Days (ML4) are separate definitions.

**Fun action and purpose:** put the household where its work needs it. She taps a castle door and the cook comes out with her pot; she drags the horse to the stable and it eats; she puts the kitchen down and watches whether the soup reaches the hall hot or cold; she fits five rooms to five spoken needs and rings the bell to check. Every level ends with **people at work in the right place**, and the castle is the island's own, "like the castles people built long ago." Learning goal: a castle was a home and a workplace; where a room goes has a reason (who walks where, what must stay cool, where the horses come in); a plan can be checked against stated needs. This is not matching alone: Habitat Helpers owns sorting creatures by need, Story Steps owns ordering a pictured sequence, and the treehouse `RoomScene` owns arranging the pet's room freely. Keep House places rooms and people by *work*, with a stated rule set and, at level 4, a solver.

## Overlap and reuse

- **Identity check** (the study's condition): a new activity is justified only if *placing a household by its work* is central. It is from level 2 on: level 2 matches a worker to the workplace its tool shows, level 3 makes the placement the whole decision (the walk shows the consequence), level 4 fits five rooms to five needs with a bell that checks. If ML2 finds level 2 is Habitat Helpers with doors, that level becomes a content note there and the saved ladder keeps its numbers (level 2 is then a short door-and-yard warm-up, never renumbered); if the cook's walk and the bell are not what holds a child, the packet is withdrawn rather than built thin.
- **Habitat Helpers / Critter Sort / Little Helpers** own sorting by need and matching helpers; level 2 is one workplace per figure, read from the tool it carries. **Toy Drawer, Unpack the Room** (notebook) and the treehouse room own free arranging; nothing here is free, nothing is kept. **Block Tower** owns stacking (Keystone is its mode, Water Road AM1's rule). **Market Stall, Clock Tower, Lemonade Stand** own the town; this is the castle. **Household Casebook / Attic Finds** own claims and sources; the ruin's plan (level 5) and *What can a plan prove?* (level 7) are ML3 and ML8, not here.
- Reuse `draggable` from `src/engine/drag.ts` for figures and room tiles (`onDrop` returns true when a room or cell takes the thing; a refused drop floats home, which is the study's "walks back"); `onTap` for doors, the well, need cards, Keep and the bell; Duck Pond's pattern for one big target that reacts on touch-down; Helper House's scene-owned step player for the walks (the cook's soup, the horses from the gate, the household moving in); Pet Kitchen's pattern for a tray of things that go back to the tray; Ferry Jam's pattern for a bot that finishes through the same inputs. Figures are code-drawn in `src/art/` (stone, thatch, straw and fire from `src/art/palette.ts`; no sepia filter, no "olde" lettering). No new engine, save field, render texture or shared helper.
- **History on screen:** the castle, its people and its rooms are the island's own. No real castle, lord, family, place name, battle, weapon or belief is drawn or spoken (decision 1 and the study's boundaries). The one historical line (below) is checked against the castle and KS1 sources the study opened in the building session, as the 2026-10-23 decision replaced the named reviewer.

## The model (`logic.ts`)

Four plans, each a finite seeded draw with `ctx.rng`. Cosmetic draws (which doors are which, which side the near spot is on, the hens' colours) never change a rule. No physics, no timer: every walk waits for her.

**The household** (`FIGURES`): six, each with a tool and one workplace: the cook (a pot, the kitchen with its hearth), the groom (a hay fork, the stable with its mangers), the ringer (a hand bell, the bell loft), the steward (a ring of keys, the storeroom with its barrels), the horse (the stable), the hens (the hen-yard with its scratching ground). `workplaceOf(figure)` is a fixed table, one room per figure; the groom and the horse share the stable, so a yard never draws both.

### Level 1, Who's at the door (`doors`)

- **Plan:** the castle front fills the stage: `doors` 2 at toddler, 3 at preschool (the plan takes the band), each with a seeded person behind it drawn from the cook, groom, ringer and steward, plus the well. State: `opened` (which doors), `bucket` (up or down).
- **Flow:** `tapDoor(i)`: on touch-down the door swings open and the person steps out with their tool and a sound (the pot clanks, the fork rustles the hay, the bell rings, the keys jingle); the pet names the job ("The cook! She makes the soup"). Tapping an opened door again: the person waves (free). `tapWell()`: the bucket comes up with a splash and goes back down, as often as she likes. Once every door is open the people wave together and the round finishes; the well is never required.
- **Invariants:** exactly `doors` distinct door taps reach the finish from any state; the well and repeat taps change nothing that counts.
- **Accounting:** nothing counts. `misses` and `hints` are always 0. If no tap comes for about eight seconds, the next closed door glows (entry guidance, as Sorting Machine level 1 and Helper House level 1 do); it records nothing, which is the study's "levels A and 2 count nothing" rule.
- **Rounds:** one castle front per round.

### Level 2, Who works where (`workplaces`)

- **Plan:** two yards per round. A yard draws three figures whose workplaces differ (never the groom and the horse together) and shows exactly their three rooms, open to the yard in a seeded order. State: `placed` (figure → room), `wrongDrops` (per figure), `misses`, `hint` (spent or not).
- **Flow:** the figures wait in the yard. Drag a figure to a room. `drop(figure, room)`: if `room === workplaceOf(figure)` it is taken: the figure walks in and starts the job (the pot bubbles, the horse eats, the hens scratch, the bell swings, the keys open a barrel), the pet names it, and it stays. Otherwise the drop is refused: the pet says why in the figure's own terms ("The horse can't cook!" / "Hens don't ring the bell"), the figure floats home to the yard, and `wrongDrops[figure]++`. A drop on no room floats home and is never counted. A placed figure cannot be picked up again (its work has started); nothing needs undoing because a placement is only ever right.
- **Accounting:** the **first** wrong drop of each figure is free (she is reading the rooms); from a figure's **second** wrong drop on, each is one **miss**. When a yard's misses reach two, the right room of the figure she holds (or, if none, the next figure in yard order) **glows** and `hints++` once for that yard; the glow retargets to the figure she picks up and stays until the yard is done, so following it always finishes. Asked-for help (the Help button) is the same glow and the same single count per yard.
- **Finish:** the second yard's third figure at work. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` with the round's totals.

### Level 3, The cook's walk (`walk`)

- **Plan:** two placements per round, in order: the **kitchen** (the hall is fixed; the soup goes kitchen → hall) and the **stable** (the gate is fixed; the horses go gate → stable). Each placement offers two empty spots on a seeded side: `near` is 1–2 walking steps from the fixed room, `far` is 4–6 steps across the yard; the plan proves `far > near` and that `near <= HOT_STEPS < far` with `HOT_STEPS = 3`. State: `spot` (none, near or far), `refusals`, `hints`.
- **Flow:** drag the room tile to either spot (both at least 100 units; a drop anywhere else floats home, free). The walk plays: from the near spot the cook arrives with the soup steaming and the hall cheers (or the horses trot in and eat); from the far spot the steam is gone by the second step, the cook sighs ("Cold again!"; the horses plod in and lie down). **Either spot finishes the placement.** After a cold arrival the pet asks "Where would it stay hot?", the tile lifts back to her hand (both spots open again) and a **Keep it** control (at least 100 units) appears. Moving to the near spot replays the walk hot and finishes. Keep it once: the walk replays cold and the pet asks once more. Keep it twice: the near spot glows, `hints++` once for that placement, the pet says "Next to the hall, it stays hot!", and the placement finishes as she left it. Her choice stands; the glow only shows the reason.
- **Accounting:** a placement is **never a miss**; `misses` is always 0. Only the second refusal counts, one hint per placement at most. Asked-for help glows the near spot before she has placed and is that placement's one hint.
- **Finish:** the stable placement's walk (hot, cold or kept). `ctx.finish({ misses: 0, hints })`.

### Level 4, Fit the household (`plan`)

- **Plan:** two plans per round. A plan is a **3 × 3 grid of large tiles** with two fixed features placed by the seed: the **well** and the **gate** (the gate on an edge cell, the well never beside it on the same edge), a **cool corner** (one corner cell, shaded by the wall, neither fixed feature) and a **sunny side** (one edge row or column of three cells, not the gate's edge). Five room tiles wait in a tray: the **hall** (a long table and fire), the **kitchen** (a hearth), the **stable** (mangers), the **storeroom** (barrels) and the **solar** (a window seat). The five **needs**, each shown as a two-picture card and spoken by the pet on request: (1) the kitchen touches the hall (shares an edge); (2) the kitchen is near the well (shares an edge or a corner); (3) the stable is by the gate (shares an edge); (4) the storeroom is in the cool corner (that cell); (5) the solar is on the sunny side (any of its three cells). The solver `solutions(plan)` enumerates every placement of the five rooms into the seven free cells (7 · 6 · 5 · 4 · 3 = 2,520) and keeps those meeting all five needs; the generator redraws any seed with fewer than **two** solutions.
- **Flow:** drag a room from the tray onto any empty cell; drag a placed room to another empty cell, onto another room (they swap) or back to the tray, all free. Tap a need card to hear it again, free. The **Finish bell** (at least 100 units, off the pet) is the one commit and is enabled once all five rooms are on the plan. `check(layout)` returns the unmet needs in order.
- **Misses and help:** each unmet need at a bell press is one **miss**, named by the pet with the rooms involved jiggling ("The cook has a long walk to the well"; "The storeroom is in the sun"). An empty bell press (a room still in the tray) is not a miss: the tray wobbles. When a plan's misses reach two, one **hint** counts for that plan: `hintFor(layout)` takes the first step of a shortest move sequence to any solution (a move is one room to an empty cell, one swap, or one room back to the tray and another in; breadth-first over the 2,520 layouts), and glows that room and its target cell; after each of her moves the glow recomputes until a solution is laid out, so following it always reaches the bell with nothing unmet. The glow never moves a room for her. Asked-for help is the same glow and the same one count per plan.
- **Finish:** the second plan's bell with no unmet need. The household walks in to its rooms (the cook to the hearth, the horse to the mangers, the steward to the barrels) and the round finishes. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`.

### Bands and ranges

`BandLevels`: `toddler: { min: 1, max: 1 }`, `preschool: { min: 1, max: 3 }`, `prek: { min: 2, max: 4 }`, `school: { min: 4, max: 4 }`. No lap window (the study: toddler A is the first rung; a one-year-old has Peekaboo Barn's doors already). School's single level is the deliberate start of the study's 3–6 window so that ML3 can append 5–6 without renumbering; a school child who reaches Keep House today plays level 4 and the pin stays valid afterwards. No `order`.

## Screen and controls

- **Stage.** Landscape: the castle front, the yard with its rooms, or the plan centred and large; the tray of figures or rooms above it; the need cards down the right; the pet bottom-left and corner buttons clear. Portrait stacks the tray above the plan above the bell and keeps the plan centred, as the study asks. Every input (door, well, figure, room tile, spot, cell, need card, Keep it, Help, bell) is at least 100 logical units after scaling; doors are planned at 150 × 200, figures at 120 × 140, grid cells at 120 × 120 (the plan 360 × 360 plus gutters), tray tiles at 110 × 110, spots at 130 × 130, need cards at 220 × 100, the bell at 140 × 110. Walls, the gate's arch and the hills are `eventMode = 'none'`.
- **Touch-down feedback.** A door starts to swing, a figure lifts and rides 40 units above the finger, a cell or spot brightens under a held tile, and the bell rocks on touch-down, before any rule runs; a refused drop still shows that it was felt (the room shakes its head as the figure floats home).
- **Speech.** `ctx.instruct` states the level's one ask ("Tap a door and see who comes out" / "Take each one to where its work is" / "Put the kitchen where the soup stays hot" / "Fit the rooms so every need is met, then ring the bell"); the pet repeats it. Needs are spoken as fixed lines with the two pictures shown, never a template with free text.
- **Feel.** `STYLES.hub` (Cozy Village's Mail Carrier uses it; she is not making music); a pentatonic note from `src/audio/notes.ts` per door, placement and met need, a lower one for a cold arrival, and the castle's own bell as the Finish sound effect. Palette stone, thatch, straw and fire; no sepia or texture filter. Reduced motion shows arrivals in place without the walk.
- **The one history line**, spoken once at the start of levels 2–4 and printed on the card: "This is the island's castle, like the castles people built long ago: a home, and a workplace for everyone in it." It names no place, family or war, matches the castle-as-home framing of the KS1 lesson and castle pages the study opened, and is checked again in the building session.

## Accounting, help and finishing

| Level | Counts a miss | Counts a hint | Never counted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | nothing | nothing | every tap, the idle glow, the well |
| 2 | each wrong drop of a figure after its first | the glow when a yard's misses reach two, or asked-for help; at most one per yard | a figure's first wrong drop, a drop on no room, repeating the instruction |
| 3 | nothing | the second Keep it on one placement, or asked-for help; at most one per placement | either spot, the first Keep it, the replayed walk |
| 4 | each unmet need at a bell press | the glow when a plan's misses reach two, or asked-for help; at most one per plan | moving, swapping or returning rooms, hearing a need again, an empty bell press |

- **Supported finish:** at levels 2 and 4 the glow always leads to a finished yard or a plan that meets every need, and at level 3 either spot finishes, so every round ends with one ordinary sticker through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`, help or not. No creation, discovery or journal entry in the first build (a kept plan in the treehouse is a later creations question, as the study says). Leaving early owes nothing.
- **Adaptation:** levels 1 and 3 are always smooth on misses (two rounds step up). At level 2 a child who misdrops each figure once is still smooth (first drops are free); at level 4 one bell press with one unmet need, fixed, is smooth. Two hints or four misses are struggling. Assisted completion is one sticker, not evidence of mastery. Nothing here claims a four-year-old understands why a kitchen stood near a hall; it claims she placed it, watched the walk and heard the reason.

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Plans:** over 300 seeds per level, level 1 draws 2 doors at toddler and 3 at preschool with distinct people; level 2 draws two yards of three figures with three distinct workplaces, never the groom with the horse; level 3 draws two placements with `near` 1–2, `far` 4–6, `near <= HOT_STEPS < far`; level 4 draws two plans whose well, gate, cool corner and sunny side obey the placement rules above and whose solver finds at least two solutions. The same seed gives the same plan.
- **The household:** `workplaceOf` is total and one room per figure; every room shown in a yard is the workplace of exactly one figure in that yard.
- **Level 1 invariants:** exactly `doors` distinct door taps finish; repeats and the well change nothing; `misses` and `hints` stay 0.
- **Level 2:** `drop(f, r)` is taken iff `r === workplaceOf(f)`; a refused drop leaves `placed` unchanged; the first wrong drop of a figure is not a miss, the second is; a yard's hint counts at most once and the glow names the held or next figure's workplace; following the glow from any state finishes.
- **Level 3:** `arrives(steps)` is `hot` iff `steps <= HOT_STEPS`; the near spot arrives hot and the far one cold for every seed; a placement never counts a miss; `refuse()` twice counts exactly one hint and finishes; a move to the near spot after a cold arrival counts nothing.
- **Level 4:** `check(layout)` returns exactly the unmet needs, in order, for authored layouts of each kind (a kitchen across the plan from the hall, a storeroom in the sun, a solved plan); `solutions(plan)` is at least two for every seed and every solution meets all five needs; `hintFor(layout)` from every reachable layout (sampled: 200 random layouts per seed over 50 seeds) returns a move that strictly shortens the distance to the nearest solution, so following it reaches one in at most as many moves as rooms; an empty bell press is not a miss; a plan's hint counts at most once.
- **Layout:** `keepLayout(level, view)` at the four views the lands test uses (landscape, portrait, two phone insets): every input at least 100 units, nothing under the bottom-left pet or corner buttons, portrait stacks rather than shrinks, cells, tray and need cards do not overlap.
- **Registry, lands, how-to and off-screen tests** pass with the new game: Cozy Village pages at `LAND_SPOTS` (17 games today, 18 once Caravan Days is built, 19 with this one), the card scoped per level, no "later levels" wording.

## Open decisions

Each has a default the build uses; none blocks ML2.

1. **A free first wrong drop at level 2.** Default: the first wrong room per figure is reading, not a miss (the study: "a miss from the second try on the same figure"). The alternative (every wrong drop counts) makes a preschool rung stricter than Pet Kitchen's.
2. **"Near the well."** Default: an edge or a corner shared with the well's cell, so "near" is looser than "touches" and the two kitchen needs feel different. The alternative (edge only) makes some seeds have no solution and is rejected by the generator anyway.
3. **Keep it at level 3.** Default: a visible Keep it control, so refusing is a choice she makes, not a timeout. The alternative (the second cold walk finishes on its own after a pause) removes the control but turns a decision into waiting.
4. **School window.** Default: `school: { 4, 4 }` now, extended by ML3. The alternative of leaving school out until ML3 would still play level 4 through `playBand`'s fallback but would leave no school pin space.
5. **Land.** Default: Cozy Village (19th game with Caravan Days, two pages). Fallback by decision 2: Story Grove.
6. **The history line.** Default: the single sentence above, source-checked in the building session (the castle pages and the KS1 "Homes in the Past" lesson the research opened). If the check wants it shorter, it becomes "This is the island's castle, like the castles people built long ago" with the home-and-workplace clause left to the pet's door lines.

## Levels 5–6 (ML3): read the ruin, stone or timber

Defined 2026-10-30 as slice ML3 of the [study](../deep-dives/subjects/medieval-life.md#build-slices-in-order), [PR #1034](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1034). The study's planning labels 4 (*Read the plan*) and 5 (*Stone or timber*) become saved levels **5** and **6**, appended above level 4 without renumbering, as the [overlap note](#overlap-and-reuse) already reserved level 5 for the ruin; *What can a plan prove?* stays ML8 and would be saved level 7. Decision 4 (a named reviewer) was replaced on 2026-10-23 by a source check in the building session, which applies to every historical line below. Both levels are school only; level 6 is the study's upper-elementary rung inside `school`, chosen by level, not a new band.

**Fun action and purpose:** level 5: the castle has lost its roofs. She looks into each roofless room (a wide hearth, a round bread oven, stone mangers, a window seat, steps going down) and drags a picture label (a table, a pot, hay, a bed, a chest) onto the room its remains say it was; when a label is right, the room's people come back for a moment and get on with their work. Learning goal: remains are evidence; one clue can fit two rooms (the hall and the kitchen both had a fire), so a second clue decides. Level 6: a new lord shows a purse of bags and how many years they will live here; she builds the hall in **timber** or **stone** and watches the years go by: the timber hall goes up in a season and rots, so it is built again; the stone hall takes years of masons and carts and then stands. Learning goal: what a building costs depends on how long it must last and what the builder can pay; a choice has costs you can count over time.

### Levels 5–6: overlap and the identity check

- **Household Casebook / Attic Finds** own claims, sources and "what can we tell from this"; **Picture Logic** and **Detective** grids own deduction from stated clues. Level 5 is Keep House's only because the clues are **the remains of the work the child has already placed** at levels 2–4 (the hearth the cook stood at, the mangers the horse ate from), read on the same 3 × 3 plan, and because the answer is a room of the household, not a suspect or a claim. **Withdraw clause:** if the build finds level 5 plays as a generic clue grid (the child matches icons to icons without the rooms meaning anything), the rung goes to Household Casebook as a case and level 5 is withdrawn rather than built thin; the saved ladder then ends at 4 or 6 and is never renumbered.
- **Lemonade Stand** owns a week of choices with a running result; **Market Stall** owns coins, paying and change. Level 6's bags are **counters, never coin denominations**: one bag is one unit; there is no paying, no change and no fewest-coins question. **Nothing accrues:** the purse belongs to the situation on screen, is cleared at the next situation, and nothing is saved, as Caravan Days' shells are.
- **Block Tower** owns stacking and *Keystone*; **Water Road** owns the arch. Nothing at level 6 is stacked or simulated: a building is a picture that stands, rots or waits for its next bag.
- Reuse level 4's board (`Board`, the 3 × 3 cells, the tray and its `draggable` handles), the room tiles' drawn features from `art.ts` (with their roofs and tops left off for the ruin), level 3's consequence grammar (`keep()`: a choice plays out, the pet asks once, a second Keep it counts one hint) and its scene-owned step player for the years. No new engine, save field, render texture or shared helper.

### Level 5, Read the ruin (`ruin`)

- **Rooms and labels:** the five rooms of level 4 (`PLAN_ROOMS`: hall, kitchen, stable, storeroom, solar). Each has a **label**, a picture card in the tray (110 × 110): the hall a long table with cups, the kitchen a pot, the stable hay, the storeroom a chest, the solar a bed. Labels are pictures; the pet names a label when it is picked up.
- **Clues** (`CLUES`, a fixed table, code-drawn remains in a roofless cell): `hearth` (a wide fireplace), `oven` (a round bread oven), `dais` (a raised step for the high table), `drain` (a channel through the wall), `mangers` (a stone trough row), `wide-door` (a door wide enough for a horse), `window-seat` (stone seats in a window), `cellar-steps` (steps going down). `cluesOf(room)` is fixed and true for every plan: hall `{hearth, dais}`; kitchen `{hearth, oven, drain}`; stable `{mangers, wide-door, drain}`; storeroom `{cellar-steps}`; solar `{window-seat}`. **Shared clues** are those in two rooms' sets (`hearth`: hall and kitchen; `drain`: kitchen and stable); every other clue is **deciding** for its one room.
- **Plan:** two ruins per round. A ruin draws **four** of the five rooms into four of the nine cells (the well and gate drawn as at level 4, never a room's cell); the tray always holds **all five labels**, so the last label is never forced and one stays in the tray. Each ruined room **shows** a seeded subset of its clues, drawn small inside its cell (up to three): always **at least one deciding clue**, plus none, one or both of its shared clues. The generator redraws until **at least one shared clue is shown in two rooms of the same ruin** (the study's shared-clue rule: the hall and the kitchen both show a hearth, or the kitchen and the stable both show a drain), so every ruin has a clue that alone does not decide. `decidingClue(ruin, room)` is the first deciding clue the room shows, in table order.
- **Flow:** **tap a room** (the whole 120 × 120 cell, one target): free; the cell brightens and the pet names what is left in it ("A wide hearth, and a bread oven"). Tapping a shown shared clue's room never names the room. **Drag a label onto a room** (the commit): `label(ruin, state, labelId, cell)`. If the room in that cell is the label's room, the label settles on the cell, the room's people come back for a moment (the cook at the oven, the horse at the mangers, the steward with a chest), the pet names the deciding clue as the reason ("A bread oven: this was the kitchen"), and it stays. Otherwise the label floats home and the pet names the deciding clue of **that room** without naming the room ("This room has stone mangers"); the cell shakes. That is one **miss**. A drop on a cell that is already labelled, on the well, the gate or empty ground floats home and is never counted. A settled label cannot be picked up again (a label is only ever settled when right).
- **Help:** after a ruin's second miss, the **deciding clue** of the room the held label belongs to (or, holding nothing or the spare label, the first unlabelled room in cell order) glows inside its cell with the label in the tray, and `hints++` once for that ruin; the glow retargets to whatever she picks up next and stays until the ruin is done, so following it always finishes. Holding the spare label (the room not on this ruin), the glow shows the tray: "That room isn't in this castle." Asked-for help is the same glow and the same one count per ruin; a second press repeats it free.
- **Finish:** the second ruin's fourth label settled. The household walks back into all four rooms for a moment, then the ruin is quiet again. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` with the round's totals.
- **Model:** `drawRuin(rng)` returns `{ board, rooms: { room, cell, shown }[] }`; `cluesOf`, `isShared(clue)`, `decidingClue`, `label(ruin, state, labelId, cell)` returning `{ settled: true }`, `{ settled: false; missed: true; clue }` or `{ settled: false; missed: false }` (not a room, or already labelled); `ruinHint(ruin, state, held)` returning `{ cell, clue, label }` or `{ spare: true }`.

### Level 6, Stone or timber (`build`)

- **The two ways to build** (`BUILDS`, a fixed table shown as a two-row picture card the whole level): **timber** costs **2 bags**, goes up in **one season**, and lasts **15 years**, after which it has rotted and must be built again (2 more bags); **stone** costs **5 bags** (shown as a row of masons and carts, one picture per bag), takes **8 years** while the household lives in a small timber shelter (drawn, free), and then **stands** for the whole stay. "Burns" appears only as a kitchen fire risk on the grown-up card; nothing is attacked.
- **Situations:** two per round. A situation is a lord (an island critter from `src/art/critter.ts`, seeded) with a **purse** of `bags` and a **stay** of `years`, drawn as a bag row and a year bar (one mark per 5 years). Timber's total over a stay is `2 × ceil(years / 15)`; stone's is 5 if the purse holds 5, else stone **stops** when the bags run out (walls half up). One situation per round is a **timber fit** and one a **stone fit**, in seeded order:
  - **Timber fit:** `years` ∈ {10, 15, 20, 25, 30} (timber 2 or 4 bags; every stay is longer than stone's 8 building years, so stone always finishes when it is affordable), `bags` from timber's total up to 6, so timber is always affordable: below 5 stone is unaffordable, at 5–6 it is affordable but costs more over the stay.
  - **Stone fit:** `years` ∈ {35, 40, 45, 50} (timber 6 or 8 bags), `bags` ∈ {5, 6, 7, 8} (stone affordable). If timber's total exceeds `bags`, timber's consequence runs out at a rebuild.
  - Timber's totals are even and stone's is 5, so the two totals never tie. The **fitting** choice is the one that is affordable and costs fewer bags over the stay; a test proves exactly one choice fits every drawn situation.
- **Flow:** **tap the timber or stone row** on the card: free look. The row lifts and its life is laid along the situation's year bar (timber's 15-year bars side by side until the stay is covered; stone's 8 building years, then one bar to the end), with no total spoken. **Tap Build** under a lifted row (the commit, at least 100 units): the years play in steps (each step a 5-year mark): bags leave the purse as each hall is built, a timber hall greys and sags at 15 years and is rebuilt if a bag pair is left, scaffolding stands while stone is built, and the household lives in the shelter meanwhile. The **account** row stays on screen at the end: bags spent and the hall at the end of the stay (standing, rotted, or half up).
  - **The fitting choice:** the household walks into a standing hall and cheers; the pet names the account ("Two bags, and the hall lasted"). The situation finishes.
  - **The other choice** still finishes the situation (a choice is never a miss); the pet names both accounts ("Timber cost six bags over forty years. Stone would have cost five"; or "The purse ran out, and the walls are half up"), asks "Which would you build for this lord?", and the card lifts back with **Build again** (free; the years replay with her new choice and finish) and a **Keep it** control (at least 100 units). Keep it once: the years replay and the pet asks once more. Keep it twice: the fitting row glows, `hints++` once for that situation, the pet names the reason ("Forty years is a long time for timber"), and the situation finishes as she left it. This is level 3's `keep()`, reused.
- **Help:** asked-for help before Build glows the fitting row and is that situation's one hint; a second press repeats it free.
- **Finish:** the second situation's account. `ctx.finish({ misses: 0, hints })`.
- **Model:** `drawSituation(rng, kind)` returns `{ lord, bags, years, kind }`; `account(situation, build)` returns `{ spent, end: 'standing' | 'rotted' | 'half-up', steps }` where `steps` lists each 5-year mark's purse and hall picture; `fits(situation)` returns `'timber' | 'stone'`; `placeBuild`/`keep` as level 3's `place`/`keep`.

### Levels 5–6: bands and ranges

`school: { min: 4, max: 6 }`; the other bands are unchanged (toddler 1, preschool 1–3, pre-K 2–4). No `order`: the ladder is still `min..max`. Nothing is renumbered, so a pinned level 4 stays level 4. `PlanId` grows to `'doors' | 'workplaces' | 'walk' | 'plan' | 'ruin' | 'build'`, `MAX_LEVEL` to 6, and `PLANS` gains 5 *Read the ruin: drag a picture label onto each roofless room its remains say it was (two ruins; a miss per wrong label)* and 6 *Stone or timber: build the hall for a lord's purse and stay, then watch the years (never a miss)*; `describeLevel` reads them.

### Levels 5–6: screen and controls

- **Level 5 stage.** As level 4: the 3 × 3 plan centred (cells 120 × 120, the plan 360 × 360 plus gutters), roofless rooms drawn as low broken walls in stone with their clues inside, the well and gate on their cells (`eventMode = 'none'`), empty cells as grass; the tray of five labels (110 × 110) above the plan in portrait and beside it in landscape. Clues are drawn at about 40–50 units inside a cell and are **not** separate targets; the cell is the one target for the free look and for a drop.
- **Level 6 stage.** Landscape: the lord, the purse (bags 60 units, a picture row, not a target) and the year bar across the top; the build card (timber and stone rows, each at least 300 × 110) at the left; the hall's site large at the right where the years play; Build, Build again and Keep it (each 140 × 110) under the card. Portrait stacks lord and bars, site, card, then the controls; nothing shrinks. Reduced motion shows each 5-year step as a still with no sag animation.
- **Touch-down feedback:** a cell brightens and a label lifts and rides 40 units above the finger; a build row lifts; Build squashes; a refused label still shows that it was felt (the cell shakes).
- **Speech.** `ctx.instruct`: level 5 "Look in each room and put its picture where the clues say it was"; level 6 "Build this lord's hall in timber or stone, then watch the years". Clue names, bag counts (1–8) and years (10–50 in fives) are spoken from bounded slots, never a template with free text.
- **Feel.** As levels 1–4: `STYLES.hub`, a pentatonic note per settled label and per year step, a lower note for a rot or an empty purse, the castle's own bell when the household walks in.
- **History lines**, printed on the card and spoken once at the start of their level, both naming no place, family or war (the island's castle only): level 5, "Long ago, when roofs fell in, the stones that stayed showed what each room was for"; level 6, "Long ago, a timber hall went up in a season, but a stone castle took most of ten years and many masons." The level 6 line rests on the castle page the study opened (a stone castle "took the best part of a decade to finish"; an average motte about 40 working days for 50 people); the level 5 line on the Nuffield lesson's "history detectives" reading plans. Both are checked again in the building session. **Fallbacks:** level 5's line becomes "The stones that stayed tell us which room was which"; level 6's becomes "Stone took much longer to build than timber, and lasted much longer."
- **Grown-up card only:** the real figures behind the masons-and-carts row (Beaumaris: about 400 masons and 2,000 other workers, with pay in arrears), with their uncertainty, as the study's boundary keeps named castles and figures off the child's screen.

### Levels 5–6: accounting

| Level | Counts a miss | Counts a hint | Never counted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | each label dropped on a room it was not | the glow after a ruin's second miss, or asked-for help; at most one per ruin | tapping a room to hear its clues, a drop on a labelled cell, the well, the gate or ground, the spare label staying in the tray |
| 6 | nothing | the second Keep it on one situation, or asked-for help; at most one per situation | tapping either row, the replayed years, Build again, the first Keep it |

- **Why the label is the commit at level 5:** the study lists "a label" among the commits that may count from level 3 on, and at school a drop is a deliberate claim about one room; a bell over the whole ruin would let her place four labels by elimination without reading a clue. The free look (tapping the cell) is what keeps trying-to-see welcome.
- **Supported finish:** the level 5 glow always leads to a finished ruin and level 6 always finishes, so every round ends with one ordinary sticker through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`. Nothing is kept: no plan, purse or account; no creation or journal entry. Leaving early owes nothing.
- **Adaptation:** a child who mislabels once in a round and then reads the clue is smooth; four misses or two hints in a round is struggling and steps down within school's 4–6. Level 6 is always smooth on misses. Assisted completion is one sticker, not evidence of mastery; level 5 claims she matched remains to rooms with a deciding clue spoken, not that she can read a real castle plan.

### Levels 5–6: rule tests

- **Plans (300 seeds each):** level 5 draws two ruins, each with four distinct rooms in four distinct non-feature cells, every room showing at least one deciding clue and only clues from its own set, at least one shared clue shown in two rooms; level 6 draws one timber-fit and one stone-fit situation with `years` and `bags` from the stated sets. Same seed, same plan.
- **Clues:** `cluesOf` is fixed; every room's set has at least one deciding clue; `hearth` and `drain` are the only shared clues; for every ruin and room, the room's shown clues are contained in **no other** room's set (the shown set identifies the room among all five labels); no room shows only shared clues.
- **Level 5:** `label` settles iff the label's room is in that cell; a wrong label is one miss and names that room's `decidingClue`, which it shows; drops on labelled, feature or empty cells change nothing; the spare label never settles; a ruin's hint counts at most once; following `ruinHint` from every reachable state (sampled: every label order on 50 seeds) finishes the ruin.
- **Level 6, enumerated:** for every drawn situation, `account(timber)` and `account(stone)` follow the stated costs (timber `2 × ceil(years / 15)` capped by the purse, stone 5 or `half-up` when `bags < 5`), the two totals differ, every number is within 1–8 bags and 10–50 years, `fits` names exactly one choice, it is affordable and its total is the smaller (or the only affordable one), and its end is `standing`; a choice never changes `misses`; the second `keep` counts exactly one hint and finishes; Build again with the fitting row counts nothing.
- **Layout:** `keepLayout` at levels 5 and 6 at the four test views: cells, labels, rows and controls at least 100 units, nothing under the pet or corner buttons, labels not over the plan, portrait stacks rather than shrinks, `null` rather than a shrunken target.
- **Registry and how-to tests** pass with school's new range and the new steps.

### Levels 5–6: open decisions

Each has a default the build uses; none blocks the build.

7. **Four rooms and a spare label.** Default: four rooms, five labels, so the last label is never forced. Alternative: five rooms on every ruin (the last label is then free).
8. **The label as the commit.** Default: each wrong label is a miss, as above. Alternative: the level 2 rule (a first wrong drop per label is free), which softens it toward a preschool rung.
9. **The free look at level 6.** Default: a lifted row is laid along the year bar with no total spoken. Alternative (easier): the look also speaks the total bags.
10. **Costs.** Default: timber 2 bags and 15 years, stone 5 bags and 8 years. They are island numbers, chosen so totals never tie and stay within eight bags; the grown-up card says the real costs varied widely.
11. **Real figures.** Default: the Beaumaris figures on the grown-up card only, with the masons-and-carts row as island pictures. Alternative: printed under the row at level 6, which the study's boundary keeps for the 11–14 rung.
12. **History lines.** As above, with the stated fallbacks after the building session's source check.
