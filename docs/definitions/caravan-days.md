# Caravan Days, levels 1–3 (definition)

Defined 2026-10-27 as slice CS1 of the [China, India and the Silk Road study](../deep-dives/subjects/china-india-silk-road.md#build-slices-in-order) ([packet](../deep-dives/subjects/RECOMMENDATIONS.md#china-india-and-the-silk-road-recommendations)), [PR #1016](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1016). Decisions 1–2 were taken 2026-10-23 on the developer's delegation: island oasis names on the child's screen, Cozy Village if the lands test passes (else Rainbow Meadow), and a first build of levels 1–3 only. **Levels 1–3 built 2026-10-27** ([PR #1020](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1020), [archived build note](../archive/COMPLETED-2026-10-09.md#caravan-days-levels-13-china-india-and-the-silk-road-cs2-2026-10-27)); CS2 built them as one [batch](../GAME-BATCHES.md#caravan-days-1-kneel-and-load-can-we-reach-it-and-day-by-day); levels 4–5 (CS3) are defined [below](#levels-45-cs3-swap-at-the-stop-worth-more-far-away) and **built 2026-10-30** ([PR #1037](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1037), [archived build note](../archive/COMPLETED-2026-10-09.md#caravan-days-levels-45-china-india-and-the-silk-road-cs3-2026-10-30)); levels 6–7 (CS4) are defined [below](#levels-67-cs4-mountains-or-sand-a-well-every-days-walk) (not built); 8 (CS5) and The Ledger (CS6) are separate definitions.

**Fun action and purpose:** pat a camel so it kneels, load it, and watch it sway down the road to the next water. At level 1 that is the whole game: load and walk, and the camel's long drink at the oasis is the ending. From level 2 the road shows **a sun for each day** and the camel's **waterskin holds two days**, so the question becomes *which water can we reach?*: one oasis of two, then a whole road planned a stop at a time so that no leg is longer than the skin. Learning goal: a journey is made of days; water has to be within reach every day; nobody went the whole way at once, so stopping places were a day apart and the road was walked a leg at a time. This is the island's first "the long road from the east, long ago" activity, and it is not routing alone: Mail Carrier owns *which house* and, at level 8, *which of two routes is shorter*; Treasure Map owns grids and counted directions; Relay Woods owns *who carries it next*. Caravan Days asks only whether the **next water is within the skin**, a counted limit on every leg, and later (CS3) what crosses hands at the stop.

## Overlap and reuse

- **Identity check** (the study's condition): a new activity is justified only if *the next stop within reach* and *what crosses hands there* are central. Levels 2–3 are the first half of that: every commit is "can the camel reach that water with the days in the skin?", never "which path is shorter" or "which house". The total length of a plan is never scored, and any plan whose every leg fits is right. If CS2 finds that level 3 plays as Hazel's two-stop plan with suns on it, the rungs go to Mail Carrier as a mode and this packet is withdrawn rather than built thin, as the study says.
- **Mail Carrier 7** supplies the planning idiom (tap stops in order, numbered flags appear, tapping a planned stop takes it and the later stops out, then Go); its map, houses and key are not reused. **Treasure Map** keeps grids: the caravan map is one drawn road with landmark oases (the study's map evidence). **Swap Meet / Market Stall** own trading and paying; nothing crosses hands before CS3. **Weather Wardrobe** owns dressing for the road (CS7).
- Reuse `onTap` for every control (there is no drag, no steering and no freehand); Duck Pond's pattern for one big target that reacts on touch-down; Helper House's scene-owned step player for the kneel, the hop, the walk and the drink; Mail Carrier's cart walk for a creature moving along a drawn path with the plan's flags; Ferry Jam's pattern for a bot that finishes through the same inputs. No new engine, save field, render texture or shared helper.
- **The camel** is code-drawn in the game's own `art.ts` (`src/art/critter.ts` has no camel and its parts do not fold): a tan body with two humps, a long neck that dips to drink, a head with a sleepy eye and long lashes, legs that fold under it when it kneels. If RW02b's illustrated pipeline is ready at CS2 a drawn camel may replace it; the inputs do not change.
- **History on screen:** the oases have island names (Palm Water, Red Rock Well, Little Spring, Blue Tile City); the road is "the long road from the east, long ago." No real city, people, date, the name "Silk Road" or any ruler is drawn or spoken (decision 1). The two historical lines (below) are checked against the sources the study opened in the building session, as the 2026-10-23 decision replaced the named reviewer.

## The model (`logic.ts`)

Three plans, each a finite seeded draw with `ctx.rng`. Cosmetic draws (which landmark each oasis wears, bale colours, dune shapes, whether the upper or lower branch is drawn first) never change a count. No physics, no timer: every walk waits for her tap.

### Level 1, Kneel and load (`load`)

- **Plan:** `bales` 2 at toddler, 3–4 at preschool (the plan takes the band). State: `kneeling`, `loaded` (bales on the camel), `walking`.
- **Flow:** the camel stands at the left of the road beside the bales; one oasis is in view at the right. The camel and each bale are the inputs. `tapCamel()`: standing and not loaded → it kneels with a grunt and the bales shuffle closer; kneeling with `loaded < bales` → it looks round at the ground and the pet says "One more bale!" (no change, nothing counted; the next bale on the ground glows once); kneeling with `loaded === bales` → it stands with bells and walks the road to the oasis, sways, and drinks for a long moment (the ending). `tapBale(i)`: camel standing → the bale wobbles and the camel glows ("Tap the camel so it kneels"), no change; kneeling and the bale on the ground → it hops onto the camel's back and the pet counts aloud ("one… two… three"); kneeling and the bale on the camel → it hops back off (free, so she can choose the order). A tap during the walk changes nothing.
- **Invariants:** `loaded <= bales` always; from any state, at most two camel taps plus `bales` bale taps reach the walk; nothing else is needed, so the finish is always reachable.
- **Accounting:** nothing counts. `misses` and `hints` are always 0. If no tap comes for about eight seconds, the next useful thing glows (the camel, then a bale, then the camel again): entry guidance, as Sorting Machine level 1 and Helper House level 1 do; it records nothing, which is the study's "level 1 counts nothing" rule, and the October 23 Relay Woods decision (a toddler glow counts one hint) does not apply, because this level has no answer to reveal.
- **Rounds:** one camel, one load, one walk per round.

### Level 2, Can we reach it? (`reach`)

- **Plan:** two roads per round. The road forks just ahead of the camel into two branches, each ending at a landmark oasis; `near` suns on one branch, `far` on the other; the **waterskin holds `skin = 2` days**. `near ∈ {1, 2}`, `far ∈ {3, 4}`, which branch is near is seeded. So exactly one oasis is within reach on every seed.
- **Flow:** the camel stands loaded at the fork; the suns are drawn on each branch, large and countable; the skin is a bar of two sun-sized drops beside the camel. The two oases are the inputs. Tapping a sun, a branch or the skin is a **free look**: the pet counts that branch's suns aloud ("one, two, three suns") or says "The skin holds two days"; nothing changes. `choose(branch)` is the one commit: `reachable` when `suns(branch) <= skin`: the camel walks the branch sun by sun, a drop leaving the skin each day, drinks for a long moment at the oasis (the skin refills) and the road ends. Otherwise `tooFar` with the difference: the camel walks `skin` suns, the skin runs empty, it shakes its head and walks back to the fork: one **miss**, named ("Three suns, but the skin holds two").
- **Help:** after the second miss on the same road, the reachable oasis glows and `hints++` once for that road. The glow stays until she taps it, so following it always ends the road. A glow never walks for her. Asked-for help (the pet's Help button) is the same glow and the same single count per road; a second press repeats it free. The second road starts with its own counts and its own one hint.
- **Finish:** the second road's drink. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` with the round's totals.

### Level 3, Day by day across the map (`plan`)

- **Plan:** two maps per round. A map is `home` at the left, `city` at the right, and between them **two branches** that leave `home` and rejoin at `city`, with 2–3 oases on each (4–6 oases in all). Adjacent stops are joined by a leg of 1 or 2 suns, except that **one branch holds exactly one leg of 3 suns** (seeded anywhere on that branch); the other branch's legs are all 1 or 2. The skin holds **2 days**. Adjacency is a small graph, so the study's BFS is real: `good` is the set of stops from which the city is reachable using only legs ≤ `skin`; the generator guarantees `home ∈ good` because the branch without the 3-sun leg reaches the city a stop at a time.
- **Flow:** she taps stops in order to build the plan; a numbered flag appears on each and the road between flags lights up with its suns (Mail Carrier 7's idiom). Tapping a flagged stop takes that flag and every later one out (free). Only a stop adjacent to the last flag, or one oasis beyond it along the same branch, can take a flag; a tap elsewhere wobbles the stop and the pet says "The road doesn't go straight there" (no change, nothing counted). A **leg** is the suns between two consecutive flags: between adjacent stops it is that road's suns; skipping an oasis joins two roads and adds their suns, so skipping is allowed exactly when two one-sun roads meet. Tapping a sun, a road or the skin is the same free look as level 2. **Go** is the one commit and is live only when the last flag is on the city; before that, Go says "Plan all the way to the city, then tap Go" (nothing counted). On Go the camel walks the plan leg by leg, a drop leaving the skin each day and the skin refilling at each flagged oasis.
- **Verdict:** `go(plan)` returns `arrived`, or `tooLong` with the index of the **first** leg whose suns exceed `skin`. `tooLong` is one **miss**: the camel walks that leg for `skin` suns, the skin runs empty, the pet counts the leg's suns aloud ("one, two, three suns, and the skin holds two"), the camel walks back to the last flag before that leg, and that flag and every later one stay while the over-long leg's destination flag and the flags after it come out, so she plans again from where the camel stands. Legs before the first over-long one are never re-walked.
- **Help:** after the second miss on the same map, `hintFor(plan)` glows and `hints++` once for that map. The glow is **the next stop on some valid plan**: if the plan already holds an over-long leg (help asked before Go), the first such leg's destination flag, to take it out; otherwise a neighbour of the last flagged stop reachable in ≤ `skin` suns that is in `good`; if the last flagged stop has no such neighbour (she is down the branch with the 3-sun leg), the glow is on its own flag, to take it out. Following the glow from any plan state reaches a valid plan to the city, because taking flags out returns to `home` at worst and from any `good` stop a `good` neighbour within the skin exists. The glow retargets as she taps and stays until Go is live on a valid plan. Asked-for help is the same glow and the same single count per map.
- **Finish:** the camel's arrival at the city on the second map: the gate opens, bells, and the long drink at the city's well. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` with the round's totals.
- **Always available:** the plan that flags every oasis on the branch without the 3-sun leg is valid on every seed, so a child who never skips a stop can always finish; skipping is a choice, not a requirement.

### Bands and ranges

`BandLevels`: `toddler: { min: 1, max: 1 }`, `preschool: { min: 1, max: 2 }`, `prek: { min: 2, max: 3 }`, `school: { min: 3, max: 3 }`. No lap window (the study: a one-year-old has no "far away"; Peekaboo Barn and Fill & Dump already give the cause-and-effect toy a kneeling camel would give). No grown-up window (The Ledger is CS6's separate Lantern Lake question). ## Screen and controls

- **Stage.** Landscape: the road across the upper half from left to right with its suns, landmark oases and (at level 3) the city at the right; the camel, its bales and the waterskin below the road at the left; Go at the lower right; the pet bottom-left and corner buttons clear. Portrait stacks the road above the camel above Go, and the level-3 map's two branches sit one above the other with the city at the right; nothing shrinks. Every input (camel, bale, oasis, stop, Go, Help) is at least 100 logical units after scaling; planned sizes: the camel 260 × 200 (a tap anywhere on it), bales 110 × 110, oases and stops 120 × 120 with the landmark drawn above the tap disc, Go 140 × 110. Suns are 44 units and countable but are not inputs beyond the free look (a tap on one counts its leg); dunes, the sky, the city wall and bells are `eventMode = 'none'`.
- **Touch-down feedback.** The camel's ears flick and it starts to fold on touch-down; a bale lifts; an oasis or stop lifts and its landmark bobs; Go squashes. A rejected tap (the far oasis, Go before the city) still shows that it was felt.
- **Speech.** `ctx.instruct` states the level's one ask ("Tap the camel so it kneels, then tap each bale" / "Tap the water the camel can reach" / "Tap the stops one at a time, all the way to the city, then tap Go"); the pet repeats it. Counts are spoken from bounded number slots (one to four), never a template with free text. The pet names each oasis as the camel arrives.
- **Feel.** `STYLES.hub` (Cozy Village's neighbour Mail Carrier uses it; she is not making music); a pentatonic note from `src/audio/notes.ts` per bale that hops on and per sun walked, a lower one for the empty skin, a two-note rise for the bells, and the long drink as a slow slide. Palette sand, sky, oasis green and the city's blue tiles; no sepia or texture filter. The walk is the reward: a sway, bells, dust puffs from `ctx.particles`, a long slurp at the water. Reduced motion shows each arrival without the walk.
- **The two history lines**, printed on the card and spoken once at the start of their levels: level 2, "Long ago, on the long road from the east, the stopping places were a day's walk apart, so there was water at the end of every day"; level 3, "Nobody walked the whole road. Each caravan went a few stops and handed its load on." Both name no place, people or date, match the caravanserai and Silk Road pages the study opened, and are checked again in the building session.

## Accounting, help and finishing

| Level | Counts a miss | Counts a hint | Never counted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | nothing | nothing | every tap, the idle glow, the walk |
| 2 | each tap on the oasis beyond the skin | the glow after the second miss on a road, or asked-for help; at most one per road | counting suns, tapping the skin, repeating the instruction |
| 3 | each Go whose plan holds a leg longer than the skin | the glow after the second miss on a map, or asked-for help; at most one per map | flagging, taking flags out, counting suns, Go before the city, the walk |

- **Supported finish:** at levels 2–3 the glow always leads to the reachable water or to a valid plan, so every round finishes with one ordinary sticker through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`, help or not. No creation, discovery or journal entry in the first build (an oasis lit on the creature home's map is the study's creature-world proposal, not this slice). Leaving early owes nothing.
- **Adaptation:** level 1 is always smooth (two rounds step up to 2 at preschool). At level 2 a child who taps the far oasis once and then the near one is still smooth (at most one miss); two hints or four misses are struggling. At level 3 a miss is one Go, not one per flag, so a child who re-plans once is smooth. Assisted completion is one sticker, not evidence of mastery. Nothing here claims a four-year-old understands a day's journey; it claims she counted the suns against the drops and tapped the water they reach.

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Plans:** over 300 seeds per level, level 1 draws 2 bales at toddler and 3–4 at preschool; level 2 draws two roads each with `near ∈ {1, 2}` and `far ∈ {3, 4}` on seeded sides; level 3 draws two maps with 4–6 oases across two branches of 2–3, every leg 1–3 suns, exactly one 3-sun leg on exactly one branch. The same seed gives the same plan.
- **Level 1 invariants:** from any reachable state `loaded <= bales`; a bale tap while standing and a camel tap with bales on the ground change nothing; at most `bales + 2` effective taps reach the walk from any state; `misses` and `hints` stay 0.
- **Level 2:** `choose(b)` is `reachable` iff `suns(b) <= skin`, with the difference named on `tooFar`; exactly one branch is reachable per road; the glow after two misses is that branch; a hint is counted at most once per road.
- **Level 3:** `good` from BFS contains `home` and `city`; `legSuns(a, b)` returns the road's suns for adjacent stops and the sum for a skipped oasis; `go(plan)` is `arrived` iff every leg ≤ `skin` and otherwise names the first over-long leg; after `tooLong` the plan keeps exactly the flags before that leg; the every-oasis plan on the branch without the 3-sun leg is always `arrived`; `hintFor(plan)` returns the first over-long leg's destination flag when the plan holds one, else a `good` neighbour within the skin when one exists, else the last flag; `flag(stop)` is accepted only for a stop adjacent to the last flag or one oasis beyond it on the same branch; following `hintFor` from every reachable plan state reaches an `arrived` plan in at most `2 × stops` steps; a hint is counted at most once per map; a flag can never be on a stop not adjacent (directly or by one skipped oasis) to the previous flag.
- **Layout:** `roadLayout(level, view)` at the four views the lands test uses (landscape, portrait, two phone insets): every input at least 100 units, nothing under the bottom-left pet or corner buttons, portrait stacks rather than shrinks, stops and oases do not overlap, the level-3 branches are at least 130 units apart.
- **Registry, lands, how-to and off-screen tests** pass with the new game: Cozy Village 17 → 18 games (paged at `LAND_SPOTS`), the card scoped per level, no "later levels" wording.

## Open decisions

Each has a default the build uses; none blocks CS2.

1. **Level 1 pacing.** Default: child-paced, the camel never walks until she taps it loaded. The alternative (it stands and walks on its own once loaded) is quicker but takes the second camel tap, the study's "tap the camel and it stands", away from her.
2. **Free looks at level 2.** Default: tapping a sun, a branch or the skin counts aloud, free and unlimited, so counting is never the obstacle. The alternative (no free look) would make level 2 a one-tap guess for a child who cannot yet count to four.
3. **Level 3's fork.** Default: two branches with one 3-sun leg, so the BFS is real and "nobody went the whole way" has a wrong road to fail on. The alternative (one line of stops, skipping as the only decision) is simpler to draw but every plan without a skip is valid, which makes the planning trivial; if CS2 finds the fork crowds portrait at 100-unit targets, it may drop to 2 oases per branch (4 in all) rather than shrink.
4. **The skin at level 3.** Default: two days, as at level 2. A three-day skin would let every adjacent leg fit and move the whole decision to skipping; it can be a later mode if someone watches a child wanting it.
5. **Land.** Default: Cozy Village (18th game, two pages). Fallback by decision 2: Rainbow Meadow.
6. **The history lines.** Default: the two sentences above, source-checked in the building session (the caravanserai page's "a day's journey" spacing and the Silk Road page's succession of middlemen). If the check finds either too strong, level 2's line drops to "Long ago, people walked the long road from the east a day at a time" and level 3's is omitted.

## Levels 4–5 (CS3): swap at the stop, worth more far away

Defined 2026-10-30 as slice CS3 of the [study](../deep-dives/subjects/china-india-silk-road.md#build-slices-in-order), [PR #1029](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1029). Docs only; **built 2026-10-30** as [batch caravan-days 2](../GAME-BATCHES.md#caravan-days-2-swap-at-the-stop-and-worth-more-far-away) ([PR #1037](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1037), [archived build note](../archive/COMPLETED-2026-10-09.md#caravan-days-levels-45-china-india-and-the-silk-road-cs3-2026-10-30)). Build decisions: three distinct wants per road (a three-stop road has three; the text's "four" is superseded); the level-5 history line uses the stated fallback (the opened sources support level 4's middlemen line, not a rarity-raises-price claim) and level 4's line is kept; the look clears after a smaller-load miss, and shells stay shown on every load looked at in a case. The withdraw clause (level 4 playing as Swap Meet 4 with a camel) is an open [device check](../DEVICE-CHECKS.md#caravan-days-levels-45-cs3-2026-10-30). Decision 3 was taken 2026-10-23 on the developer's delegation: levels 4–5 are defined here, cross-listed to Swap Meet, and there are never two relay engines. Both levels are school only, appended above level 3 without renumbering.

**Fun action and purpose:** level 4: the camel arrives at an oasis, an island friend takes its bale and holds out two others, and she picks the one the **next** stop wants; on Go the camel walks there, and the stop either takes it gladly or shrugs and sends the camel back. Learning goal: what crosses hands at a stop is chosen for the stop after it; nobody carried a thing the whole way, each stop passed on what the next one wanted. Level 5: the same three bales are worth more shells at one stop than another; two loads are offered and she sends the one that brings back more from the stop named, once to the far city and once to the near oasis, so the better load reverses. Learning goal: a thing's worth depends on where it is; goods made far away were worth more where they were rare.

### Levels 4–5: overlap and the identity check

- **Swap Meet** (levels 1–4, built) owns trading among friends in any order with Undo, and its level 4 *Chain of two* trades twice toward a pictured goal with a BFS solver over traders. Level 4 here is Caravan's only because of three things Swap Meet does not have: a **one-way road** (the camel never returns to an earlier stop by choice), each pick judged by **the next stop's want** rather than a final goal held in the basket, and a rejection played out as **a walk to that stop and back**. Swap Meet's long-road mode is not claimed anywhere ([its definition](swap-meet.md) and the notebook have none). **Withdraw clause:** if the build finds level 4 plays as Swap Meet 4 with a camel (the child matches the final goal and ignores the next bubble), the rung goes to Swap Meet as a mode and level 4 is withdrawn here rather than built thin, as CS1's clause does for level 3.
- **Relay Woods** owns *who carries it next* (a parcel handed between carriers). Level 4 asks *what is carried next*: the carrier is always the camel and the question is which good the next stop wants. **No second trade engine:** level 4's logic is a fixed chain of two picks, each between two offers, so the whole decision space is four pick sequences, enumerated in tests; it reuses no Swap Meet or Relay Woods state, solver or Undo history.
- **Market Stall** owns paying, coins, change and fewest coins. Level 5's shells are **counters, never coin denominations**: one shell is one unit, there is no paying, no change and no fewest-coins question. **Nothing accrues:** the shells a load brings are drawn for that case only and cleared at the next case; there is no purse that grows across cases or rounds, no running total, no profit or score, and nothing is saved.
- **Mail Carrier and Treasure Map** stay as in [Overlap and reuse](#overlap-and-reuse). Every leg at levels 4–5 is 1–2 suns (within the skin), so the water is never the decision; the suns stay drawn for continuity and the card says so.

### Level 4, Swap at the stop (`swap`)

- **Goods:** six pictured goods, code-drawn in `art.ts` and told apart by shape and colour: `silk` (a rolled bolt), `tea` (a pressed brick), `salt` (a white block), `glass` (a green jug), `paper` (a roll), `beads` (a looped string). Each is a 120 × 120 card with the good drawn large.
- **Plan:** two roads per round. A road is `home`, oasis 1, oasis 2, and the city (three stops), each leg 1–2 suns. Each stop has a **want**, drawn in a bubble above it; every bubble is visible from the start. The camel leaves home carrying `carry0`; oasis 1 wants `carry0` (the merchant always takes what arrives correctly). At each oasis an island friend (bunny, duck, cat or bear from `src/art/critter.ts`; seeded, no people) offers **two goods**: the **right** one, the next stop's want, and a **decoy**. Oasis 1's decoy is the **city's** want on at least one road per round (the skip-ahead temptation: right thing, wrong stop); otherwise a good no later stop wants. Oasis 2's decoy is a good the city does not want. The four wants on a road are distinct goods; the seeded draws also choose the friend, the landmark and which offer is drawn on the left.
- **Flow:** the camel walks home → oasis 1 on the round's first tap of Go (or at once on entry; see decision 7); there the friend takes `carry0` and holds out the two offers. **Tap an offer** and it hops onto the camel (the bale it had stays with the friend); **tap the other offer** and they swap back and forth, free and unlimited. Tapping a bubble is a free look: the pet names the want ("The next stop wants salt"); tapping a good on the camel names it. **Go** is the one commit and is live once an offer is on the camel; Go before picking says "Pick one for the camel to carry" (nothing counted). On Go the camel walks to the next stop: if it carries that stop's want, the friend there takes it with a happy bob and holds out the next two offers (or, at the city, the gate opens, bells and the long drink: the road ends). Otherwise **`notWanted`**: the stop's friend shrugs, the pet names both goods ("This stop wants salt, not tea"), and the camel walks back to the stop it came from. That is one **miss**. State after a miss: the camel stands at the previous oasis carrying the good it took, both offers are there again, swapping is free, and Go is live.
- **Help:** after the second miss on the same road, the right offer at the current stop glows and `hints++` once for that road; the glow then **retargets stop by stop** (the right offer at each later oasis) until the road ends. A glow never picks or walks for her. Asked-for help (the pet's Help button) is the same glow and the same single count per road; a second press repeats it free. Two-offer picks mean one miss usually reveals the answer, so the two-miss glow rarely fires; that is intended (the miss is still counted).
- **Finish:** the second road's drink at the city. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` with the round's totals.
- **Model:** `swapRoad(rng)` returns `{ legs, wants: [w1, w2, wCity], carry0, offers: [[a, b], [c, d]], friends, landmarks }`; `pick(state, side)`, `goSwap(road, state)` returning `{ wanted: true }` or `{ wanted: false; wants; carried }`, `swapHint(road, state)` returning the right side at the current stop. Pure, as level 3's functions are.

### Level 5, Worth more far away (`worth`)

- **Plan:** two cases per round on one road: `home`, the **near oasis**, the **middle oasis** and the **far city**, each leg 1–2 suns. Two goods per round, seeded from the six: an **up good** made at home (cheap near home, dear far away) and a **down good** that came the other way. Every stop shows a **price board**: the up good costs `up = [1, 2, 4]` or `[1, 3, 5]` shells at near/middle/far, and the down good the same row reversed. Shells are drawn as a row of shell counters beside each good on the board, as many shells as its price.
- **Loads:** the camel carries **three bales**. Two loads are offered, each three bales of the two goods, `(upA, downA)` and `(upB, downB)` with `up + down = 3` and `upA ≠ upB`. The **named stop** wears a big glowing bubble with a shell in it; case 1 names one end of the road (near or far, seeded) and case 2 names the other, with the **same two loads**. So at the far city the load with more up-goods brings more, and at the near oasis the load with more down-goods does: the better load **reverses** between the two cases. The middle oasis is drawn with its board but is never named (at `[1, 2, 4]` it pays the same for both goods, so two three-bale loads could tie there).
- **Totals:** a load's total at a stop is `up × price(up good) + down × price(down good)`; with three bales and prices at most 5, every total is 3–15 (at most 20), and the two loads' totals at a named end stop always differ because `upA ≠ upB` and the two prices differ there.
- **Flow (look versus commit):** **tapping a load is a free look**: it lifts, each of its bales shows its shell counters under it at the named stop's price, and the pet counts the total aloud from a bounded slot (one to twenty). Tapping the other load looks at that one instead. Tapping a price board names a good's price there ("Silk is four shells here"). **Go is the one commit**: it sends the load last looked at (Go before looking at either says "Tap a load to look, then Go", nothing counted). The camel walks to the named stop, the friend takes the bales and pours the shells out beside the camel. If that total is the larger, bells and the long drink: the case ends. Otherwise **`less`**: one **miss**, both sums spoken ("Six shells. The other load brings twelve"), and the camel walks home where both loads wait again; the poured shells are cleared.
- **Help:** after the second miss on the same case, the better load glows and `hints++` once for that case; asked-for help is the same glow, counted once per case, repeated free.
- **Finish:** the second case's drink. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` with the round's totals. The shells shown in a case are gone at the next case; nothing is kept.
- **Model:** `worthPlan(rng)` returns `{ goods: [up, down], prices, loads: [[upA, downA], [upB, downB]], named: [first, second] }`; `total(plan, load, stop)`, `better(plan, caseIndex)`, `sell(plan, caseIndex, load)` returning `{ better: true }` or `{ better: false; got; other }`.

`school: { min: 3, max: 5 }`; the other bands are unchanged (toddler 1, preschool 1–2, pre-K 2–3). No `order`: the ladder is still `min..max`. Nothing is renumbered, so a pinned level 3 stays level 3. `planFor`'s id union grows to `'load' | 'reach' | 'plan' | 'swap' | 'worth'` and `LEVEL_NAMES` gains 4 *Swap at the stop* and 5 *Worth more far away*; `describeLevel` reads them. ### Levels 4–5: accounting

| Level | Counts a miss | Counts a hint | Never counted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | each Go carrying a good the next stop does not want | the glow after the second miss on a road, or asked-for help; at most one per road | picking and swapping offers, tapping bubbles or the carried good, Go before picking, the walk back |

Assisted completion is one sticker, not evidence of mastery; level 5 claims she compared two counted totals at a named stop, not that she understands prices.

### Levels 4–5: rule tests

- **Plans (300 seeds each):** level 4 draws two roads, each with three stops, legs 1–2 suns, four distinct wants, two offers per oasis containing the next stop's want exactly once, and oasis 1's decoy equal to the city's want on at least one road per round; level 5 draws two goods, prices `[1, 2, 4]` or `[1, 3, 5]` and the reverse, two loads of three bales with different up-counts, and two named stops that are the two ends of the road. Same seed, same plan.
- **Level 4, enumerated:** over every seed, try all four pick sequences against `goSwap`: **exactly one** reaches the city with every stop accepting, and it is the sequence `swapHint` follows from the start; a decoy picked at either oasis is `notWanted` at the very next stop with both goods named; after `notWanted` the state is the previous oasis with the same offers and the taken good; picking and swapping never change `misses`; a hint is counted at most once per road; following `swapHint` from every reachable state reaches the city.
- **Level 5, enumerated:** for every seed and both cases, the two totals differ, each is between 3 and 20, `better` is the larger, and the better load at case 1 is **not** the better load at case 2; `sell` with the smaller load returns both totals; the middle oasis is never named; nothing in the plan or state carries shells across cases.
- **Legs:** at levels 4–5 every leg is ≤ `SKIN`.
- **Layout:** `roadLayout(4, …)` and `roadLayout(5, …)` at the four test views: offers, loads, bubbles, boards and Go at least 100 units, nothing under the pet or corner buttons, offers and loads not overlapping, portrait stacks or wraps rather than shrinks, and `null` rather than a shrunken target if a view cannot fit.
- **Registry and how-to tests** pass with school's new range and the new steps.

## Levels 6–7 (CS4): mountains or sand, a well every day's walk

Defined 2026-10-31 as slice CS4 of the [study](../deep-dives/subjects/china-india-silk-road.md#build-slices-in-order), [PR #1074](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1074). **Built 2026-11-01** ([PR #1106](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1106)); the deviations are listed under *Built* below. Both levels are school only (the study's upper-elementary rungs, about 9–12), appended above level 5 without renumbering. The study's "reviewer for the caravanserai and edict lines" gate is, since the [2026-10-23 decisions (S1)](../archive/DECISIONS-2026-10-23.md#standing-rules), the **building session's source check** against the sources listed [below](#levels-67-history-lines-and-sources); it blocks merging a line that fails the check, not starting the build.

**Fun action and purpose:** level 6: two roads lead to the same city, a short-looking one over a cold mountain pass and a long one round the sand; she picks either, says how many days it will take, and the camel walks it with a day counter ticking. Learning goal: how far a road looks on the map is not how long it takes; in the mountains a day's walk covers less ground and snow can close a pass for a day, so the days are counted, not guessed from length. Level 7: the planner's side of the road. A bare road from home to the city has a few places where a well could be dug; she taps where the wells go so that **there is water at the end of every day's walk**, with as few wells as she can. Learning goal: stopping places were a day apart because someone placed them so; covering a road with the fewest wells is a real planning problem with more than one best answer.

### Levels 6–7: overlap and the identity check

- **Mail Carrier 8** (*choose the shorter of two paths*) owns picking the shorter route. Level 6 is not that: **either road is accepted**, the round has one case where each road is quicker, and the only checked answer is **the day count of the road she chose**, which includes a snow day the map shows but the road's drawn length does not. If the build finds level 6 plays as Mail Carrier 8 with suns (she only ever compares the two roads and ignores the count), the day-count check stays and the road choice becomes fixed per case rather than the level being withdrawn, because the count is the identity here.
- **Weather Wardrobe** owns dressing for weather, including CS7's *Dress for the road* (cold pass, sandstorm scarf). The coat at level 6 is a **visible cost of the pass**, drawn on the pass leg and put on by the camel in the walk; it is never an input, a choice or a check.
- **Level 7 against covering puzzles.** Block Garden owns fewest pieces covering squares; Lantern Lights is Lights Out (pressing flips neighbours), not covering. Level 7 is one-dimensional coverage by distance on one road (the classic fewest-stops problem): each site is a point, and the rule is a gap of at most one day's walk between neighbouring water. No grid, no shapes, no engine shared with either.
- **Levels 1–5** keep their models. The waterskin and `SKIN` stay as drawn at level 6 (every leg is within it, so water never decides the road) and are not drawn at level 7, whose rule is the planners' one-day spacing, not the skin.
- **Input:** `onTap` only, as at levels 1–5. The study's level 7 said "drag wells and shade trees onto it"; this definition taps a site to put a well on it or take it off (decision 13), so the game still has no drag.

### Level 6, Mountains or sand (`pass`)

- **Plan:** two cases per round. Each case is one map with `home` at the left, the `city` at the right and two roads between them:
  - the **pass road**, drawn short and climbing over grey peaks: `passLegs ∈ {3, 4, 5}` legs of exactly 1 sun each, the inns drawn close together; a **pass hut** at its top where, on some seeds, a **snow cloud** sits (`snow ∈ {0, 1}`): a snow day is drawn before she chooses as a grey sun with a flake under the cloud, and costs one day of waiting at the hut. The pass leg also shows the **coat** the camel will wear. Pass days = `passLegs + snow`, so 3–6.
  - the **sand road**, drawn long and flat round the dunes: 2–3 legs of 1–2 suns, inns far apart. Sand days = the sum, so 4–6 (two legs of 2, or three legs summing 4–6).
  - Every leg is ≤ `SKIN`. **No ties:** the two roads' day counts differ in every case. **Neither road always quicker:** in each round one case has the pass quicker and the other the sand quicker, in seeded order; across seeds each road is quicker in about half the cases.
- **Flow:** the two roads are inputs, and so are five **day cards** (3, 4, 5, 6, 7), each 110 × 110 with the numeral and that many small suns. **Tapping a road** chooses it: it lights, and the camel turns to face it; tapping the other road switches, free and unlimited. **Tapping a day card** sets the guess (one at a time; tapping another moves it), free. Tapping a sun, an inn, the snow cloud or the coat is a **free look**: the pet counts that leg's suns, says "Snow on the pass: we wait one day" or "Cold on the pass: the camel wears its coat". **Go** is the one commit, live once a road and a card are set; Go before both says "Choose a road and how many days, then tap Go" (nothing counted). On Go the camel walks the chosen road leg by leg, a day counter above it ticking one sun per day (the snow day ticks while it waits in the hut).
- **Verdict:** `goPass(map, road, guess)` returns `{ right: true }` when `guess` equals the road's days: the camel arrives on that day, bells, the long drink, and the pet names both roads' days ("Five days over the pass; round the sand was four"), so the comparison is seen without being checked. Otherwise one **miss**, with the direction but not the number: if `guess < days`, the camel stops at the end of day `guess` with the city still ahead ("Four days, and the city is still ahead"); if `guess > days`, it arrives early ("We got here before day six"); either way it walks back home, the road and card stay set, and she may change either.
- **Help:** after the second miss on the same case, the pet counts the **chosen** road's days aloud sun by sun (the snow day included, lighting each), the right card glows, and `hints++` once for that case. With no road chosen, the help counts the pass road (drawn on top). The glow retargets if she switches roads and stays until Go. Asked-for help is the same, once per case, repeated free.
- **Finish:** the second case's drink. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` with the round's totals.
- **Model:** `passMap(rng, passQuicker: boolean)` returns `{ passLegs, snow, sandLegs: number[], landmarks }`; `days(map, road)`, `goPass(map, road, guess)` returning `{ right: true } | { right: false; days; short: boolean }`, `passHint(map, road | null)` returning `{ road, days }`, `passPlan(rng)` drawing the two cases with opposite `passQuicker` in a seeded order. Pure, as at levels 3–5.

### Level 7, A well every day's walk (`wells`)

- **Plan:** two roads per round. A road is a line of **hoofprint marks** from `home` (mark 0) to the `city` (mark `L`), `L ∈ {12, …, 16}`; **a day's walk is `DAY = 4` marks**, shown once on a **day stick** at the top (a sun over four hoofprints). Along the road are **5–7 sites** at distinct marks, each a patch of green where a well can be dug, at least 2 marks from each other and from home and the city. The generator guarantees: (a) wells on every site make a valid road (no stretch between neighbouring water points longer than `DAY`), so a valid placement always exists; (b) the fewest wells, `minWells(road)` by search, is at least 2 and at most `sites − 2`, so at least two sites are never needed and the choice is real; (c) on at least one road per round there is more than one minimal placement.
- **Flow:** **tapping a site** digs a well there (a well with a rope and bucket rises; a pentatonic note); tapping a well takes it off. Both are free and unlimited. A well counter beside Done shows how many wells are on the road (a number and that many well icons), never a target. Tapping the day stick is a **free look**: "A day's walk is four hoofprints". Tapping home, the city or a well-free stretch of road does nothing (decoration, `eventMode = 'none'`). **Done** is the one commit and is always live; Done with no wells says "Tap the green places to dig wells, then tap Done" (nothing counted).
- **Verdict:** `checkWells(road, wells)` returns `{ ok: true; extra }` or `{ ok: false; from; to }` naming the **first** stretch (from the home end) between neighbouring water points longer than `DAY`. Not ok is one **miss**: the camel walks from home, drinking at each well, until it has walked `DAY` marks past the last water before the long stretch; it stops, the skin empties, the long stretch glows red-gold and the pet says "That stretch is more than a day's walk", and the camel walks back home. The wells stay where she put them. Ok: the camel walks the whole road, drinking at every well, a **shade tree** sprouts beside each well as it passes (decoration; decision 14), and at the city the bells and the long drink end the road. If `extra > 0` (valid but not the fewest), it is **not a miss**: after the drink the pet says "It works! Planners could do it with three wells", and a minimal placement shows as faint ghost wells for a moment (the minimal placement sharing the most wells with hers; ties go to the one found first from the home end).
- **Help:** after the second miss on the same road, `wellHint(road, wells)` glows one site and `hints++` once for that road: starting from the last water point `a` before the first too-long stretch, the **farthest site within `DAY` of `a`** that has no well. If the placement is already valid, the hint is Done. Because wells on every site make a valid road, such a site always exists when a stretch is too long, and placing it either fixes that stretch or moves its start forward, so following the hint from any state (extra or misplaced wells included) reaches a valid placement in at most `sites` taps. The glow retargets as she taps; asked-for help is the same, once per road, repeated free.
- **Finish:** the second road's drink. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` with the round's totals. Wells, trees and ghost wells are gone at the next road; nothing is kept.
- **Model:** `wellRoad(rng)` returns `{ length, sites: number[], landmarks }`; `stretches(road, wells)`, `checkWells(road, wells)`, `minWells(road)` (greedy from home, which is optimal for points on a line), `minimalPlacements(road)` (every minimal set, enumerated by search over subsets of at most seven sites), `ghostFor(road, wells)` and `wellHint(road, wells)` returning `{ site } | { done: true }`.

### Levels 6–7: bands and ranges

`school: { min: 3, max: 7 }`; the other bands are unchanged (toddler 1, preschool 1–2, pre-K 2–3). No `order`: the ladder is still `min..max`. Nothing is renumbered, so a pinned level 3, 4 or 5 stays where it is. `planFor`'s id union grows to `… | 'pass' | 'wells'` and `LEVEL_NAMES` gains 6 *Mountains or sand* and 7 *A well every day's walk*; `describeLevel` reads them. Assisted completion is one sticker, not evidence of mastery.

### Levels 6–7: rule tests

- **Level 6 plans (300 seeds):** two cases per round; pass days = `passLegs + snow` ∈ 3–6 with every pass leg 1 sun; sand days ∈ 4–6 with 2–3 legs of 1–2 suns; every leg ≤ `SKIN`; the two roads' days differ in every case; in every round exactly one case has the pass quicker; over all seeds snow appears both in cases where the pass is quicker and in cases where the sand is (so snow never gives the answer away); every answer is on a day card (3–7). Same seed, same plan.
- **Level 6 verdicts:** `goPass` is right iff the guess equals `days(map, road)` for **either** road; a wrong guess returns the true days and `short === (guess < days)`; `passHint` with a road returns that road's days and with `null` the pass road's; a hint is counted at most once per case; choosing, switching and setting the card never change `misses`.
- **Level 7 plans (300 seeds):** `L ∈ 12–16`, 5–7 sites at distinct marks at least 2 apart and at least 2 from either end; wells on every site pass `checkWells`; `2 ≤ minWells ≤ sites − 2`; at least one road per round has more than one minimal placement. Same seed, same plan.
- **Level 7, enumerated:** for every seed, over **every subset** of sites (at most 2⁷ = 128): `checkWells` is ok iff every stretch is ≤ `DAY`, and when not ok names the first too-long stretch from the home end; `minWells` equals the size of the smallest ok subset; `minimalPlacements` is exactly the set of ok subsets of that size; every ok subset of that size is accepted with `extra = 0`, and every larger ok subset with `extra = size − minWells`; `ghostFor` is a minimal placement sharing the most wells with the given subset. From every subset, repeatedly applying `wellHint` (digging the glowing site) reaches an ok placement in at most `sites` steps, and from an ok placement the hint is Done. A hint is counted at most once per road.
- **Layout:** `roadLayout(6, …)` and `roadLayout(7, …)` at the four test views: roads' bands, day cards, sites, the day stick, Go and Done at least 100 units, nothing under the pet or corner buttons, no two targets overlapping, the day cards wrap rather than shrink, level 7's mark spacing at least 56 units, and `null` rather than a shrunken target if a view cannot fit.
- **Registry and how-to tests** pass with school's new range and the new steps.

### Levels 6–7: ghost finger, how-to card and voice

- **Ghost finger:** level 6: tap the quicker road, then its day card, then Go; repeat for the second case (so it demonstrates both roads across a round). Level 7: tap the sites of `minimalPlacements(road)[0]` from home outward, then Done; repeat on the second road. The bot never makes a wrong Go or Done and never calls finish; it reads `game.state` and the layout after every resize.
- **How-to steps:** "Choose either road and tap how many days it will take (count the suns and any snow day), then tap Go" `{ from: 6, to: 6 }`; "Tap the green places to dig wells so no stretch is longer than a day's walk, then tap Done; fewer wells is better but any road that works is fine" `{ from: 7, to: 7 }`. Notes: "Either road is right at level 6; only the day count is checked." "At level 7 extra wells are never a mistake." The level 6 and 7 notes carry their history lines, and level 7's grown-up note carries the edict line (below).
- **Voice lines** under `cd.*`: `instruct.6`, `instruct.7`, `history.6`, `history.7`, `days.1`–`days.7`, `marks.1`–`marks.16`, `pass`, `sand`, `snow-day`, `coat`, `choose-first`, `still-ahead`, `got-here-before`, `took-days`, `round-the-sand-was`, `over-the-pass-was`, `day-stick`, `dig-first`, `too-long-stretch`, `it-works`, `planners-could`, `wells.2`–`wells.6`. Lines compose from these slots ("Four days" + "and the city is still ahead"); `{pet}` optional. Feedback names days, hoofprints and wells, never a site number.

### Levels 6–7: history lines and sources

Printed on the card and spoken once at the start of their levels, island names only (decision 1: no real city, people, date or "Silk Road" on the child's screen), and **source-checked in the building session** (S1) against the pages the study opened:

- **Level 6:** "Long ago, there was an inn at the end of every day's walk. In the mountains a day's walk didn't go far, so the inns were closer together." Source: the [Caravanserai](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Caravanserai) page's spacing "intended to be equivalent to a day's journey", about 30–40 km on the Iranian plains and about 10 km in mountains ([study](../deep-dives/subjects/china-india-silk-road.md#anchors-and-evidence-limits)). **Fallback** if the check finds the mountain claim too strong: "Long ago, travellers stopped at an inn at the end of each day's walk."
- **Level 7:** "Long ago, some rulers had wells dug and shade trees planted along the roads, so travellers had water and shade every day." Source: the [Edicts of Ashoka](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Edicts_of_Ashoka) page's Major Pillar Edict 7 (banyan trees "to afford shade to cattle and men", wells "at intervals of eight kos"). **Fallback:** "Long ago, people dug wells along the roads so travellers would find water."
- **Grown-up card only** (decision 1 and the study's CS4 row): "Level 7 follows the Indian emperor Ashoka's seventh pillar edict (3rd century BCE), which describes wells at intervals of eight kos and banyan trees for shade along roads. Historians caution that the edicts show what a ruler intended more than what was built." The building session checks the date and wording against the opened page; no religious content is drafted (study decision 7; the 2026-10-23 cultural exception), so the card names the edict's road works only.

### Levels 6–7: built (2026-11-01)

Both levels shipped in [PR #1106](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1106) (school range 3–7). Deviations from the definition: at level 6 the suns, inns, snow cloud and coat are scenery inside the road band (tapping the band chooses the road and names it, with the snow-day or coat line) rather than separate free looks, and the day-card row never wraps because the logical view is always at least 1024 wide. Source check in the build session: the Caravanserai page supports the level 6 line (spacing "equivalent to a day's journey"; 30–40 km on open Iranian landscapes, about 10 km or less in mountains); the Edicts of Ashoka page (Major Pillar Edict 7: banyan trees for shade, wells at intervals of eight kos, 3rd century BCE) supports the level 7 line and the grown-up edict note, which was trimmed for the card length limit. Neither fallback line was used.

### Levels 6–7: open decisions

Each has a default the build uses; none blocks the build. Numbered after CS3's 7–11.

12. **When she says the count (level 6).** Default: a **prediction** set before Go, so the snow day has to be counted from the map and the level is planning. Alternative (the study's wording): she taps the number after the camel arrives, a count of what she watched; simpler, but at school a count of a ticking walk is not a decision.
13. **Tap, not drag (level 7).** Default: tap a marked site to dig or fill a well; the game has no drag, sites are 110-unit discs, and placing on marked sites is the same decision. Alternative (the study's wording): drag wells from a tray onto any mark, which needs `src/engine/drag.ts`, a snap rule and a tray, and makes the search space every mark rather than the sites.
14. **Shade trees (level 7).** Default: a tree sprouts beside each well on a valid walk, decoration only. Alternative: trees as a second resource ("shade every half day"), a separate later mode if wanted.
15. **Extra wells (level 7).** Default: accepted, not a miss, a minimal placement ghosted. Alternative (a harder later mode, not this slice): Done with extras says "Can you do it with fewer?" and only a minimal placement ends the road.
16. **The day's walk (level 7).** Default: `DAY = 4` marks, the planners' one-day spacing, with the skin not drawn. The skin-of-two model (water within two days) was rejected because it contradicts the edict's "a well every day's walk" and the level's history line.
17. **History lines.** As above, with the stated fallbacks after the building session's source check.
