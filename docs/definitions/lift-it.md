# Lift It, levels 1–6 (definition)

Defined 2026-10-17 for [chunk 27](../ROADMAP.md#chunks) (physics games), from the [PHYSICS-GAMES candidate](../PHYSICS-GAMES.md#new-candidates), its [IDEAS row](../IDEAS.md#candidates) and the [machines and tools study](../deep-dives/subjects/machines-tools.md#candidates-and-fit) (its [build slice 3](../deep-dives/subjects/machines-tools.md#build-slices-in-order)). **Built 2026-10-20** (levels 1–6, [PR #692](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/692), on every default; build notes in the [archive](../archive/COMPLETED-2026-10-09.md#lift-it-levels-16-2026-10-20); not built: the replay button from level 3, and a drawn pet paw for the level 1–2 hint). Originally docs only. **Was ready to build:** every open call below has a default the build used. The study's mechanism reviewer is wanted before any *real-world* claim is spoken; this packet speaks only its stated ideal rule, so the reviewer does not gate the build. The calls are listed in [Open decisions](#open-decisions) and in [WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER](../WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER.md).

**Fun action and purpose:** a friend waits up on the tree's ledge for a parcel, and she hauls the crate up to them by pulling a rope, hand over hand, with a creak of the wheel and a cheer at the top. Then the crate gets heavy, and she decides where to hang the wheels: over the branch, on the crate, or both. The learning goal is **what a pulley actually does**: a wheel on the branch turns the pull around (pull down, the crate goes up) but does not make it lighter; a wheel on the crate, held by two strands of rope, makes the pull half as heavy, and the rope has to come twice as far. There is no clock, no move count and nothing to drop; a crate that will not lift only wobbles, and every wheel arrangement that can lift it counts.

**The model is a toy, stated as one.** Every rope is weightless and stretchless (except the visible "too heavy" stretch, which is a game cue), every wheel is weightless and frictionless, and the crate rises steadily while the rope moves. Force is a whole number of **paws**: a crate weighs `w` stones, and a rig whose pull needs `p` paws moves when at least `p` friends are on the rope's free end. Travel is whole **knots**: the rope has a knot every rung, and lifting the crate one rung pulls `ratio` knots past the hand. Nothing in the speech or the card describes a real rope, friction or a real winch; a line about the real world needs the [study's reviewer](../deep-dives/subjects/machines-tools.md#decisions-for-the-developer).

## The model

- **A line** is one parcel: a crate of weight `w` (1–6 stones, drawn as stone dots on its side), a ledge at height `h` (1–3 rungs up the tree), `g` friends on the ground (0–3) and `l` friends on the ledge (0–2, beside the one waiting for the parcel). The friends are the pullers; the corner pet stays the guide and never pulls.
- **Two hooks:** the **branch hook** under the branch above the crate, and the **crate hook** on the crate's top. A wheel from the tray can hang on either; two wheels fill both. The rope's standing end is always tied to the **branch ring** when a wheel hangs on the crate (drawn as a plain knot on a ring, so the two supporting strands are visible), and to the crate otherwise.
- **Four rigs** come from which hooks hold a wheel (`rigOf(hooks)`):

  | Rig | Wheels | Supporting strands | `ratio` | Paws needed `p = ceil(w / ratio)` | Knots per rung | Pull | Pullers |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `direct` | none | (the friends push the crate from below) | 1 | `w` | 1 | up, on the crate | ground |
  | `fixed` | branch | 1 | 1 | `w` | 1 | down, on the ground | ground |
  | `movable` | crate | 2 | 2 | `ceil(w / 2)` | 2 | up, at the ledge | ledge |
  | `both` | branch and crate | 2 | 2 | `ceil(w / 2)` | 2 | down, on the ground | ground |

  `works(rig, line)` is `pullers(rig, line) ≥ paws(rig, line)`, where `pullers` is `g` or `l` by the Pullers column. `travel(rig, line)` is `h × ratio` knots. In the ideal model `paws × travel ≥ w × h` for every rig, with equality whenever `w` divides evenly; a test asserts both. The free end of a movable rig is pulled **up** by the ledge friends (a single movable pulley's free end rises); adding the branch wheel brings it back down to the ground friends, which is the whole difference between `movable` and `both`.
- **Hauling.** Pulling is a drag on the **handle**, the big knot at the rope's free end, in the rig's pull direction, measured from pointer movement as Bouncy Launch measures a pull. A drag of `d` logical units pulls `d / KNOT` knots (one knot = 120 units), and the crate rises `knots / ratio` rungs, eased so it never jumps; a drag the wrong way does nothing but tug the handle. On release the handle springs back to its grab spot and the crate **stays where it is** (a toy rule: a held crate never slides back), so she hauls in strokes. The crate stops at the ledge; extra pulling only sways the rope.
- **Too heavy.** A drag on a rig that does not work stretches the rope's drawn end by at most 30 units, the crate rocks on the spot, the paw count on the handle flashes and the pet says why ("Four paws. Only two friends!"). Nothing breaks and nothing is lost. A rig whose pullers are zero (a `movable` rig with nobody on the ledge) shows a greyed handle at the ledge and the pet says "Nobody up there to pull."
- **Wheels** go from the tray to a hook by the object drag helper (`src/engine/drag.ts`); a drop that is not on a hook returns the wheel to the tray, never a miss. A wheel on a hook can be dragged back to the tray or across to the other hook. The rope is redrawn at once for the new rig, so the strands and the handle's place and paw count are visible before any pull; a **replay** button (from level 3) plays the last successful haul again, with the knots counted aloud.
- **Knots and the counter** (levels 4–6): knots on the rope pass a mark at the wheel (or at the crate for `direct`), each with a tick; the counter beside the ledge shows the count so far, so travel is a thing she sees, not a number she is told.
- `logic.ts` holds plain functions: `rigOf(hooks)`, `ratio(rig)`, `paws(rig, line)`, `pullers(rig, line)`, `works(rig, line)`, `travel(rig, line)`, `workingRigs(line)`, `bestRig(line)` (a working rig needing the fewest wheels, ties to `fixed`), `pullDirection(rig)`, `applyPull(state, dragUnits)`, `planFor(level)`, `makeLine(plan, rng)` (seeded; built from the plan's filter, at most 50 tries, then an authored fallback a test asserts is never needed over 500 seeds) and `liftLayout(line, view)`.

## Accounting, help and finishing

- **Never a miss:** any pull or stroke; a drag the wrong way; dropping a wheel back in the tray or moving it between hooks; a tug on a rig that does not work at levels 1–4 (that wobble is the lesson at 3); the level 4 knot guess, at any level (a prediction, as Sink or Float's are); repeating the instruction; asking for the replay; at levels 5–6, tugging a rig already tried on this parcel (the pet says "We tried that one").
- **Levels 1–4 record no misses.** Adaptation there moves only through hints ([decision 2](#open-decisions)).
- **A miss (levels 5–6):** the first tug on a rig that does not work or that ends at the wrong pullers, once per rig per parcel, since the paw count and the handle's place are readable before pulling; and at level 6 a wrong paws or knots card (the right card then lights and the pet counts it: "Two ropes, six stones: three paws each"). The gentle response is the wobble and the spoken reason, or the count; her wheels stay where she put them.
- **The glow after two misses:** after a parcel's second miss the hooks of `bestRig(line)` glow (a hook that should be empty glows faintly and its wheel's tray slot glows) until the rig matches; at level 6 after two wrong cards the right card glows. Each counts `hints++` once per parcel.
- **Show a hint** in the pause sheet (`Scene.pauseActions`): at levels 1–2 the pet's paw pulls the next stroke with her; at levels 3–6 it gives the glow above; each request counts `hints++` once; asking when the parcel is done only says what to do next.
- **Finish:** when every parcel of the round has reached its friend, the ledge friends hold up their parcels and the pullers bow, then `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` gives one ordinary sticker, with or without help. No creation, discovery or journal entry is filed in this build ([decision 4](#open-decisions)).

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **The rule:** `ratio` is 1 for `direct` and `fixed`, 2 for `movable` and `both`; `pullDirection` is up for `direct` and `movable`, down for `fixed` and `both`; `pullers` is the ledge only for `movable`; `paws × travel ≥ w × h` for every rig and line, with equality when `ratio` divides `w`; `paws(fixed) === paws(direct)` for every `w` (the fixed pulley never halves the pull); `travel(movable) === 2 × travel(fixed)`.
- **Working rigs:** `works` enumerated over every `w` 1–6, `g` 0–3, `l` 0–2 and rig against the table; `workingRigs` is never empty for any generated line; `bestRig` is always in `workingRigs` and uses the fewest wheels.
- **Hauling:** `applyPull` raises the crate by `drag / KNOT / ratio` rungs, never above `h` and never down; a wrong-direction drag changes nothing; a drag on a non-working rig changes nothing; the knot count after a full haul equals `travel`.
- **Each authored first line** has the working rigs and the spoken numbers named above; level 5's line 1 has exactly `both` working and line 2 exactly `movable`.
- **Generation**, for every plan over 500 seeds: well formed, the plan filter in the table holds, the same seed gives the same line, and the fallback is never used. Level 6's paws card has one right answer and two wrong ones that differ from it; the knots cards likewise.
- **Hints:** following `bestRig` always lifts the crate; the glow's hooks match it.
- **Layout:** `liftLayout` at the four views for `h` 1, 2 and 3, against the assertions above, including the ledge handle clear of the ledge friends and the crate's raised position inside `view`.
- **Registry, lands and how-to tests** pass with the new game, including its land still fitting on one screen at every band.
