# Little Conductor, levels 1–3 (definition)

Defined 2026-10-18 as slice **M1** of the [music and instruments study](../deep-dives/subjects/music-instruments.md#build-slices-in-order), from its [proposed ladder](../deep-dives/subjects/music-instruments.md#first-games-proposed-ladder-little-conductor) (plans 1–3 only; 4–8 are later packets, one property at a time), its [candidates table](../deep-dives/subjects/music-instruments.md#candidates-and-overlap-decisions), the [IDEAS row](../IDEAS.md#candidates) and the WAITING [Music row](../WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER.md#decisions-that-block-work). Docs only: no game, ID registration, voice line, sound or save field exists yet. **Ready to build:** every open call below has a default the build can use. M2 builds levels 1–2 (tap pads, hearing marks, Done); M3 builds level 3 (the two pictured requests and Check).

**Fun action and purpose:** three friends stand on a wooden bandstand and play one little four-beat tune, over and over. Two big pads sit under them, a **turtle** and a **rabbit**. She taps one and the band changes pace at the next beat: the same notes, in the same order, slow and steady or quick and light. She is the conductor; the friends follow her. The learning goal is **tempo as a property of a performance**: the tune stays the same while how fast it goes changes, and she is the one who changes it. Level 1 is pure cause and effect; level 2 asks her to hear the tune both ways before she picks the one she likes; level 3 asks her to follow two pictured requests, one per half of the tune. No beat-tapping accuracy, no "which was faster?" quiz, no instrument names and no music theory words: the pads say *slow* and *fast*, and the pet says *turtle* and *rabbit*.

## Overlap

- **Sound Garden** owns the listening question *fast or slow?* (its bunny and turtle) and keeps its background silenced so a tempo underneath cannot blur it. Little Conductor never asks her to judge a tempo she hears; she **sets** it and hears the result. Level 3 follows a picture, not a sound. The turtle and rabbit pictures are deliberately the same vocabulary as Sound Garden's ([decision 3](#open-decisions)), so the island says *turtle* for slow in one voice.
- **Beat Builder** owns deciding which beats sound; the squares are the composition. Here no note, beat or part can be edited. Its toddler level 1 has the same gentle Done shape (the check appears once a whole cycle has sounded), which this definition reuses on purpose as the heard gate.
- **Song Maker** owns the note grid and plays at the island clock's tempo; it never changes pace. Its *answer the phrase* proposal (M4) stays there.
- **Jelly Drums** and **Rhythm Neighbors** own free notes and call-and-response taps; neither has a band that keeps playing on its own.
- **Sing-Along Barn** advances a song at her pace by taps between lines; its tempo is not a property she sets.
- **Dance Workshop** and **Cue Studio** (proposals) are movement and pet behavior; the band is not a choreography editor.
- No shipped game lets her control a performance's tempo while the notes stay fixed. Strong or gentle (plan 5), cueing a player (6), a baton (4) and shaped performances (7–8) are later definitions; Sound Garden's open *Loud or quiet* lean waits on plan 5, not on this slice.

## The model

- **Paces** are `PaceId ∈ {turtle, rabbit}` with `PACES = { turtle: 60, rabbit: 90 }` beats per minute ([decision 2](#open-decisions)). They are playback choices, never targets for her taps.
- **A motif** is `{ id, melody: [step×4], bass: [beat…], drum: [beat…] }`: four beats, one melody note per beat from `PENTATONIC` steps within one octave (`src/audio/notes.ts`), the bass on beats 1 and 3, the drum on every beat. Three authored motifs rotate by seed, one per round, so the first screen stays familiar while the tune varies:

  | Motif `id` | `melody` (pentatonic steps) | Shape |
  | --- | --- | --- |
  | `hop` | 4, 7, 9, 7 | up, up, back |
  | `slide` | 9, 7, 4, 2 | down the hill |
  | `wave` | 2, 7, 2, 9 | low, high, low, higher |

- **The score clock** is the game's own, not the island's: `music.beats()` runs at the registered style's fixed bpm, so the game registers `music: silenced(STYLES.jelly)` only to keep the island's loop silent and the engine's clock alive, and advances a pure `Score` in `update(dt)`. `Score` holds `{ pace, pending: PaceId | null, beat: number (fractional), played: number (whole beats emitted) }`. `advance(score, dt)` returns `{ score, events }`: it moves `beat` by `dt × bpm(pace) / 60`, emits each whole beat's events (melody step, bass, drum) exactly once in motif order, and applies `pending` at the first beat boundary it crosses (so a tap lands on the next beat, at most 1 s away at 60, 0.67 s at 90). Events are then voiced in `index.ts` with the synthesized marimba, pluck and drum; the model never touches Web Audio.
- **Hearing marks:** `heard: Record<PaceId, number>` counts whole beats emitted at each pace since that pace was last selected (not since the round began), and `heardEver: Record<PaceId, boolean>` records that a pace has reached four. A pace change resets `heard[pace]` for the newly selected pace to 0.
- **The heard gate:** `canFinish(score, level)` is `heard[pace] ≥ 4` at level 1 (one whole phrase at the current setting since the last change) and `heardEver.turtle && heardEver.rabbit && heard[pace] ≥ 4` at level 2 (both contrasted, and the version she finishes with heard whole since she chose it).
- **Requests** (level 3): a round is `{ motif, requests: [PaceId, PaceId] }` with the two requests different ([decision 4](#open-decisions)), so each round contains one change and the seed picks which half is slow. The round moves through `phrase ∈ {0, 1}`; each phrase has `choice: PaceId | null` and `submitted: boolean`. `check(round, phrase)` is correct iff `choice === requests[phrase]`: a pure comparison, no timing. The truth table is the four cells of choice × request; two are correct and two name the requested pace.
- **A round** is the seeded motif, and at level 3 the seeded request order. Nothing else varies with the seed; the paces, voices and friends' instruments are fixed per build.

## Screen and controls

- **Landscape (1024×768 design area):** a wooden **bandstand** across the upper half with three friends in a row (`CRITTERS`, three seeded from the cast with the host bear excluded, [decision 5](#open-decisions)), each holding a prop that is their voice: a marimba, a little guitar (the pluck) and a drum. Each friend bobs in time at the current pace and its prop flashes on its beat. Below the bandstand, two **pace pads**, 180×180, centered with a 60-unit gap: the **turtle** (left, "slow" in small print under it) and the **rabbit** (right, "fast"). Under each pad four **beat marks** (dots) light one by one as that pace plays, and show how much of a whole phrase has been heard since the pad was chosen. At the right edge, **Listen again** (a 120-unit round ear button, replays the current phrase from its start) above **Done** (a 120-unit green check, dim until the heard gate opens). The pet is the corner guide, bottom-left, with 150 units kept clear; the pads sit from x = 300 to 740, well clear of it. All targets are at least 100 logical units after scaling.
- **A pad tap:** immediate touch-down feedback (the pad lifts and glows, the animal gives one hop) and a short preview tick at that pace; the pad shows a small **pending ring** that counts the fraction of the beat left until the band changes; at the boundary the band is at the new pace, the ring fades, the active pad stays lit and the other dims. Tapping the active pad again does nothing but the hop. Before the first tap the band is silent and the friends stand ready; after six seconds without a tap the turtle pad pulses once, an invitation that is never a hint ([decision 9](#open-decisions)).
- **Static fallback:** the active pad is also framed in a thick dark outline and the pending state shows a small clock icon, so the active/pending distinction never depends on color or motion alone.
- **Done** (levels 1–2): dim before the gate; tapping it dim makes the beat marks under the active pad blink the dots still unlit and the pet says the hear-whole line (level 1) or the hear-other line (level 2, if the other pace has not been heard); nothing is counted. Lit: the band plays the tune once more at her chosen pace as the **performance** (the pet claps on each beat), the friends bow, and the round finishes.
- **Level 3:** a fourth friend, the **listener**, sits at the top-right with a **sign** (a 200-unit card) showing the request for the current phrase: the same turtle or rabbit picture as the pads. The band holds at the start of each phrase (the friends sway in place, silent) until she taps a pad; the tap **auditions** the phrase at that pace once (free, repeatable, the pad stays chosen); **Check** (a 120-unit button in Done's place) submits the chosen pad. Correct: the phrase plays at that pace while the listener nods on each beat, then the sign turns to the second request (or the round finishes after the second). Wrong: a gentle boing, the sign bounces and the pet names the requested pace; the choice stays so she can see what she picked against the sign, and another tap changes it. Check with no pad chosen: the pads wiggle, "Pick a pad first", nothing counted. Listen again replays the current phrase at the chosen pace.
- **Portrait:** the bandstand takes the top third with the three friends closer together (the listener and sign below it at the right at level 3), the pads sit in a row across the middle at the same 180 units, and Listen again and Done/Check sit at the bottom-right, the pet keeping its corner. `conductorLayout(view, level)` returns every rectangle and a test asserts the sizes at the four views.
- **Speech and the band:** the intro and the level's instruction play before the band starts, so they never compete. Feedback lines during play (an early Done, the wrong-Check line, the finish) duck the band to half gain while spoken and restore it; the clock is not stopped and heard beats during ducking still count at levels 1–2 ([decision 8](#open-decisions)). At level 3 the band is already holding at a boundary whenever the pet speaks about a Check.
- **Sound:** three synthesized voices from the existing helpers (`sfx.marimba`, a pluck like the `pluck` lead, `sfx.drum`), one short tick for a pad preview, a boing for a wrong Check, claps on the performance, and `sfx.tada` on the bow. No dynamics: one fixed gain per voice ([decision 2](#open-decisions) also fixes that the drum is quieter than the melody so the pitch is what she hears). Nothing here needs device volume above the island's setting.

## Levels

| Level | Name | Bands (default) | What she decides | Finish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Lead a little band** | toddler, preschool | Which pace, and when to change it | A whole phrase heard at the pace she last chose, then Done: one performance, one sticker |
| 2 | **Two ways to play** | toddler, preschool, pre-K | Hear the tune both ways, then which version to finish with | Both paces heard whole, the chosen one heard whole since she chose it, then Done |
| 3 | **Two pictured requests** | preschool, pre-K | Which pace the sign asks for, for each half of the tune | Two correct Checks |

Default windows: toddler 1–2, preschool 1–3, pre-K 2–3; no school window until plans 4–8 are defined ([decision 1](#open-decisions)). The IDs are frozen as 1–3 so later plans append above 3.

### First rounds

- **Level 1 (`hop`):** the pet: "Here's your little band, {name}. Tap the turtle or the rabbit, and they'll play." The friends stand ready, silent. She taps the rabbit: it hops, the pad glows, the band begins at once (nothing is pending before the first beat) at 90, the three friends bob quickly, the beat marks under the rabbit light 1, 2, 3, 4 and Done lights up. She taps the turtle: the ring ticks for the rest of the beat, then the band slows to 60 and the friends sway long; the rabbit's marks dim, the turtle's start filling and Done dims again. She taps Done while two marks are lit: the two unlit dots blink and the pet says "Listen to the whole tune, then tap Done." The fourth mark lights, Done lights, she taps it: the band plays the tune once more, slow, the pet claps the four beats, the friends bow. Finish, misses 0, hints 0.
- **Level 2 (`slide`):** "Hear it slow and hear it fast, then pick the one you like." She taps the turtle, hears four beats (the turtle's marks fill and a small check appears beside that pad), taps Done: it is dim; "Now hear the rabbit's way too." She taps the rabbit, four beats fill, Done lights. She taps the turtle again to finish slow: Done dims until four turtle beats have played since this tap, then lights; Done. The performance plays slow; bow; finish.
- **Level 3 (`wave`, requests `[turtle, rabbit]`):** "The listener has a sign. Play the first part the way the sign shows, then tap Check." The sign shows the turtle; the band holds. She taps the rabbit: the band auditions the phrase fast, the pad stays chosen. She taps Check: boing, the sign bounces, "The sign says turtle: slow and steady." One miss. She taps the turtle (audition, slow), Check: the phrase plays slow, the listener nods; the sign turns to the rabbit. She taps the rabbit, Check: the second phrase plays fast, the listener claps, the friends bow. Finish, misses 1, hints 0.

## Accounting, help and finishing

- **Levels 1–2 never miss:** pad taps in any number and order, early Done, Listen again, tapping the active pad, tapping during the performance (ignored), repeating the instruction. `ctx.finish({ misses: 0, hints: 0 })` always. The difficulty system therefore steps a toddler from 1 to 2 after two rounds and a preschooler from 2 to 3; that is **interface progression**, the next thing to try, not evidence she hears tempo, and the how-to card says so. The global rules are untouched; a grown-up can pin.
- **Level 3, a miss:** a Check whose chosen pad is not the phrase's request. The response is specific and visible: the sign bounces and the pet names the requested pace by its animal. Auditions, re-taps, Listen again, Check with nothing chosen and repeated taps during feedback are never counted.
- **Glow and hints (level 3):** after two misses on one phrase the requested pad glows and the band previews that pace once; `hints++`, once per phrase. **Show a hint** in the pause sheet (`Scene.pauseActions`) does the same for the current phrase and counts at most once per phrase; asked again it repeats the words. Start again rebuilds the same seeded round at phrase 0 and keeps misses and hints.
- **Finish:** after the bow, with or without help: one ordinary sticker from the shell. Nothing is saved: the performance is transient ([decision 7](#open-decisions)); no creation, discovery or journal entry.
- **Leaving:** `destroy()` stops the score clock, releases every synthesized node and timer, and drops the pending change; a continuation after destruction never emits a note.

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Motifs:** each has exactly four melody steps, every step is a `PENTATONIC` step within one octave, bass on beats 1 and 3, drum on all four; the three motifs are pairwise distinct.
- **Pitch and order are pace-independent:** advancing a score through one phrase at `turtle` and again at `rabbit` emits identical event sequences; only the time stamps differ by the ratio 90/60.
- **No skipped or repeated events:** for 500 seeded sequences of random pace taps at random moments (including several within one beat), the concatenated events equal the motif repeated, each whole beat exactly once; `played` is monotone; a pending pace applies at the next whole beat and never earlier.
- **Timing bound:** a pending change always lands within one beat of the current pace (≤ 1.0 s at 60, ≤ 0.667 s at 90).
- **Heard gate:** at level 1, `canFinish` is false until four whole beats at the current pace since its selection, true after, false again right after a pace change; at level 2, false until both paces have reached four and the current pace has four since its latest selection; Listen again does not reset `heard`.
- **Requests:** over 500 seeds, every level-3 round has two different requests, both orders appear, every motif appears at every level; the same seed gives the same round.
- **Check:** the truth table: `check` is true for the two matching cells and false for the two others; a wrong Check leaves `choice` in place; `glowTarget(round)` is the current phrase's request and following it finishes the phrase.
- **Accounting:** a scripted level-3 round of rabbit, Check, turtle, Check, rabbit, Check gives misses 1, hints 0; three wrong Checks on phrase 0 then the right one gives hints 1, and a hint asked again on the same phrase leaves it at 1; a Check with no choice changes nothing; any scripted level-1 or level-2 sequence gives misses 0, hints 0.
- **Layout:** `conductorLayout` at the four views for levels 1 and 3: every target ≥ 100 units, pads not overlapping each other or the buttons, the sign inside `view`, the pet's corner clear.
- **Registry, lands and how-to tests** pass with the new game, including the `silenced` list in the registry test and Music Mountain still fitting on one screen at every band with seven games ([decision 6](#open-decisions)).

## Ghost finger, how-to card, voice and registration

- **Ghost finger:** `touchDemo: true`; `autotouch()` at level 1: tap the turtle, wait until the turtle's four marks are lit (about 4 s), tap the rabbit, wait four marks (about 2.7 s), tap Done. Level 2: the same taps, then Done once it lights. Level 3: read `round.requests[phrase]` (shown on the sign, nothing hidden), tap that pad, wait for the audition to end, tap Check, twice. The bot never taps a wrong pad or a dim Done; the scripted browser play adds one wrong Check, one early Done and one requested hint itself. The bot waits by polling the public score state, not by fixed sleeps, so a slower device still works.
- **How-to card** (`src/content/howto.ts`). Goal: "Lead a little band: tap the turtle to play the tune slowly or the rabbit to play it fast."
  - Steps: "Tap the turtle or the rabbit; the band changes pace at the next beat" (all levels); "Hear the whole tune at your pace, then tap Done" `{ from: 1, to: 1 }`; "Hear it both ways, then pick the way to finish with and tap Done" `{ from: 2, to: 2 }`; "Look at the listener's sign, tap the pad it shows, then tap Check, once for each half of the tune" `{ from: 3, to: 3 }`.
  - Finish: `{ from: 1, to: 2 }` "The band plays your tune once more and takes a bow."; `{ from: 3, to: 3 }` "Both halves match the signs and the band takes a bow."
  - Notes: "Tapping, switching and listening again are never counted; the notes never change, only how quickly they come, and fast is not better than slow" (all levels); `{ from: 1, to: 2 }` "Moving up a level here means she is ready for the next thing to try, not that she can tell tempos apart"; `{ from: 3, to: 3 }` "A Check that does not match the sign counts as a miss; after two the right pad glows, which counts as a hint".
- **Voice lines** under `little-conductor.*`: `intro`, `instruct-tap` (1), `instruct-both` (2), `instruct-sign` (3), `hear-whole`, `hear-other`, `request-turtle`, `request-rabbit` (said when a sign turns), `wrong-turtle`, `wrong-rabbit` (naming the requested pace), `pick-first`, `match`, `hint-turtle`, `hint-rabbit`, `bow`, `done`. `{pet}` and `{name}` are optional in each. No line says "tempo", "beat" or an instrument's name; the words are *slow*, *fast*, *turtle*, *rabbit*, *tune* and *band*.
- **Registration:** ID `little-conductor`, name "Little Conductor", spoken title `game.little-conductor`. Region (subject) `music-mountain`. Skills `['listening', 'tempo', 'cause and effect', 'following a picture']` (free strings). Bands: toddler 1–2, preschool 1–3, pre-K 2–3 ([decision 1](#open-decisions)). `music: silenced(STYLES.jelly)`, listed with the other silenced games in the registry test. Home land **Music Mountain** (6 games today, 7 with this one; [decision 6](#open-decisions)). Hub icon: a bandstand with a turtle and a rabbit under it. Sticker: a seeded friend holding its instrument with a little pace animal beside it.
- **Co-play:** one person is the conductor and the other says "slow" or "fast" to ask for a change, then swap; at level 3 the grown-up can be the listener and read the sign aloud. **Off-screen** (a concept only; the study's *Our Little Band* card keeps its named-review requirement before anything printed): with a willing grown-up, hum any family song, one person signals "turtle" or "rabbit" with a hand, the other hums at that pace, then swap; listening and declining a turn are fine.

- **Rework couplings:** none required and nothing saved. ## Check tier

New-game tier at build time, in two sessions. **M2 (levels 1–2):** typecheck and unit tests (the rule properties above plus registry, lands and how-to tests). One scripted play through a new `browser-check.mjs` case (its README row is part of the build), filtered to level 1 and level 2, with one early Done, at least one pace change inside a beat and Done at the gate; one portrait screenshot at level 2 with both pads' marks visible. `fingerdemo` at levels 1 and 2. `npm run build`, because the registry, lands, voice and how-to files change. Wrong-answer and hint paths do not exist at 1–2, so the scripted play does not claim them. **M3 (level 3):** new-interaction tier: typecheck, the level-3 rule tests, one filtered play at level 3 with one wrong Check and one requested hint, `fingerdemo` at level 3, one portrait screenshot with the sign.

Device checks stay open: whether 60 and 90 sound clearly different on an iPad speaker with these voices; whether a two-year-old reads the pads as "tap me" and the pending ring as "wait a moment"; whether the drum masks the melody at the chosen gains; device speech of the animal names; whether any child hears fast as "winning" despite the equal bows.

## Open decisions

Each has a default the build uses. None blocks it. The study's decision 1 (identity beside Sound Garden) is settled here as a default by the [overlap audit](#overlap): she sets the pace and hears the consequence, Sound Garden asks her to judge one; its decision 2 (placement) is defaulted below; its decision 3 (music-educator review of terminology) is not needed for a card that says only *slow*, *fast*, *tune* and *band*, and stays open for any later level that names a musical property; its decision 4 (participation and persistence) is defaulted below.

1. **Bands.** Default toddler 1–2, preschool 1–3, pre-K 2–3, no school window. Alternatives: pre-K 1–3 (a four-year-old may still enjoy level 1), or a one-level school window at 3 until plans 4–8 are defined. Lap stays with Jelly Drums and Sing-Along Barn, as the study found.
2. **Paces.** Default turtle 60 and rabbit 90 beats per minute, a contrast of half. Alternative: 56 and 112 (a doubling, the most audible contrast); either is a one-line table change. The drum sits below the melody in gain; equal numeric gain is not equal loudness and the device check decides.
3. **Pictures.** Default the turtle and rabbit, the same vocabulary Sound Garden uses for slow and fast. Alternative: a snail and a bird, if sharing the icons turns out to blur the two games' different questions.
4. **Level 3 requests.** Default: the two requests always differ, so each round holds one change. Alternative: allow same-pace pairs (turtle, turtle) so the second half can be "keep going", a smaller decision that some children may find harder to trust.
5. **The band's cast.** Default: three friends seeded from `CRITTERS` with the Music Mountain host (the bear) excluded, on marimba, pluck and drum. Alternative: a fixed trio for a more familiar first screen. The rework may later replace them with her creatures.
6. **Home land.** Default Music Mountain as its seventh game; the land test decides whether it still fits at every band. Alternative: none sensible; this is a music game.
7. **Keeping a performance.** Default: nothing saved; the performance is transient. Alternative: a `tune`-style creation slot ("my slow tune"), a save change better folded into RW06.
8. **Speech during play.** Default: the band ducks to half gain under a spoken line and keeps its clock, since the only lines during play at 1–2 are an early-Done reminder and the finish. Alternative: pause at the next beat and resume from the phrase start, if ducked speech proves hard to hear on a device.
9. **The idle invitation.** Default: the turtle pad pulses once after six seconds with no tap, never counted. Alternative: the pet points instead, or nothing.
10. **Adaptation.** Default: the current difficulty rules stand; two smooth creative rounds step up within the band's range and the how-to note explains this as interface progression. Alternative: pin level 1 for toddlers by default (a grown-up's pin does that today).
