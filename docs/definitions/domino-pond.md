# Domino Pond levels 1–3 (definition)

Proposed touch game `domino-pond`, PP2 of the [puzzle-pages study](../deep-dives/subjects/puzzle-pages.md#build-slices-in-order). Defined 2026-11-18 from `fdb427957` in [PR #1802](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1802), following the [November 17 decision](../archive/DECISIONS-2026-11-17.md#games). The three levels form one implementation batch.

Lay two-ended stepping stones over shallow pond patches. When every patch meets its clue, a duck crosses and the round finishes. The player first matches two quantities, then combines the dots under a patch outline, then compares that total with a visible threshold. Domino Run uses dominoes for toppling and prediction. Shell Rows uses placement to preserve a sequence. Domino Pond asks which two linked values can go into which patch.

## Ladder and sample boards

| Level | Band | Board and rule | Example |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 *Match the dots* | Pre-K | Two separate horizontal two-cell patches. Match the target dots on each cell in order. Use two distinct stones with 1–4 dots per end. Rotate by swapping the ends. | Left patch wants `1, 3`, right wants `4, 2`. Tray has `3│1` and `2│4`, so both stones need turning. |
| 2 *Make the totals* | School | One 2×2 board with one patch per row. Use two distinct stones with 1–4 dots per end. Each row has an exact total. Stones can span the row boundary. | Tray `1│2`, `2│3`. Top 3, bottom 5. A horizontal solution places the stones in tray order. A vertical solution places 1 above 2 in the left column and 2 above 3 in the right column. |
| 3 *Read the signs* | Grown-up | One 2-row × 3-column board with one patch per column. Use three distinct stones with 1–4 dots per end. One patch each shows less than, exactly and more than, with a numeral and spoken wording such as “fewer than four dots”. | Tray `1│2`, `2│3`, `3│4`. Left <4, middle =5, right >5. Vertical stones in tray order make 3, 5 and 7. |

One board per round. Set `BandLevels` to `prek: 1–1`, `school: 2–2`, `grownup: 3–3`. Existing signpost and `playBand` rules supply age access. Higher bands' how-to cards show the same stone motion and the clue of that band. For co-play, one person reads a clue or points to its dots and the other lays a stone. At a table, draw two stepping-stone spaces on paper and find a domino that fits both dot counts.

## Screen, scoring and help

- Keep the entire board and tray visible at 768 × 1024 upright and on the landscape view. Give cells and operable controls at least 100 logical units after scaling at every level. Use the shared touch-down feedback. Tap a tray stone to select it, tap a turn button to cycle through its distinct orientations, then tap its outlined anchor cell to lay it. Highlight both candidate cells on selection and keep the anchor end marked. Level 1 cycles the two horizontal directions. Later levels use four directions, including reversal, with doubles using the right and down directions from the left or top anchor. Each occupied half can remove its whole stone back to the tray. Undo restores the previous placement or removal and the available pieces.
- On level 1, speak “Match the dots on both ends.” The wrong count on either end gently rejects the drop, counts one miss and points out the mismatched cell. On levels 2–3, permit every geometrically legal trial placement. Outline each patch and show its running count alongside its clue, with an empty-cell marker on partial patches. Only judge the totals when the full board is submitted. A wrong submission counts one miss and names and highlights the first unmet patch. Require a board edit before judging it again. On every level, out-of-bounds or occupied targets, turning, selecting, removal, undo and cancellation are free.
- After two misses, show the next helpful move as a glow. Explicit Help counts once per request via the coach and speaks the same move. Prefer extending the current placements. If none extends, point to a placed stone to return for which a completion retains the most current placements, then recompute after the player acts. Keep other placed stones stable during a hint. The hinted cells and stone must agree with its spoken direction.
- Give a short crossing animation on a valid full board. Level 1 completes automatically after the second match. Levels 2–3 use a picture check button that appears when the board fills. Finish once through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`, including after assistance. Voice cues and the check mark also explain success on mute.

## Generation and acceptance

Use seeded rules in `logic.ts`. Draw a small exact tiling of the level's mask and assign pair values before deriving its clues. Shuffle the tray and initial direction independently of solution order. Use stable per-stone IDs, even if future levels admit repeated values. Keep the level 1 masks separate, and allow either distinct-end orientation in each playable direction on levels 2–3. For level 2, generate two different row totals and at least one legal first placement that has no completion until the stone is moved. For level 3, derive strict thresholds one or two dots from the example total with thresholds in 2–7. Verify on the empty board that each sign could pass and fail across geometric full tilings for that tray.

Enumerate non-overlapping orthogonally adjacent cell pairs with the allowed orientations, taking the first empty cell at each step. Deduplicate identical placements of the same stone, including a double turned end-for-end. Keep a bounded seeded retry budget and fall back to the table's audited example if a candidate fails. Require at least one valid tiling and at least one geometric arrangement that fails a clue at each level. Validate a submitted board by covering every cell exactly once, using every stone exactly once and meeting all clues. Accept every solution the rules allow. A reference tiling can seed the generator, and hints search from the player's current board. When a board needs revision, maximizing retained stones gives a finite route that can remove one conflicting stone at a time.

## Land and sources

Choose Puzzle Peaks. At the source base, its 16 registrations span the bands, with 15 visible on the pre-K and school side and Marble Jump on the older side. With this game, pre-K and school each page into 8 + 8, with the older signpost on both pages. Grown-up pages into 9 + 8, with no signpost. Lap, toddler and preschool keep 2, 5 and 11 visible respectively. Their older sides page into 8 + 7, 6 + 6 and a single page of 6 respectively. These are source counts using `landPages`' 13-spot rule, including a host, page arrows and signpost where present. The future build checks actual geometry through the existing land test. The game has one land at every band.

Counting each dot and comparing two groups draws on Common Core K.CC.B.4–5 and K.CC.C.6. Joining the two counts and finding different pairs draws on K.OA.A.1–3. Grade 1's 1.NBT.B.3 confirms sign conventions for two-digit comparisons. The grown-up board applies that notation to small patch totals. These anchors explain the tasks and carry no learning-outcome claim. [Counting and Cardinality](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/CC/), [Operations and Algebraic Thinking](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/OA/), [Grade 1 Number and Operations in Base Ten](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/1/NBT/).
