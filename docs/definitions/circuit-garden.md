## Terminal graph and admissible boards

Use an undirected finite graph whose nodes are **electrical terminals/junctions**, separate from their screen positions. Battery terminals are `P` and `N`; the one nonpolar lamp has two distinct terminals `L` and `R`. No internal conductor joins `P` to `N`: the ideal source is a labelled component, excluded from conducting-path searches. The lamp is a labelled edge `L–R`, distinct from ordinary wires. A fixed wire or fitted connector adds a conducting edge between its two named endpoints. A closed switch adds its edge; an open switch removes it. Drawn line crossings do not connect unless an explicit junction dot/node says so. Draw both component terminals and all existing connections, including the return side; never treat a component's entire sprite as one shared node.

For a legal board/state, exclude the source edge and ask whether a simple path from `P` to `N` passes through the lamp edge. Equivalently for the admitted boards, after removing the lamp edge, one lamp terminal must be reachable from `P` and the other from `N` (either orientation). **Validation also rejects any `P`–`N` path with the lamp removed**, so a wire bypass cannot accidentally satisfy the lamp rule. A source-to-lamp stub, joined lamp terminals alone, or a closed path disconnected from the source cannot light it. This is a Boolean connectivity model, not a calculation of current. No arbitrary graph is player-placeable: only the authored edge slots below exist.

| Level graph | Fixed edges | Variable edges / initial state |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `P–L`; lamp `L–R` | One connector slot `R–N`, initially absent. Exactly one tray connector. |
| 2 | `P–U`, `V–L`, `R–N`; lamp `L–R` | Switch `U–V`, initially open. No connector tray. |
| 3 | Lamp `L–R` | Two independent connector slots `P–L` and `R–N`, initially absent. Exactly two interchangeable tray connectors; no switch. |

Each slot is a broad bridge target spanning its two large socket illustrations; placing one connector occupies the entire slot atomically. No half-fitted conductive wire, hidden intermediate terminal, extra junction, polarity puzzle, bypass socket or misleading decoy. A bend in a fixed route is only visual. Each legal slot holds at most one piece; fitting either interchangeable piece in either level-3 slot is valid. Reversing a connector changes nothing. Removal returns that piece to the tray. Electrical acceptance uses endpoint connectivity, never connector identity, angle, wire length, screen coordinates or a reference screenshot.

E1's finite model table is exhaustive: level 1 absent/present gives off/on; level 2 open/closed gives off/on; level 3 neither/only supply/only return/both gives off/off/off/on. The two possible fill orders and the permutation of level 3 pieces must all succeed. Every starting board is off, every gap can be filled, and a useful next connection exists in every unsolved placement state. Seeded variation may mirror the board horizontally or use one of two fixed decorative wire bends and palette choices; none changes topology, quantities or difficulty. No unbounded random graph generation or shuffled socket meanings. Future tests enumerate these states and variants, not just a successful authored solution.

Compute a complete new electrical state atomically on each committed placement/removal/toggle, then show the lamp's result immediately. Show a steady whole-loop outline when lit, with a distinct gap/break cue when relevant. There is no travelling supply droplet, sequential lamp arrival, consumed charge or representation of electron speed. Fixed brightness is a display choice, not evidence about resistance/efficiency. The battery supplies energy; the lamp transfers energy to light and surroundings; charge is not used up. These are model boundaries for recommended educator review, not a quantitative energy lesson or a physical-realism/transfer claim.

## Three rounds, observation and finish

| Level | First round and decision | Exact completion gate |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 **A way back** | Lantern off; supply connection already drawn, return gap clearly visible. Drag the connector or select it then tap its bridge target. Watch it light; remove/reconnect freely. | Pictured Done becomes available after one lit state has actually been displayed in this round. The player may finish with the lamp currently off after further exploration. No Check. |
| 2 **A switch changes it** | Complete wiring; switch starts open. Tap its large lever to close/open and compare. Two pictured marks show which off/on states have been seen. Choose either final state. | Done after both an off and an on state have been displayed. Starting off counts only after the first visible frame; no requirement to toggle back to off after on. No preferred final state and no Check. |
| 3 **Mend the lantern** | Two visible gaps and an always-visible pictured lit-lamp request. Fit/remove pieces in either order and observe freely. Deliberate Check compares the current lamp state with on. | Successful Check of the current complete graph enables Done. A real edit/removal/Undo invalidates that Check; a new correct Check is needed. Tapping an already occupied slot or changing selection alone is inert and preserves it. |

Observation flags are round-local monotone facts that the rendered state was visible, not that a computed state briefly existed while covered or paused. Apply flags on the first visible frame, with no timed dwell or reaction deadline. Both observation and Check feedback work with sound off. In 1–2, Done before eligibility explains the next observation and offers Help, with zero misses and no automatic finish. In 3, an early Done points to Check, with zero misses. Passing a gate never awards immediately: the player chooses Done; the game calls `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` once, the shell awards one ordinary sticker including with help. Lamp activations, Check and repeated Done never award extra stickers.

Restart restores that level's authored initial board and clears observation/Check eligibility; it retains round misses, hint totals and assistance identities. Pause holds state. Resize repositions the same graph and controls, cancels an in-progress drag/selection safely and returns its uncommitted piece; it cannot complete a connection, set an observation or issue a Check on its own. Leaving discards the transient board. Destroy releases drag handles, listeners, owned textures and guarded callbacks; nothing can light, speak or finish after destruction. Future asynchronous work uses scene-owned timing and the shell's destroyed-promise contract.

## Exploration, misses, help and adaptation

Free actions: selecting, fitting/removing, toggling, Undo, comparing, prediction/preferences, speech replay, cancelled/invalid drops and unproductive exploration. An invalid drop returns the piece to its previous home with a soft visual nudge; no boing/miss. An occupied target is inert. Undo restores the preceding committed edit (one snapshot), including a help placement, then clears that snapshot; no redo cycle. Empty Undo is inert. Undo does not erase observations, misses or help.

At 3 only, a deliberate incorrect Check while off counts one miss per completed Check press, gives gentle boing plus spoken/local break feedback and preserves all pieces. No continuous rechecking or pointer-down repeat. If both gaps remain, indicate the supply gap first, then the return gap when it is the remaining break; never imply the return is optional. After two incorrect Checks, automatically reveal a useful next empty bridge with a nonblocking glow. The same persistent clue is not counted repeatedly. Invalid placement alone never triggers that threshold.

Help has stable per-round identities: `return` at 1, `show-on`/`show-off` at 2, `supply`/`return` at 3. At1 reveal and fit the return piece; at 2 operate the switch to a not-yet-observed state and show its observation mark; at 3 reveal and fit one missing connection in supply-then-return order. Count one hint when a new assistance identity is first supplied, whether requested or automatic. The automatic glow at 3 and later placement for that same identity share one count. Repeating speech/glow or restoring a previously helped piece after Undo/restart adds no count; it remains assisted evidence. Help with no missing observation/connection only points to Check/Done and adds no new hint. Two Help presses can complete a two-gap repair, but the player still chooses Check then Done. No inaccessible puzzle can block supported completion.

Use the existing global adaptation rule, without shared difficulty changes: two smooth rounds step up and two struggling rounds step down within approved ranges; pinned levels stay pinned. Zero-miss creative comparisons at 1–2 may therefore step up. That records interface progression, not independent circuit mastery; assisted completion remains counted help, not evidence of understanding. Developer confirmation of registration/model scope includes these initial bands and this accounting policy. No new mastery metric, medal, timer, life or failure screen.

## Required future switch topology semantics (E4 dependency only)

An older packet must retain terminal identity and define split/rejoin junctions independently of spatial routing. Its permitted switch network sits between `P` and one lamp terminal; the lamp's other terminal returns to `N`. Branches rejoin **before** the lamp. No branch bypasses the lamp or directly joins source terminals. With switches A and B, series places their edges in one path; parallel places them on separate branches joining the same two junctions. Open means edge absent, closed means present. Normalize degree-two ordinary wire bends if useful, while preserving labelled source/lamp/switch endpoints and explicit junctions.

| A closed | B closed | Series lamp | Parallel lamp / study's future either-friend brief |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| no | no | off | off |
| yes | no | off | on |
| no | yes | off | on |
| yes | yes | on | on |

A rotated/mirrored/rerouted network with the same labelled connectivity has the same behavior. Do not accept just one spatial picture or one successful switch setting; the future brief requires all four rows. Do not merge distinct topologies merely because both light with both switches closed. At least two spatial realizations of a valid parallel graph must be representable in that future kit. This is a required model invariant, not an authored older board, construction kit, truth-table UI, mystery pair, hint system or E4 definition. E4 must separately enumerate legal graphs/alternatives, counterexamples and useful tests, layouts, placement and accounting. Series/parallel **switch** behavior says nothing about multiple-lamp brightness or numerical loading; those require a different model packet.
