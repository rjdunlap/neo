# Paint Mixer, levels 1–4 (definition)

Defined 2026-10-27 as slice 1 of the [ratio and proportion study](../deep-dives/subjects/ratio-proportion.md#build-slices-in-order) ([packet row RP1](../deep-dives/subjects/RECOMMENDATIONS.md#ratio-and-proportion-recommendations)), [PR #1024](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1024). Decisions 1–7 were taken 2026-10-23 on the developer's delegation: a new game (not Rainbow Fingers' top), one ratio-table look shared with the cooking batch table, Counting Cove if the lands test passes (else Rainbow Meadow), levels 7–8 at `school`'s top, Mixing Room's home after the Lantern Lake capacity test, source-checked wording, a mixed colour as a permanent palette unlock. **Levels 1–2 are built** ([PR #1026](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1026), 2026-10-27; small deviations from this text: Help is the pause sheet's "Show me", the shell's own hint button, rather than a pet button; `pm.too-many` says "Too much {pot}"; loose drops wrap in rows of six); levels 3–4 are the next batch. The two build batches were ([batch plans](../GAME-BATCHES.md#paint-mixer-1-copy-the-row-and-twice-as-much)); levels 5–6 (RP2), 7–8 (RP3) and Mixing Room (RP4) are separate definitions.

**Fun action and purpose:** tap a big pot and a drop flies into the bucket with a plip; the bucket's colour moves at once. A small **sample jar** shows its recipe as a row of drops (1 red, 2 yellow), and the job is to make the bucket the same: first by copying the row (level 1), then twice as much (level 2), then to a fill line three batches deep while a **ratio table** fills (level 3), then any amount at all, and then the same colour again at a different amount (level 4). Learning goal: *the same mix at any size is the same colour; one more of each is not*. "For every 1 red, 2 yellow" is spoken from level 3. This is the island's only activity that compares two mixes with an exact count rule: Rainbow Fingers mixes two whole pours for a page by eye, Pet Kitchen doubles fruit on a plate, Picture Graph compares by difference, and recipe amounts stay in the cooking packet.

## The colour rule, measured

The study made the colour rule the first thing a definition must prove: *the colour judges the ratio*. Measured at definition time with the palette's pots (`src/art/palette.ts`: red `0xf2545b`, yellow `0xffd54a`, blue `0x4fa3f7`, white `0xffffff`), a drop-weighted mean in linear RGB and distances in Oklab (×100, where about 1–2 is a just-noticeable step side by side):

| Authored pair | Pots apart | 1:2 against 2:3 (one more of each) | 1:2 against 1:1 | 2:3 against 1:1 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| red + yellow (orange) | 30 | 1.7 | 4.5 | 2.8 |
| red + white (pink) | 39 | 2.1 | 5.5 | 3.4 |
| blue + white (sky) | 33 | 1.9 | 5.0 | 3.0 |
| red + blue (purple) | 31 | 2.0 | 5.1 | 3.1 |
| yellow + white (cream) | 19 | 1.1 | 2.9 | 1.8 |

Averaging in Oklab instead of linear RGB gains about a sixth and changes nothing below. Blue + yellow averages to grey (`194, 190, 187`), not green or even olive, so it stays unauthored (the study's rule). Three pots (1:2:1 peach against 2:2:1 or 1:2:2) differ by about 3.8.

**What this settles.** Equal ratios give **exactly the same colour**, by construction, and the game leans on that: a matched bucket held beside the sample is the same swatch, with no "close enough". But the study's "visibly wrong orange" for the additive trap is **not** available from an honest mean: one more of each is a careful-look difference, not a glance. So in this definition **the drop rows judge the ratio on screen and the colour is the feel.** Inside the bucket the drops arrange themselves as repeated copies of the sample's row (below); a mix is in ratio exactly when every row is complete, and a row that is short shows its missing places. The colour still rewards a match (the two swatches become one) and still separates a mix that is a whole step off (1:1 against 1:2, about 5), which is where a child's guess usually lands. The pot colours are constants in `logic.ts` with the mean and the distance function beside them, so the numbers above are re-derived by a test, not remembered. The cream pair (1.1) is **withheld from levels 1–4** until the iPad art check; the other four pairs are authored. Whether any of these steps reads on an iPad in a bright room is a [device check](../DEVICE-CHECKS.md), and the thresholds in the rule tests are toy-consistency defaults the build may raise after it.

## Overlap and reuse

- **Identity check** (the study's condition): the activity stands only if *two mixes compared by an exact count rule* is central. Every Check here asks whether the bucket's counts are the sample's counts at this size (levels 1–3) or any whole multiple of them (level 4). Nothing is judged by eye; nothing is a recipe to follow for its own sake. If the building session finds level 1 plays as Rainbow Fingers with outlines, levels 1–2 go to that game's dive as a mode and the packet restarts at the table.
- **Pet Kitchen** supplies the doubling idiom (two rows of the same plate) and, when the cooking packet's *Scale every ingredient* lands, the one shared ratio-table look (decision 2): a row per ingredient or pot, a column per batch, the multiplier spoken. Until then Paint Mixer draws the table and the cooking packet reuses it. **Fruit Sums** supplies "two different ways": the first way celebrated and listed under the sign, the same way again named and not counted. **Market Stall** supplies the tap-to-add, tap-to-take-back mat and the one-hint-per-order rule. **Rainbow Fingers** keeps painting pages and mixing by eye; Paint Mixer takes none of its recipes.
- Reuse `onTap` for every control (pots, Undo, Tip out, Check, Help; no drag, no steering); Duck Pond's pattern for a big target that reacts on touch-down; `ctx.particles` for the plip's splash; `src/audio/notes.ts` for the pentatonic plips; `view` for layout in both orientations. No new engine, save field, render texture or shared helper in levels 1–4. The journal's paint shelf and the palette unlock (decision 7) are RP2's (level 5), not this packet's.
- **Pots and bucket** are code-drawn in the game's own `art.ts`: four fat pots with a word on the belly and a shape mark (red dot, yellow stripe, blue star, white ring), a tin bucket with a visible inside, a small glass sample jar. Colour is never the only cue: the mark is on the pot, on every drop and on every table row.

## The model (`logic.ts`)

Pots `red | yellow | blue | white`. A **sample** is `{ pair: [PotA, PotB], counts: [a, b] }` drawn by `ctx.rng` from the level's authored pool below (cosmetic draws such as the jar's label colour, the bucket's dent and which pot stands left never change a count). A **bucket** is a count per pot on screen plus the order the drops arrived (for Undo), capped at **12 drops**. The mean colour `mix(counts)` is the drop-weighted mean of the pot constants in linear RGB, converted to sRGB for drawing; `distance(c1, c2)` is Oklab distance ×100.

**Pools.** Four authored pairs: red + yellow, red + white, blue + white, red + blue (the cream pair withheld, above). Ratios are written pot A : pot B in the pair's order; the pool holds both orders where listed.

| Level | Band | Pool (ratio × pair) | Bucket size | Pots on screen |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Copy the row (`copy`) | school | 1:1, 1:2, 2:1 | the sample's total (2–3), outlined | the pair only |
| 2 Twice as much (`double`) | school | 1:1, 1:2, 2:1, 1:3 | 2 × total (4–8), outlined as two rows of the sample | the pair only |
| 3 Fill to the line (`line`) | school (U) | 1:2, 2:1, 1:3, 3:1, 2:3, 3:2 | fill line at `batches × total`: 3 batches for totals 3–4 (9, 12), 2 for total 5 (10); no outlines until a batch starts | the pair and one stray pot |
| 4 Any amount (`any`) | school (U) | 1:2, 2:1, 1:3, 3:1, 2:3, 3:2 | no line; the cap of 12 is the only limit | the pair and one stray pot |

Every bucket size is a whole multiple of its sample's total, so every level is solvable with whole drops. The stray pot at 3–4 is seeded from the two pots the pair does not use (never the pair's partner that would make an authored pair ambiguous; the stray is named on the card as "a pot this sample doesn't use").

**Inputs.** `pour(pot)`: one drop of that pot enters the bucket (counts and order grow; the colour and the rows update). At 12 drops the bucket sloshes, "It's full: tip out or undo", nothing changes, nothing counted. `undo()`: the last drop flies back to its pot (free). `tipOut()`: every drop leaves with a glug (free). `check()`: the one commit (below). `help()`: asked-for help (below). Pouring, undo, tip-out and help are exploration; only Check can count a miss.

**The drop rows.** Inside the bucket the drops sit as **batches**, each a copy of the sample's row (pot A's drops then pot B's, in the sample's order, with their marks). Batch `k` is complete when every pot's count is at least `k × sample[pot]`; drops beyond the last complete batch sit in the **next row with outlined places for what it still needs**, and a drop of a pot that row already has in full (or of the stray pot) sits **loose** at the end of the row, outside any outline. Levels 1–2 pre-draw the outlines for every place the bucket holds; levels 3–4 draw a row's outlines when its first drop arrives. The rows are the proof: a bucket is in ratio exactly when there are no outlined gaps and no loose drops. `rows(sample, counts)` returns the batch list with each row's filled, missing and loose places, so the picture is a pure function the tests can read.

**The ratio table** (level 3 and up, the shared look): a header row with the sample's counts under the pots' marks, then a column per batch (`1 | 2 | 3` over `2 | 4 | 6`); column `k` fills when batch `k` is complete and empties again if Undo breaks it. The table never shows a column for a loose drop, so it only ever shows multiples: it is the ratio made into numbers, the rows are the ratio made into a picture, and the colour is the same for every filled column.

**Check.** `check(level, sample, counts, done)` returns one of:

- `match`: the counts are the target. Levels 1–3: exactly `k × sample` with `k × total` equal to the bucket size. Level 4: exactly `k × sample` for some `k ≥ 1` (any amount); on the second way, additionally `k ≠ done` where `done` is the first way's multiple.
- `moreSameWay` (levels 2–3 only): the counts are `k × sample` for some `k ≥ 1` below the size. The pet says "That's the colour. More, the same way, up to the line." **Not a miss.**
- `sameWay` (level 4, second way only): `k × sample` with `k === done`. "That's the same way. Make it a different amount." **Not a miss.**
- `wrong` with `{ pot, direction }`: anything else, **one miss**. The named fact is the stray pot if its count is above zero ("No blue in this one"); otherwise, with the target `t = k* × sample` (levels 1–3 `k*` is the bucket's multiple; level 4 `k*` is the whole multiple in `1..⌊12 / total⌋`, excluding `done`, whose counts are nearest the bucket's by total difference, ties to the smaller), the pot with the largest `|count − t[pot]|`, ties to pot A, as `too-many` or `short`. An empty bucket is `wrong` naming pot A as `short` ("Start with one red").

**Help.** After the **second miss on the same bucket**, or on the Help button, the mix's next useful control glows and `hints++` **once per bucket**: the pot of the first missing place in the rows when the bucket has no loose drop and is under its target; **Undo** when the last drop is loose; **Tip out** when a loose drop is buried and the pet says the plan ("Tip it out. For every 1 red, 2 yellow, three times"); **Check** when the counts already match. The glow retargets after every pour, undo or tip and stays until the match. A second Help press after the glow pours the rest as a ghost (the same taps a child would make, one per second), then Check glows for her: the supported finish, still one hint for that bucket. Following the glow from any state reaches `match`, because Undo and Tip out remove every loose drop and the pots then add exactly the missing places. At level 4's second way the glow aims at the smallest unused multiple.

**Rounds.** Levels 1–2: two buckets per round (two samples, a different pair each). Level 3: two buckets per round. Level 4: one sample, two ways (the first way celebrated and listed under the jar as its rows, the second listed beside it). The round finishes on the last match: the bucket and the sample slide together, the swatches become one, a chime, and `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` with the round's totals. Tip-out between buckets is automatic (a glug) and free.

## Screen and controls

- **Stage.** Landscape: the sample jar upper left with its row of drops beneath it (and, from level 3, the table to its right); the bucket centre, large, its inside showing the rows and its colour; Undo, Tip out and Check in a column to the bucket's right; the pots in a row along the bottom right; the pet bottom left and the corner buttons clear. Portrait stacks jar and table, then the bucket, then the three buttons in a row, then the pots along the bottom right; nothing shrinks. Planned sizes: pots 120 × 130 (a tap anywhere on the pot), the bucket 320 × 320 (a picture, not an input), Undo / Tip out / Check 130 × 110, the sample jar 150 × 190 (a tap on it repeats the instruction and names the recipe, free), Help the pet's own button. Drops are 30 units with their mark, up to 12 in four rows of three inside the bucket, so 100-unit targets and readable drops coexist at both orientations; `layout(level, view)` is audited at the lands test's four views (landscape, portrait, two phone insets).
- **Touch-down feedback.** A pot squashes and tips on touch-down and the drop leaves it at once; the bucket's colour moves before the plip ends. Undo lifts the last drop as the finger lands; Tip out tilts the bucket; Check lifts the bucket toward the jar. A rejected pour (the bucket full) still sloshes.
- **Speech.** `ctx.instruct` states the level's one ask ("Make the bucket the sample's colour: copy its row" / "Make twice as much: two rows of the sample" / "Fill the bucket to the line, the same way every time" / "Make the sample's colour, any amount; then make it again, a different amount"); the pet repeats it. From level 3 the pet says the recipe aloud when a bucket starts: "For every 1 red, 2 yellow". Counts are spoken from bounded number slots (one to four); colour words (orange, pink, sky, purple) are labels the pet says once on a match and are never required reading. Feedback names a pot and a direction ("Too red" / "One more yellow"), never a colour judgement of her mix.
- **Feel.** `STYLES.paint` (she is not making music, so the island's quiet bell style plays); a plip per drop rising one pentatonic step per completed batch, a low glug on tip-out, a two-note chime on a match. Palette pots; the bucket's colour is the only computed colour on screen. Reduced motion shows drops appearing in place without the flight.

## Accounting, help and finishing

| Level | Counts a miss | Counts a hint | Never counted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1–2 | each Check whose counts are not the target | the glow after the second miss on a bucket, or asked-for help; at most one per bucket | pouring, undo, tip-out, the full-bucket slosh, tapping the jar, "more, the same way" (level 2) |
| 3 | the same | the same | the same, plus "more, the same way" below the line |
| 4 | each Check that is not a whole multiple (or, on the second way, the first way's multiple again) | the same, once per way | the same, plus "that's the same way" |

- **Supported finish:** the glow always leads to a match (and the ghost pour reaches it without her), so every round finishes with one ordinary sticker through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`, help or not. No creation is kept and nothing is filed in the journal in levels 1–4 (the paint shelf and the palette unlock begin at level 5, RP2). Leaving early owes nothing.
- **Adaptation:** a child who checks one wrong bucket and fixes it is still smooth (at most one miss); two hints or four misses in a round are struggling. A miss is one Check, not one per drop. Assisted completion is one sticker, not evidence of mastery. Nothing here claims a seven-year-old understands ratio; it claims she copied, doubled and tripled a row and then found two amounts that are the same way.

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Colour:** the pot constants equal the palette's fills; `mix(k × counts)` is bit-identical to `mix(counts)` for `k` 1–12 and every authored sample (equal ratios, the same colour); blue + yellow is not in the authored pairs; for every authored pair, `distance(mix(1:2), mix(2:3)) ≥ 1.5` and `distance(mix(1:2), mix(1:1)) ≥ 4` (the table above, re-derived; the thresholds are defaults the device check may raise); `distance(a, a) === 0`.
- **Pools and sizes:** over 300 seeds per level the sample comes from that level's pool and pair set (never cream, never blue + yellow), the bucket size is a whole multiple of the total and at most 12, level 2's size is exactly twice the total, level 3's batches are 3 for totals 3–4 and 2 for total 5, level 4's total is at most 6 (so two distinct multiples fit under the cap); the stray pot at 3–4 is never one of the pair; the same seed gives the same sample.
- **Rows:** `rows(sample, counts)` has no gaps and no loose drops iff the counts are a positive whole multiple of the sample; a loose drop appears iff some pot exceeds its share of the next batch or is the stray pot; the number of complete batches is `min over pots of ⌊count / sample[pot]⌋`; rows are deterministic in the arrival order.
- **Check:** `match` iff the counts are the level's target (levels 1–3 the size's multiple; level 4 any positive multiple, and on the second way a multiple other than `done`); `moreSameWay` only at levels 2–3 and only for a positive multiple below the size; `sameWay` only on level 4's second way; otherwise `wrong`, naming the stray pot when poured, else the pot farthest from the nearest target with the right direction; an empty bucket names pot A short; every level-4 sample has at least two distinct multiples under 12, so the second way is always possible.
- **Help:** `glowFor(level, sample, counts, lastDrop, done)` returns the pot of the first missing place when there is no loose drop, Undo when the last drop is loose, Tip out when a loose drop is buried, Check when the counts match; following `glowFor` from every reachable bucket state (all count vectors up to 12 drops over the pots on screen, every arrival order class) reaches `match` in at most 24 steps; a hint is counted at most once per bucket (and per way at level 4); the ghost pour's tap list, applied to the state, ends in `match`.
- **Layout:** `layout(level, view)` at the four views: every input at least 100 units, pots at least 120 wide, nothing under the bottom-left pet or corner buttons, the bucket's inside holds twelve 30-unit drops without overlap, portrait stacks rather than shrinks, the table (level 3) does not overlap the bucket.
- **Registry, lands, how-to and off-screen tests** pass with the new game: Counting Cove 12 → 13 games (`LAND_SPOTS` is 13, so still one page), the card scoped per level, no "later levels" wording, `music` a playing style.

## Open decisions

Each has a default the build uses; none blocks the first batch.

1. **What judges the ratio on screen.** Default: the drop rows (complete copies of the sample's row, gaps and loose drops visible), with the colour as the feel and the exact-swatch reward; measured above. The alternative, exaggerating the colour step beyond the mean, would make the toy lie about mixing and is not taken.
2. **The averaging space.** Default: linear RGB, as the study stated; Oklab averaging gains about a sixth of a step and can replace it inside `mix()` without changing any rule, if the art check prefers its mid-tones.
3. **The cream pair.** Default: withheld from levels 1–4 (the weakest step); restored by the iPad art check, which may also decide the fifth pair for level 5's three-pot mixes.
4. **The stray pot at levels 3–4.** Default: one pot the sample does not use, named on the card and in the first `wrong`; it makes "which pot" a decision and gives the rows a loose drop to show. The alternative (the pair only) is simpler and drops to it if the building session finds the stray reads as a trick.
5. **Buckets per round at level 3.** Default: two (eighteen taps at most, as Fruit Sums' two ways). One if the play-through finds the second bucket a chore.
6. **Land.** Default: Counting Cove (13th game, one full page). Fallback by decision 3: Rainbow Meadow.
7. **The supported finish.** Default: a second Help press after the glow pours the rest as a ghost, one hint per bucket in all. The alternative (the glow only, retargeting drop by drop) keeps every tap hers and is a one-line change if someone watches a child wanting it.
