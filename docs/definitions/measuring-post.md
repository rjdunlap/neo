# Measuring Post 1–4 (measurement M3–M4)

Documentation packet, 2026-10-23, [PR #911](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/911), branch `codex/measurement-m3-m5`, source base `236d8a22`. [Study and decisions](../deep-dives/subjects/measurement-units.md#decisions-for-the-developer). Proposed game `measuring-post`; nothing registered or implemented. Scope is school levels 1–4, default 1; level 4 is upper school, about 10 and up, accessible without a new band or age gate. Pre-K trial and precision/“Too big to tell” remain outside this packet.

The fun is running a measuring counter for friends: take a question, choose a tool, watch it measure, and send back a useful answer. Learning: select an attribute/tool, relate unit size to count, compare only after establishing a common unit, and use an observed equivalence to complete a conversion table. No estimating, recipe scaling, elapsed time, torque or new mastery claim. Inchworm owns laying units; Seesaw owns balancing unknowns; Fill and Dump owns pouring. Here the tools animate a measurement automatically after selection. All results are authored pretend measurements, not estimates from screen pixels or real-world dimensions.

## Fixed evidence model and complete rounds

`logic.ts` in a future build should hold integer quantities, attribute, unit identity and size, evidence records, prompt/answer IDs and hint steps. A count is quantity divided by unit size; require exact integer division. A unit belongs to exactly one attribute. Never compare a worm count with a block/cup count. Visible size/colour alone never determines mass or capacity. Every numeric result includes its pictured/spoken unit. Seeded variation chooses records and left/right order, never arbitrary conflicting measurements.

| Level | One task, evidence and answer | Round and depth |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Which tool? | Question cards: “How long is this ribbon?” → knotted measuring rope; “How heavy is this parcel?” → equal-arm balance; “How much does this jug hold?” → measuring cup. Shelf always contains all three. Right selection animates respectively 4 little worm lengths, 4 little blocks balancing the parcel, or 4 little cups filling the jug. Result repeats the question's attribute and unit. | Three tasks, exactly one of each, seeded order; tap the correct tool and watch the result, then pictured Send. First task is ribbon/rope. Send appears only after the measurement is visibly complete. No numerical answer required. |
| 2 · Whose unit was smaller? | Same object measured twice. Ribbon: 3 big worms / 6 little worms; parcel: 2 big blocks / 4 little blocks; jug: 3 big cups / 6 little cups. A big unit is exactly two little units **in the shown attribute**. Each row visibly aligns, balances or pours one big against two little before the counts appear. Ask which friend used smaller units; answer is little-unit row. | Three tasks, one per attribute, shuffled; choose tool, see both measurement records and equivalence, then tap a row. Final task may ask the reverse, “Who used bigger units?” Correct row sends automatically after spoken comparison. |
| 3 · Measure them the same way | Two objects and incomparable raw counts: ribbons A=3 big worms/B=5 little (A longer); parcels A=2 big blocks/B=5 little (B heavier); jugs A=3 big cups/B=6 little (same capacity). Two same-unit tokens represent big/little. Choose either token to remeasure **both** objects; result is exact only for little in these records. Big token explains that B falls between marks for first two and offers little; this is exploration, no miss. Little gives 6:5, 4:5 or 6:6, respectively. | Three tasks, one per attribute. Common-unit evidence must be observed before the three answer pads A/B/same activate. Prompt names longer/heavier/holds more. Side swaps preserve answer identity. Retain original records beside new ones, so the larger raw number cannot silently become proof. |
| 4 · How many little ones? | Balance shows one `1 kg` weight level with four `250 g` weights, then groups those four into a labelled `1000 g` tray. Speech: “One kilogram balances one thousand grams.” These are two names for the same mass. Table first row 1 kg = 1000 g is visibly measured, not a given conversion quiz. Animate 2 kg against two such groups, filling 2 kg = 2000 g; player fills one checked row 3 kg = ? g from 1000/3000/4000. | Two mass-table tasks: first the 3 kg row above, then 4 kg = ? g from 2000/4000/5000 after replaying the same physical equivalence. Metric at school top only; grams name the smaller metric unit needed for this observed kilogram equivalence. No length/litres conversion, decimal, customary units or typed arithmetic. Each task completes on correct checked row, then Send. |

Level 1's jug is the object, and the pictured cup is the tool (not an unlabelled second jug). Level 2's big/small cups are capacity units; big/small blocks are masses. Different-looking containers may hold equal amounts. Never imply twice the drawn height means twice the mass or capacity. Level 3 with equal results accepts “same” only for the named attribute; it does not claim the objects are identical. Level 4 balance is equal-arm, weights share fixed pan centres; position never changes a result. The metric labels describe the authored mass model; the screen is not a real measuring instrument.

Completion is the final task sent through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`, once. GameScene supplies the single round sticker including supported completion; no sticker per task or internal reward/save. A round can be paused/left at any point without loss or obligation. Replay shuffles sides, friends, question order and pad order within the fixed records; no hidden deadline or daily seed.

## Feedback, support and adaptation

Selecting any tool is permitted. A wrong tool on levels 1–3 measures its own attribute and says why that does not answer the question (“The balance tells how heavy. We need how long.”); this is one committed-answer miss, gentle boing, then shelf retry. Level 2 wrong row, level 3 wrong comparison and level 4 wrong row Check each count one miss. Tool re-taps during animation do nothing; animation cannot double-count or finish. Exploratory remeasurement, choosing a coarse unit, replay, cancellation, selecting a pad before Check at 4 and Undo never count misses. At 4 change choice freely, then Check explicitly; at 2–3 tapping an active answer commits it.

After two misses at the current task, glow the useful next tool/row/pad without intercepting input. Count a hint once per newly revealed task step: tool selection, evidence/common-unit step, answer. Help first points/explains that step; repeated Help may perform it, retaining the same identity/count. A new downstream step adds one hint; repeating speech/glow, replays or undoing a helped choice never erase or recount it. At level 3 Help can select little units and then identify the correct comparison; at 4 it replays the observed group, then highlights/chooses the matching pad. The player still presses Check/Send where present. Supported completion is always available. Automatic glow and requested assistance for the same step share one hint count.

The global two-smooth/two-struggling adaptation stays within school 1–4, pins respected. Assisted completion is not independent measurement evidence; zero-miss exploratory actions are not scored predictions. No new progress fields. Undo returns the current answer selection at 4; at other levels Retry returns to current task evidence without clearing assistance or adding misses. No undo across a sent task. Use scene timing, guard asynchronous speech/animations and destroy all drag/tap/tween resources.

## Input, layout, speech and demonstration

All input uses `onTap`; no object dragging is required in this game. Shelf tools and answer rows lift/outline immediately on touch-down; speech, pictures and shape/position carry meaning without colour or reading. Use the saved `ctx.companion()` at bottom-left to replay `ctx.instruct`. Results can be replayed separately with `ctx.say`. Quiet existing music, synthesized pentatonic feedback, no new audio assets.
