# Adventure Walk, levels 1–5 (definition)

Defined 2026-10-22 as the first packet of the [movement study's](../deep-dives/subjects/movement-exercise.md#recommended-follow-ups-requested-2026-10-14) **Adventure Walk** follow-up, from the [recommendation row](../deep-dives/subjects/RECOMMENDATIONS.md#movement-and-family-play-recommendations) and the notebook's [Bear Hunt Walk seed](../IDEAS.md). Docs only: no game, ID registration, voice line, art or save field exists yet.

**Status: levels 1–3 built 2026-10-23; levels 4–5 [defined 2026-11-17](#levels-45-aw3); level 4 build claimed 2026-11-17** (branch `claude/adventure-walk-way-home`; expected files: `src/games/adventure-walk/{logic,index,art,voice,howto}.ts`, `logic.test.ts`, the `adventurewalk` browser case, docs)**; level 5 not built.** Levels 1–3 (AW2; levels 1–3 on the defaults below, with the deviations at the end of this paragraph). Deviations: home land Barnyard, not Rainbow Meadow (Rainbow Meadow's and Story Grove's scene decor tests fail at 13 games; Barnyard 10 to 11 passes); no cross-round landmark bag, a seeded shuffle of three landmarks per round, with all five appearing across seeds (tested); the friends are duck, cow and pig (critter drawings; the frog is not a critter); way phrases are passed as `{way}`/`{text}` variables to `walk.call.one/two`, `walk.went` and `walk.other` instead of ten `walk.way.*`/`walk.went.*` lines; every wrong tap counts a miss (two at one stop light the asked way, one hint per stop); card pictures are code-drawn. Original status: ready to build (AW2) on the defaults below. The slice is **screen-only**: the companion does all the walking, and no voice line, card or demonstration asks the person playing to move. The study's gate (a named movement reviewer before any physical instruction, demonstration or off-screen movement invitation) therefore does not block it, and stays on [decision 4](#open-decisions). The recommendation's retelling and way-back-home rounds are the successor definition **AW3** ([levels 4–5](#levels-45-aw3)), because a spatial retelling needs a map view, which is a new interaction.

**Fun action and purpose:** the companion sets off from home to a friend's picnic along a little island path. At each landmark on the way (a brook, tall reeds, a fallen log, a boulder, a puddle) there are two ways past, drawn as two big glowing paths: over the bridge or across the stepping stones; through the reeds or around them. She taps a way, and the companion goes that way with its own sound and the path word spoken ("Over the bridge!"). After three landmarks the friend waves from the picnic blanket. The learning goal is **words for moving along a route**: *over, under, through, across, around*, first heard as the result of her own choice, then followed when the friend calls the way, then held for two steps of a spoken route. This is the T–K rows of the study's [follow-up table](../deep-dives/subjects/movement-exercise.md#recommended-follow-ups-requested-2026-10-14) (spatial language and sequencing) and the CDC three-year tip of two- and three-step instructions that the [Pet Says research](../deep-dives/pet-says.md) opened, as a design hypothesis rather than a milestone claim.

## Identity and overlap

The notebook's [overlap row](../IDEAS.md) says build the walk only if **moving along a route** proves to be the new decision. It is: at each landmark she decides **which way the walker goes past it**, and the two ways differ in the path word. No shipped game asks that.

- **Peekaround Island** (Puzzle Peaks) owns **static positions**: in front of, behind, next to, judged from her side as the island turns. Adventure Walk owns **path words**: over, under, through, across, around, each naming a movement past an obstacle. No word is shared, and a test keeps them apart.
- **Pet Says** (Cozy Village) owns guided body actions she copies; its proposed level 9, *A walk in the woods* (not built), is a story of stops the pet performs for her to imitate. Here nobody imitates anything: she chooses or follows the walker's way, and the companion's walk is the consequence. If Pet Says level 9 is built, its stops stay body moves, not path words.
- **Story Steps** (Story Grove) owns ordering picture cards. This slice has no picture strip; AW3's retelling is defined on the map itself (tapping landmarks in the order walked), not as a card ordering.
- **Owl Walk Home** and **Mossy Path** move a token along stones or tiles to cover or reach; **Treasure Map** and **Robot Path** follow grid directions (left, up, steps). None of them names how a walker gets past a landmark.
- **Duckling Parade** steers continuously. Here each step is one tap on a large target.

The notebook's source picture book is inspiration only: no refrain, sound words, bear, chase or illustration from it, and the name "Bear Hunt" appears in no game text or voice line. The landmarks, friend and lines below are original.

## The model

- **A landmark** is an authored record in `logic.ts`: `id`, a spoken name and exactly **two ways**, each `{ word, line, sfx }`, the two words always different.

  | Landmark | Way A | Way B | Drawing |
  | --- | --- | --- | --- |
  | `brook` | **over** the plank bridge | **across** the stepping stones | a shallow blue brook with a little arched bridge on one side and three flat stones on the other |
  | `reeds` | **through** the tall reeds | **around** the reeds | a clump of tall reeds with a parted gap in the middle and a mown path curving past it |
  | `log` | **under** the fallen log | **over** the fallen log | a log resting on two rocks with a crawl-gap beneath and a little step on top |
  | `boulder` | **around** the boulder | **over** the boulder | a big mossy boulder with a worn path curling round it and footholds up its side |
  | `puddle` | **across** the puddle on its stones | **around** the puddle | a wide rain puddle with two stones in it and a dry rim |

  All five path words appear; *over*, *across* and *around* each appear at more than one landmark, so a word is learned as a way of moving, not as a picture. Each way has its own short sound from the existing `sfx` set (for example hollow plank `knock` steps, stone `splash` hops, reed `brush`, a log `squish`, a boulder `pop` hop, a `whoosh` for going round); the build picks and the test only asks that the two ways at one landmark sound different. No new audio files.
- **A route** (`planFor(level)` plus `ctx.rng`) is three different landmarks in order from home to the picnic, the left/right placement of each landmark's two ways, and the picnic friend (seeded from the duck, cow and frog drawings). Landmarks come from a shuffled bag so all five turn up across rounds before any repeats.
- **A request** (levels 2–3) is the way the friend asks for at a landmark: one of its two ways, drawn so that over a round both left and right placements are asked for, and at least two different words are asked.
- **Choose** `choose(route, stop, way)` returns `go` (the walker goes that way) or, when a request is set and `way` is not it, `other` (a miss) with a reason naming both ways.

## Screen and controls

- **Landscape (1024×768 design area):** one landmark fills the middle of the screen at a time, as a stop on the walk. Its two ways are two tappable **way zones** of at least 280×260 each, side by side, each outlined with a dotted path and a soft glow, with a gap of at least 40 units between them. The **companion** (`ctx.companion()`, at about 0.5 scale) stands at the near edge below the zones. A thin, non-interactive **trail line** across the top shows home, the three stops and the picnic, the current stop lit (`eventMode = 'none'`). The home button stays top-left; the companion stands clear of the guide's bottom-left corner. At levels 2–3 the **friend's picture** (a 140×140 badge at the top right, under the trail line) speaks the request; tapping it replays the request, free.
- **Portrait:** the zones stack one above the other (each at least 300 wide and 240 tall), the companion below them, the friend's badge top-right. `stopLayout(view, level)` returns every rectangle; a test asserts sizes (every target at least 100 units each way after scaling), the gap, no overlap with the home button, the friend's badge or the guide's corner, and everything inside `view` at the four views the lands test uses.
- **A way:** touch-down squishes its zone and brightens its dotted path at once. A tap sends the companion along that path (about 1.2 s, tweened through two or three points of the drawing: up and across the bridge, ducking under the log), the way's sound on each step, and the word line ("Over the bridge!"). The landscape then slides on to the next stop (about 0.6 s), and taps during the walk or slide are ignored. The walk is the reward; nothing is timed.
- **The companion:** a tap gives a happy bounce and repeats the current instruction (the large-pet rule).
- **The picnic:** after the third stop the scene slides to the friend's blanket, the companion sits down beside the friend, a short pentatonic rise plays, and the friend says the arrival line; then `ctx.finish`.
- **Music:** the land's style keeps playing underneath (this is not a game where she makes the music), quieter during speech in the usual way.

### First rounds

- **Level 1 (route: brook, reeds, log; friend: duck).** The pet: "Let's walk to Duck's picnic! Which way shall we go?" At the brook she taps the stepping stones: the companion hops stone to stone, splash, splash, "Across the stones!" The path slides on to the reeds; she taps the parted gap: a brushing walk through, "Through the reeds!" At the log she taps the top step: a hop over, "Over the log!" The blanket: "You made it! Duck saved you a sandwich." No choice was wrong, and nothing was counted.
- **Level 2 (route: reeds, puddle, boulder; friend: frog).** Frog's badge: "Come around the reeds!" She taps around: the companion curls round the clump. Frog: "Come across the puddle!" She taps around the puddle: boing, the companion takes one step that way, stops and turns back, and Frog says "That's around the puddle. Come across, on the stones!" (one miss). She taps the stones: across. Frog: "Come over the boulder!" She taps over: the companion climbs the footholds. Frog's blanket.
- **Level 3 (route: brook, log, puddle; friend: cow).** Cow's badge, before the first stop: "Come over the bridge, then under the log!" At the brook she taps the bridge (over). At the log she pauses and taps Cow's badge: "Over the bridge, then under the log!" (free). She taps under: the companion crawls through the gap. Cow: "Now come around the puddle!" She taps around. Cow's blanket.

## Accounting, help and finishing

- **Never a miss:** any choice at level 1; a tap on the companion, the friend's badge (replay), the trail line or the background; taps during a walk or slide. Choosing at level 1 is free exploration, so a level-1 round always counts 0 misses and 0 hints and moves her along the ladder by the ordinary rule; the grown-ups' card says a smooth level-1 round shows she reached the picnic, not that she knows the words.
- **A miss** (levels 2–3): tapping the way the friend did not ask for. The walker turns back from the edge of that way (no fall, no splash on her), and the reason names both ways ("That's around the puddle. Come across, on the stones!"). A second tap on the same wrong way before choosing again repeats the reason and counts nothing more.
- **After two misses at one stop**, the requested way glows and `hints++` (at most once per stop). Show a hint in the pause sheet (`Scene.pauseActions`) does the same at the current stop, still at most once per stop; asked again there, it repeats the request.
- **Replays** of the request through the friend's badge are a listening support, free at every level and never a hint.
- **Adaptation:** the ordinary rule (`src/progress/difficulty.ts`). At levels 2–3 a zero-miss round means she followed the spoken path words, with replay allowed; it is not evidence she would use the words herself.
- **Start again** (pause sheet) returns to the first stop of the same seeded route, keeping misses and hints.
- **Finish:** `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` at the picnic, with or without help: one ordinary sticker from the shell. Nothing is kept, filed in the journal or saved by the game ([decision 6](#open-decisions)).

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Table:** five landmarks; each has exactly two ways with different words; every word is one of over, under, through, across, around and all five occur; no path word is one of Peekaround Island's position words (in front of, behind, next to), checked against that game's `WHERE_WORDS`; every way has a word line and a sound, and the two sounds at a landmark differ.
- **Routes, over 500 seeds per level:** three different landmarks; every landmark appears across rounds and the bag gives all five before any repeats; both left and right placements occur for each landmark; the same seed gives the same route.
- **Requests (levels 2–3):** every request is one of its landmark's two ways; across each round at least two different words are asked and both screen sides are asked at least once; at level 3 the first call names the first two stops' requests in route order and the second call names the third.
- **Choices:** at level 1 `choose` returns `go` for every way at every stop; at levels 2–3 it returns `go` for exactly the requested way and `other`, with a reason naming both words, for the other.
- **Hints:** the glowed way is the requested way; a scripted level-2 round (wrong, wrong again on the same way, a different wrong at the next stop, Show a hint twice at that stop) counts the misses once per distinct wrong tap and at most one hint per stop.
- **Layout:** `stopLayout` at the four views for each level: every target at least 100 units each way, way zones at least the sizes above and 40 apart, nothing overlapping, the home button, the friend's badge and the guide's corner clear, everything inside `view`.
- **Registry, lands and how-to tests** pass with the new game, including its home land still fitting on one screen at every band.

## Levels 4–5 (AW3)

Defined 2026-11-17, docs only: nothing below is built. Both levels add one new view, **the map**: the whole path from home to the picnic on one screen, with the round's three landmarks as tiles along it. Level 4 uses the map to lead the walk home; level 5 draws three trails on it and asks which one the friend is describing. They are appended, so no saved level or pin moves, and both stay **screen-only**: the companion walks, and nothing invites the person to move ([decision 4](#open-decisions) is unchanged).

| Level | Name | Bands (default) | What she decides | Finish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | **The way home** | pre-K, school | After a free walk out, which landmark the walk home comes to next, three times, by following the drawn path back from the picnic | Reach home |
| 5 | **Which trail?** | school | Which of three drawn trails matches the friend's three-step call, on the way there and again (two trails left, the ways named in the order met from the picnic) on the way home | Reach home |

Bands become toddler 1, preschool 1–2, pre-K 2–4, school 3–5 ([decision 7](#open-decisions)). No claim is made about what a four-year-old can do with a map: the band placement is a design hypothesis for the phone playtest and later play to correct, like the rest of the ladder.

**What changed from the sketch.** The earlier note here proposed a fork at level 5: two routes past different landmarks. That is replaced, because a fork between different landmarks is settled by hearing one landmark's name (the brook is on only one branch), so the path words would stop mattering. Three trails past the **same** three landmarks differ only in the way taken at each one, which keeps *over, under, through, across, around* as the thing she listens for and reads on the map. Level 4's "tap the way taken, in reverse order" became "tap the landmark the path comes to next": the order is the decision, and the companion goes back past it the way it came by itself, so each tap is one decision. The fork stays as an alternative in [decision 9](#open-decisions).

### The map

- **A map** is drawn from the same seeded route: a home marker (the companion's little door), the three landmark **tiles** in route order, the picnic blanket with the friend on it, and one dotted path joining them. Each tile is the landmark's drawing with both of its ways visible, at least 170×170 in both orientations and at least 30 apart; a tile is one tap target at level 4 and is not a target at level 5 (`eventMode = 'none'` there).
- **Shapes.** `MAP_SHAPES` holds three authored paths, each five points (home, three tile centres, picnic) in a unit square that `mapLayout(view, level, shape)` scales into the map rectangle; the portrait form swaps the axes. `sweep` runs from one side to the other in a gentle wave. `zigzag` climbs in three turns. `hook` goes out along one edge and comes back along the other, so the picnic ends up close to home and **the tile nearest the picnic on screen is the first landmark of the walk, not the next one on the way home**: only following the path finds the order. Level 4 draws its shape from all three with the round's seed ([decision 8](#open-decisions)); level 5 always uses `sweep`, because its trails are the thing to read.
- **The walker on the map** is the companion at about 0.35 scale with a tap area of at least 120×120; a tap gives a bounce and repeats the current instruction, as at every level. The trail line across the top of the stop screen is hidden while the map is shown (the map is the trail). The home button, the friend's badge (level 5) and the guide's bottom-left corner stay clear of every tile, flag and marker.
- **Footprints.** A way the companion has walked keeps a faint line of footprints on its tile. At level 4 these show the way she chose on the way out; at level 5 they show the trail she took there.

### Level 4: The way home

- **The walk out** is level 1 exactly: three stops on the ordinary stop screen, either way right at each, no friend's badge, nothing counted. `WalkState` gains `taken`, the way index chosen at each stop (recorded at every level; only levels 4–5 read it).
- **At the picnic** the companion sits beside the friend, the arrival line plays, and the view zooms out to the map (about 0.8 s) with the companion on the blanket and footprints on the three ways taken. The companion gives the map instruction: "Time to go home! Which place do we come to first?"
- **A leg.** She taps a tile. `stepHome(state, stop)` returns `back` when it is the next landmark along the path from where the companion stands (the stops in the order 2, 1, 0): the companion walks the dotted path to the tile and past it by the way it came (about 1.2 s, the way's own sound), and says "Back over the log!" After the third tile it walks on to the door by itself, a short pentatonic rise plays, "Home again! We came back the way we went.", then `ctx.finish`. The third leg has only one tile left; she still taps it, and it cannot be wrong.
- **A wrong tile** (one not yet passed that is not next) returns `later`: a boing, the companion takes one step along the path the right way and stops, and says "Not yet! Follow the path from the picnic." The line does not name the answer. A miss is counted for every such tap, as levels 2–3 are built.
- **Never a miss at level 4:** anything on the walk out; a tap on a tile already passed (a wiggle), the companion, the home marker, the blanket or the background; taps during a walk or the zoom.
- **Help:** after two misses on one leg the next tile glows, the companion says "We get to the log first." and `hints++`, at most once per leg. Show a hint (pause sheet) does the same on the map, at most once per leg, and repeats the instruction if asked again; on the walk out it does nothing, as at level 1.
- **Start again** returns to the first stop of the walk out with `taken` cleared, keeping misses and hints.
- **First round (route: brook, reeds, log; friend: pig; shape: `hook`).** She goes across the stones, around the reeds and under the log. At the picnic the map opens: the path leaves home along the bottom past the brook, turns at the reeds on the right and comes back along the top past the log to the blanket, which sits just above the brook. She taps the brook, the nearest tile: boing, "Not yet! Follow the path from the picnic." (one miss). She taps the log: "Back under the log!" Then the reeds: "Back around the reeds!" Then the brook: "Back across the stones!" The companion walks to its door. One miss, no hints.

### Level 5: Which trail?

The whole round is on the map; there are no stop screens.

- **Trails.** `makeTrails(route, rng)` returns three trails, each one way index per stop. They are pairwise different and **at every landmark both of its ways are used** (two trails share one way, the third takes the other), so no single landmark tells all three apart and no landmark is the same on all three. There are exactly 192 ordered sets with that property; the generator draws one of them. Each trail is a ribbon through its way on every tile, in its own palette colour **and** its own mark (dots, dashes, small triangles), so colour is never the only difference; where two trails share a way their ribbons run side by side.
- **Flags.** Each trail has a flag carrying its colour and mark: three tap targets of at least 120×120, at least 40 apart, in a row clear of the guide's corner. The flags are the only way to choose a trail.
- **The call there.** The friend's badge (140×140, top right) speaks one trail in full, in route order: "Come over the bridge, through the reeds, then under the log!" (`walk.call.three`). As each way is named its landmark's tile pulses (the tile, not the way). Tapping the badge replays the call with the same pulses, free, at any time. The called trail is drawn from the three by the seed, so every flag position is asked over many rounds.
- **Look, then go.** A first tap on a flag **looks**: that trail brightens, the other two dim, and a sparkle runs along it stop by stop (about 1.5 s, silent, cut short by any other tap). Looking is free and unlimited. A second tap on the flag that is being looked at **goes**. The look is silent on purpose: reading the drawn ways is the decision, and speaking a trail's words would turn the level into matching two recordings ([decision 10](#open-decisions)).
- **Going.** `goTrail(state, trail)` returns `go` for the called trail: the companion walks all three landmarks on the map, each way's sound and word line as at the other levels ("Over the bridge!"), and sits down at the picnic. For another trail it returns `other` with the **first stop, in the order walked, where that trail and the call differ**: the companion walks to that tile, starts the wrong way, turns back with a boing and returns to the start (about 0.8 s), and the friend says the built line "That's across the stones. Come over the bridge!" (`walk.other`). One miss. That trail then carries a small turned-back mark at that tile and cannot be walked again in this pick; its flag still looks, and a second tap repeats the reason, free.
- **The call home.** At the picnic the trail she walked there keeps its footprints and its flag dims; tapping that flag looks at it and the friend says "We came that way." (free; it is not a candidate). The friend calls one of the other two trails, seeded, **in the order met from the picnic**: "Let's go home a different way: under the log, through the reeds, then across the stones!" (`walk.home-call`). Look and go work the same way; a wrong trail turns back at the first differing stop counted from the picnic. The right one walks home: "Home again, by a different way!", a pentatonic rise, then `ctx.finish`.
- **Never a miss at level 5:** looking at any trail, any number of times; replaying a call; tapping a tile, the companion or the background; a second tap on a trail already turned back; tapping the dimmed flag on the way home.
- **Help:** when her wrong tries leave exactly one trail unmarked in a pick (after two misses there, after one on the way home) that trail's flag glows with "Look, this way!" (`walk.hint-glow`) and `hints++`, at most once per pick. Show a hint glows the called trail's flag, the same single hint for that pick; asked again, it replays the call. A round therefore counts at most three misses and two hints, so only a round that needed both glows is a struggling round under the ordinary rule; the grown-ups' card says so.
- **Start again** returns to home with every mark and footprint cleared and the same trails and calls, keeping misses and hints.
- **First round (route: brook, reeds, log; friend: duck).** Red dots: over the bridge, through the reeds, under the log. Blue dashes: across the stones, through the reeds, under the log. Yellow triangles: over the bridge, around the reeds, over the log. Duck: "Come over the bridge, through the reeds, then under the log!" She taps the yellow flag (the sparkle runs over the bridge, around the reeds, over the log), then yellow again: the companion crosses the bridge, starts around the reeds and turns back. "That's around the reeds. Come through the reeds!" (one miss). She replays the call, looks at red, and goes: bridge, reeds, log, picnic. Duck: "Let's go home a different way: under the log, through the reeds, then across the stones!" She looks at blue and goes. Home. One miss, no hints.

### Tests, demo, card, voice and registration for 4–5

## Open decisions

Each has a default the build uses; none blocks a build.

1. **Identity.** Default: a new game, because choosing and following a way past a landmark is a decision no shipped game asks. Alternative: a Peekaround Island mode, if play shows the walk adds nothing to the words.
2. **Bands.** Default toddler 1, preschool 1–2, pre-K 2–3, school 3 (AW3 extends school). Alternatives: no school range until AW3; or lap 1 as a watch-and-tap entry, which the study does not recommend.
3. **Subject and home land.** Default region `story-grove` (language) and home land Rainbow Meadow (11 games today, 12 with this one, beside Owl Walk Home; 13 if [Sprout Shuffle](sprout-shuffle.md) also lands there on its default, which Puzzle Peaks already lays out). Alternatives: Story Grove (12 today, 13), or Puzzle Peaks beside Peekaround (13 today, 14, more than `lands.test.ts` has covered).
4. **Moving along (reviewer-gated).** Default: omitted. No voice line, card or demonstration invites the person to walk, crawl, hop or act the route, and the off-screen tip uses a toy. An "act it out with me" option, on screen or off, needs the study's named movement and accessibility reviewers, with seated and non-walking alternatives, before it is written.
5. **Level 1 accounting.** Default: every level-1 choice is free and a round always counts 0/0. Alternative: none sensible; a free choice is never a miss.
6. **Journal.** Default: nothing filed. Alternative: a discovery page of the five path words, each with its landmark, after she has gone each way once.
7. **Bands for 4–5.** Default: level 4 pre-K and school, level 5 school only (pre-K 2–4, school 3–5). Alternative: level 4 school only, if the map proves too much at four; that changes `LEVELS` alone, since no level number moves.
8. **The hook map at level 4.** Default: `hook` is one of the three seeded shapes, so the nearest tile is sometimes not the next one and the drawn path has to be followed. Alternative: `sweep` and `zigzag` only, which makes the order readable from position.
9. **Level 5's shape.** Default: three trails past the same three landmarks, differing only in the ways. Alternative: the earlier sketch's fork between two routes past different landmarks, rejected because one landmark's name settles it.
10. **A silent look.** Default: looking along a trail plays no words, so the drawn ways are what she reads. Alternative: the companion says the looked trail's three ways, which is a stronger support and turns the choice into comparing two spoken routes; a later supported mode could offer it after a miss.
