# Species Comeback, levels 1–4 (definition)

Defined 2026-10-19 as slice **Z7** of the [zoo-animals study](../deep-dives/subjects/zoo-animals.md#build-slices-in-order), from its [audience table](../deep-dives/subjects/zoo-animals.md#an-answer-for-every-audience) (the U, M and G rungs), its [candidates row](../deep-dives/subjects/zoo-animals.md#candidates), its [overlap boundary](../deep-dives/subjects/zoo-animals.md#overlap-boundaries) with Meadow Plans and the [IDEAS row](../IDEAS.md#candidates). Docs only: no game, ID registration, voice line, art or save field exists yet. **Ready to build** as one new-game slice (levels 1–3) and one new-levels slice (level 4): every open call below has a default the build uses. The one review the study asks for before anything ships is [decision 1](#open-decisions): the needs card and every spoken line about recovery are checked by a named person, because this game says things about real animals' care even though its island turtles are invented.

**Fun action and purpose:** a family tree of the island's pond turtles hangs on the recovery centre's wall: the parents along the top, their grown-up young along the bottom, each with a name tag. She chooses who pairs with whom by tapping two turtles into a nest, with one rule: **no pair who are close family**. When the nests are full she presses Spring, and the tree grows a new row of hatchlings under every nest. At level 3 the hatchlings are ready to go, and she picks the release site that has everything they need. At level 4 the keeper offers two plans that both follow the rule, with a note about this year, and she says which plan fits and why. The learning goal is **recovery as a sequence** (pair, raise, release), **variation kept by pairing distant relatives** (NGSS 3-LS3-1's "look like their parents, not exactly", modelled as a family tree and never as genes), **matching a place to needs** (3-LS4-3: a site can lack what an animal needs, said without any animal coming to harm) and, at level 4, **evaluating two acceptable plans under a stated constraint** (MS-LS2-5). Nothing is lost, nobody dies, and no plan is a failure: a plan the rule allows is a good plan, and the keeper's note only says which good plan fits this year.

## Overlap

- **Meadow Plans** (ecosystems F7, not built) owns patches, sockets and food paths. Species Comeback never lays out a habitat: a site is a card with features, and the choice is whether it has what the hatchlings need. Corridors stay out of both.
- **Patchwork Conservatory** (genetics G7, not built) plans crosses to meet a two-trait brief with explicitly independent loci. Species Comeback has no traits and no loci: relatedness is read off a tree. The two never share a helper.
- **Hidden Sprouts** and **Sprout Shuffle** (genetics, defined) model what offspring can be. Here the hatchlings are drawn, never predicted; a hatchling looks like a small turtle in its parents' two shell tints and nothing is inferred from that.
- **Habitat Helpers** owns needs for young bands; the needs card at level 3 is the same idea for an older player and reuses no code. **Animal Snack** owns feeding: no feeding happens here.
- **Island Bridges**, **Picture Logic** and **Sudoku Garden** are Lantern Lake's deduction puzzles with one answer. This is Lantern Lake's first puzzle with several answers by design, so its Check is "does the rule hold", never "is this the answer".
- No shipped game pairs individuals by a family tree. The two-sites rung the study names for grown-ups (G, play, deeper) is a later level 5 of the same ID, never a new registration.

## The model

- **A turtle** is `{ id, name, sex, parents }`: a seeded name from a sixteen-name table in `logic.ts` (Pebble, Moss, Fern, Reed, Willow, Brook, Sorrel, Thistle, Bramble, Dune, Lichen, Marsh, Pine, Ash, Sage, Flint; never the island's named friends), a `sex` of `'f'` or `'m'` shown as a small ♀ or ♂ on the name tag ([decision 4](#open-decisions)), and `parents` (two ids, or none for a founder). The island's turtles are **invented**: no number of them is ever said, and "very few were left" is one spoken line at the intro with the Z8 card as its source ([study decision 2](../deep-dives/subjects/zoo-animals.md#decisions-for-the-developer)).
- **A tree** is a list of turtles in generations: **founders** in pairs along the top, their **young** beneath, and from level 2 a **third generation** under one or two young pairs that already nested. Every founder pair has two or three young; the sexes are drawn so that at least two pairs are possible.
- **Candidates** are the turtles with no nest yet: the unpaired young and the third generation. Founders and turtles who already nested are drawn faded and are not touch targets.
- **Kin** is the one rule: `ancestors(t, 2)` is a turtle's parents and grandparents inside the tree, and `kin(a, b)` is true when the two sets overlap. At level 1 the tree has one generation up, so kin means siblings; from level 2 it also catches cousins and an aunt or uncle with a niece or nephew. The rule is spoken as "no brothers and sisters together" (1) and "and no cousins either" (2–4), and the explanation names the shared relative and what she is to each ("Fern is Moss's mother and Brook's mother"; "Fern is Brook's mother and Lichen's grandmother").
- **A pair** is one `'f'` and one `'m'` who are not kin. **A plan** is a set of pairs with no turtle in two of them. `allowed(tree, a, b)` is the pair rule; `complete(tree, plan)` is true when `plan.length` equals the round's **target**.
- **The solver**, `plans(tree, depth)`, enumerates every maximal matching over the candidates under `kin` at the given depth (1 for siblings only, 2 for siblings and cousins) and returns them with their sizes; `best(tree)` is the largest size, which is the round's target. Candidate counts stay at or below ten, so enumeration is exact and fast (a test caps it).
- **Help**, `extend(tree, plan)`: a valid completion of the plan she has, or null when her pairs so far block every completion; `blocking(tree, plan)`: the one nest whose undo makes the plan extendable again, found by trying each. The one-step hint glows the next pair of `extend` or, when it is null, the nest `blocking` names.
- **Spring**, `hatch(tree, plan, rng)`: each nest gets two hatchlings with seeded names and sexes, added to the tree as a new generation. At level 3 they are what the site must suit.
- **A site** (level 3) is `{ id, name, features }` from an authored table of six: Reedy Pond, Sunny Bank, Mill Stream, Quarry Pool, Old Orchard Ditch and Harbour Marsh, each with a subset of the five **features** `water` (still water with reeds), `logs` (sunning logs), `sand` (soft sand to dig a nest), `plants` (water plants and snails to eat), `quiet` (no road or dogs beside it). **The needs card** lists the hatchlings' four needs: `water`, `logs`, `sand`, `plants`; `quiet` is the feature that decides nothing (a reviewer case, [decision 1](#open-decisions)). `suits(site)` is true when the site has every need. A round shows three sites of which two suit, and the one that does not lacks exactly one need; the sites' order and which two suit are seeded.
- **A keeper's plan** (level 4) is a plan the solver produced: **Plan A** is a largest matching at depth 1 (siblings forbidden, cousins allowed) that contains at least one cousin pair; **Plan B** is a largest matching at depth 2 (no cousins either), strictly smaller. Both follow the rule the level states ("no brothers and sisters together"). The **note** is one of two seeded lines: "Room for two nests this spring. Keep the pairs as far apart in the family as you can" (B fits) or "A big spring: we need as many hatchlings as we can raise" (A fits). Each plan carries a **badge** the pet reads aloud: "three pairs, two of them cousins" or "two pairs, no cousins". The **reason cards** are three fixed lines: "It has more pairs", "Its pairs are less closely related", "It uses every turtle"; the first fits A, the second fits B, the third fits neither plan in any round (a test proves no plan ever uses every candidate).
- **Generation** (`planFor(level)` plus `ctx.rng`): draw the founders, young and third generation by the level's counts, assign sexes and names, run the solver, and accept the tree only when the level's conditions hold (below); otherwise redraw, bounded (a test proves it terminates in the seeds it runs). The same seed gives the same tree, sites, note and plans.

## Screen and controls

- **Landscape (1024×768 design area, `grownup` band: targets at least 64 units):** the centre's wall. The **tree** fills the upper two thirds: founder pairs along the top row as linked cards, their young in the row beneath, a third generation in a third row at levels 2–4, with soft lines from each pair down to its young. A **turtle card** is 96×96 at up to eight per row and 80×80 at nine or ten (never below 64), showing a small turtle (Mossy Path's `turtle()` drawing at card size, its shell in one of six tints) over a name tag with the name and its ♀/♂ mark. The **nest row** sits along the bottom right: one round nest per target pair (120 units wide), with **Spring**, a 150×80 button at the row's right end, dimmed until the nests are full. The pet is the corner guide, bottom left, and the nest row keeps 150 units clear of it.
- **Pairing:** tap one candidate (it lifts and its tag brightens), then tap another: the two slide into the next empty nest. A second tap on the lifted turtle puts it down, free. A tap on a nest returns both turtles to the tree, free (**undo**). A pair the rule forbids is a miss: the nest bounces, the shared relative's card and the lines down to the two turtles light for two seconds, the pet names the relative and what she or he is to each, and both turtles slide back.
- **Spring:** when every nest is full, the button lights; tapping it shakes each nest gently and two hatchlings appear in a new row under the nests, each sliding into the tree beneath its parents with a soft two-note rise. At levels 1–2 the round finishes here. A tap on the dimmed button repeats the instruction with the count ("Two more pairs"), free.
- **Level 3's site step:** the tree shrinks to the upper third, the **needs card** (four feature icons, 300×120) hangs centre, and three **site cards** (280×220, a painted scene and its feature icons along the bottom) stand across the middle. Tapping a site that suits: the hatchlings walk to it in a line and the round finishes. Tapping one that does not: the missing need's icon shakes on the needs card and on the site, the pet says what is missing ("No soft sand to dig a nest there"), a miss.
- **Level 4's plan step:** before any nest is free to use, the keeper's **note** (a 420×110 sign) hangs over the nest row and two **plan cards** (each 380×180, its pairs drawn as little nests with the badge beneath) sit side by side over the lower tree. Tapping a plan: the one that fits the note settles into the nest row and the other slides away; the one that does not is a miss, with the trade spoken ("Plan A has more pairs, but two of them are cousins. The note asks for pairs far apart"). Then three **reason cards** (each 300×90) replace the plan cards; tapping the fitting one lights it and Spring opens; a wrong one is a miss with the pet's one-line reason. The round then runs as level 3's site step.
- **Portrait:** the tree keeps its rows, cards at 80, with a founder row of four pairs spread over two lines; the nest row moves under the tree, Spring under it, the pet keeps its corner. At level 3 the three sites stack two over one; at level 4 the plan cards stack. A board that cannot fit scrolls vertically (the first time in Lantern Lake a board scrolls; `treeLayout(view, tree, nests)` returns every rectangle and the scroll height, and a test asserts sizes, clearances and that nothing scrolls in landscape at any level).
- **Finish:** the hatchlings settle (levels 1–2 in the tree, 3–4 at the site), the pet says the comeback line ("More turtles on the island this year"), then `ctx.finish`.
- **Sound and music:** a wooden tap when a card lifts, a soft slide into a nest, the gentle boing on a miss, the two-note rise per hatchling; the land's music keeps playing (not `silenced`).

## Levels

| Level | Name | Bands (default) | Tree | Rule and target | What she decides | Finish |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Not brothers and sisters** | grownup | 2 founder pairs, 5 or 6 young (3 ♀, 2–3 ♂) | depth 1; target 2 | Which two turtles may nest together | Spring: hatchlings in the tree |
| 2 | **Not cousins either** | grownup | 3 founder pairs, 7 young, one young pair already nested with 2 or 3 third-generation turtles; 7–8 candidates | depth 2; target 3 | The same, reading grandparents too | Spring |
| 3 | **Where will they live?** | grownup | as level 2 | depth 2; target 3, then a site | A site with every need | The hatchlings walk to the site |
| 4 | **Two plans** | grownup | 4 founder pairs, 9 young, two young pairs nested with 2 third-generation turtles each; 9–10 candidates | depth 1 for the keeper's plans; A has 3–4 pairs, B has 2–3 | Which plan fits this year's note, and why; then the site | The hatchlings walk to the site |

**Conditions every round meets** (the solver proves them at generation): at least **two distinct plans** reach the target (several valid plans, by design); at least one **tempting pair** exists (an opposite-sex candidate pair the rule forbids), so the rule is tested at least once in every tree; from level 2, at least one tempting pair is cousins or aunt-and-nephew rather than siblings, so depth 2 is needed; at level 4, Plan A is strictly larger than Plan B and contains at least one cousin pair, and no plan uses every candidate. The target is the largest size the solver finds, so a plan with fewer pairs is never asked for as a finished plan; it is the blocked state `extend` reports.

Level 5 (two release sites with different notes, the study's deeper G rung) and larger trees are later definitions appended above 4. A `school` window for an upper-school child is [decision 3](#open-decisions); nothing is renumbered when it arrives.

## Accounting, help and finishing

- **Never a miss:** lifting and putting down a turtle, undoing a nest, tapping the dimmed Spring (it repeats the count), tapping a founder or a nested turtle (its tag brightens and the pet says its name, free), tapping the needs card or a plan's badge (read aloud, free), tapping the pet, or a tap during a slide, Spring or the walk (ignored).
- **A miss:** completing a pair the rule forbids (with the relative named), tapping a site that lacks a need (with the need named), tapping the plan that does not fit the note (with the trade spoken), or tapping a reason card that does not fit the chosen plan.
- **The glow after two misses** (`hints++` once per step): in the pairing step, the two cards of the next pair of `extend(tree, plan)` glow together, or the nest `blocking` names glows and the pet says "Try undoing this nest"; at the site step, the sites that suit glow; at the plan step, the plan that fits glows; at the reason step, the fitting card glows. "Per step" means pairing, site, plan and reason count their own hint at most once each.
- **Show a hint** in the pause sheet (`Scene.pauseActions`): the same glow for the current step, counted at most once per step; asked again it only repeats the instruction. **Start again** rebuilds the same seeded round and keeps misses and hints.
- **Blocked plans:** a plan that cannot be completed is never a miss by itself; the dimmed Spring keeps repeating the count, and the two-miss glow or a requested hint points at the nest to undo. A test proves every blocked plan has a blocking nest whose undo makes `extend` succeed.
- **Finish:** `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` when the hatchlings settle, with or without help: one ordinary sticker from the shell. No creation, discovery or journal entry is filed in this build ([decision 7](#open-decisions)).

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Kin:** `ancestors(t, 2)` of a founder is empty; of a young turtle its two parents; of a third-generation turtle its parents and four grandparents. `kin` is symmetric, true for siblings, half-siblings, cousins and an aunt with her nephew, false for two founders and for two turtles from different founder families with no nested link.
- **Pairs and plans:** `allowed` rejects same-sex pairs, kin and a turtle already in a nest; `complete` is true only at the target size; `plans` returns only valid matchings, every maximal matching, and no duplicates.
- **Rounds:** for every level over 500 seeds the tree matches the level's counts and generations, sexes and names are unique, at least two distinct plans reach the target, at least one tempting pair exists (and from level 2 one that needs depth 2), the target equals `best`, the same seed gives the same round, and generation terminates within the redraw bound.
- **Help:** for every reachable partial plan built from any valid plan's prefix, `extend` returns a completion; for every blocked plan the test can construct (a tempting-but-allowed pair at depth 1 taken first at level 2, where it blocks), `blocking` names a nest whose undo makes `extend` succeed.
- **Hatching:** `hatch` adds exactly two turtles per nest, each with both parents set, names unique within the tree, and `ancestors(h, 2)` covering all four grandparents.
- **Sites (level 3):** exactly two of the three sites suit, the third lacks exactly one need, every need is a feature some suiting site has, `quiet` never decides, and the six authored sites between them lack every need at least once (so each missing-need line can be heard).
- **Plans (level 4):** Plan A is a maximal depth-1 matching with at least one cousin pair, Plan B a maximal depth-2 matching, A larger than B, both free of siblings; each note fits exactly one plan; the reason table maps the first card to A, the second to B, the third to neither; no plan in any seed uses every candidate.
- **Accounting:** a scripted level 1 round with one forbidden pair, a valid pair, two more forbidden pairs produces misses 3, hints 1 (the glow after two on the pairing step); a requested hint on the same step leaves hints at 1; a scripted level 3 round with one unsuitable site produces misses 1, hints 0.
- **Layout:** `treeLayout` at the four views for every level's largest tree: every card at least 64 units, the nest row clear of the pet's corner, no scrolling in landscape, the sites and plan cards inside `view`.
- **Registry, lands and how-to tests** pass with the new game, including Lantern Lake still fitting on one screen at every band with thirteen games (Puzzle Peaks holds thirteen today, so the layout is known to fit).

## Check tier

The first build (levels 1–3) is the new-game tier: typecheck and unit tests (the rule properties above plus registry, lands and how-to tests); one scripted play through a new `browser-check.mjs` case (its README row is part of the build), filtered to level 1 and level 3, with one forbidden pair, one undo, one unsuitable site and one requested hint; one portrait screenshot at level 3 with the three sites showing; `fingerdemo` at levels 1 and 3; `npm run build`, because the registry, lands, voice and how-to files change. Level 4 is the new-levels tier: typecheck, the rule tests, one browser play of level 4 and `fingerdemo` at it.

Device checks stay open: whether a thirteen-year-old reads the lines from a pair down to its young as "these are their children" without the word being said; whether the lit relative reads as the explanation of a forbidden pair; whether ♀/♂ on an 80-unit tag is legible on an iPad; whether the tree at level 4 is readable in portrait without scrolling on an iPad mini; and whether the intro's "very few were left" line lands as a recovery story rather than a sad one, which only a person can judge.

## Open decisions

Each has a default the build uses. Decision 1 is the one review the study asks for before anything ships; the rest block nothing.

**Decided 2026-10-23 (on the developer's delegation):** decisions 2–8 keep their defaults. Decision 1 changes: instead of the developer's check, the building session checks the four needs, the six sites and the `quiet` omission against reputable sources on pond-turtle care and head-start programmes (zoos, universities, government wildlife agencies), lists them here and records the check in its VERIFICATION entry; the first build merges after that check, with no outside reviewer, and the printed recovery card (Z8) uses the same check.

1. **Who reviews the needs card and the recovery lines.** Default: the developer checks the four needs and the six sites against two general references on pond-turtle care and head-start programmes before the first build merges, and confirms `quiet` as a feature the needs card leaves out (the honest line is that a road matters, but the puzzle's needs are what a hatchling cannot live without); a named zoo educator or biology teacher ([study decision 5](../deep-dives/subjects/zoo-animals.md#decisions-for-the-developer)) replaces that review when one exists, and the printed recovery card (Z8) waits for that person. The rule tests check consistency with the tables, not biological truth.
2. **The species.** Default: the island's pond turtles, invented, drawn with Mossy Path's `turtle()` at card size in six shell tints and new hatchling, nest and site art in Pixi `Graphics` (study [decision 6](../deep-dives/subjects/zoo-animals.md#decisions-for-the-developer)). Alternative: the black-footed ferret with a new drawing (M), which would make the intro's recovery line a true story and move its source onto the round; the model is the same with "kits" for "hatchlings" and burrows for sand.
3. **Home and bands.** Default: Lantern Lake, `grownup` 1–4, in `GAMES`; a school child reaches it through the signpost and plays at `grownup`, as with every Lantern Lake game. Alternatives: a `school` window (say 1–2) so it appears in a land a school child sees directly, which needs the shell check Hidden Sprouts' decision 3 also waits on (no game carries both `school` and `grownup` today, and Lantern Lake's test expects grown-up games); or a school top-level entry in Barnyard with the same window.
