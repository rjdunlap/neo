# Swing Pals, levels 1–6 (definition)

Defined 2026-10-17 for [chunk 27](../ROADMAP.md#chunks) (physics games), from the [PHYSICS-GAMES candidate](../PHYSICS-GAMES.md#new-candidates) and its [IDEAS row](../IDEAS.md#candidates). Docs only: no game, ID registration, voice line or save field exists yet. **Ready to build:** every open call below has a default the build can use, and none needs a content reviewer. They are listed in [Open decisions](#open-decisions) and in [WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER](../WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER.md).

**Fun action and purpose:** push a friend on a tree swing and watch them swing higher and higher until their feet ring the bell in the leaves, with a giggle on every swoop. Then hang a second swing beside it on a short rope or a long one, and later choose the rope that makes her friend swing along with the pet's swing. The learning goal is **a pattern of motion she can see and use**: a swing comes back again and again at its own steady beat, pushes add up, a short rope swings back and forth faster than a long one, and who rides it or how high it goes does not change its beat. There is no clock, no move count and nothing to lose; height never falls away from a goal she has reached.

**The model is a toy, stated as one.** It is the small-swing pendulum with the rope length chosen so the beats are easy to see: rope lengths 1 : 2.25 : 4 give a full back-and-forth of 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 seconds (the beat scales with the square root of the length, as for a real pendulum). The friend's weight and the swing's height do not change the beat. Pushes are a game rule: they always help, and only a push that lands as the friend comes back to the pet's paws counts.

## Overlap

- **Bouncy Launch** is the nearest physics game (a pull sets a flight); its Kass Basher "timed swing" reference is about launching. Swing Pals never launches anyone and has no landing to miss.
- **Seesaw Balance** is the other 3-PS2-2 clarification example, but its question is weight; here weight is the tempting variable that does *not* matter.
- **Ramp Race** keeps fair tests (change one thing). Swing Pals level 6 borrows its "one change matters" idea but asks for an answer (which rope), not a test design.
- **Music Mountain's rhythm games** (Beat Builder, Rhythm Neighbors, Jelly Drums) ask her to follow or build a beat. Here the **swing's own physics sets the beat** and she discovers it; the island music keeps playing underneath and is not the thing to match.
- **IDEAS Vine Swing** (a Pitfall!-style swing over a pond to a numbered pad) is a crossing game with a release; **Skip Rope** is a cooperative jump-rope rhythm game. Neither is a pendulum-pattern game. If either is built it must not take the ID `swing-pals`.

## The model

- **A swing** has a rope hung from one of three **hooks**: short (length 1), middle (2.25) and long (4), with beats `T` of 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 s. The hooks sit at different heights on the tree, so every seat hangs at the **same height** above the grass; only the rope (and the hook's height) changes.
- **Motion** is analytic: `angle(t) = A · cos(2π(t − t0) / T)`, released from the **back** (the pet's side) at `t0`. `period(hook)` takes nothing but the hook: the friend's weight and the height `A` are not arguments, so tests can assert they do not matter.
- **Height** `A` runs from 0 to the **bell height** `A_bell` (25° on one swing, 18° when two swings share the screen). When a push would go past it, it stops at it.
- **Pushes** (levels 1–3). A push raises the target height by a **step**; the drawn height eases up to the target over the next half swing, so the motion never jumps. At level 1 a tap pushes **at once** (the friend is nudged forward wherever they are), step `A_bell / 4`. From level 2 a tap is **stored** and the push lands the next time the friend reaches the back, where the pet's paws meet the seat; step `A_bell / 4`. Taps while a push is already waiting merge into it, so at most one push lands per swing and a steady tap once per swing is simply the quickest way up. A tap never lowers the height, and no tap is ever a miss.
- **The bell latches.** Once a swing's feet ring its bell, that swing is done for the line and stays done: if she stops pushing, the swing settles slowly (height × 0.85 per swing) and the bell stays lit. Before the bell, a swing she is not pushing **keeps its height** (no decay), so a goal half-reached is never lost.
- **Swing together** (levels 4–6). The pet's swing is fixed on a seeded hook with a seeded friend. Her swing starts still on another hook with its own friend. She taps a hook; her rope moves there (0.4 s); the pet pulls both swings back to their release heights and lets go at the same moment. They swing for a **watch** of two of the pet's swings (at most 4 s), then settle. They **match** when her hook equals the pet's hook. `together(line, hook)` returns match or not, and for a mismatch the side (`quicker` when her rope is shorter, `slower` when longer). At every wrong hook the two swings visibly part: the largest difference in angle during the watch is at least half the smaller release height (rule test), so "not together" can always be seen.
- **Release heights** default to the same `A` for both swings; level 6 gives them different heights (a big push and a small push).
- `logic.ts` holds plain functions: `period`, `angleAt`, `pushStep(level)`, `applyPush(swing, tapTime, level)`, `nextBackTime(swing, t)`, `together`, `rightHook(line)`, `planFor(level)`, `makeLine(plan, rng)` (seeded; built from the plan's filter, at most 50 tries, then an authored fallback that a test asserts is never needed over 500 seeds) and `swingLayout(swings, view)`.

## Screen and controls

- **The tree** fills the top of the screen: a thick branch carries the long hook; two lower boughs carry the middle and short hooks. Seats hang at the same height. Friends are critters from `src/art/critter.ts` (bunny, duck, mouse, bear, frog; at level 1 the first friend is her own pet via `ctx.petSpec`); weight is shown by size and a heavy friend's seat dips a little, never by a number.
- **Layout** (`swingLayout`, tested at the four standard views; computed from the hook lengths, not yet measured on a device).
  - **One swing (levels 1–2), landscape 1024 × 768.** Long-rope scale 560 units for length 4 (the middle rope used at levels 1–2 is 315). Pivot about x 560, so at 25° the seat travels about ±133 units at middle length; the pet sits at the left of the arc (outside the bottom-left corner pet, which keeps its usual place). Bell in the leaves at the forward end of the arc.
  - **Two swings (levels 3–6), landscape.** Scale 460 units for length 4; at 18° the long seat travels about ±142. Pivots at x 380 and 800; the right column ends near x 997, the left starts clear of the corner pet (seat height y 620, pet corner below y 640 and left of x 150).
  - **Portrait 768 × 1024.** Scale 360 units for length 4 at 18° (long seat ±111); pivots at x 210 and 560; seats at y 620, so the pet's corner (below y 874) and the shell's top-left buttons stay clear.
  - **Targets.** At levels 1–2 the whole play area (everything except the shell's buttons) is the push target. At level 3 each swing's half of the screen is its push target, full height, split at the midline. At levels 4–6 the three hooks of her swing are 120-unit circles at least 100 units apart (offset sideways, since they sit at different heights), plus the rope itself, which is not a target. Friends are drawn at least 110 units tall.
  - The layout test asserts targets ≥ 100 units, no overlapping hit areas, no arc point of a seat or friend inside the pet's corner or under the shell's buttons, both arcs inside `view` at the bell height, and the same line on every view.
- **Touch-down feedback.** Every tap makes the pet's paws reach out at once (and, from level 2, a soft ring at the back point shows the push is waiting). At levels 4–6 a hook brightens under the finger.
- **The "now" ring** (level 2 only, first two friends): at each back point a soft ring pulses where the pet's paws meet the seat, so the beat is visible. It is a cue, always on, not a hint.
- **Sound and feel:** each push a soft whoosh; each swoop a giggle on the way forward (a seeded animal voice from `sfx.ts`); the bell rings a pentatonic note from `src/audio/notes.ts` (a different note per friend, so a round plays a short tune). At levels 4–6, while the two swings move together, each back point gives a little high-five clap; apart, no clap. Music: a calm existing style (`STYLES.paint` by default), not `silenced`: she is not making the music. Pixi `Graphics` only, `palette.ts` colours: a tree in leaf, rope, wooden seats, a brass bell.
- **Speech:** `ctx.instruct` gives the instruction and the corner pet repeats it. Navigation never needs reading.

### First rounds

- **Level 1, friend 1 (authored):** her pet sits on the middle swing, still. "Tap to push!" Each tap: paws, whoosh, the swing rises a quarter of the way; the fourth rings the bell. The pet cheers, the swing settles, the pet hops off and a bunny climbs on. Friends 2–3 are seeded critters.
- **Level 2, friend 1 (authored):** a duck on the middle swing, already swinging low (`A_bell / 4`) so the beat can be seen. "Push when Duck comes back to me." The "now" ring pulses at each back point. A tap mid-swing makes the paws reach and wait; the push lands when the duck arrives. Three more landed pushes ring the bell (the starting sway counts as the first step).
- **Level 3, line 1 (authored):** a mouse on the short rope (left), a frog on the long rope (right), both still. "Push both friends up to their bells." Tapping the left half pushes the mouse, the right half the frog; the mouse comes back twice for every one of the frog's swings, so the left side wants quicker taps. When both bells have rung, both swing on together for one long swing while the pet says "Short rope, quick-quick. Long rope, slo-ow." Line 2 swaps sides.
- **Level 4, line 1 (authored):** the pet's bunny on the long hook; her bunny on the short hook. "Make your swing go just like mine. A long rope swings slower." Only the long hook matches. The short hook (her start) or the middle one parts, and the pet says why ("Yours is quicker. Try a longer rope.").
- **Level 5, line 1 (authored):** the pet's swing carries a little mouse on the middle hook; her swing carries a big bear on the long hook. "The bear is heavy. Which rope makes the bear swing like the mouse?" The middle hook matches. The tempting long hook ("heavy goes slow") parts slower: "Heavy or light, the rope sets the beat."
- **Level 6, line 1 (authored):** the pet's duck on the short hook, pushed small; her frog on the long hook, to be pushed big. "A big push and a small push. Which rope swings together?" The short hook matches; the big swing and the small swing then come back together, which the pet points out: "Big or small, the same rope keeps the same beat."
- Later lines at every level are seeded from the plan.

## Accounting, help and finishing

- **Never a miss:** any tap or push at levels 1–3, at any timing; a tap while a push waits; pushing a swing whose bell already rang; repeating the instruction; at levels 4–6, tapping the hook her swing already hangs on before trying it (that runs a real try) and re-trying a hook already tried on this line (it replays the watch, and the pet says "We tried that one").
- **Levels 1–3 record no misses.** Adaptation there moves only through hints ([decision 2](#open-decisions)).
- **A miss (levels 4–6):** choosing a hook that does not match, the first time on this line. The gentle response: the two swings visibly part during the watch, a soft boing as they settle, and the spoken reason with its direction ("Yours is quicker. Try a longer rope." / "Yours is slower. Try a shorter rope."), plus at level 5 the weight line and at level 6 the height line when that is the tempting variable. Her rope stays where she put it.
- **The glow after two misses:** after a line's second miss the matching hook glows until she taps it; that counts `hints++` once. With three hooks, two misses always leave the right one, so the glow confirms rather than replaces her reasoning; the doc says so on the card.
- **Show a hint** in the pause sheet (`Scene.pauseActions`): at levels 1–3 the pet gives the next two pushes with her (landing at the back points) and counts `hints++` once per request; at levels 4–6 it gives the same glow as above, `hints++` once per glow; asking when the line is done only says what to do next.
- **Finish:** when every line's bells have rung (levels 1–3) or every line swings together (levels 4–6), all the friends swing in a row once, then `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` gives one ordinary sticker, with or without help. No creation, discovery or journal entry is filed in this build ([decision 4](#open-decisions)).

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **The beat:** `period` gives 1.0, 1.5 and 2.0 s for the three hooks; it is strictly increasing in rope length; it takes no weight or height, and `angleAt` with the same hook and different heights crosses the back point at the same times.
- **Pushes:** at level 1, four taps at any times (sampled random times over 500 seeds) ring the bell, and no sequence of taps lowers the height; at levels 2–3, a tap waits and lands at `nextBackTime`, taps while one waits merge (one push per swing at most), four landed pushes ring the bell, and any tap sequence with at least one tap per swing for four swings rings it. The bell latches: no later event un-rings it, and an unpushed swing below its bell keeps its height.
- **Together:** for every pair of hooks, `together` matches only when the hooks are equal and returns `quicker`/`slower` by rope length; for every wrong hook the largest angle difference during the watch is at least half the smaller release height; level 5 and 6 weights and heights never change the answer.
- **Level 3 pairs** have a beat ratio of at least 1.4 (they are always short and long, 2).
- **Each authored first line** has the right hook named above, and its named tempting hook gives the named side.
- **Generation**, for every plan over 500 seeds: well formed, the starting hook is wrong (levels 4–6), the plan filter in the table holds, the same seed gives the same line, and the fallback is never used.
- **Hints:** following `rightHook` always matches; the level 1–3 hint pushes always raise height until the bell.
- **Layout:** `swingLayout` at the four views for one and two swings, against the assertions above.
- **Registry, lands and how-to tests** pass with the new game, including its land still fitting on one screen at every band.

## Open decisions

Each has a default the build uses. None blocks it.

1. **Bands.** Default lap 1, toddler 1–2, preschool 2–3, pre-K 3–4, school 4–6, matching PHYSICS-GAMES's lap–school. Not in the first build: a school prediction mode ("which comes back first?", unscored, as Planet Hop's predictions are), a counting mode (count the swings in a song phrase) and the grown-up calm toy.
2. **Accounting.** Default: levels 1–3 record no misses at all, so adaptation there moves only through hints; misses only from level 4, where the rule is stated before she chooses. Alternative: no misses at pre-K (level 4 counts from school only).
3. **Home land.** Default Rainbow Meadow (9 with the pending defaults). Alternatives: Tinker Lab, whose 14th game needs `landLayout` to fit, or Bubble Beach, already the default for Domino Run, Fruit Slice and Puddle Gobble (7 with those).
4. **Journal.** Default: no discovery filed. Alternative: file "a longer rope swings slower" once level 3 has actually shown both swings ringing, as Seesaw Balance files only what a round showed.
5. **Level 2's waiting push.** Default: a mistimed tap is stored and lands at the back point. Alternative: a tap outside a generous window does nothing (the paws reach and wait visibly); either way it is never a miss and never lowers the height.
