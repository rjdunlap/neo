# Draw the Pattern, levels 1–8 (definition)

Defined 2026-10-18 for [chunk 25](../ROADMAP.md#chunks) (dexterity: slice, cut and draw), from the [IDEAS candidate](../IDEAS.md#candidates). This is a docs-only definition: no game, ID registration, voice line or save field exists yet. **Ready to build:** each open call below has a default the build can use, and none needs a content reviewer. They are listed in [Open decisions](#open-decisions) and in [WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER](../WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER.md). It is the third and last of the chunk's slice, cut and draw candidates after [Fruit Slice](fruit-slice.md) and [Cut Along](cut-along.md), and it shares Cut Along's tracer.

**Fun action and purpose:** a long strip of paper lies across the screen. The pet picks up a fat crayon and starts a pattern on it: a line, a zigzag, a wave, a row of loops. Then it holds the crayon out, and she carries on from where the pet stopped. Her line scratches softly and its note climbs as she goes, and when the strip is finished the pattern turns into part of a picture: the wave becomes the sea with a boat bobbing on it, the zigzag becomes mountains, the loops become a flock of sheep, the straight line becomes the ground and the short lines a fence or rain. Two or three strips make one picture, and the round ends with it.

The learning goal is **the pre-writing strokes in the order they develop**: scribbles and marks, then vertical and horizontal lines, then zigzags and waves, circles and loops, and finally the cross, square and triangle, which handwriting programmes and occupational therapists place before letters. On top of that come two things Letter Trails does not do: **continuing a stroke from memory** once the printed guide fades (level 5), and **continuing a repeating pattern of strokes** (levels 7–8, Pattern Train's AB, AAB and ABB in crayon). It is the toddler on-ramp to Letter Trails, which starts at preschool. Speed never matters: nothing moves on its own and nothing is timed.

**What it does not claim:** a finger on glass is not a pencil. The sources below say the link between screen tracing and handwriting is unproven, so the grown-up's text says "pre-writing lines and patterns", not "handwriting", and the off-screen suggestion is a crayon on a long strip of paper, or a finger in sand. The ages quoted below are the usual order, with the sources disagreeing by half a year or more; nothing here is a developmental check.

## Overlap

- **Letter Trails and Number Trails** own forming letters and numerals. Draw the Pattern uses their tracer (below) on strokes that are not symbols, two bands younger. The one shape both draw is a circle: here it is a bubble or the sun, in either direction, not the letter O.
- **Cut Along** (defined, this chunk) uses the same tracer to follow a printed line with scissors. Cut Along always prints the line; Draw the Pattern fades it (level 5) and makes her choose the stroke (levels 7–8). Both have a lap or toddler free level; Cut Along's is taps, this one's is marks.
- **Rainbow Fingers** owns free painting with colors. Level 1 here is also free marks, and that overlap is deliberate: the marks land on the pet's strip and sprout flowers, which is the first rung of a ladder that leads to the ring and the dashes. Rainbow Fingers' ladder leads to colors and mixing instead.
- **Pattern Train** owns picking the next car. Levels 7–8 here are the same AB, AAB and ABB idea, but the unit is a stroke she draws, so it practices continuing a motor pattern, not matching a picture. Its spoken rules reuse Pattern Train's wording ("red, blue, red, blue: what comes next?" becomes "hill, dip, hill, dip: what comes next?").
- **Dot Link** draws free lines through dots. **Lasso Loops** draws loops around groups. Neither follows a stroke.
- **Mirror Brush** and **Stamp Studio** are creative drawing; there is no right stroke there.

## The model

- **A strip** is a long paper banner with a fixed aspect: coordinates are normalized by its **height** (y 0–1, x 0–3.4), so a corridor is the same width both ways and the pattern's repeat count never depends on the view. The strip is drawn at least 680 × 200 logical units in every standard view (portrait included) and scales up in landscape, up to about 950 × 280.
- **A stroke** is `{ kind, points, lead, candidates? }`. `points` is a sampled point list in strip units, built in `logic.ts` (`stripFor(plan, rng)`) from straight segments and sampled arcs, and sampled with **Letter Trails' `sampleStroke`**. It is followed with **`advanceTrace`**, and the bot's corners come from **`traceWaypoints`**, all imported from `src/games/letter-trails/strokes.ts` exactly as Number Trails does. **The stray test is Letter Trails' `offTrail`**, the glow stretch is its `showTheWay`, the climbing note its `tuneStep` and the start arrow its `startArrow`, all from `src/games/letter-trails/logic.ts`. No new tracer is written.
- **The pet's part and hers.** Each stroke's points are split at index `lead`: the pet draws points `0..lead` (the first part of the pattern, animated with a crayon at the round's start and again whenever she taps the pet) and leaves a green **start ring** (at least 110 units across, with the start arrow) at point `lead`. Her checkpoints run from `lead` to the end. At level 1 `lead` is the whole stroke and there are no checkpoints.
- **Patterns** are built from **units**: `zig` (up to a peak, down to a trough), `wave` (a smooth hill then a dip), `loop` (a garland loop that crosses itself once), `hill` (an arcade arch above the baseline), `dip` (a garland arch below it), `tall` and `short` (a zig whose peak is at y 0.2 or y 0.5). A pattern stroke is `repeat(units, n)` laid along the baseline y 0.5 from x 0.2, each unit 0.6 wide, so the strip holds five units: the pet draws two (levels 3, 5), or four or six (levels 7–8), and she draws the rest.
- **Candidates (levels 7–8).** Where she has to choose, a unit has two `candidates`: the stroke that continues the pattern and the one that breaks it (the other kind of the pair). Both are sampled lists that share the unit's first points up to a `divergence` index. `chooseUnit(unit, index)` works like Cut Along's `chooseOrder`: the first advance past the divergence picks one candidate for that unit, and the other is dropped. The miss is counted on the wrong candidate once she has advanced **four checkpoints past the divergence** (about 0.1 strip heights), never before, so a hesitant start is free.
- **Either way around a circle (level 4).** A circle has two candidate orders, `ccw` (from the top, going left, as the pet draws it and as Letter Trails' O runs) and `cw`, sharing the start. The first advance picks one. The loops are not symmetric and have one order.
- **The corridor** is `tolerance(level)` in strip units, times the strip's height in logical units: 0.18 (levels 2–3: 36 units at the smallest strip), 0.16 (4: 32), 0.15 (5–8: 30). A floor: never under 30 logical units in any standard view (a rule test), which is the same floor as Cut Along and Letter Trails' corridor at its smallest box. `advanceTrace`'s corner rule stands: a checkpoint on the way back waits until the finger turns, which is what makes the zigzag's peaks and the square's corners honest.
- **The crayon** is a code-drawn fat crayon about 120 units long, parked at the current checkpoint with its tip on the line. Between drags it is the resume target, with a hit area of at least 110 units. Lifting keeps all progress. A new touch near the crayon carries on; a touch anywhere else on the paper does nothing but make the crayon wiggle.
- **Sound:** her line makes a soft crayon scratch (filtered noise, gated by movement) and every fifth of the stroke plays the next pentatonic step from `src/audio/notes.ts` (`tuneStep`). The pet's demonstration plays the same notes. A finished strip plays a short rising figure and the picture appears. Nothing plays for a stray but the gentle boing.

`logic.ts` holds plain functions: `planFor(level)`, `stripFor(plan, rng)` (the strokes, their `lead`, candidates and the picture piece each becomes), `tolerance(level)`, `chooseUnit(unit, index)`, `chooseOrder(stroke, index)`, `nextUnit(pattern, shown)` (the kind that continues an AB, AAB or ABB pattern after the shown units), `markCount(plan)` (level 1), and `hintStretch(points, progress)` (which reuses `showTheWay`).

## Screen and controls

- **The strip** lies across the middle of `view`, clear of the home button (top-left), the picture (top-right) and the pet corner (bottom-left). In portrait it sits a little lower and the picture shrinks above it. One strip is shown at a time; a finished strip rolls up into the picture and the next unrolls.
- **The pet** sits bottom-left as usual and holds the crayon while it draws its part along the strip (a tween along the stroke's points, about 1.5 seconds), then holds the crayon out toward the ring. Tapping the pet replays its part and the instruction; that is not a hint.
- **The draw layer** uses custom pointer handling on the paper only, with a `hitArea`, added last. It ignores input while `palmOnGlass()`. A drag ends on `pointerup`, `pointercancel`, `pointerupoutside`, or a lost release (no events for 0.5 s after the finger leaves the canvas). It is cleared in `destroy()`.
- **Touch-down feedback:** the crayon tip dents the paper and a dot appears under the finger at once, before any movement. Her line is drawn in the pet's crayon color behind the crayon, so finished progress is always visible; the dashes ahead stay until she covers them.
- **The picture** (top-right, at least 200 units) is the land host's crayon drawing, with soft outlines where each strip's pattern will go. Tapping it repeats the instruction.
- **Speech:** `ctx.instruct` gives each instruction, and the corner pet repeats it. Navigation never needs reading. When a strip finishes, the pet names what it became ("Your zigzag is the mountains!").
- **Music:** the land's calm style keeps playing quietly under the crayon (decision 5), not `silenced`, since she is not composing.

### First rounds

- **Level 1:** a yellow strip. The pet draws a wavy crayon line across it and holds the crayon out. "Draw on the paper!" Any touch draws a fat line in the pet's color, a pat makes a dot, and when the finger lifts, flowers sprout along what she drew with a little chime each. After four lifted touches the strip rolls up and becomes a flower meadow in the picture. Three strips. There is no wrong touch, and a touch outside the paper does nothing.
- **Level 2:** a blue strip with a thick dashed line along its middle. The pet draws the left third and stops at a green ring with an arrow pointing right. "Start on the green ring and draw the line to the end." Her line becomes the ground. The second strip has three short dashed lines, each with a ring at the top and an arrow pointing down; the pet draws the first. "Start at the top and go down." Each line becomes a fence post, and the picture gets its fence.
- **Level 3:** the pet draws two zigs of a zigzag and holds out the crayon; she draws three more along the dashes. The peaks wait for her finger to turn. The zigzag becomes mountains. The second strip is a wave, which becomes the sea with a boat.
- **Level 4:** the pet draws a circle from the top, going left, and the ring sits at the top. She may go either way; the first move picks the way. It becomes the sun. The second strip is loops: the pet draws one, she draws three. They become sheep.
- **Level 5:** as 3 or 4 (zigzag, wave or loops by seed), but after the pet's two units the dashes fade over a second, leaving dots at the turning points. "Keep the pattern going. The dots show where it turns." If she strays, the dashes for the next stretch fade back in while the pet says "Come back to the crayon", and fade out again after it.
- **Level 6:** a cross: a ring at the top with an arrow pointing down, then, when that stroke is done, a ring at the left with an arrow pointing right. It becomes a window. Then a square from its top-left corner, then a triangle from its apex. They become a house and its roof.
- **Level 7:** the pet draws hill, dip, hill, dip, saying the words as it goes. "Hill, dip, hill, dip. What comes next? Draw it." Between the next two baseline dots both a hill and a dip are printed faintly; she draws the hill. Three units, each chosen. The finished arcade and garland row becomes a bridge.
- **Level 8:** the pet draws hill, hill, dip, hill, hill, dip. "What comes next?" Three more chosen units.

## Accounting, help and finishing

- **Never a miss:** any touch at level 1; a touch away from the ring and crayon; lifting partway; tapping the pet or the picture; at level 4, either way around the circle; at levels 7–8, a start on the wrong candidate that stays within four checkpoints of the divergence.
- **A miss (levels 2–8)** is a drag that wanders off the corridor: `offTrail` from the current checkpoint while the finger is down. This is the same rule Letter Trails and Number Trails use, with at most **one miss per drag**. The crayon stays at the last checkpoint, there is a gentle boing, and the pet says "Come back to the crayon." Progress is never lost. At level 5 the stray also brings the next stretch's dashes back, which is the level's own help and not a counted hint.
- **The wrong stroke (levels 7–8):** advancing four checkpoints past the divergence on the candidate that breaks the pattern is a miss, once per unit. The crayon stops, her partial stroke fades, and the pet says the rule in Pattern Train's words: "Hill, dip, hill, dip. After a dip comes a hill." The correct candidate stays faint, and she draws again from the unit's start dot.
- **The two-miss glow:** at the second miss on one stroke or unit, and every second miss after, `hintStretch` lights the next 12 checkpoints with yellow dots and the crayon's tip sparkle flies that stretch and back (a drawing, not the ghost finger). At levels 7–8 the correct candidate glows whole. Each glow adds one to `hints`.
- **Finish:** when the round's strips are done, the picture is complete. The pet holds the crayon up and names what is in it, then `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` gives one ordinary sticker, with or without help. Nothing is saved or filed (decision 2).
- **Adaptation** follows the unchanged shell rule. Level 1 can produce neither misses nor hints, so a lap player steps up by finishing, and level 2's long line is the first stroke that can be left.

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Fit:** for every plan, every seed in a sample and the four standard views, the strip, every stroke, ring and the picture lie inside `view`, clear of the home button, the picture and the pet corner. Each ring is at least 110 units across, and rings on one strip never overlap (level 2's posts, level 6's cross).
- **Corridor floor:** `tolerance(level) × stripHeight` is at least 30 logical units for every level and view, and `tolerance` never widens as the level rises.
- **Patterns fit:** every pattern stroke's units lie within x 0.2–3.2, and the pet's `lead` leaves her at least three units (levels 3, 5, 7, 8) or three loops (4).
- **No skipping:** at every corner from `traceWaypoints` (zigzag peaks and troughs, the square's and triangle's corners, `tall`'s peak), a straight chord from the checkpoint before the corner across to the far leg consumes no checkpoint past the corner. Away from corners (a loop's two sides, the circle's far side), no two sample points more than 6 apart along the path come within `2 × tolerance`, except the single self-crossing of a `loop`, which is exempt and tested separately: following the loop's waypoints never consumes the far side early, because `advanceTrace` consumes only consecutive checkpoints.
- **Completable:** feeding `advanceTrace` the `traceWaypoints` route (densified to 10-unit steps) from `lead` reaches the last checkpoint for every stroke. The circle completes in both `cw` and `ccw`, and `chooseOrder` picks the order the first move follows.
- **Candidates:** for every level 7–8 pattern and seed, the two candidates coincide up to `divergence` and then stay at least `2 × tolerance` apart for at least four checkpoints; `nextUnit` returns the kind that continues the shown units for every AB, AAB and ABB pattern and every shown prefix; the wrong candidate's kind has a `rule.*` voice line.
- **Stray rule:** a drag that leaves the corridor gives exactly one miss, level 1 never gives one, and progress after a stray equals progress before it.
- **Level 1:** `markCount` is 4, and any point inside the paper is a valid mark.
- **Hint:** `hintStretch` starts at the current checkpoint and moves forward along the chosen order or candidate.
- **Seeds:** the same seed gives the same strips, patterns and candidates.
- **Registry, lands and how-to tests** pass with the new game.

## Check tier

New-game tier, with a new interaction: a stroke that continues from where the pet stopped, a faded path, and a choice between two candidate strokes.

- **Unit tests:** typecheck, and the rule properties above plus the registry, lands and how-to tests.
- **Scripted play:** one through a new `browser-check.mjs` case (its README row is part of the build), filtered to level 1 and level 8. It makes one stray at level 3 (counted miss) and a second stray on that stroke (glow), has the bot draw the level 4 circle counterclockwise and a kit drag draw another clockwise, and at level 8 draws one wrong candidate past the divergence (counted miss, rule spoken) before the bot finishes.
- **Screenshots and demos:** one portrait screenshot at level 5 with the dashes faded. Run `fingerdemo` at levels 1 and 7.
- **Build:** run `npm run build`, since the registry, lands, voice and how-to files change.

Device checks stay open:

- Whether a 30-unit corridor suits a two-year-old's finger on the zigzag, or level 3 should widen to 0.2.
- Whether the crayon under the finger hides the dashes ahead. The default is that the crayon leans forward, its tip on the line and its body trailing behind the finger.
- Whether level 5's stepping stones are enough for a three-year-old, or the dashes should stay faint instead of fading out.
- Whether level 7's two faint candidates read as a choice to a four-year-old.

## Open decisions

Each has a default that the build uses. None blocks it.

1. **Home land.** Default: Paint Pier (the craft land: 6 games with Cut Along and this one). The alternative is Story Grove beside Letter Trails, its follow-on, which would have 10 games; `lands.test.ts` decides whether either still fits at every band.
2. **Keeping a strip.** Default: nothing saved. The alternative is a `pattern` creation in `src/content/creations.ts` that home customization could put on a creature's blanket or scarf. That is a save-field change at the shell/save tier, better folded into [RW06](../rework/WORK-ITEMS.md#rw06-playable-home-and-customization).
3. **Lap entry.** Default: lap plays levels 1–2, free marks and then the one long line, since the sources place an imitated vertical or horizontal line at about 2. The alternative is lap 1 alone.
4. **Circle direction.** Default: either way around at level 4, the first move choosing. The alternative is anticlockwise only, as Letter Trails' O and the handwriting sources prefer for o, c and d.
5. **Music.** Default: the land's calm style plays under the crayon. The alternative is `silenced(STYLES.quiet)`, which would also add the game to the registry test's list.
6. **The triangle at pre-K.** Default: level 6 keeps the cross, square and triangle at pre-K with the corridor and glow carrying the triangle. The alternative is a school band `{ min: 6, max: 8 }` and the triangle only there, which the sources' 5½ would support.
7. **Level 5's judging.** Default: the faded path keeps its corridor and stepping stones, so the tracer is reused and a stray is the same rule. The alternative is a feature judge (does her stroke go up and down the right number of times?) that would be a new rule to write and test.
