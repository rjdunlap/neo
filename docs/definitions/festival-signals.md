# Festival Signals, Sorting Machine levels 8–9 (definition)

**Built:** levels 8–9 (2026-11-11, [PR #1497](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1497), slice H5); nothing further to build here; the deviations are in “Build notes” at the end.

Defined 2026-11-10 as slice H4 of the [how-computers-work study](../deep-dives/subjects/how-computers-work.md#build-slices-in-order) ([packet](../deep-dives/subjects/RECOMMENDATIONS.md#how-computers-work-recommendations), [the case](../deep-dives/subjects/how-computers-work.md#festival-signals-one-older-family-case)). Built 2026-11-11 (see the line above and [Build notes](#build-notes)). It extends [Sorting Machine](sorting-machine.md) (levels 1–2 built, level 3 defined), keeping one game ID as decided on 2026-10-23. The study's younger rungs 4–7 (*Either badge*, *No hat needed*, *Hat, without whiskers*, *Same welcome, different machine*) keep their draft IDs and are **not** defined here.

**Fun action and purpose:** build a small four-part machine that lights the festival lantern for a friend wearing **just one** of two badges, test it on any friend, then check it on all four. Learning goal: a rule that sounds simple ("one or the other, but not both") is built from smaller parts (OR, AND, NOT) whose signals can be read one at a time; a machine is right only when it is right for every row, so one lucky test is not enough, and a nearly-right machine is repaired by finding the row it gets wrong. For an older school child or a grown-up it is a complete design puzzle; the shortest machine, speed and the number of tests never matter.

**Framing.** The lantern is a festival signal, not a door: every friend walks into the festival whatever the lantern does, and the lights are a game of signals, never a rule about who may come. The words *XOR*, *exclusive*, *circuit*, *power* and *electricity* are never spoken or shown (the [base definition](sorting-machine.md#overlap-and-reuse)'s rule).

## The brief

Two badges `P` and `Q` (a seeded pair from `PAIRS`: hat and whiskers, hat and boots, whiskers and boots), four fixtures, one per row, and the sign:

| `P` | `Q` | Lantern |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 0 | 0 | rests |
| 0 | 1 | lights |
| 1 | 0 | lights |
| 1 | 1 | rests |

All four rows are on screen from the start, each fixture card showing the lantern the sign wants under it (a lit lantern, or a dark one with a hollow ring; never colour alone). Test results stay on the cards for the round, so remembering earlier tries is never the challenge.

## The model (`logic.ts`)

Levels 1–3's tree (`Node`: sensors and two-input gates) stays as it is. Levels 8–9 add a second, flat machine kind beside it, so nothing built changes:

- **Sources** `P` and `Q` are fixed sensors at the left of the machine.
- **Four slots** `s1`–`s4`, in a row. A slot is `empty` or holds a **part**: `NOT(x)` or `AND(x, y)` or `OR(x, y)`, where each input is a source or an **earlier** slot (`s3` may read `P`, `Q`, `s1` or `s2`). Only forward connections exist, so a machine is always acyclic and there is nothing to untangle. An input left unchosen is empty.
- **The lantern** reads one slot. At level 8 it reads `s4`, fixed and drawn. At level 9 the lantern is itself a socket whose wire she can move (`s1`–`s4`).
- **Values** are `true`, `false` or `incomplete`. `evaluateFlat(machine, fixture)` follows only the slots the lantern's wire reaches: an empty slot or input on that path is `incomplete`, never `false`; a slot off the path is ignored (drawn dimmed, "not wired to the lantern"), whatever it holds.
- **Check** is the base `check` contract over the flat machine: `incomplete` (with the first empty slot on the path, in source order) before `fail` (every failing row) before `pass`, over all four fixtures whether or not they were sent.
- **The accepted set.** Any machine whose four outputs equal the sign passes; acceptance compares outputs, never shapes. Under this grammar there are exactly **24 accepted machines**, all four parts long and all lighting the lantern from `s4`: three slot orders (`OR`, `AND`, `NOT` of the `AND`; `AND`, `OR`, `NOT` of the `AND`; `AND`, `NOT` of it, `OR`) times eight input-order swaps, all `(P OR Q) AND NOT (P AND Q)`. Eight of them keep `P OR Q` in `s1`. The other well-known form, `(P AND NOT Q) OR (NOT P AND Q)`, needs five parts, so it is not promised here (a five-slot palette is later work, as the study says). These figures come from enumerating all 2,005,696 fillings of the four slots and the lantern's wire during this definition; the rule test re-derives them.
- **Nearest solution.** `nearest(machine)` is the accepted machine (from a frozen list of the 24, generated by the test's enumeration and checked in) that agrees with the most of her filled slots and wire, counting `AND(x, y)` and `AND(y, x)` (likewise `OR`) as agreeing; ties go to the list's order. Help and the glow use it, so they keep her correct work.

| Level | Name (`describeLevel`) | The machine as the round opens | Rounds |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 8 | Festival Signals | Four empty slots; the lantern reads `s4` | 3 seeded briefs, one per pair; seeded fixture order and cast |
| 9 | Fix the lantern | `s1 = P OR Q`, the lantern wired to `s1`, `s2`–`s4` empty | 3 seeded briefs, one per pair |

At level 9 the starting machine lights for three of the four friends: sending the both-badges friend (`11`) shows the lantern lit where the sign wants it resting. The repair keeps `s1`, adds `P AND Q`, its `NOT`, and an `AND` of `s1` with that, then moves the lantern's wire to `s4`; any other route to one of the 24 machines is equally right, including clearing `s1`.

- **`grownup`**: `{ min: 3, max: 9, order: [3, 9, 8] }`. Level 3 (*Both badges*, one gate) is the way into Send, Check and the marks; *Fix the lantern* (9) comes before *Festival Signals* (8) because it starts from a machine that is three-quarters right. This needs level 3 built (H3) first.
- **`school`** stays `{ min: 1, max: 3 }` (after H3). Levels 8–9 join the school top only when rungs 4–7 (OR, NOT and composing two gates) are defined and built, as `order: [1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 8]`. A school child is not sent from one gate straight to four.
- **Plans by ID.** Today `planFor` indexes `PLANS` by position and `MAX_LEVEL = PLANS.length`; H5 keys plans by level ID so 8 and 9 never read as 4 and 5. Two shared checks in `src/content/howto.test.ts` (the card's level line, and the island demo's level) walk `min..max`, which would visit the unbuilt 4–7: H5 switches those two loops to `levelsInOrder`, as the file's other walk already does (a shared test, covered by H5's build and full unit run).
- The game's `bands` become `['school', 'grownup']`. Its land stays Tinker Lab (one land per game); a grown-up already sees it there, and today plays its school range through `playBand`. Saved levels and pins keep their IDs; 4–7 stay reserved for the study's younger rungs.

## Screen and controls

The stage, Send, Slow, Check, Undo and Help work as at levels 2–3 ([screen and controls](sorting-machine.md#screen-and-controls)). What is new:

- **The overview** is a picture of the whole machine: `P` and `Q` at the left, the four slot tiles in a row, the lantern at the right, and thin wires from each input to its slot. The wires are drawn, never touched (`eventMode = 'none'`). Each **slot tile** is a button (at least 116 × 116 units, as `SLOT`) showing its part's picture (AND: two arms joining; OR: two arms, either; NOT: a flip arrow) and, after a run, the value token it settled on for the friend on stage (star, ring or "?", as the base). At level 9 the lantern tile is a button too.
- **The selected-slot inspector.** Tapping a slot tile selects it (a thick ring) and opens its inspector under the overview, never a floating picker: one row of **part** buttons (AND, OR, NOT, and Clear), then one row per input of **source** buttons (`P`, `Q` and each earlier slot by number). `s4`'s input row has five choices; every button is at least 110 × 110 units (`PICK` is 116). NOT shows one input row. Choosing replaces; Clear empties the slot (her inputs return to unchosen). At level 9 the lantern's inspector is one row, `s1`–`s4`. The rows never shrink to fit: on a short view the inspector scrolls nothing and the overview gives way instead (its tiles stay 116; the wires shorten).
- **Send** runs the friend on stage stage by stage: the sources settle, then each slot on the lantern's path in order with its pentatonic note, then the lantern lights or rests. Slow waits for a Send tap between stages. A slot off the path stays dim and silent.
- **Check, misses and marks** as at level 3: one miss per failing complete Check, a badge on every failing friend, the first failing friend run once. The spoken line names what the **sign** wanted for that friend, not the fix ("The friend with both badges lit the lantern, but the sign says it rests for both.").
- **Layout.** Upright (the 768 × 1024 area and taller): fixtures in a row of four along the top, clear of the Home corner; the overview under them; the inspector under that; Send, Check, Slow and Undo in the bottom row, starting right of the pet corner. Wide (1024 × 768 and wider): fixtures two by two at the left as levels 2–3, the overview and inspector at the right, the buttons under them. `machineLayout(8 | 9, view)` returns one or the other, or `null` rather than shrink a target; its test runs at every view the lands test uses and at `UPRIGHT_VIEWS`. Targets stay at least 100 units for both bands (the grown-up minimum of 64 is not used: one layout serves both, and the school top will use it).
- **Feel:** the festival's lantern and bunting are code-drawn Pixi shapes in palette colours; a calm existing music style.

## Accounting, help and finishing

- **Never a miss:** any Send, choosing or clearing a part or input, moving the lantern's wire, Slow, Undo, Start again, an incomplete Check, repeating the instruction, opening the how-to.
- **One miss:** a complete machine whose Check fails, however many rows differ.
- **The two-miss glow:** at the round's second miss, and every second miss after, the first slot (or, at level 9, the lantern) where her machine differs from `nearest` glows, and `hints++` once.
- **Requested Help**, one hint per press. First press: run a friend her machine gets wrong and say what the sign wants for it; while the path is incomplete, select the first empty slot on it and say what it needs ("This slot needs a part"). Each later press **fixes one thing** toward `nearest`: the first differing slot in source order, or the lantern's wire, with its inputs, then says what changed. When her machine passes, Help says to tap Check. A Help never ends the round; Check does. Undo takes back a Help's change like any other (the hint stays counted).
- **Finish:** one ordinary sticker per round through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`, helped or not. All four friends walk into the festival under their lanterns. Nothing is filed as a creation or discovery.
- **Adaptation:** the shell rule. A machine finished with Help is assisted, not mastery; the grown-up text says so.

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Grammar:** only forward inputs can be chosen; every filling is acyclic; `evaluateFlat` is `incomplete` for any empty slot or input on the lantern's path and ignores slots off it.
- **The accepted set:** enumerating every filling of the four slots (and, at level 9, the wire) finds exactly 24 accepted machines for each pair, all four parts long, all with the lantern on `s4`, equal to the checked-in list; `check` accepts each and nothing else. No three-part machine passes.
- **Distinguishable:** `P OR Q` (level 9's start) fails on exactly row `11`; `P AND Q` fails on `01`, `10` and `11`; so one test of the right friend tells each nearly-right machine apart from the sign.
- **Check:** order-independent over shuffled fixtures, evaluates untried rows, `incomplete` before `fail`, one failing submission is one miss.
- **Help:** from every machine reachable from level 9's start by up to two changes, and from a sample of 2,000 seeded fillings at level 8, repeated presses reach an accepted machine in at most four fixes (five at level 9, counting the wire), never undoing a slot that already agrees with `nearest`; the first press always selects a friend the machine gets wrong (or the first empty path slot).
- **Seeds:** the same seed gives the same pair, fixture order and cast; three seeds cover the three pairs at each level.
- **Bands:** `levelsInOrder(rangeFor(LEVELS, 'grownup'))` is `[3, 9, 8]`; the school range is unchanged; `describeLevel` covers 8 and 9.
- **Layout:** `flatLayout(view)` (one layout for both levels; as built) at every lands-test view and `UPRIGHT_VIEWS`: every target at least 100 units (pickers 110), inside the view, clear of the pet and corner buttons.

## Ghost finger, how-to card, voice and registration

- **Ghost finger** (`autotouch()`, the same taps a player uses). Level 8: tap the `11` friend, Send; fill `s1` OR `P` `Q`, `s2` AND `P` `Q`, `s3` NOT `s2`, `s4` AND `s1` `s3`; Send again; Check. Level 9: tap the `11` friend, Send (the lantern lights wrongly), fill `s2`–`s4` the same way, move the lantern to `s4`, Send, Check.
- **How-to card** (`howto.ts`, level-scoped `{ from: 8, to: 9 }` where it differs). Goal: "Build a machine that lights the lantern for a friend with just one badge." Steps: "Tap a slot to choose its part and where its signals come from."; "Tap a friend, then Send, and read each slot's star or ring."; level 9: "The lantern listens to one slot: tap it to move its wire."; "Check tries every friend at once." Finish: "Every friend's lantern matches the sign." Note: "Trying friends is never counted. There are several right machines; any one that matches every row is right."
- **Voice lines** (`voice.ts`, `sm.fs.*`; one line per pair wherever a badge is named, so device speech never fills a template): `sm.fs.instruct.8.<pair>`, `sm.fs.instruct.9.<pair>` ("This lantern lights for a hat or whiskers, but it should rest when a friend has both. Try the friend with both, then fix the machine."), `sm.fs.check-fail.<row>.<pair>` for the four rows, `sm.fs.empty-slot`, `sm.fs.off-path` ("That slot isn't wired to the lantern yet."), `sm.fs.part.and|or|not`, `sm.fs.help.run`, `sm.fs.help.fixed`, `sm.fs.help.check`, `sm.fs.check-pass`. Parts are named "and", "or" and "not", spoken plainly.
- **Registration:** `LEVELS` gains the grown-up range; `bands` gains `grownup`; levels 8–9 get `describeLevel` entries and how-to cards. No new game ID, land, hub icon or sticker; the sticker adds the lantern. `node scripts/content-barrels.mjs` and `npm run game-docs` as usual.
- **Co-play (role swaps):** one person builds; the other is the tester, choosing which friend to send and saying aloud whether the lantern should light before it runs; swap after each Check. **Off-screen:** two coins on paper (heads for a badge) and a four-row card; one person names a rule, the other fills the lantern column.
- **Couch:** none. The upright layout is the phone's.

## Open decisions

Each has a default the build uses.

1. **Grown-up entry.** Default: `order: [3, 9, 8]`. The alternative is starting grown-ups at 9, skipping the one-gate warm-up.
2. **School top.** Default: levels 8–9 join school only after rungs 4–7 are built. The alternative (8–9 for school after 3) is rejected here as too large a step.
3. **Lantern socket at level 8.** Default: fixed to `s4` (every accepted machine ends there). A socket at both levels would be more uniform but adds a control with only one right setting.
4. **Review.** The base definition's CS-educator read of spoken lines and the card covers these lines too; the building session's source check stands in under the [October 23 rules](../archive/DECISIONS-2026-10-23.md#standing-rules).
