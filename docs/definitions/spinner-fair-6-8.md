# Spinner Fair, levels 6–8 (definition)

**Built:** levels 6–8, all of CS2 (PR #1720, #1724, #1727); levels 9–11 (CS3) are in [their own definition](spinner-fair-9-10.md).

Defined 2026-11-16 as the CS2 definition of the [chance and statistics study](../deep-dives/subjects/chance-statistics.md#build-slices-in-order) (slice 3, its definition half), on the built levels 1–5 of the [levels 1–5 definition](spinner-fair.md). The study's decisions of 2026-10-23 stand ([record](../archive/DECISIONS-2026-10-23.md#decisions)); nothing here needs a new one. Three build slices follow (below); levels 9–10 stay CS3's.

**Fun action and purpose:** spin a wheel again and again and watch a strip of colour wobble and then settle on the wheel's share; roll two dice and build the grid of every way they can land; spin a number wheel, stack each spin as a tower and level the towers. Learning goal: *a share of the wheel shows up as about that share of many spins; seven is the likeliest two-dice sum because more pairs make it; a typical value is what everyone would have if it were shared out evenly, and the range is how far apart the biggest and smallest are.* Standards it serves: CCSS 7.SP.C.6 (approximate a probability from a long run), 7.SP.C.8 (sample spaces as organized lists and tables) and England Year 6's mean, where the study found the catalog thinnest.

## The accounting rule carries over unchanged

The [levels 1–5 rule](spinner-fair.md#the-accounting-rule) binds 6–8 word for word: **a spin or a roll is never a miss; a miss comes only from a question the picture decides; every spin and roll is an honest seeded draw, never filtered, retried or authored.** Two of the study's proposed rule tests break that rule, so this definition replaces them (departures from the study, decided here):

- **Level 6.** The study asked that "the band around the true share holds the seeded runs for every seed the level uses". That can only be met by choosing seeds, which authors the runs. Here the right answer comes from the **wheel's wedges**, never from a run, and the test checks the spins' honesty statistically over many seeds. A run that lands outside its band is spoken as *unusual*, never a miss and never re-spun.
- **Level 8.** The study asked that "the ten spins total a multiple of four" so the levelled towers come out exactly even. That filters spins. Here the spins are honest, levelling stops at **nearly level** (the tallest and shortest towers differ by at most one block), and when the mean is not whole **both neighbouring heights are accepted** as "about" the typical spin. "About" is one of the words the October 23 rule source-checks before it is spoken.

## Changes from the study's ladder

The [study's ladder](../deep-dives/subjects/chance-statistics.md#a-first-games-ladder-spinner-fair) predates the CS1 decision that **every wheel is six wedges** (eight wedges belong to CS3's *rig it*). On six wedges a quarter share cannot exist and a 1–4 number wheel cannot be fair, so:

| Study | Here | Why |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Level 6: a half-blue spinner; 10, 50 and 200 spins; "about how many blues in 100?" on bands around 25, 50, 75 | A two-critter wheel with **1, 3 or 5** of six wedges to Duck; runs to **12, 60 and 240** spins; "about how many Duck in 60 more?" on **about 10, about 30, about 50** | Sixths make the bands exact (a wedge is ten in sixty), and multiples of six keep every expected count whole. Two sets with different wheels, so the answer is not always "half" |
| Level 6: a second run of 20 beside the first | Dropped; the 60-spin check run after the question does that job | One strip to watch is enough on a phone |
| Level 7: one question, "which sum is most likely?" | Two questions: the most likely sum, then **which of two sums comes up more** | Seven is always the answer to the first, so on its own it is learned, not read; the pair question makes her count squares |
| Level 8: ten spins of a 1–4 number spinner, totals divisible by the bar count | **Six** spins of a **1–6** number wheel (each number once, so each is a sixth); honest totals; nearly level and "about" | Fair on six wedges; six towers fit a phone; see the accounting rule above |
| Level 8: range after levelling ("tallest minus shortest") | Range **before** levelling | Levelling destroys the range |

## The model (`logic.ts`)

**Plans.** `PLANS` gains three rows: `{ level: 6, ask: 'settle', name: 'Watch it settle', sets: 2, spins: 240 }`, `{ level: 7, ask: 'dice', name: 'Two dice', sets: 1, spins: 12 }`, `{ level: 8, ask: 'typical', name: 'The typical spin', sets: 2, spins: 6 }`. `Ask` gains `'settle' | 'dice' | 'typical'`. `LEVELS.school` becomes `{ min: 3, max: 8 }` (no `order`); levels 1–5 keep their numbers, so saved levels and pins stay valid, and CS3 appends 9–10.

### Level 6, Watch it settle

- **A set.** A two-critter wheel: Duck (the set's favourite, drawn from `KINDS`) has `k` wedges and a friend has `6 − k`, `k ∈ {1, 3, 5}`, contiguous (`raceWheel`). The round's two sets draw two **different** `k` (seeded order). The favourite is the same critter in both sets; the friend is redrawn.
- **The runs.** Three runs, each started by her tap on the spinner or on the visible fast-forward button: spins 1–12 (each visibly spun, about 0.35 s each), spins 13–60 (fast-forward, the wheel blurring, the strip updating every spin, about 3 s) and spins 61–240 (faster, about 4 s). Every spin is `spin(rng)`: no run is shortened, filtered or redrawn. A run never lasts more than 5 s; under reduced motion the wheel does not turn and the strip steps once per twelve spins.
- **The share strip.** A horizontal strip (the picture of the record) split into Duck's colour and the friend's at Duck's count over spins so far, with a fixed six-tick ruler under it and a dashed marker at the wheel's share `k/6`. The two counts show as numerals with the critters' faces and are spoken at the end of each run ("Duck 7, Pig 5"). After 12 spins the strip may sit far from the marker; after 240 it is close. The pet says so, from the picture: "The more we spin, the closer it gets to Duck's share of the wheel." A run that ends far from the marker is never commented on as wrong.
- **The question.** After the third run: "If we spin 60 more times, about how many will be Duck?" on three cards: *about 10*, *about 30*, *about 50* (each with a small strip drawn at one, three or five sixths, so the card reads without its numeral). Right is `10 · k`. Then a **check run** of 60 spins plays (about 3 s) and the pet names the count: within ten of `10 · k` it is "34: about 30!"; outside, "That's unusual. Chance does that sometimes." The check run is never a miss and is not re-spun.
- **Helpers.** `settleSet(rng, used)` returns the wheel, favourite, friend and `k`; `expected60(k) = 10 · k`; `aboutRight(card, k)`; `inBand(count, k) = |count − 10k| ≤ 10`.

### Level 7, Two dice

- **The rolls.** Two code-drawn dice (pips, each a different soft colour) sit in a tray; a tap on the tray rolls both (about 0.8 s tumble, then a click and a pop); the **sum** is spoken and a block drops onto one of **eleven display-only bars** (sums 2–12, each labelled with its numeral and spoken on landing). Twelve rolls, each `rng.int(1, 6)` twice. The rolls are never judged; if twelve rolls make seven look rare, the pet says so after the grid ("Twelve rolls is only a few. The squares show every way.").
- **The grid.** Then "Let's find every way two dice can land": a 6×6 grid fills **a row at a time** (about 1.2 s a row; the row's first die shown at its left, the second die along the top, each square showing its sum and in the sum's colour), each row's sums spoken ("One and one make two, one and two make three…" shortened after the first row to the sums alone). Beside it a second set of eleven bars fills with the count of squares per sum: 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1. The grid builds itself; she taps nothing during it (taps on it bounce).
- **Question one.** "Which sum is most likely?" on three sum cards: **7** and two others with different square counts from each other and fewer than six (seeded; sides seeded). Right is 7.
- **Question two.** "Which comes up more: 4 or 9?" on two sum cards: a seeded pair from 2–12 whose square counts differ (never 7, never a tie). Right is the one with more squares.
- **Helpers.** `roll(rng) → [a, b]`; `ways(sum)` = squares making it (`6 − |sum − 7|` for 2–12); `gridRows()` (the 36 sums by row); `mostCards(rng)`; `pairCards(rng)`; `rollTally(rolls)` (eleven counts).
- **The journal.** When the grid finishes building, the round reports the discovery **`spinner-fair:two-dice`** (`entryId('spinner-fair', 'two-dice')`; stable forever). It files on the grid completing, which happens every time she reaches it, help or not, and **never on what the dice rolled**: it records a picture she watched being built, not a result, so chance stays a subject and never a reward. `JournalGame` gains `'spinner-fair'`; the entry's caption "Two dice", line `journal.spinner-fair.two-dice` ("Seven can be made six ways, more than any other sum"), source `find`: "fill the two-dice grid in Spinner Fair", `found`: "the two-dice grid showed seven has the most ways". Nothing else in levels 6–8 is kept or filed.

### Level 8, The typical spin

- **The number wheel.** A six-wedge wheel with the numbers 1–6 once each (a `NumWheel = readonly number[]`, a permutation of 1–6, seeded order so it does not always read clockwise). Each wedge shows its **numeral and its pips** (a die face), in six palette colours, and each landing is spoken ("Four!"). `spin(rng)` picks the wedge as everywhere else, so each number is exactly a sixth. `Wheel` (critters) stays as it is; the numbered wheel is its own type with its own art function.
- **The towers.** Six spins she taps; spin *i* stands as tower *i* (left to right) of as many blocks as the number, each block showing a pip. Towers are 110 wide with blocks 45 high (six blocks is 270).
- **Range first.** When the six spins stand: the pet points at the tallest tower and the shortest (both glow; on a tie, the leftmost of each), the gap above the shortest shows as dashed outline blocks up to the tallest's height, and "How far apart are the biggest and smallest spins?" on three number cards: the range and two other numbers in 0–5 (seeded, distinct). Right is `max − min`; then "Six take away two is four. That's the range." If every spin is the same, the range is 0, the gap is empty and 0 is among the cards.
- **Levelling.** "Now share the blocks out so the towers are as level as they can be." She drags from a tower (its whole column is the grab area; the drag takes its top block) onto another tower's column (`drag.ts`). A move onto a tower at least as tall as the one it left is allowed and spoken ("That tower got taller"), **not a miss**; the second such move in a set glows the tallest and shortest towers (counted as the set's hint, as in Lemonade Stand). When the towers are **nearly level** (`max − min ≤ 1`) a level line glows across them and the question follows; already nearly level after the range, the step is skipped ("They're already about level!").
- **The typical spin.** "If every spin were the same, what would it be?" on three number cards from 1–6. Right is any height the nearly level towers show: `floor(mean)`, and also `ceil(mean)` when the mean is not whole (both cards can be among the three; tapping either is right). Spoken: whole: "Exactly 3. That's the typical spin, the mean."; not whole: "About 3. Some towers are 3 and some are 4: the typical spin is between."
- **Helpers.** `numWheel(rng)`; `towersOf(spins)`; `range(towers)`; `rangeCards(rng, r)`; `moveBlock(towers, from, to)` (keeps the total; refuses an empty source); `tallerMove(towers, from, to)`; `nearlyLevel(towers)`; `typicalAccepted(towers)` (the set of right answers: one or two heights); `typicalCards(rng, towers)` (three distinct numbers including every accepted height); `nextMove(towers)` (tallest to shortest, for the hint and the bot).

## Accounting, help and finishing

| Level | Counts a miss | Counts a hint | Never counted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 6 | an *about* card that is not `10 · k` | the glow after the second miss in a set, or asked-for help; at most one per set | every spin and run, the check run's count and whether it is *unusual*, the fast-forward |
| 7 | a sum card that is not right (each question) | the same, at most one per question | the rolls and what they showed, taps during the grid |
| 8 | a wrong range card; a wrong typical card | the same, at most one per set; the second *taller* move counts as that set's one hint | spins, every block move (either way), the order she levels in |

- **Spoken reasons.** Level 6: "Look at Duck's share of the wheel: how many sixths?" Level 7: "Count the squares that make each sum." Level 8: "Count the outline blocks" (range); "Look at the level line" (typical).
- **Help.** After the second miss, or on the pause sheet's *Show me* (`askForHint`, as levels 1–5): level 6 glows Duck's wedges and the strip's marker and the card at that share; level 7 glows the grid squares of each card's sum (the diagonals) and then the card with the most; level 8 glows the outline gap (range), the move `nextMove` names (levelling), or the level line and the cards it shows (typical). Asked again while the glow is on, the ghost finger makes the move: the supported finish, still one hint. No hint ever names a spin's or roll's outcome as the answer.
- **Finishing.** The round ends after the last question (level 6: the second set's check run; level 7: question two; level 8: the second set's typical card) with `ctx.finish({ misses, hints, discoveries })` (discoveries only at level 7) and one ordinary sticker, help or not. A run, a roll or a tower never gives, takes or unlocks anything.
- **Adaptation.** As levels 1–5: two smooth rounds step up, two struggling rounds step down, clamped to school's 3–8. Nothing claims she understands the law of large numbers or the mean; it claims she matched a share to *about* a count, counted squares for a sum and levelled towers to read a typical value and a range.

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Plans and bands:** levels 6–8 exist with the asks above; `school` is 3–8; levels 1–5 unchanged (their tests stand).
- **Level 6:** each set's wheel has six wedges, two critters, Duck's `k ∈ {1, 3, 5}`; a round's two `k` differ; `expected60(k) = 10k` and exactly one card is right; over 2 000 seeds, each set's 240 spins have Duck within 0.08 of `k/6` in at least 97% of sets (about 2.5 standard deviations at `k = 3`) (honesty, measured, never enforced); over 2 000 seeds the 60-spin check run is `inBand` in at least 97% of sets for each `k` (about 2.6 standard deviations at `k = 3`; it documents how often *unusual* is said); the generator draws spins only through `spin(rng)` and never re-draws.
- **Level 7:** `ways(s)` is 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1 for 2–12 and sums to 36; `gridRows()` matches `ways`; `mostCards` always holds 7 and two other sums with different `ways`, each fewer than 6; `pairCards` never holds 7 or a tie; over 60 000 rolls each face is within 1 point of a sixth (honest dice).
- **Level 8:** `numWheel` is a permutation of 1–6; `range` is `max − min`; `rangeCards` holds the range and two other distinct values in 0–5; `moveBlock` keeps the total and never leaves a tower below zero; from every tower set of six spins, repeated `nextMove` reaches `nearlyLevel` in at most `sum(|h − mean|)` moves and never makes a *taller* move; `typicalAccepted` is `{floor(mean)}` when the mean is whole and `{floor(mean), ceil(mean)}` otherwise, and equals the set of heights the nearly level towers show; `typicalCards` includes every accepted height and three distinct values in 1–6.
- **Help:** every glow names a wedge, the marker, a card, a grid square, a tower, a move or the level line, never a spin's or roll's outcome; following it from any state reaches a right answer; at most one hint per set or question.
- **Journal:** `spinner-fair:two-dice` exists with its line and source; level 7 reports it once per round when the grid completes, whatever the rolls; levels 6 and 8 report nothing.
- **Layout:** each level's layout at the lands test's four views: every card, button, the spinner, the dice tray and each tower column at least 100 units; nothing under the pet or corner buttons; portrait stacks rather than shrinks; the level-7 grid and both bar sets fit without overlapping the cards.

## Open decisions

Each has a default the builds use; none blocks CS2a.

1. **Level 6's wheel shares.** Default `k ∈ {1, 3, 5}` (about 10, 30, 50 of 60). Two or four wedges (about 20 or 40) are a harder later variant.
2. **Level 6's check run.** Default: 60 spins after the question, never judged. Dropping it shortens the round; it is not a rule.
3. **Level 7's rolls.** Default twelve. More rolls bring the roll bars closer to the ways bars but lengthen the level.
4. **Level 8's spins.** Default six (six towers fit a phone). Eight would need narrower columns, which the target rule forbids.
5. **"About" for a non-whole mean.** Default: both neighbouring heights accepted. The alternative (a half-block share) is England Year 6's decimal mean and belongs to a later level, not this one.
