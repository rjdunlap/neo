# Water Road, levels 1–3 (definition)

Defined 2026-10-27 as slice AM1 of the [ancient-mediterranean study](../deep-dives/subjects/ancient-mediterranean.md#build-slices-in-order) ([packet](../deep-dives/subjects/RECOMMENDATIONS.md#greece-and-rome-recommendations)), [PR #1018](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1018). The study's planning labels 1, 2 and 3 become saved levels **1, 2 and 3**; levels 4–8 (AM3, AM4) are appended later and never below a saved one. Decisions 1–2 were taken 2026-10-23 on the developer's delegation: island names on the child's screen ("long ago, by the middle sea"; no Athens, Rome, date or name below the grown-up card), Tinker Lab if the lands test passes (else Storybook Square when it exists), and a first build of these three levels only. **Nothing is built.** AM2 builds levels 1–3 as one [batch](../GAME-BATCHES.md#water-road-1-keystone-which-one-stands-and-hold-it-up); the water levels (AM3, AM4) and The Water Commissioner (AM5) are separate definitions.

**Fun action and purpose:** close a ring of stones and pull the frame away. She taps and a stone flies onto the wooden frame; the last one is the keystone and it chimes; she pulls the frame and the arch stands on its own while the pet walks through. Then she pulls two frames and sees that the ring with a gap falls, and says which will stand before pulling. Then she puts the buttress block where the arch has nothing to push against, and pulls. Every level ends with **an arch standing with nothing under it**. Learning goal, in one stated rule the pet says and the card prints: *a ring that is closed and pushed back at both ends stands.* Level 1 is the closed ring; level 2 is closed against open; level 3 is pushed back at both ends. No water until level 4; no physics engine; the outcome of every pull is decided by that rule and shown by a tumble animation.

## Overlap and reuse

- **Identity check** (the study's condition): a new activity is justified only if *the closed ring* is central. It is: level 1's stones take their own places so that the ring closes from both ends and the pull is the payoff; level 2's whole decision is closed or open; level 3's is where the push goes. **Block Tower** owns stacking with its centre-over-the-block balance rule (`topples`), and its "tap anywhere to stack" lap levels are a column, not a ring: nothing here balances, and no stone's position is hers to choose. If AM2 finds level 1 is Block Tower with wedge stones, level 1 becomes a short Block Tower entry and this packet's levels 2–3 are withdrawn rather than built thin; the medieval-life study's *Keystone* (ML6) is the same ring and reuses this packet's `stands` rule rather than a second one.
- **Sink or Float** owns a prediction that is never a miss until the last asked one; level 2 follows its rule exactly. **Seesaw Balance** 17 (*predict the tip before release*) owns the asked prediction before a commit; the asked pair is the same shape. **Chain Reaction** owns parts moved before a run; level 3's free dragging before the pull is that pattern. **Rain Garden** and **Welcome Ways** are the water and ramp boundaries for AM3, not here.
- Reuse `onTap` for the stage, the frames and the pull handle; `draggable` from `src/engine/drag.ts` for the buttress block (`onDrop` returns true when a slot takes it; a refused drop floats home); Helper House's scene-owned step player for the stone flights, the pull, the tumble and the pet's walk through; Duck Pond's pattern for one big target that reacts on touch-down. Stones, frame, walls and block are code-drawn in `src/art/` (pale stone, timber and a terracotta ground from `src/art/palette.ts`; no sepia filter). No new engine, save field, render texture or shared helper.
- **History on screen:** the island's own stones and frame. One historical line (below) is checked against the arch and aqueduct pages the study opened in the building session, as the 2026-10-23 decision replaced the named reviewer. Nothing says who invented the arch; nothing names a people or a city at these levels.

## The model (`logic.ts`)

One rule decides every level: `stands(arch) = arch.closed && arch.left.held && arch.right.held`. An arch is `{ stones, gap, left, right }`: `stones` is its seeded count, `gap` is `null` or the index of the missing stone, each side is `held` when a low wall or a placed block is there. Three plans, each a finite seeded draw with `ctx.rng`; cosmetic draws (stone tints, which side flies first, the pet's side) never change a rule. No physics, no timer: every pull waits for her.

**The ring:** stones are placed in a fixed order from the two ends inward, left springer, right springer, then alternating, so the keystone is always the last place. `placeOrder(count)` lists the indices; `count` is odd so there is exactly one keystone.

### Level 1, Keystone (`keystone`)

- **Plan:** a wooden frame between two low walls (both sides `held`) and a pile of stones. `stones` 3 at toddler, 5 or 7 at preschool (the plan takes the band). State: `placed` (how many), `pulled`.
- **Flow:** `tapPile()`: any tap on the stage outside the pet and corner buttons lifts the next stone on touch-down and it flies to `placeOrder(stones)[placed]`, left then right, with a pentatonic step up per stone and the count spoken ("one", "two", …); the last one lands with the keystone chime and the pet says "The keystone!". Taps while a stone is flying queue the next (never lost). Once `placed === stones` the pile is empty, the frame's handle glows gently and the pet asks her to pull; `pullFrame()`: the frame slides out with a creak, the arch holds with a low hum, the pet walks through and the round finishes. At toddler any tap pulls once the ring is closed (the pile is empty, so nothing else is asked); at preschool the handle or the frame is the target, and if no tap comes for about six seconds the handle glows brighter (entry guidance, recorded nowhere).
- **Invariants:** exactly `stones` taps close the ring from any state; `pullFrame()` before closure does nothing (the frame wobbles, free); the ring is always closed when pulled, so `stands` is always true here.
- **Accounting:** nothing counts. `misses` and `hints` are always 0.
- **Rounds:** one arch per round.

### Level 2, Which one stands? (`pairs`)

- **Plan:** three pairs per round. A pair is two arches on frames side by side between low walls (both sides `held` on both), of the same seeded count (5 at preschool, 5 or 7 at pre-K); exactly one has `gap === null`. The gap is the keystone at preschool; at pre-K the seed draws the keystone or a shoulder stone (never a springer), so she scans the whole ring rather than the top. Which side holds the closed ring is seeded. Pairs 1–2 are **free**; pair 3 is **asked**. State: `pulled` (per frame), `asked`, `misses`, `hint`.
- **Flow, free pairs:** tap either frame to pull it. The closed ring holds and hums; the open ring's stones slide softly to the ground (the tumble animation) and the pet says what happened in the gap's terms ("No top stone, so nothing held the ring" / "A stone was missing on the left, so the ring came apart"). Pulling the other frame is the rest of the pair; after both are pulled the fallen stones climb back onto fresh frames and the next pair appears. The pet never says "wrong"; a pull is a prediction (Sink or Float's rule).
- **Flow, the asked pair:** both arches wait on their frames and the pet asks "Which one will stand?" `predict(side)`: tapping the closed ring pulls both frames together, hers stands, the other tumbles, and the pair finishes. Tapping the open ring is one **miss**: its gap blinks, the pet explains ("That one has a gap at the top; look for a ring with no gap"), and both stay on their frames for another tap. When the asked pair's misses reach two, the closed ring **glows** and `hints++` once; the glow stays until she taps it, so following it always finishes. Asked-for help in a free pair blinks the gap and repeats the instruction (nothing is asked, nothing counts); in the asked pair it is the same glow and the same single count.
- **Invariants:** `explain(arch)` names the gap's place and is defined exactly when `gap !== null`; exactly one ring per pair has `stands === true`; the asked pair finishes on the first tap of the closed ring from any state.
- **Finish:** the asked pair's pull. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`.

### Level 3, Hold it up (`buttress`)

- **Plan:** two arches per round, one at a time. Each is a closed ring (`gap === null`, 5 or 7 stones) on a frame; the seed sets `left.held` and `right.held` from a low wall: at pre-K exactly one side is walled; at school the second arch may also draw **both** walled (so the decision includes "it needs nothing"). A buttress block waits in a tray below; two slots, one at each foot of the arch, take it. Faint push arrows show on each unheld side while a finger is down on the arch. State: `block` (`tray`, `left` or `right`), `misses`, `hint`.
- **Flow:** drag the block to either slot (a slot sets that side `held`; the block can be dragged again to the other slot or back to the tray, all free; a drop anywhere else floats home, free). `pull()`: the one commit, the frame's handle at least 100 units and always enabled. If `stands(arch)`: the frame slides out, the arch holds, the pet says "Pushed back at both ends!" and, on the second arch, walks through to finish. Otherwise the ring **spreads**: the stones slide outward and down on the unheld side, the push arrow on that side shows for a moment, the block floats home, the stones climb back onto the frame, and that is one **miss**, explained ("Nothing pushed back on the right, so the ring spread"). A pull with no block placed and a side open is the same miss (the pull is the commit; moving the block first is what the level asks). When one arch's misses reach two, the unheld side's slot **glows** and `hints++` once for that arch; the glow stays until the block is there and the arch pulled, so following it always finishes. Asked-for help is the same glow and the same single count per arch. On a both-walled arch nothing can miss; placing the block anyway is free and the pull stands.
- **Invariants:** `stands` decides every seed: with the block on the unheld side (or no unheld side) the pull stands, otherwise it spreads; `hintFor(arch)` returns the unheld side, or `null` when both are held; every seed has at most one unheld side, so one block always suffices.
- **Finish:** the second arch's standing pull. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`.

### Bands and ranges

`BandLevels`: `toddler: { min: 1, max: 1 }`, `preschool: { min: 1, max: 2 }`, `prek: { min: 2, max: 3 }`, `school: { min: 3, max: 3 }`. No lap window in this slice (open decision 1). School's single level is the deliberate start of the study's 3–8 window so that AM3 appends 4–5 without renumbering; a school child who reaches Water Road today plays level 3 and the pin stays valid afterwards. No `order`.

## Screen and controls

- **Stage.** Landscape: the frame (level 1), the two frames (level 2) or the one frame with its slots (level 3) centred across the upper half on a terracotta ground line, the pile or tray below, the pet bottom-left and corner buttons clear. Portrait keeps the frame or frames across the upper half and the pile or tray below them, as the study asks; two level-2 frames stack side by side at a smaller stage width only until each is still at least 260 units wide, otherwise one above the other. Every input is at least 100 logical units after scaling: wedge stones drawn 110 × 90 with a 110 hit box (never themselves targets at level 1, where the whole stage is the target), each level-2 frame 300 × 260, the pull handle 140 × 110 (off the pet), the buttress block 120 × 120, each slot 140 × 140, Help in its corner. Walls, the ground and the sky are `eventMode = 'none'`.
- **Touch-down feedback.** A stone lifts off the pile the instant a finger lands; a frame darkens and its handle dips under a finger; the block lifts and rides 40 units above the finger; a slot brightens under a held block; the push arrows fade in while the arch is touched. A refused drop still shows that it was felt (the block floats home with a small bounce).
- **Speech.** `ctx.instruct` states the level's one ask ("Tap, and the stones fly into a ring; then pull the frame" / "Pull a frame and see which ring stands" / "Put the block where the arch has nothing to push on, then pull the frame"); the pet repeats it. The asked pair's question and the stated rule are fixed lines.
- **Feel.** `STYLES.stickers` (Gadget Bench and the Tinker Lab builders use it; she is not making music); a pentatonic step up per stone from `src/audio/notes.ts`, the keystone's chime, a wooden creak for the pull, a low hum when the arch holds, a soft stone slide for a tumble. Reduced motion places stones and shows outcomes in place without the flights and the tumble.
- **The one history line**, spoken once at the start of levels 2–3 and printed on the card: "Long ago, by the middle sea, people built rings of stone like this one; some of them are still standing." It names no people, city, date or inventor, matches the arch and aqueduct pages the study opened (true arches stood across that world long before and after any one city), and is checked again in the building session.

## Accounting, help and finishing

| Level | Counts a miss | Counts a hint | Never counted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | nothing | nothing | every tap, a pull before the ring is closed, the idle glow |
| 2 | each tap of the open ring in the asked pair | the glow when the asked pair's misses reach two, or asked-for help there; at most one per round | every pull in a free pair, help in a free pair, hearing the instruction again |
| 3 | each pull that spreads | the glow when one arch's misses reach two, or asked-for help; at most one per arch | dragging the block anywhere before the pull, returning it to the tray, touching the arch to see the arrows |

- **Supported finish:** at level 2 the glow leads to the closed ring and at level 3 to the unheld side, so every round ends with one ordinary sticker through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`, help or not. Level 1 cannot fail. No creation, discovery or journal entry in the first build (an arch on the creature home's hill is the study's RW05 proposal, not this slice). Leaving early owes nothing.
- **Adaptation:** level 1 is always smooth (two rounds step up). At level 2 one wrong tap in the asked pair is still smooth; at level 3 one spread per round is smooth. Two hints or four misses are struggling; a child who spreads both arches twice steps down to level 2's pairs. Assisted completion is one sticker, not evidence of mastery. Nothing here claims a four-year-old understands thrust; it claims she pulled the frame, watched what held and heard the rule.

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Plans:** over 300 seeds per level, level 1 draws 3 stones at toddler and 5 or 7 at preschool, always odd, both sides held; level 2 draws three pairs of equal odd counts with exactly one `gap === null` per pair, the gap at the keystone at preschool and at the keystone or a shoulder (never a springer) at pre-K, and both sides held; level 3 draws two closed arches with exactly one unheld side at pre-K and at most one at school. The same seed gives the same plan.
- **The ring:** `placeOrder(count)` is a permutation of `0..count-1`, alternates ends inward and ends at the keystone index; `stands` is total and true exactly when closed and held on both sides.
- **Level 1:** exactly `stones` taps close the ring; `pullFrame()` before closure changes nothing; `misses` and `hints` stay 0 through any tap sequence.
- **Level 2:** `explain(arch)` is defined iff `gap !== null` and names its place (top, left shoulder, right shoulder); free pulls change no count; in the asked pair `predict(open)` is one miss and `predict(closed)` finishes; the hint counts at most once and the glow is on the closed ring; from any state the glowed ring finishes.
- **Level 3:** `stands` after `place(side)` is true iff the placed side was the unheld one or no side was unheld; a spreading pull is one miss and leaves the block in the tray; `hintFor` returns the unheld side or null; an arch's hint counts at most once; the bot's placement stands for every seed.
- **Layout:** `waterRoadLayout(level, view)` at the four views the lands test uses (landscape, portrait, two phone insets): every input at least 100 units, nothing under the bottom-left pet or corner buttons, level 2's frames side by side or stacked rather than shrunk, the slots and tray never under the arch.
- **Registry, lands, how-to and off-screen tests** pass with the new game: Tinker Lab pages at `LAND_SPOTS` (19 games today, 20 with this one), the card scoped per level, no "later levels" wording.

## Open decisions

Each has a default the build uses; none blocks AM2.

1. **A lap window.** Default: none; toddler 1 is the first rung, as the study's windows say. The alternative (`lap: { 1, 1 }` with three stones and any tap pulling) is one range line and no new interaction; add it if the building session finds the toddler round plays like Peekaboo Barn's.
2. **Pre-K and level 3.** Default: pre-K 2–3, one level beyond the study's tentative "pre-K 2 and 4", so pre-K has two rungs now and AM3's `prek: { 2, 4 }` stays contiguous. The alternative (pre-K 2 only) leaves a one-level window until the water levels.
3. **Where the gap is.** Default: the keystone at preschool, keystone or shoulder at pre-K; never a springer, which sits on the wall and reads as a wall stone. The alternative (keystone only at both) keeps the study's wording exactly but makes pre-K the same round twice.
4. **Both-walled arches at school.** Default: the second arch at school may need nothing, so "does it need the block?" is a real decision. The alternative (always one open side) is the study's wording and makes the block a formality.
5. **Three pairs at level 2.** Default: two free pairs and one asked, read from the study's "two pairs a round" plus "one last pair asks". The alternative (one free pair and one asked) is shorter for a three-year-old; the building session may take it if the round runs past about ninety seconds.
6. **The history line.** Default: the single sentence above, source-checked in the building session against the arch and aqueduct pages the study opened. If the check wants it shorter, it becomes "Long ago, by the middle sea, people built rings of stone like this one."
7. **Land.** Default: Tinker Lab (20th game, two pages). Fallback by decision 2: Storybook Square when it exists.
