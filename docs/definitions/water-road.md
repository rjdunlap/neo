# Water Road, levels 1–3 (definition)

Defined 2026-10-27 as slice AM1 of the [ancient-mediterranean study](../deep-dives/subjects/ancient-mediterranean.md#build-slices-in-order) ([packet](../deep-dives/subjects/RECOMMENDATIONS.md#greece-and-rome-recommendations)), [PR #1018](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1018). The study's planning labels 1, 2 and 3 become saved levels **1, 2 and 3**; levels 4–8 (AM3, AM4) are appended later and never below a saved one. Decisions 1–2 were taken 2026-10-23 on the developer's delegation: island names on the child's screen ("long ago, by the middle sea"; no Athens, Rome, date or name below the grown-up card), Tinker Lab if the lands test passes (else Storybook Square when it exists), and a first build of these three levels only. Levels 1–3 were built 2026-10-27 ([PR #1022](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1022)); levels 4–5 are defined [below](#levels-45-am3-downhill-all-the-way-over-the-valley) (AM3, 2026-10-31) and not built. AM2 built levels 1–3 as one [batch](../GAME-BATCHES.md#water-road-1-keystone-which-one-stands-and-hold-it-up); the water levels (AM3, AM4) and The Water Commissioner (AM5) are separate definitions.

**Fun action and purpose:** close a ring of stones and pull the frame away. She taps and a stone flies onto the wooden frame; the last one is the keystone and it chimes; she pulls the frame and the arch stands on its own while the pet walks through. Then she pulls two frames and sees that the ring with a gap falls, and says which will stand before pulling. Then she puts the buttress block where the arch has nothing to push against, and pulls. Every level ends with **an arch standing with nothing under it**. Learning goal, in one stated rule the pet says and the card prints: *a ring that is closed and pushed back at both ends stands.* Level 1 is the closed ring; level 2 is closed against open; level 3 is pushed back at both ends. No water until level 4; no physics engine; the outcome of every pull is decided by that rule and shown by a tumble animation.

## Overlap and reuse

- **Identity check** (the study's condition): a new activity is justified only if *the closed ring* is central. It is: level 1's stones take their own places so that the ring closes from both ends and the pull is the payoff; level 2's whole decision is closed or open; level 3's is where the push goes. **Block Tower** owns stacking with its centre-over-the-block balance rule (`topples`), and its "tap anywhere to stack" lap levels are a column, not a ring: nothing here balances, and no stone's position is hers to choose. If AM2 finds level 1 is Block Tower with wedge stones, level 1 becomes a short Block Tower entry and this packet's levels 2–3 are withdrawn rather than built thin; the medieval-life study's *Keystone* (ML6) is the same ring and reuses this packet's `stands` rule rather than a second one.
- **Sink or Float** owns a prediction that is never a miss until the last asked one; level 2 follows its rule exactly. **Seesaw Balance** 17 (*predict the tip before release*) owns the asked prediction before a commit; the asked pair is the same shape. **Chain Reaction** owns parts moved before a run; level 3's free dragging before the pull is that pattern. **Rain Garden** and **Welcome Ways** are the water and ramp boundaries for AM3, not here.
- Reuse `onTap` for the stage, the frames and the pull handle; `draggable` from `src/engine/drag.ts` for the buttress block (`onDrop` returns true when a slot takes it; a refused drop floats home); Helper House's scene-owned step player for the stone flights, the pull, the tumble and the pet's walk through; Duck Pond's pattern for one big target that reacts on touch-down. Stones, frame, walls and block are code-drawn in `src/art/` (pale stone, timber and a terracotta ground from `src/art/palette.ts`; no sepia filter). No new engine, save field, render texture or shared helper.
- **History on screen:** the island's own stones and frame. One historical line (below) is checked against the arch and aqueduct pages the study opened in the building session, as the 2026-10-23 decision replaced the named reviewer. Nothing says who invented the arch; nothing names a people or a city at these levels.

## The model (`logic.ts`)

One rule decides every level: `stands(arch) = arch.closed && arch.left.held && arch.right.held`. An arch is `{ stones, gap, left, right }`: `stones` is its seeded count, `gap` is `null` or the index of the missing stone, each side is `held` when a low wall or a placed block is there. Three plans, each a finite seeded draw with `ctx.rng`; cosmetic draws (stone tints, which side flies first, the pet's side) never change a rule. No physics, no timer: every pull waits for her.

**The ring:** stones are placed in a fixed order from the two ends inward, left springer, right springer, then alternating, so the keystone is always the last place. `placeOrder(count)` lists the indices; `count` is odd so there is exactly one keystone.

### Level 1, Keystone (`keystone`)

- **Plan:** a wooden frame between two low walls (both sides `held`) and a pile of stones. `stones` 3 at toddler, 5 or 7 at preschool (the plan takes the band). State: `placed` (how many), `pulled`.
- **Flow:** `tapPile()`: any tap on the stage outside the pet and corner buttons lifts the next stone on touch-down and it flies to `placeOrder(stones)[placed]`, left then right, with a pentatonic step up per stone and the count spoken ("one", "two", …); the last one lands with the keystone chime and the pet says "The keystone!". Taps while a stone is flying queue the next (never lost). Once `placed === stones` the pile is empty, the frame's handle glows gently and the pet asks her to pull; `pullFrame()`: the frame slides out with a creak, the arch holds with a low hum, the pet walks through and the round finishes. At toddler any tap pulls once the ring is closed (the pile is empty, so nothing else is asked); at preschool the handle or the frame is the target, and if no tap comes for about six seconds the handle glows brighter (entry guidance, recorded nowhere).
- **Invariants:** exactly `stones` taps close the ring from any state; `pullFrame()` before closure does nothing (the frame wobbles, free); the ring is always closed when pulled, so `stands` is always true here.
- **Accounting:** nothing counts. `misses` and `hints` are always 0.
- **Rounds:** one arch per round.

### Level 2, Which one stands? (`pairs`)

- **Plan:** three pairs per round. A pair is two arches on frames side by side between low walls (both sides `held` on both), of the same seeded count (5 at preschool, 5 or 7 at pre-K); exactly one has `gap === null`. The gap is the keystone at preschool; at pre-K the seed draws the keystone or a shoulder stone (never a springer), so she scans the whole ring rather than the top. Which side holds the closed ring is seeded. Pairs 1–2 are **free**; pair 3 is **asked**. State: `pulled` (per frame), `asked`, `misses`, `hint`.
- **Flow, free pairs:** tap either frame to pull it. The closed ring holds and hums; the open ring's stones slide softly to the ground (the tumble animation) and the pet says what happened in the gap's terms ("No top stone, so nothing held the ring" / "A stone was missing on the left, so the ring came apart"). Pulling the other frame is the rest of the pair; after both are pulled the fallen stones climb back onto fresh frames and the next pair appears. The pet never says "wrong"; a pull is a prediction (Sink or Float's rule).
- **Flow, the asked pair:** both arches wait on their frames and the pet asks "Which one will stand?" `predict(side)`: tapping the closed ring pulls both frames together, hers stands, the other tumbles, and the pair finishes. Tapping the open ring is one **miss**: its gap blinks, the pet explains ("That one has a gap at the top; look for a ring with no gap"), and both stay on their frames for another tap. When the asked pair's misses reach two, the closed ring **glows** and `hints++` once; the glow stays until she taps it, so following it always finishes. Asked-for help in a free pair blinks the gap and repeats the instruction (nothing is asked, nothing counts); in the asked pair it is the same glow and the same single count.
- **Invariants:** `explain(arch)` names the gap's place and is defined exactly when `gap !== null`; exactly one ring per pair has `stands === true`; the asked pair finishes on the first tap of the closed ring from any state.
- **Finish:** the asked pair's pull. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`.

### Level 3, Hold it up (`buttress`)

- **Plan:** two arches per round, one at a time. Each is a closed ring (`gap === null`, 5 or 7 stones) on a frame; the seed sets `left.held` and `right.held` from a low wall: at pre-K exactly one side is walled; at school the second arch may also draw **both** walled (so the decision includes "it needs nothing"). A buttress block waits in a tray below; two slots, one at each foot of the arch, take it. Faint push arrows show on each unheld side while a finger is down on the arch. State: `block` (`tray`, `left` or `right`), `misses`, `hint`.
- **Flow:** drag the block to either slot (a slot sets that side `held`; the block can be dragged again to the other slot or back to the tray, all free; a drop anywhere else floats home, free). `pull()`: the one commit, the frame's handle at least 100 units and always enabled. If `stands(arch)`: the frame slides out, the arch holds, the pet says "Pushed back at both ends!" and, on the second arch, walks through to finish. Otherwise the ring **spreads**: the stones slide outward and down on the unheld side, the push arrow on that side shows for a moment, the block floats home, the stones climb back onto the frame, and that is one **miss**, explained ("Nothing pushed back on the right, so the ring spread"). A pull with no block placed and a side open is the same miss (the pull is the commit; moving the block first is what the level asks). When one arch's misses reach two, the unheld side's slot **glows** and `hints++` once for that arch; the glow stays until the block is there and the arch pulled, so following it always finishes. Asked-for help is the same glow and the same single count per arch. On a both-walled arch nothing can miss; placing the block anyway is free and the pull stands.
- **Invariants:** `stands` decides every seed: with the block on the unheld side (or no unheld side) the pull stands, otherwise it spreads; `hintFor(arch)` returns the unheld side, or `null` when both are held; every seed has at most one unheld side, so one block always suffices.
- **Finish:** the second arch's standing pull. `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`.

### Bands and ranges

`BandLevels`: `toddler: { min: 1, max: 1 }`, `preschool: { min: 1, max: 2 }`, `prek: { min: 2, max: 3 }`, `school: { min: 3, max: 3 }`. No lap window in this slice (open decision 1). School's single level is the deliberate start of the study's 3–8 window so that AM3 appends 4–5 without renumbering; a school child who reaches Water Road today plays level 3 and the pin stays valid afterwards. No `order`.

## Screen and controls

- **Stage.** Landscape: the frame (level 1), the two frames (level 2) or the one frame with its slots (level 3) centred across the upper half on a terracotta ground line, the pile or tray below, the pet bottom-left and corner buttons clear. Portrait keeps the frame or frames across the upper half and the pile or tray below them, as the study asks; two level-2 frames stack side by side at a smaller stage width only until each is still at least 260 units wide, otherwise one above the other. Every input is at least 100 logical units after scaling: wedge stones drawn 110 × 90 with a 110 hit box (never themselves targets at level 1, where the whole stage is the target), each level-2 frame 300 × 260, the pull handle 140 × 110 (off the pet), the buttress block 120 × 120, each slot 140 × 140, Help in its corner. Walls, the ground and the sky are `eventMode = 'none'`.
- **Touch-down feedback.** A stone lifts off the pile the instant a finger lands; a frame darkens and its handle dips under a finger; the block lifts and rides 40 units above the finger; a slot brightens under a held block; the push arrows fade in while the arch is touched. A refused drop still shows that it was felt (the block floats home with a small bounce).
- **Speech.** `ctx.instruct` states the level's one ask ("Tap, and the stones fly into a ring; then pull the frame" / "Pull a frame and see which ring stands" / "Put the block where the arch has nothing to push on, then pull the frame"); the pet repeats it. The asked pair's question and the stated rule are fixed lines.
- **Feel.** `STYLES.stickers` (Gadget Bench and the Tinker Lab builders use it; she is not making music); a pentatonic step up per stone from `src/audio/notes.ts`, the keystone's chime, a wooden creak for the pull, a low hum when the arch holds, a soft stone slide for a tumble. Reduced motion places stones and shows outcomes in place without the flights and the tumble.
- **The one history line**, spoken once at the start of levels 2–3 and printed on the card: "Long ago, by the middle sea, people built rings of stone like this one; some of them are still standing." It names no people, city, date or inventor, matches the arch and aqueduct pages the study opened (true arches stood across that world long before and after any one city), and is checked again in the building session.

## Accounting, help and finishing

| Level | Counts a miss | Counts a hint | Never counted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | nothing | nothing | every tap, a pull before the ring is closed, the idle glow |
| 2 | each tap of the open ring in the asked pair | the glow when the asked pair's misses reach two, or asked-for help there; at most one per round | every pull in a free pair, help in a free pair, hearing the instruction again |
| 3 | each pull that spreads | the glow when one arch's misses reach two, or asked-for help; at most one per arch | dragging the block anywhere before the pull, returning it to the tray, touching the arch to see the arrows |

- **Supported finish:** at level 2 the glow leads to the closed ring and at level 3 to the unheld side, so every round ends with one ordinary sticker through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`, help or not. Level 1 cannot fail. No creation, discovery or journal entry in the first build (an arch on the creature home's hill is the study's RW05 proposal, not this slice). Leaving early owes nothing.
- **Adaptation:** level 1 is always smooth (two rounds step up). At level 2 one wrong tap in the asked pair is still smooth; at level 3 one spread per round is smooth. Two hints or four misses are struggling; a child who spreads both arches twice steps down to level 2's pairs. Assisted completion is one sticker, not evidence of mastery. Nothing here claims a four-year-old understands thrust; it claims she pulled the frame, watched what held and heard the rule.

## Rule tests (`logic.test.ts`)

- **Plans:** over 300 seeds per level, level 1 draws 3 stones at toddler and 5 or 7 at preschool, always odd, both sides held; level 2 draws three pairs of equal odd counts with exactly one `gap === null` per pair, the gap at the keystone at preschool and at the keystone or a shoulder (never a springer) at pre-K, and both sides held; level 3 draws two closed arches with exactly one unheld side at pre-K and at most one at school. The same seed gives the same plan.
- **The ring:** `placeOrder(count)` is a permutation of `0..count-1`, alternates ends inward and ends at the keystone index; `stands` is total and true exactly when closed and held on both sides.
- **Level 1:** exactly `stones` taps close the ring; `pullFrame()` before closure changes nothing; `misses` and `hints` stay 0 through any tap sequence.
- **Level 2:** `explain(arch)` is defined iff `gap !== null` and names its place (top, left shoulder, right shoulder); free pulls change no count; in the asked pair `predict(open)` is one miss and `predict(closed)` finishes; the hint counts at most once and the glow is on the closed ring; from any state the glowed ring finishes.
- **Level 3:** `stands` after `place(side)` is true iff the placed side was the unheld one or no side was unheld; a spreading pull is one miss and leaves the block in the tray; `hintFor` returns the unheld side or null; an arch's hint counts at most once; the bot's placement stands for every seed.
- **Layout:** `waterRoadLayout(level, view)` at the four views the lands test uses (landscape, portrait, two phone insets): every input at least 100 units, nothing under the bottom-left pet or corner buttons, level 2's frames side by side or stacked rather than shrunk, the slots and tray never under the arch.
- **Registry, lands, how-to and off-screen tests** pass with the new game: Tinker Lab pages at `LAND_SPOTS` (19 games today, 20 with this one), the card scoped per level, no "later levels" wording.

## Levels 4–5 (AM3): downhill all the way, over the valley

Defined 2026-10-31 as slice AM3 of the [ancient-mediterranean study](../deep-dives/subjects/ancient-mediterranean.md#build-slices-in-order), from its ladder's rows 4 and 5. The study's planning labels 4 and 5 become saved levels **4 and 5**, appended after the built 1–3; nothing below them is renumbered and every existing pin stays valid. **Nothing in this section is built.** One build batch (water-road 2) builds both levels; levels 6–8 (AM4) and The Water Commissioner (AM5) stay separate definitions.

**Fun action and purpose:** water arrives at last. A spring bubbles on a hill at the left, a fountain waits in a little town at the right, and a blue channel runs between them on stone piers. She sets the piers (or, at pre-K, picks the whole channel), taps **Open the spring**, and watches the water run stretch by stretch; where a stretch goes up or lies flat the water stops and pools there, and where every stretch goes down it reaches the town and the fountain splashes. Then the road meets a valley: she drags arches across on their piers and an abutment block where an end has nothing to push on, opens the spring, and the channel runs over the arches to the town. Learning goal, in two stated rules the pet says and the card prints: *water runs only where every stretch goes down* (level 4) and *each arch is pushed back on both sides, by its neighbour or by the end* (level 5, the level-3 rule extended to a row). Every level ends with **the fountain running**.

### Levels 4–5: overlap and the identity check

- **The study's identity condition** is that *water that must fall* is central. It is: level 4's only decision is the height of each stretch against its neighbours, and level 5's arcade exists to carry that falling channel across a gap. Nothing soaks, stores, routes or counts drops.
- **Rain Garden** (defined 1–3, [definition](rain-garden.md), not built, registration waiting on the developer) owns rain falling on ground: what one changed surface does to the same four drops, what soaks in, what is stored and what reaches the pond. **Boundary, recorded here:** Water Road's water never touches soil; it is one line of water in one channel from a fixed spring to a fixed fountain, and its only property is whether each stretch falls. If a later Water Road level compares surfaces, stores water, or asks where rain goes, that level belongs to Rain Garden instead.
- **Welcome Ways** (built 1–4, [definition](welcome-ways.md), same land) owns parts placed in rise and door sockets so that each pictured friend can get in: ramps and steps judged by who can pass. **Boundary, recorded here:** Water Road has no visitor, no ramp or step piece and no "who can pass"; a pier's height is set for the water's fall, and level 5's arches are judged by the support rule, not by a friend. The two share a *shape* at level 5 (drag a part into a socket, test, the first problem pulses with a reason), so the identity rests on the rules, not the interaction. **The accounting differs on purpose in the same land:** every Welcome Ways Try is free and its misses are always zero; here **Open the spring is the commit** and a failed opening is one miss, as level 3's pull is. A builder must not import Welcome Ways' free-Try accounting. If a later level becomes "make it passable for a friend", it belongs to Welcome Ways.
- **Chain Reaction** owns a marble rolling through placed ramp pieces and the bell it reaches; **Water Paths** and **Puddle Pipes** ([notebook](../IDEAS.md)) are route-drawing and pipe-turning proposals about connection. Water Road's route is fixed and always connected; only heights change. Nothing here rolls, and no piece is rotated or drawn.
- **Reuse:** level 3's slots, tray and `draggable` block (`onDrop` returns true when a slot takes it; a refused drop floats home) and Welcome Ways' socket drag for the arches and abutments; level 2's "two whole things side by side, or stacked, never shrunk" layout for the pre-K channel cards; Helper House's scene-owned step player for the water running stretch by stretch, the pool, the slide and the fountain; `onTap` for the arrows, the channel cards and Open the spring. Spring, channel, water, piers, hill, town, fountain, valley, rock, arches and abutments are code-drawn in `src/art/` from `src/art/palette.ts` (pale stone, a blue channel, green hillside, terracotta town; no sepia). No new engine, save field, render texture or shared helper; the stage stays `STYLES.stickers`.

### The water model (`logic.ts`)

Heights are whole **steps** from 0 (the town's ground) to 6. A road is `{ spring, fountain, piers }`: `spring` is the outlet height at the hill, `fountain` the inlet height at the town, `piers` the interior pier tops left to right. Its stretches run spring → pier 1 → … → fountain. One rule decides level 4: `falls(road) = every stretch drops at least one step` (strictly decreasing from spring to fountain). **A flat stretch does not fall**: the water stands still and fills it, so "every stretch goes down" is the whole rule, spoken and printed, and the pet's line for a flat stretch is "Flat here, so the water stood still". `firstStuck(road)` returns the index of the first stretch (from the spring) that does not drop, or `null` when the road falls; the water runs every stretch before it and pools in that one. Nothing is timed; the water runs only when she opens the spring.

**Level 5's arcade** is `{ spans, ends: { near, far } }`: `spans` is the seeded count of arch places across the valley (3 or 4), each end is `rock` or `earth`; placed state is `{ arches: boolean[], abutment: { near: boolean, far: boolean } }`. The channel on top is authored to fall one step per span, so the fall rule always holds over the valley and the arcade is the only decision. An arch is **held on a side** when that side has a placed neighbour arch, or it is the end arch and that end is rock or carries an abutment. `standsArcade = every span holds an arch && every arch is held on both sides`. `firstUnsupported(arcade, placed)` scans from the spring end and returns the first problem: `{ kind: 'end', end }` (the end arch has nothing at that end), `{ kind: 'gap', span }` (an empty span), or `null` when it stands. A gap is reported as the gap, not as the two arches beside it.

### Level 4, Downhill all the way (`downhill`)

The plan takes the band (as level 1 does). Two roads per round, one at a time. Every road has three stretches (two interior piers), as the study's row says.

- **Pre-K (two whole channels).** The hill, the spring, the town and the two piers' places, with the channel missing between them; below, two whole channels on their own piers, drawn as cards side by side. Exactly one falls all the way; the other **climbs unmistakably** in one seeded stretch (by at least two steps, never flat), so a four-year-old can read it from the drawing. Which card falls is seeded. `chooseChannel(side)`: tapping a card lifts that channel into the road (free; tapping the other swaps it). Open the spring with no channel chosen does nothing but repeat the ask (free).
- **School (her piers).** The channel already sits on the road, and each interior pier has a big up arrow and down arrow beneath it that raise or lower its top by one step (clamped to 0–6; a press at the limit is a soft bump, free). The plan draws `spring` 5 or 6 and `fountain` 0 or 1, so `spring − fountain ≥ 4` and a falling setting always exists in range. The **start is a falling setting made wrong by one pier**: road 1 starts with a **hump** (one pier raised to at least the height before it, so the first useful move is down); road 2 starts with a **dip** at school (one pier lowered to at most the fountain's height, so the next stretch climbs and the first useful move is up). A tick scale on the hill shows the steps from the spring down to the town (the fall budget, not interactive).
- **Flow, both bands:** `open()` is the one commit, Open the spring at least 160 × 110 and always enabled. If `falls`: the water runs every stretch with a rising trickle, the fountain splashes, the pet says "Downhill all the way!" and the next road appears (or, on the second road, the round finishes). Otherwise the water runs up to `firstStuck`, pools there, the stuck stretch **glows blue**, the pet names it ("It went up here, so the water stopped" / "Flat here, so the water stood still"), and that is one **miss**; the water then drains back to the spring, the piers and the chosen channel stay exactly as she left them, and she changes something and opens again. Moving a pier and choosing or swapping a channel are always free.
- **Hint.** When one road's misses reach two, or on asked-for Help: at pre-K the falling card **glows**; at school `hintFor(road)` glows one pier and the one arrow to press, taken from the **nearest falling setting** (fewest total steps of change; ties broken by the lowest settings, then by the pier nearest the spring), and after each press it moves to the next pier that still differs, so following it always reaches a falling setting. From road 1's seeded start the first hint lowers a pier, as the study says; after free moves, or from road 2's dip, it may raise one (open decision 3). `hints++` at most once per road; the glow stays until the road falls.
- **Invariants:** `falls` is total; `firstStuck` is `null` iff `falls`; any falling setting is accepted (the check is the rule, never a target setting); an opening that pools changes no pier and costs exactly one miss; a press never moves a pier outside 0–6.

### Level 5, Over the valley (`valley`)

School only (the study's upper-school rung). Two valleys per round, one at a time. The road comes off the hill already falling and meets a valley; the far side carries on to the town.

- **Plan.** `spans` 3 on the first valley and 3 or 4 on the second. Each valley has an end slot at each side; the seed makes **one end rock** (it needs nothing) and the other **earth**, or, on the second valley only, **both earth**. The tray holds exactly `spans` arches and **one abutment per earth end** (one or two). Stone piers already stand in the valley between the span places; the arches sit on them.
- **Flow.** Drag an arch from the tray onto any empty span place and an abutment onto either end slot (both end slots always exist, on rock or on earth). Every drag, swap and return to the tray is free; a drop of the wrong kind of piece (an abutment on a span, an arch on an end) or anywhere else floats home, free. Faint push arrows show at an unheld side of a placed arch while a finger is down on it (level 3's arrows). `open()` is the commit, Open the spring at least 160 × 110 and always enabled. If `standsArcade`: the water runs over the arches and on to the town, the fountain splashes, the pet says "Pushed back on both sides, all the way across!" and the next valley appears (or the round finishes). Otherwise one **miss**: the water runs to `firstUnsupported` and spills into the valley there; **every placed arch that is unheld on a side slides down and returns to the tray**, every held arch and every abutment stays where she put it, the problem place pulses, and the pet names it ("Nothing pushed back at this end, so the arch slid" / "A gap here, so the arches beside it slid"). An abutment put on the rock end is legal and free to place, but it leaves the earth end with nothing when only one abutment exists: that is the decision level 3 began, now at the ends of a row.
- **Hint.** When one valley's misses reach two, or on asked-for Help: the place `firstUnsupported` names **glows** (the empty span with an arch in the tray, or the end slot with an abutment in the tray; if the only abutment is on the rock end, the rock end's abutment glows to be moved). After each placement the glow moves to the next problem, so following it always ends standing. `hints++` at most once per valley.
- **Invariants:** `standsArcade` decides every seed; the tray always holds exactly enough pieces to stand; `firstUnsupported` is `null` iff it stands; a failed opening moves only unheld arches and costs exactly one miss; the bot's placement stands for every seed.

### Levels 4–5: bands and ranges

`BandLevels` becomes `toddler: { 1, 1 }`, `preschool: { 1, 2 }`, `prek: { 2, 4 }`, `school: { 3, 5 }` (the AM1 definition's open decision 2 kept pre-K contiguous for this). No `order`. Pre-K plays level 4 with the two whole channels and never reaches 5; school plays level 4 with its piers, then 5. `planFor(level)` gains `{ 4, 'downhill' }` and `{ 5, 'valley' }`; `Tally` and `botMove` take the two new plans. No save field, no renumbering; a school pin at 3 or a pre-K pin at 2 or 3 stays valid.

### Levels 4–5: screen and controls

- **Their own layout.** `RINGS` and `waterRoadLayout` are ring-shaped; the water levels get `waterLayout(level, band, view, spans)`, returning null rather than shrinking a target. Logical views are at least 768 high and 768 wide (portrait is the narrowest at 768 × 1024; the two phone views are 1662 × 768 and 1562 × 768 after scaling).
- **Level 4.** The hill and spring at the left of the upper half, the town and fountain at the right, the road between at least 600 units long with each step about 36 units of height, all clear of the home button's corner. Pre-K: the two channel cards (each at least 300 × 200) side by side below the road, stacked when the stage is too narrow (level 2's rule). School: each pier's up and down arrows (each 110 × 100) stacked below the ground under that pier, pier columns at least 160 apart. Open the spring below the hill, right of the pet's corner.
- **Level 5.** The valley across the upper half: end slots 100 × 130 and span places 130 × 120, so four spans and two ends need 720 units and fit the portrait width; arches in the tray are 130 × 110, abutments 100 × 120, the tray below the ground in up to two rows clear of the pet. The layout test must prove four spans at all four views; if a build finds they do not fit, the plan caps at three spans everywhere (the plan never varies by view).
- **Touch-down feedback.** An arrow dips and the pier's top twitches the way it will move; a channel card lifts; an arch or abutment lifts and rides 40 units above the finger; a span place or end slot brightens under a held piece; Open the spring presses down and the spring bubbles at once.
- **Speech.** `ctx.instruct`: "Set the piers so the water goes down all the way, then open the spring" (school 4), "Pick the channel that goes down all the way, then open the spring" (pre-K 4), "Put the arches across and hold the ends, then open the spring" (5). The two stated rules are fixed lines.
- **Feel.** A pentatonic step down per stretch as the water runs (from `src/audio/notes.ts`), a trickle that stops dead in a pool, a fountain splash, a stone slide for an arch that falls, a soft bump at a pier's limit. Reduced motion shows the water's reach and the outcome in place.
- **The history line**, spoken once at the start of levels 4–5 and printed on the card: "Long ago, by the middle sea, people carried water to their towns in channels that went down a little all the way, and across valleys on rows of arches." It names no people, city, date or builder, matches the aqueduct and arch pages the study opened, and is source-checked in the building session (as the 2026-10-23 decision replaced the named reviewer). The words *aqueduct* and *gradient* stay on the grown-up card.

### Levels 4–5: accounting

| Level | Counts a miss | Counts a hint | Never counted |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 4 | each opening of the spring that pools | the glow when one road's misses reach two, or asked-for Help; at most one per road | moving a pier, a press at the limit, choosing or swapping a channel, opening with no channel chosen, hearing the instruction again |
| 5 | each opening of the spring that slides or spills | the glow when one valley's misses reach two, or asked-for Help; at most one per valley | every drag, swap and return to the tray, an abutment on the rock end before opening, a refused drop, touching an arch to see the arrows |

- **Supported finish:** every glow leads to a falling road or a standing arcade, so each round ends with one ordinary sticker through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`, help or not. Nothing is kept: no creation, discovery or journal entry (an arch on the creature home's hill stays the study's RW05 proposal). Leaving early owes nothing.
- **Adaptation:** one pooled or slid opening per round is smooth; two hints or four misses are struggling and step down within the band's range (school 5 → 4 → 3; pre-K 4 → 3). Assisted completion is one sticker, not evidence of mastery; nothing here claims a child understands gradient or thrust, only that she set the road, watched the water and heard the rule.

### Levels 4–5: rule tests

- **Plans** over 300 seeds per band: pre-K level 4 draws two roads, each with exactly one falling card and one that climbs by at least two steps in one stretch and is never flat; school level 4 draws `spring − fountain ≥ 4`, at least one falling setting in 0–6, road 1's start not falling with a hump and road 2's start not falling with a dip; level 5 draws 3 then 3 or 4 spans, ends as specified, a tray of exactly `spans` arches and one abutment per earth end, and a channel that falls one step per span. The same seed gives the same plan.
- **The fall rule:** `falls` is true exactly when strictly decreasing; flat counts as not falling; `firstStuck` is the first non-dropping stretch and `null` iff `falls`; every falling setting is accepted, not only the one the hint aims at.
- **The level-4 hint from any reachable state, not only the seed:** over random sequences of presses from each seed, `hintFor` names a pier and a direction whose press strictly reduces the distance to the nearest falling setting, and following it from any state reaches a falling setting in at most `distance` presses; from road 1's seeded start the first hint is down.
- **Level-4 accounting:** an opening that pools is one miss and changes no pier or choice; presses, swaps and a no-channel opening change no count; the hint counts at most once per road.
- **The arcade rule:** `standsArcade` is true exactly when every span is filled and every end arch is held by rock or an abutment; `firstUnsupported` names the first problem from the spring end and is `null` iff it stands; with one abutment, placing it on the rock end makes the opening fail at the earth end; a failed opening returns exactly the unheld arches to the tray and leaves held arches and abutments in place; following the hint repeatedly stands from any placement; the bot's placement stands for every seed.
- **Layout:** `waterLayout` at the four views: every input at least 100 units, nothing under the pet or corner buttons, no two inputs overlapping, the pre-K cards side by side or stacked rather than shrunk, four spans fitting at every view.
- **Registry, lands, how-to and off-screen tests** pass with the new ranges and steps.

### Levels 4–5: open decisions

Each has a default the build uses; none blocks the batch.

1. **Pre-K's level 4.** Default: two whole channels to pick from, as the study's row says. The alternative (pre-K sets one pier with two arrows) is closer to school but asks a four-year-old to hold three heights in mind.
2. **Flat stretches.** Default: flat does not fall; the water stands still and it is the same miss as a climb. The alternative (flat counts as falling) would make the stated rule "never goes up", which a child can satisfy with a level channel no real water road used.
3. **Which way the hint moves.** Default: toward the nearest falling setting, up or down; road 1's seeded hump makes the first hint a lowering, as the study says, and road 2's dip makes it a raising. The alternative (both roads humped, so every first hint lowers) keeps the study's wording exactly and loses the "too low" case.
4. **Stretch count.** Default: three stretches (two piers) on every road, as the study's row says. A four-stretch second road at school is a later choice if level 4 plays too short.
5. **The rock end.** Default: one end rock and one abutment on the first valley, so where the block goes is a real decision; the second valley may draw both ends earth. The alternative (both ends always earth) is the study's wording and makes the abutments a formality.
6. **What slides.** Default: only unheld arches slide back to the tray; held arches and every abutment stay, so a failed opening never undoes her good work. The alternative (everything returns, as level 3's block does) is simpler and harsher.
7. **The history line.** Default: the sentence above, source-checked in the building session. If the check wants it shorter: "Long ago, by the middle sea, people carried water to their towns in channels that went down all the way."
