# Animal Snack deep dive

*ID `animal-snack` · bands lap, toddler, preschool · levels 1–5 on 2026-10-10 · session `claude/roadmap-item-14-game-content-fno5j7` ([PR 99](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/99))*

## The game today

She feeds farm friends their favorite foods. Cow, bunny, dog, cat, duck, pig and bear each eat one food (hay, carrot, bone, fish, seeds, apple, honey), and every question has one answer because no two animals share a food. The ladder starts with cause and effect (tap an animal, it munches in its own voice), moves to the animal-food association (a snack floats to its eater; "who eats the carrot?"; drag each snack to its eater) and ends with counting out 2 to 4 snacks. Its purpose is animal words, the animal-food pairing and, at the top, counting out a number. `skills`: animals, words, matching, counting.

| Level | Plan | Round | Bands whose window includes it | What she decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | munch, 3 animals | 6 taps | lap | nothing: any tap is right |
| 2 | float, 3 animals | 6 snacks | lap, toddler | which snack to tap (every tap is right) |
| 3 | who, 3 animals | 4 questions | toddler, preschool | which animal eats the food in the bubble |
| 4 | match, 3 animals | 3 snacks, one round | toddler, preschool | which animal each dragged snack goes to |
| 5 | count, 3 animals | 3 questions, 2–4 snacks | preschool | how many to give, then when to ring the bell |

The ranges are lap 1–2, toddler 2–4 and preschool 3–5 (`LEVELS` in `src/games/animal-snack/index.ts`). A level's mode and size are `PLANS` in `logic.ts`; a level above 5 would silently replay level 5 (`planFor` clamps).

**What a round draws from.** Seven animals (`ANIMALS`), each with one synthesized voice (`sfx.animal`) and a spoken word, and seven code-drawn foods (`foodArt`; the apple is the shared `prop`). Each question or play set deals three of the seven, so there are 210 ordered triples. Free play and matching keep one set of three for the whole level; the question levels deal new animals each time and never ask about the same animal twice running. One scene (`Backdrop`, seed 73), one blanket, one `sfx.munch` for every food. The count levels always put five identical snacks on the blanket, so a child can overshoot by one.

**Misses and hints.** Levels 1 and 2 have none. In level 3 a wrong animal shakes its head and says what it does eat ("No thank you! The cow eats hay. Who eats carrot?"); in level 4 a snack dropped on the wrong animal is the same miss and slides home; a drop that is not near any animal is nothing. In level 5 the bell with the wrong count is a miss: too many sends every snack beyond the number back, too few says what she has and what is wanted. Two misses glow the right animal, or the next snack, or the bell: one hint. The saved score is 0 and 0 for levels 1–2 and (in the play-through) 2 and 1 for the rest. Each animal she watches eat its favorite food files a discovery-journal entry.

**Couplings.** How-to card: five `LevelLine` steps, one finish line and a note. Ghost finger: `touchDemo` with `autotouch()` through `snackTouch` (a rule with a test that never touches a wrong animal and rings only on the exact number). Voice: `snack.munch`, `.float`, `.match`, `.who`, `.count`, `.yum`, `.nothanks`, `.full`, `.more`, `.toomany`, `.done`, plus seven `journal.snack.*` lines. Journal: `src/content/journal.ts` makes one entry per member of `ANIMALS`, and `journal.test.ts` asserts the entries equal `ANIMALS`. Land: Barnyard, fifth in its list. Music: `STYLES.hub`. Sticker: a seeded pick from a literal list of the seven plus its food; map icon: the bunny and a carrot. Browser: `WOODS_ONLY=snack` plays levels 1–5 (animals on screen, a wrong animal at levels 3–5 and the hint, saved score and sticker); `fingerdemo` plays 1 and 5. No couch entry, picnic step or saved creation.

**Known gaps** (read from the code and docs; none has been seen on a device unless said).

1. **Counted speech is ungrammatical.** `snack.count` is "Give the {animal} {n} {food}!" and `FOOD_WORDS` holds singular words, so the instruction says "Give the bunny 3 carrot" and "Give the dog 4 bone". Mass foods are no better ("3 hay", "3 honey"). `snack.yum` ("loves carrot", "loves bone") and `snack.nothanks` ("Who eats apple?") have the same missing article or plural. Each food needs a counted unit and a plural (bales of hay, pots of honey, handfuls of seeds). **Needs a listen on a device.**
2. **The count is only spoken.** The bubble at level 5 shows the food, never how many. A three-year-old has to hold "3" from one spoken sentence while the pet's repeat is the only reminder. Garden Grow and Market Stall both show the number as dots and a numeral.
3. **The how-to card has an unbounded step and a wrong finish line.** The last step is `{ from: 5 }`, so a level 6 would inherit "Give the animal the number of its snack that was asked for, then ring the bell" (the same trap as [Peekaboo Barn's](peekaboo-barn.md#the-game-today) second gap). The finish line is a plain string, "The round ends when every animal has its snack", true only at level 4: level 1 ends after six taps, level 3 after four questions and level 5 after three. `howto.test.ts` checks that steps are bounded only for Bubble Pop.
4. **Five snacks overlap in portrait.** The count levels lay the snacks out with `spread(n, 220, view.w - 80, 140)`, which steps by the smaller of 140 and the width divided by n. A snack's hit circle is 55 local units at scale 1.2, so 66 on screen and 132 across. In a 768-wide portrait view five snacks are 94 apart, so neighbours overlap by about 38 units (landscape at 1024 steps 140, which fits). **Needs a screenshot** before it is called a bug. It also caps what any new level can put on the blanket.
5. **Animals' tap areas are wider than the animals, and overlap in portrait.** An animal's hit circle is 190 local units at scale 0.6, so 114 on screen and 228 across; `spread(3, 200, view.w - 60, 270)` gives 254 apart in landscape but about 169 in a 768-wide portrait view, a 59-unit overlap that the later-drawn (right-hand) animal wins ([AGENTS.md](../../AGENTS.md#input-and-rendering-pitfalls) asks for the nearest target). A fourth animal would be about 127 apart. **Needs a screenshot** and a nearest-animal tap.
6. **Free play never nudges.** Level 1 has only the pet's spoken instruction; a child who does nothing sees nothing change (as Peekaboo Barn's level 1).
7. **Taps are ignored while an animal is eating.** `tapSnack` and `tapFriend` return while `busy`, and an eat takes about half a second, so a quick second tap at level 5 is dropped. **Person check:** whether that feels laggy to a three-year-old.
8. **The pinned sticker and icon are not a gap.** `sticker(seed)` picks from a literal list of the seven, not from `ANIMALS`, so new animals can join the game without changing a saved sticker. The coupling to watch is the journal (below).

**Couplings a change here must respect.** The journal makes an entry per `ANIMALS` member, so adding an animal adds an entry, a `journal.snack.*` voice line and a change to the "twenty-five observations" in the README and roadmap. `eaterOf` uses `find`, so foods must stay unique across the cast. Foods that look alike on a 66-unit snack (hay, oats, corn, seeds are all yellow-gold) need a screenshot and a distinct shape or band.

**Developmental sources** (secondhand; none checked against the original).

- **Giving a number is learned one word at a time.** In the Give-N task a child is asked to hand over a number of objects. Children learn "one" around two and then "two", "three" and "four" at roughly six-month steps; those who can give any number are tested up to about six. A 2021 replication with 213 children aged 39–74 months found the whole process slower than the original work assumed and that number words are learned one at a time ([replication](https://jnc.psychopen.eu/index.php/jnc/article/view/7029), [overview](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2021.703598/pdf)). Level 5's 2–4 sits in the preschool window; counts of 5 and 6 are the pre-K step.
- **Kindergarten asks the same task with more.** K.CC.B.5 is "count to answer how many" and, given a number, "count out that many objects", to 10 when scattered ([standard text](https://tasks.illustrativemathematics.org/content-standards/CC/K/B/standards.html)). K.CC.C.6 compares two groups ([text](https://www2.cde.ca.gov/cacs/id/web/263)).
- **Pretend feeding is early.** A state milestone guide suggests a spoon for feeding a stuffed animal at 18 months and lists feeding a block to a doll as if it were food at 30 months ([Early Connections](https://earlyconnections.mo.gov/families/development-milestone/toddler/playing-learning)). Feeding a friend is a gesture a lap child already has.
- **"Dog and bone" is a thematic relation.** Children link things that go together in an event (a dog and its bone) as well as things of one kind (a dog and a lion). The older story of a shift from thematic to taxonomic is contested ([review](https://www.frontiersin.org/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2020.537672/pdf)). The whole ladder up to level 5 is thematic; "who else eats it?" is the first step toward grouping the eaters.
- **Diets are a real school topic, with a catch.** Kindergarten lessons tie to NGSS K-LS1-1 (animals need food) and sort animals into plant eaters, meat eaters and both ([Virginia lesson](https://www.vai.org/wp-content/uploads/2023/03/K-What-Animals-Need.pdf), [Gynzy](https://www.gynzy.com/teach/en/library/item/2461)). The game's favorites are cartoon facts (a bunny's carrot is a treat; the dog, pig, duck and bear eat both plants and meat). So the standard is **not** cited for the current ladder, and a diet sort needs a curated, true table ([decision 5](#decisions-for-the-developer)).

## What makes sense here

**The game's own axis is the animal-food association and its words.** The first audit left the 13 younger ladders at pre-K "by design"; round three then planned two levels and a pre-K window for this one (6 *Snacks for two* and 7 *Who is still hungry?*), and the roadmap counts them among its 37 new levels on top. Both overlap games the island already has: Monster Munch holds exact orders and fair shares among monsters, Garden Grow holds "two colors with a number of each", and Duck Pond holds "how many more". Repeating them with a cow would add a theme, not a decision. This deep dive therefore **replaces those two planned levels** and puts the new decisions on what is unique here: who eats what, said three ways (a food, a sound, a name on a board), counted out, and then said back as a set.

**Fits.**

- **A taste test** (*Toca Kitchen*): a wrong food is a funny "no thank you", not a miss.
- **The sound as the question** and a second step after it (Peekaboo Barn's below-1 "Who says moo?"; here it is followed by feeding that animal): two associations in one answer.
- **Counting out with the identity of the food kept** (*Feed the Woozle*, the Give-N task): the instruction names animals and numbers and never the food, so the association has to come from her.
- **An animal with more than one food** (the Twinkl cards): the step from one answer to a set.
- **A menu board** with a printed numeral and a printed food word, read aloud on request: reading-lite, with the voice always available.
- **One new mechanic per level** (*Cut the Rope*): each level on top changes one thing.

**Arc by band.** Lap: open wide, then tap an animal, then tap a snack. Toddler: a taste test, then who eats it, what the cow eats, dragging each snack home. Preschool: give 2 to 4. Pre-K: who says moo and gets its snack, then two animals' own snacks, then who else eats it. School (a new window): the same ideas with a menu board to read. Sharing, subtraction and cooking stay in Monster Munch, Duck Pond and Pet Kitchen.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Replace round three's planned levels 6–7 with 6–8, and add a school window with 9–10?** The plan of record is *Snacks for two* and *Who is still hungry?* plus a pre-K window (the roadmap counts them among its 37 new levels on top). Lean: replace them. They repeat what Monster Munch, Garden Grow and Duck Pond already hold, while 6–8 each add a decision unique to this game (a sound chain, two animals' own snacks, a set of answers) and 9–10 add reading a board.
