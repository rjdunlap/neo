# Jelly Drums deep dive

*ID `jelly-drums` · bands lap, toddler, preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–9 on 2026-10-10 · session `claude/fervent-keller-f86ol8` ([PR 104](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/104))*

## The game today

Five big jellies stand on a wooden stage under a string of eight bulbs, with four fans (a duck, a bunny, a cat and a pig) at the bottom. Big jellies sing low and little ones sing high: C, D, E, G, A from left to right, the island's pentatonic steps 0–4, in red, orange, yellow, green and blue. A tap squashes a jelly, sings a marimba note, floats a music note and sends a fan hopping. Levels 1–3 are free play; levels 4–9 play a tune, then she copies it. The learning purpose is cause and effect, then short-term memory for a sequence of sounds (`skills`: cause-effect, rhythm, sequence-memory). It lives in Music Mountain, first in its list. The island's loop is silenced here (`silenced(STYLES.jelly)`) so the jellies' own notes are the only music, while the jellies keep bobbing to the loop's quiet clock.

| 8 | echo, 5 tunes of 4 notes | pre-K, school | the same, one more tune |

The windows are lap 1–3, toddler 1–3, preschool 4–7, pre-K 5–9 and school 8–9 (`LEVELS` in `src/games/jelly-drums/index.ts`). The toddler window is the lap window, so a two-year-old never meets a tune.

**What a round draws from.** Five jellies (the only pitches there are), one timbre (`sfx.marimba(step)`), four fans picked at random to hop, one stage and one garland of eight bulbs. Free play lights a bulb for every eighth of the goal. Tunes come from `makeTune(rng, length)`: random jelly indexes, no jelly twice in a row up to length 3, never one jelly alone. That is 20 ordered pairs at length 2, 80 tunes at length 3, 620 at length 4 and 3,120 at length 5. Nothing stops a round from drawing the same tune twice: at level 4, four tunes from 20 pairs repeat about one round in four. A tune has no name, no melody and no shape beyond its notes.

**How a tune round runs.** After a two-second pause the voice says "Listen!", waits 0.3 s, and the jellies sing and glow one note each 0.6 s apart. Then "Your turn!" and she taps. A tap before her turn is only music and is never an answer. A wrong jelly counts a miss, waits 0.5 s, says "Hmm, listen again!", and **restarts the copy from the first note** (`at = 0`) after playing the whole tune again. The second wrong jelly on one tune counts a hint (once per tune) and replays slowly (0.85 s a note); from then on the jelly she should tap next glows after each correct tap, and the first jelly glows after the replay. Every correct tune cheers the fans and the pet, lights a bulb and says "Beautiful!". The finale is a run up the scale, a chord, confetti and `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`.

**Misses and hints.** Free play has none. In tune rounds a miss is a wrong jelly; misses add up across the round's tunes, and a hint is counted once on any tune where she gets two wrong. Two smooth rounds (at most one miss and no hints) step her up; two struggling rounds (four or more misses, or two hints) step her down. The browser suite expects `[2, 1]` for its scripted round.

**Couplings.** How-to card: two steps (`to: 3`, `from: 4`), two finish lines and one note, with a ghost finger (`touchDemo`, `autotouch()` through `jellyToTap`: the scale up and down in free play, the tune copied note for note in a tune round). Voice: five lines (`jelly.free`, `.listen`, `.turn`, `.oops`, `.nice`). Sticker: a seeded jelly in one of five colors with a music note, so there is no pool for new content to change. Map icon: `JellyStand`. Music `silenced(STYLES.jelly)`, named in the registry test's list of games where she makes the music. Browser: `BROWSER_SUITE=originals ORIGINALS_ONLY=jelly` plays levels 1–9 (`scripts/browser-check.mjs` hard-codes nine levels in two places), `fingerdemo` plays the first and last level, `howto` has its card. **The picnic** uses it as the invitation step at `GAME_STEPS` levels lap 1, toddler 1, preschool 4, pre-K 5 and school 7, so those plan rows must never change meaning. No couch entry, journal entry or saved creation. [IDEAS](../IDEAS.md) lists no new game that reuses the jellies.

**Known gaps** (read from the code and docs; none has been seen on a device unless said).

1. **"Replaying the tune is always available" is not true.** The how-to note and [GAMES.md](../GAMES.md) say so, but `index.ts` has no replay control. The only replay is `oops()` after a wrong jelly, which costs a miss, and the pet's tap repeats the line "Your turn!", not the tune. A child who forgot a tune has to guess wrong to hear it again.
2. **A slip restarts the copy.** At note four of five she loses the four right notes and starts again, which is the memory load the [ladder audit](../IDEAS.md#short-ladder-audit-2026-10-09) names as the reason to stop at nine.
3. **Echo levels open with the free-play line.** `start()` always says `jelly.free` ("Tap the jellies to make music!"), then two seconds later "Listen!". The unscored first taps are harmless but the instruction contradicts the round.
4. **The tunes are not tunes.** They are random, so there is nothing to hum, a round can repeat a tune, and the picnic's "invitation song" is a different jumble every time. [DESIGN](../DESIGN.md#proposed-story-results) already sketches a typed `{ kind: 'tune', … }` story result for this.
5. **Free play never nudges and never answers.** A child who stops sees nothing change. Levels 1–3 differ only in note count (24, 32 and 40), the fans hop at random, and the garland does not react to what she plays. Whether 24 notes is a long round for a one-year-old is a person check.
6. **The jellies are never named.** The voice never says a color or "big" and "little", so the one rule the game teaches by design (size, color and pitch go together) is never spoken.
7. **One sound.** Every note is the same marimba, on every level.
8. **The how-to card's last lines are unbounded.** `{ from: 4 }` on the step, the finish line and the note would carry the tune text into a level 10 (the same trap as Splish Splash, Peekaboo Barn and Animal Snack). `howto.test.ts` checks that every step is bounded for Bubble Pop only.
9. **Fragile couplings.** The plan test asserts the last echo level has length 5, `jellyToTap` knows only `free` and `echo`, and `browser-check.mjs` expects nine levels and a `[2, 1]` score. Each new level must update all three (see "Couplings every level slice must update").
10. **Person checks open:** lap level 1 with a real child ([roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#needs-a-person-or-a-device)); and the roadmap's open question of whether the bare, loop-free room feels empty to a small child or her own sound carries it.

**Developmental sources** (secondhand; none checked against the original).

- **Pitch and size go together.** A review summary reports that three-year-olds reliably matched high tones to small bouncing balls and low tones to large ones (Mondloch and Maurer, 2004), and that 6-month-olds, but not 4-month-olds, show the association (Fernández-Prieto, Navarra and Pons, 2015; the only link I have is the [author's page](https://ub.academia.edu/ferranpons)). The jellies' size cue therefore fits a toddler or preschooler, and removing it (jellies of one size) is a real step up in ear-only listening.
- **Same or different.** Edwin Gordon's *Primary Measures of Music Audiation* asks kindergarten to grade 3 children to decide whether two short tonal or rhythm patterns sound the same or different, by circling a picture, with no reading ([Wikipedia on Gordon's theory](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gordon_music_learning_theory), [GIA](https://giamusic.com/resource/primary-measures-of-music-audiation-kgrade-3-audio-cd-recording-g2242cd)). Level 11 below borrows the question, not the test.
- **Early pitch play.** A parent-guide summary says 12–15-month-olds may imitate the contour of songs sung to them and that children sing small parts of songs from about two and a half ([summary of the research](https://fagottobooks.gr/blog/how-to-raise-musical-children-birth-to-age-5/)); a dissertation record found 3–6-month-olds vocalizing at sung pitches ([UConn](https://digitalcommons.lib.uconn.edu/dissertations/AAI8111923)). The ages are approximate and the sources secondary, so the lap and toddler entries below are guesses for a person check.
- **Corsi and tapping span.** A search for age norms returned none for four- to seven-year-olds, so this document cites no memory-span number.

## What makes sense here

**Jelly Drums is the instrument, not the studio.** Song Maker, Beat Builder and Sound Garden own building, looping and rhythm. The jellies' own lane is five big, always-musical notes that she plays one at a time, with the size, color and pitch of each tied together. The ladder today climbs one axis only: how many notes she must remember. The audit's verdict ([IDEAS](../IDEAS.md#short-ladder-audit-2026-10-09), line "Jelly Drums") is to leave the ceiling, because more notes is load, not a decision, and the verdict is right about length. So every proposal below keeps tunes at five notes or fewer and grows **what she listens for**: a rule applied to a heard tune (backwards), a comparison of two tunes (same or different), and an ordering by ear alone (lowest to highest). Below the existing ladder the jellies **answer** a toddler, and the ear is introduced one note at a time before tunes.

**The pentatonic set is a gift.** C, D, E, G and A are Kodály's do, re, mi, so and la, which are the notes children sing first, and the Simon designer made the same choice for the same reason (any order sounds pleasant). It limits the repertoire, but a real one exists: *Hot Cross Buns*, *Mary Had a Little Lamb*, the chorus of *Jingle Bells*, *Oh! Susanna*, *Camptown Races* and the start of *Old MacDonald* fit these five notes. *Twinkle*, *Ode to Joy*, *Row, Row, Row Your Boat* and *Happy Birthday* need F and B and are out (the [pentatonic exception](../ROADMAP.md#waiting-on-the-developer) row would admit them; decision 3).

**Overlap with the neighbours, settled.** Most obvious modes already have an owner.

| Idea | Owner today | Verdict for Jelly Drums |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Copy a song from shadow notes or a small card | Song Maker 3–5 | Left to Song Maker. The jellies stay ear-first. |
| Find a four-note tune by ear | Song Maker 7 | Jelly Drums' tunes are already ear-first; the difference is five big jellies instead of a grid, and the new levels add a rule, not a longer tune. |
| High or low, up or down | Sound Garden | Left out. Size is the cue here, never the question. |
| Call and response, short and long | Rhythm Neighbors, Beat Builder, Clap the Syllables | Left out: no timing judge. A held note is a sketch. |
| Known songs, tap to advance | Sing-Along Barn (proposed) | Songs here are copied or finished, not advanced. Whichever game is built first owns one tune table both import. |
| Composing, looping, keeping a tune | Song Maker and its tune plaque | Left out. Free play is not kept. |

**Arc by band.** Lap: the jellies answer her tap, one big jelly, then the five. Toddler: follow the glowing jelly through a song, then find the jelly that sang. Preschool: copy short tunes, finish a song she knows. Pre-K: longer tunes, then backwards, same or different, and lowest to highest. School: the same top levels with the size cue gone, and the grown-up couch route for the long memory challenge that the audit says does not belong in a child's ladder.

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

All ready except where it says so. The picnic's rows (levels 1, 4, 5, 7) keep their plan numbers: fresh content changes what a round draws, never what a level means.

1. **Guards first.** A big **listen-again target** (a speaker or ear, 100 units or more, free: no miss, no hint) on tune levels, which makes the how-to note true (gap 1). Echo levels no longer say `jelly.free` (gap 3). Bound the how-to's last step, finish line and note (`{ from: 4, to: 9 }` today, then to each new level) and make the bounded-step test generic (gap 8). A round never draws the same tune twice (gap 4). An idle nudge in free play: after eight seconds without a touch the nearest jelly wiggles and glows, with nothing counted (gap 5). Tests: a tune round's tunes are all different; the listen-again target never changes `misses` or `hints`; every how-to line is bounded; the nudge never fires while she taps.
2. **Tunes with shapes and names** (gap 4). A `TUNES` table in `logic.ts` replaces `makeTune`'s random notes for levels 4–9. Three kinds: **shapes** (a staircase up, a staircase down, a hop, a wiggle: the same shape at every length), **songs** (a phrase of a real song on the five jellies, with a spoken title) and the **invitation tune** (decision 6). The pools follow the plan lengths (2, 3, 3, 4, 4, 5 notes at levels 4–9), and a song phrase is never longer than five notes, the child's cap. Candidate phrases, as jelly indexes with C = 0, D = 1, E = 2, G = 3, A = 4: *Hot Cross Buns* `2 1 0` (three notes, no repeat); *Oh! Susanna*, opening `0 1 2 3` (four) and `3 4 3 2` (four); *Mary Had a Little Lamb*, opening `2 1 0 1 2` (five); *Jingle Bells* chorus ending `2 3 0 1 2` (five); *Old MacDonald*, only a five-note slice of its opening such as `0 3 4 4 3` (the full phrase `0 0 0 3 4 4 3` is seven notes, so it is couch-only). **These are transcribed from memory and must be checked against a score before they are built**, and every song here is traditional. By level: **level 4 draws shapes only** (two-note tunes are too short to be a song), **levels 5–6 draw shapes and *Hot Cross Buns***, **levels 7–8 add the four-note phrases**, **level 9 adds the five-note ones**. `makeTune`'s "no jelly twice in a row" rule applies only up to length 3 and all of those pools respect it; at lengths 4 and 5 a repeat is allowed. Tests: every note of every tune is a jelly 0–4; a tune has at least two different jellies; tunes of length 3 or less never repeat a note back to back; every song has a title line; a round's tunes are distinct, and where a level's pool has more than one shape or song, no two tunes in a row share a subtype (staircase, hop, wiggle, or the same song).
3. **Resume where she was** on four- and five-note tunes (gap 2; decision 4). After a wrong jelly the tune replays in full but she keeps the notes she had right and continues from there, with the right ones shown ticked. Three-note tunes keep the restart, because a restart there is short. Test: the notes she must still play after a miss equal the tune from her place; a miss counts once.
4. **Free-play twists** (gap 5). Level 1 stays plain. Level 2: **the fans answer**: a fan sings back the jelly's color in its own animal voice (`sfx.animal`) every fourth note, in turn. Level 3: **the scale sparkle**: three neighbouring jellies in order, up or down, send a rainbow sparkle across the stage and light a bulb at once (a `scale()` rule with a test: any run of three adjacent steps in either direction counts; nothing else does). Nothing is a goal beyond the note count, and no touch is wrong.
5. **Name them** (gap 6). In free play the voice says a jelly's color and size now and then ("Red! Big and low."): `jelly.name.<color>` lines, at most one in eight taps, never twice in a row, and a line for "Big jellies sing low. Little jellies sing high." once per round. Test: every color has a line; the rate limit holds. Device speech for the color words is unchecked.
6. **Sound sets** (gap 7). A round's instrument comes from the seed: marimba (today), bells (`bell` in `src/audio/instruments.ts`, which `sfx` already uses for sparkles), and a soft held tone, each on the same `stepHz` pitches, so a tune is the same tune on any of them. The stage tints to match (a night stage for the bells). Tests: every set maps steps 0–4 to the same five pitches. Whether the bells and tone read well on an iPad speaker is unchecked.
7. **Stages.** Music Mountain (today), a beach bandstand and a moon stage, each one `resize()` branch and a palette; a screenshot per stage, landscape and portrait.
8. **More audience.** The fans are four animals; sheep, horse, hen and mouse join when the Peekaboo Barn and Animal Snack decisions land. Fans hop on the beat rather than at random.
9. **Co-play.** Two or three lines per band ("Hum the low one and the high one", "Can you sing it back?"), beside the existing pots-and-pans suggestion.
10. **Journal entries (decision 7).** The journal is a fixed collection of things she has actually seen. Two entries: **low and high** ("Big jellies sing low and little jellies sing high": found when a round included a tap on the biggest and on the littlest jelly) and **a scale** (the three-neighbours sparkle). `JournalGame` gains `'jelly-drums'`, the spoken lines are checked against the game's data, and `RoundResult.discoveries` reports an entry only if the round showed it.

### Grown-up targeting

*"Targeting for grown-ups" is read here in two ways, because the project has two routes.*

**Couch play is the route that exists today.** Jelly Drums is not a couch game, and couch play is where scores, face-offs and long memory challenges are allowed (unlocks and scores never reach the child's save). A couch entry would be a **memory duel with Simon's shape**: tunes of length 6, 8, 12 and then 16 across a trip's stops, as the original's skill levels (8, 14, 20, 31) grow, with one play of each seeded tune for both players so the face-off is fair.

| Mode | How it plays | Score | Why it suits a grown-up |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Pass the tune** (team) | Player 1 plays a tune; Player 2 repeats it and adds a note, and so on. Both hear every added note. A miss ends the chain gently ("lovely, you made 11!") | the longest chain they made together | the classic add-a-note party game, cooperative, no loser |

### Left out on purpose

- **Longer tunes for children.** Six notes and more are the memory load the audit rules out. They live in the couch entry above.
- **A timing judge and rhythm.** Sound Garden and Rhythm Neighbors own it, and nothing here ends a round on a clock. A "held note" is a sketch, not a promise.
- **A game over, a lives count or a streak of tunes.** One sticker per completed round, as everywhere.
- **Songs that need F or B** (*Twinkle*, *Ode to Joy*, *Row, Row, Row Your Boat*, *Happy Birthday*): they sound wrong on five notes, and the [pentatonic exception](../ROADMAP.md#waiting-on-the-developer) is not decided.
- **Reading a score, or a card of colored boxes.** Copying from a card is Song Maker 5; the jellies' colors are note names, not a notation.
- **Composing and keeping a tune.** Song Maker, Beat Builder and the treehouse tune plaque. Free play is not recorded.
- **Perfect-pitch drills and interval names.** The ear-training apps' later steps are theory; here the ear levels end at lowest-to-highest.
- **Copyrighted recordings or imported sound.** Every sound is synthesized and every tune is traditional.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Override the audit's leave for three new top levels.** Lean: yes. The audit's reason (more notes is load, not a decision) is honored: tunes stay at five notes or fewer and each new level asks a different question (backwards, same or different, lowest to highest). If no, only the fresh content and the between entries below are built.
2. **How the new levels sit in the step-up rule.** Level 11 is easier than 9 in notes and level 12 changes the skill, so two smooth rounds at 9 step her up to something that is not simply harder. Lean: build them in pre-K 5–12 and school 8–12 and accept it (the same trade Peekaboo Barn and Splish Splash made), or fold this into the level-order decision.
3. **The pentatonic exception.** Lean: **no** for Jelly Drums. The repertoire of five-note songs is large enough (decided as "build only the tunes that fit first"). If the developer allows F and B for tap-to-advance tunes in Sing-Along Barn, Jelly Drums' songs stay five-note. **Add Jelly Drums to the "A pentatonic exception for tunes" row's "Blocks" column** so this is visible.
4. **Resume where she was on four- and five-note tunes.** Lean: yes. It changes what a miss costs, and so the adaptation (a long tune no longer restarts), but not how a miss is counted. If no, the restart stays and the listen-again target carries the load.
5. **A couch entry (Pass the tune and Long tune).** Lean: yes, but **after Lands stage B** (a `grownup` window to hold the 6–16 note plans) or after a couch-side plan table is agreed, because the catalog tests keep a stop's level inside a band's range. A school-range couch entry (five notes at most) could ship earlier, but it is a short memory duel. If no, the grown-up route is the how-to card's level picker and the `grownup` band later.
6. **The invitation tune for the picnic.** The picnic shows a fixed picture of "Juniper's tune" and the round carries only misses and hints. Lean: do nothing until the story layer carries a typed result (`{ kind: 'tune', … }` in [DESIGN](../DESIGN.md#proposed-story-results)), then make the invitation round play one fixed tune and let the picnic replay it. Until then, the picnic's levels stay 1, 1, 4, 5 and 7.
7. **Journal entries** (low and high; a scale). Lean: yes, two. They are small and each is a true observation she made.
8. **Sound sets and stages.** Lean: yes, bells and a soft tone, a beach and a moon stage. The instruments are unchecked on an iPad speaker.
9. **Person checks to add:** lap level 1 with a real child (already open) and whether 24 notes is too long; whether a one-year-old waits for the echo; device speech for the color words and song titles; whether a four-year-old can say "same or different" about two three-note tunes; whether backwards is fun or frustrating for a five-year-old; whether the listen-again target is found; and whether the bare room, with the new instruments, still sounds like music.
