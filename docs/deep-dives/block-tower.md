## The game today

The fun action is building a tower and watching a happy crash; the learning moves from cause and effect to equal-unit height, comparison and explaining support. Source baseline: `b0014e2`, integrated with coordinator dispatch `efb120b`; inspected [implementation](../../src/games/block-tower/index.ts), [rules](../../src/games/block-tower/logic.ts), [rule tests](../../src/games/block-tower/logic.test.ts), [game description](../GAMES.md#block-tower), [how-to](../../src/content/howto.ts), [voice script](../../src/content/voice-script.ts) and [couch catalog](../../src/couch/catalog.ts). This is a source audit, not a playtest.

### Content pools and model

- Levels 1–2: fixed six-block target at 1; four named friend heights at 2, shuffled and the first three used. No alternative scenes. Block colors cycle through `RAINBOW`; they do not change weight or height.
- Level 3: four flag values 3, 4, 5, 6. Level 4: four Bear heights crossed with three relations (12 semantic combinations). Bounded rerolls try to avoid adjacent repeats; this is not a strict guarantee for every seed. Tests sampled 200 seeds, not an exhaustive proof of the reroll.
- Level 5: seven authored pair IDs, mirrored and side-swapped (28 variants). Pre-K uses three distinct clear IDs; school uses two clear IDs and one matched-reach ID. Side and direction bags each guarantee one/two of each per run. Both towers share a seeded color sequence. All choices are frozen for replay; only Continue records an observation. Original `makeStand` remains an explicitly tested fallback, unused by authored play.
- Level 6: exactly two fixed `(blocks,star)` tasks: `(2,4)` and `(3,5)`. Solver slots are integers -12 through 8 (21 positions) in eighths of a width. The drop preview, accepted drop, hint solver and finger bot now share those slots (slice 2); the broad forgiving drop region clamps to that domain.
- All blocks have equal mass and width; stacked heights are fixed. `topples` checks every upper substack's mean x against the block below, with strict support (edge equality falls). At the table it checks the whole stack mean against its right edge, modeled as extending indefinitely left. Cartoon motion does not simulate friction, rolling, rotation, different masses, bridging across two supports or three-dimensional construction.
- `finish` searches stable prefixes, appropriate to the current immediate-settle interaction. It cannot solve a build that needs temporary ropes and becomes stable only when its last block lands. Wider blocks or masses cannot be introduced as decorative variants while retaining this rule.
- Sticker drawing uses its own seeded RNG (three/four colored blocks); pin those original seed results before extending art or content. No kept tower, journal discovery or picnic step is implemented. Block Tower has no couch registration or `control()`/`autoplay()` support; the two-seat idea remains a proposal.

### Known gaps and person checks

The how-to's level-2 basket/remove instruction is false: basket, bell and tower undo are only visible on 3–4; level 2 taps anywhere. The level-5 finish line says a tower is built, although the child predicts, and the whole-ladder goal omits predicting/revising. Bound the open-ended level-6 text before adding later modes. Put the actual inputs and number of tasks on each card.

The taller glow appears on the first wrong check at 3–4, while counted assistance begins after two. Preserve this distinction in the first wording slice; changing hint timing/accounting needs an explicit choice. Level-6 experimental building currently feeds adaptation through misses, unlike level-5 predictions. This needs a decision, not a claim that it is already free exploration.

Slice 2 reproduced a pickup during landing, cancellation committing a table placement, and stale help during pickup. The fixes lock pickup through settlement/result, order committed blocks by depth rather than animated y, return cancellation to the tray, clear help on pickup and share the solver slot domain. Resize during a fall and leaving it produced no errors in the isolated probe; no extra fall/lifecycle changes were made. The focused regression covers a resize during landing and subsequent top-lift recovery. Actual device cancellation and dragging remain open.

Existing [person checks](../DEVICE-CHECKS.md#needs-a-person-or-a-device) remain: whether a one-year-old enjoys tumbling, device speech and counting, top-block undo discoverability, subtle predictions for pre-K, and eighth-width dragging on iPad. The current off-screen suggestion includes cups/books and a table edge; prefer large lightweight blocks on the floor in a future wording slice. Do not infer developmental readiness from an automated solver.

## What makes sense here

Lap play stays a few large actions with a rewarding tumble. Toddler play can notice a friend's height without stopping at a number independently. Preschool and pre-K build/check/undo with height as the meaningful quantity. School play predicts, observes support and revises a plan. Keep the existing first six IDs, and expand their content before inventing tools.

The [audit](../archive/IDEAS-SHORT-LADDER-AUDITS-2026-10-09.md#short-ladder-audit-2026-10-09) wondered whether a counterweight changes the decision. It can, but a block behind the edge needs temporary support during construction: adding a farther star to the current immediate-settle mode is insufficient. Keep all weights equal; change the build/test sequence, show the whole group being supported, then release deliberately. A repair round prepares that interaction. An optional later removal round asks which support is essential. Shape copying, volume/packing and different masses stay with their own activities or a later definition.

### Left out on purpose

No fast stacking, survival clock, lives, tallest-ever streak or losing possessions. No shrinking blocks to fit bigger numbers. Wide/thin/round blocks change contact and mass; defer the IDEAS shape row to a separate model definition, rather than making colors imply physics. A teeter-totter with variable weights belongs to Seesaw Balance; a route for a marble belongs to Chain Reaction; shape silhouettes to Puzzle Pals/Tangram Town. No retained tower or discovery is promised: storage needs a bounded schema and actual demonstrated content. Two-seat play needs its own controls, fair seeds, solo fallback and couch decision; it cannot piggyback on dead pointer controls.
