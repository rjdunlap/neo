# Rhythm Neighbors deep dive

*ID `rhythm-neighbors` · bands lap, toddler, preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–6 on 2026-10-17 · session `claude/dive-rhythm-neighbors` ([PR #558](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/558))*

## The game today

A woodpecker knocks a call on its tree and two frogs answer from the pond. In the lap levels she taps the woodpecker to hear a call and a sample answer, and taps a frog to join in. From level 3 the game is turn-taking: the bird calls, a pictured reply appears as a row of frog circles, she plays that reply on the frogs left to right and sends it with the green arrow. Levels 5 and 6 also ask for the reply's **short and long gaps**, judged relatively (a long gap only has to be clearly longer than a short one, the `judgeEcho` rule shared with Sound Garden), with no deadline for the first tap. Three exchanges finish with a short bird-and-frog ending and one sticker. The learning is **conversation in music**: wait for the call, answer with a part that is different from it, keep two voices in order, then shape the answer's rhythm. The island loop is silenced (`silenced(STYLES.jelly)`), so the bird and the frogs are the only rhythm.

| Level | Plan (`PLANS` in `logic.ts`) | Band windows (`levels`) | What she decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | free, one frog: *Tap a woodpecker and hear a frog answer* | lap (1–2) | Nothing is checked. The bird plays its call and the first phrase's reply; any frog tap croaks. The green arrow appears after her first frog tap and finishes the round |
| 2 | free, two frogs: *Choose two frog voices for a little chorus* | lap, toddler (2–3) | Which frog to tap; the arrow finishes |
| 3 | turns, one frog: *Take turns: answer the bird with two or three frog notes* | toddler, preschool (3–4) | How many times to tap the frog, from the pictured reply (two notes, then three, then three); any pace |
| 4 | turns, two frogs: *Play a pictured reply with two frog voices* | preschool, pre-K (4–6) | Which frog for each pictured note (green and teal alternate), from the picture; any pace |
| 5 | timing, one frog: *Answer a short bird call with a different long-and-short frog phrase* | pre-K, school (5–6) | When to tap: the reply's gaps, short or long, in relation to one another (replies of three, three and four notes) |
| 6 | timing, two frogs: *Alternate two frog parts, then join a final duet* | pre-K, school | Which frog and when. The "final duet" is the same 1.7-second ending every level plays; nothing is joined |

**Content pools.** `makePhrases` shuffles **three** reply shapes, `['S','L']`, `['L','S']` and `['S','L','S']`, and the bird's call is always **two knocks** with one gap, the opposite of the reply's first gap. Levels 3 and 4 cut the shapes down to two notes for the first phrase and three for the others; levels 5 and 6 keep them whole. So every round of every level is the same three exchanges in a different order, and "a different reply" only ever means a different first gap. Voice 0 is `sfx.croak`; voice 1, the teal frog, rings `sfx.bell(4)`, not a frog sound. The score's circles are spaced by the reply's gaps even at levels 3–4, where timing is not judged.

**Mistakes and hints.** A wrong reply (wrong count, wrong frog order, or at 5–6 a long gap not clearly longer than a short one) is a boing, "Let us listen to that reply again" and the whole exchange played once more; a second miss on the same phrase turns on guided play, which glows the next frog and accepts the right sequence at any pace (one hint). The music-note button asks for guided play (one hint). The again button replays the exchange freely. Frog taps during the bird's demonstration sound but are not recorded.

**Known gaps** (found reading the code; **slice 1 fixed gaps 1–7 and part of 8 on 2026-10-21**, see the note after the list; the text above describes the game before that slice):

1. **Three exchanges, always.** The pool of three replies and the fixed two-knock call make every round the same material; the reply is never genuinely a reply to a varied call.
2. **The teal frog is a bell.** Level 2's "two frog voices" and level 4's "two frog voices" are a croak and a bell; a child cannot hear that the second voice belongs to the second frog.
3. **Level 6 promises a duet that does not exist.** The plan name, `describeLevel` and the how-to's level-6 step say she joins a final duet; the code's ending is the same four events at every level and takes no touches. Level 6 is level 5 with the second frog.
4. **No ending a lap child can reach alone.** Levels 1–2 end only through the green arrow at the top right (`v.w - 80, 65`), far from a one-year-old's reach at the bottom of the screen, and only after a frog tap. Jelly Drums' free play ends by itself after a count of taps.
5. **A thinking pause is a miss.** At levels 5–6 `judgeEcho` rejects any gap over 2.5 s, so a child who stops to look at the picture mid-reply gets a boing, though the game's own text says a reply "need not be perfect" and nothing else in it is timed. Guided play removes it, at the price of a hint.
6. **Silent extra taps.** In turn-taking levels a tap beyond the reply's length plus one is ignored without a sound. *(Correction found in the build: extra taps already sounded; the cap recorded one extra tap, which became a length miss. Fixed by capping at the reply's length.)*
7. **No idle invitation, no journal entry, no story use.** Nothing wiggles when she stops; the discovery journal has no "call and answer" page; the picnics do not use the game.
8. **The level table is a function, not a `BandLevels` table**, so no band has an `order`, and `planFor` indexes `PLANS` by position; an appended entry level needs both changed first (Sound Garden's slice 1 does the same for its game).

**Slice 1 (built 2026-10-21).** Gaps 1–7 are fixed: twelve reply shapes (the dive's SLS addition was already in `RHYTHMS`, so SLS from the old pool is the twelfth, with LL, SLL and LSL), varied two- or three-knock calls, a croaking teal frog, a joinable finale, free play ending after eight taps with an idle wiggle, the pause rule and the extra-tap fix. Gap 8 is half done: `PLANS` is keyed by level ID and `levels` is a `BandLevels` table; `order` waits for slice 2.

**Couplings.** Rhythm Neighbors is a couch game (team, school band, stops at levels 5 then 6; `demoLevel` 5; left and right play the frogs, bottom sends, left button replays). Its ghost finger (`autotouch`) taps each frog once at the free levels and follows `nextNote` at the others, resting longer on a long gap; the couch bot (`autoplay`) schedules the reply from `schedule`. Appended levels leave the couch stops alone, and the bots work from the phrase data, so a larger pool costs them nothing as long as `nextNote`'s rests still satisfy `judgeEcho` (the existing test asserts it at every level and seed).

**Person and device checks.** The quiet-games row in [DEVICE-CHECKS](../DEVICE-CHECKS.md) covers the silenced loop. Nobody has watched whether a one-year-old waits for the frog's answer, whether a three-year-old reads the frog circles left to right, or whether the second frog's voice carries on an iPad speaker.

**Developmental and standards references.**

- **Serve and return.** Harvard's Center on the Developing Child ([key concept page](https://www.developingchild.harvard.edu/key-concepts/serve-and-return/), opened) describes the back-and-forth between an infant and an adult as "a bit like a game of catch": "The human brain expects this game of back and forth, and it is essential for healthy development." *Use:* the lap rung is not a copying task; it is **her knock, their answer**, every time, with nothing to get right.
- **Babies move to a beat; toddlers slowly learn to slow down.** Zentner and Eerola (2010, PNAS 107(13); [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC2851927/), search summary) found 5–24-month-olds move rhythmically more to music and drumbeats than to speech, and smiled more the better they coordinated. Yu, Todoriki and Myowa (2022, *Frontiers in Psychology*; [PMC](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9558294), opened) had toddlers drum with their mother or a robot: at 24 months they began slowing toward a 600 ms beat, and accurate timing to it arrived at about 30 months; 18-month-olds showed only a marginal effect, with their mothers. Kirschner and Tomasello (2009, *JECP* 102(3); [Duke record](https://scholars.duke.edu/publication/1458233), search summary) found 2.5-year-olds adjust tempo to a beat outside their own only with a human partner, and all ages synchronized better with a partner than with a machine. Provasi and Bobin-Bègue (2003, *IJBD* 27(3); [HAL record](https://hal.parisnanterre.fr/hal-01645056v1), cited in later papers as a search summary) found acceleration matures before deceleration: young children find **slowing down** harder than speeding up. *Use:* a long gap is the hard part of a reply, so the long gap arrives after the count and the frog order are secure (as the ladder already does), never on a deadline, and a toddler's "answer" is a tap, not a timed one. Co-play with a grown-up as the caller is likely to help a two-year-old more than any bot; the bird's call stays something a person can also knock.
- **Standards.** The Core Arts kindergarten performing standard MU:Pr4.2.Ka asks children, with guidance, to show awareness of contrasts such as same/different (a [Diocese of Greensburg kindergarten guide](https://www.dioceseofgreensburg.org/wp-content/uploads/Music_K.pdf), search summary; the official site was not opened), and the creating standard MU:Cr1.1.K places beat and contour. *Use:* *Copy or answer?* (same rhythm or a different one) is a kindergarten-level listening question; rhythm reading from notation is a grade 1–2 standard and stays out.

## What makes sense here

Rhythm Neighbors owns **the conversation**: a call, then an answer that belongs to someone else (the [music study](subjects/music-instruments.md#on-the-island-today): "it owns the conversational rhythm action"). Sound Garden owns listening questions and the plain echo, Jelly Drums note memory and tune "same or different", Song Maker kept melodies, Beat Builder the edited score, and the proposed Little Conductor tempo and dynamics. So the ladder grows along the conversation's own arc, which is the arc the references agree on:

1. **My knock, your answer** (lap): every action is answered, nothing is judged.
2. **Wait, then answer** (toddler): the call comes first; one tap answers it.
3. **Copy the pictured answer** (preschool, built): count and order.
4. **Shape the answer** (pre-K and school, built): short and long.
5. **Is that a copy or an answer?** (pre-K and school): the listening judgment, after she has produced both.
6. **Make your own answer** (school): the improvised reply the tradition is about, where the only rule is "not an echo".
7. **Longer conversations** (school): calls with their own rhythm and four-note pictured replies, which is more material, not more memory, because the reply stays pictured.

Two things fit the creature-world [rework](../rework/README.md) and are proposals there, not here: a new neighbor with a third voice who joins the pond for good after a duet (a permanent unlock through play), and this game as the "make and repeat musical patterns" step of the concert project. Both wait on the home and collection slices (RW05–RW07).

**Fits the rules:** more exchanges and a bigger pool (quantity is welcome); pictured replies at every copying level (no memory load); the relative gap judge (no metronome); guided play for everyone; one sticker per round; a call a grown-up can also play. **Does not fit, and stays out:** a sequence that grows until a mistake, a beat that speeds up, a timing grade, a combo, elimination, a deadline for the first tap, a round-ending timer.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action.** At the lap entry, a knock anywhere on the big tree. Today, at level 1, a tap on a frog (it croaks and bounces). Neither needs a grown-up to say anything.
- **Why it feels good.** The answer: a knock gets a croak back a beat later, every time, like catch. At the turn levels the pleasure is the moment the frogs sing her reply back as a line of music with the bird. Touch-down feedback is the frog's 1.12 bounce and its croak; the proposed additions are a **throat puff** (a pale ellipse under the chin, `Graphics`, tweened from 0.6 to 1 over 0.15 s and back) and a ripple ring on the pond under the frog that tapped, and for the woodpecker a head peck (the red cap rotates 12° toward the trunk for one frame of knock). The second frog's voice should be a frog: a short synthesized croak two pentatonic steps above the green frog's (a 0.12 s sawtooth burst with a downward glide from `notes.ts` step 5, through `sfx`), not a bell.
- **The consequence is shown, not only heard.** The score's frog circles already fill yellow as she plays; a long gap should look long: a small hop arc between two circles for `L`, a short tick for `S` (the Kodály walk/run), drawn in the same `drawScore` pass. Replaying the demonstration bounces each circle as its note sounds (already done through `drawScore(active)`).
- **What varies on replay.** With the bigger pool: which reply shapes come, how many knocks the bird plays, which frog starts, and the finale's order. The rule never changes. A scenery variation (dusk, rain on the pond) is optional and not part of any slice here.
- **Reasoning, reading, memory, motor.** Nothing is read. Memory is removed by the picture at every copying level. Motor demand enters only at 5–6 (the long gap), and guided play removes it. The reasoning levels are *Copy or answer?* (a judgment) and *Your own answer* (a choice with one constraint).
- **Supported completion and stopping.** Two misses glow the next frog; the music-note button asks for the glow at once. Every round ends in three exchanges and a finale; free play should end by itself after eight frog taps as well as through the arrow. "Again" replays the level just finished.
- **Roles.** Solo stays. The natural co-play role is **the caller**: a grown-up knocks the woodpecker's tree and the child answers; the *Knock a call* level makes the tree playable so this needs no new seat, and a two-seat version where the frogs judge the child's answer to a grown-up's call waits on the [seat contract](../IDEAS.md#the-seat-contract).
- **A person or a device still has to judge:** whether a one-year-old waits for the frog's answer or knocks continuously (both are fine; the design should answer either); whether a two-year-old hears the glow-and-wait as "my turn"; whether a three-year-old reads the frog circles left to right or taps the frog as many times as there are circles without order; whether the second frog's voice is distinct on the iPad speaker; whether the long-gap hop arc reads as "wait longer".

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

- **A reply pool of twelve and calls that vary** (levels 3–6). `REPLIES`: the eight shapes Sound Garden's `RHYTHMS` already lists (`['S']` to `['S','S','L']`) plus `['L','L']`, `['L','S','S']`, `['S','L','L']` and `['L','S','L']`; the call becomes two or three knocks with its own gap pattern, drawn so that it is never the reply's pattern and, at levels 3–4, so the reply's note count differs from the call's knock count in at least two of three exchanges (so "answer, don't copy" is audible). Levels 3–4 still cut replies to two then three notes; 5–6 use three- and four-note shapes. A rule test asserts: every reply is playable on the offered frogs, no reply equals its call, two-voice replies alternate voices, the three exchanges in a round are distinct, and `nextNote`'s rests still pass `judgeEcho` for every shape.
- **The teal frog gets a frog voice** (levels 2, 4, 6): the pitched croak above. A test is not possible for a sound; the device check row covers it.
- **The duet level 6 promises.** After the third exchange at any turn-taking level, a four-second finale: the bird knocks its call, both frogs play the last reply together, and her taps on either frog during it sound along, un-judged; the round finishes when it ends. Level 6's plan name and how-to step become true; a test asserts the finale takes no misses and ends on its own.
- **An ending a lap child can reach** (levels 1–2): after eight frog taps the frogs sing the finale and the round finishes by itself; the arrow stays for an earlier stop. After six seconds without a touch, a frog wiggles and croaks once (never a hint, never counted).
- **The pause rule** (levels 5–6): a gap over 2.5 s inside a reply clears her taps with the circles emptying and the spoken line "Take your time; start the reply again", **no miss** (decision 2; lean yes). An extra tap past the reply's length croaks but is not recorded (replacing the silent cap).
- **Voice:** "Your turn! Play the frog reply" stays; add "Listen: the woodpecker knocks, then the frogs answer" for the toddler rung and "Any answer that is not an echo" for the creative one.

### Younger-band openings

Lap and toddler are already served; the openings above are **entries placed before the built levels**, not new bands:

- **Lap** gains level 7 *Knock, knock* as its first level (`order [7, 1, 2]`, `entryLevels [7]`), so a new lap player starts where every knock is answered; a child who has already played keeps level 1 or 2.
- **Toddler** gains 8 *My turn, your turn* between 2 and 3 and 9 *Knock a call* before 3 (`order [2, 8, 9, 3]`). Neither moves a saved level; toddlers who are at 3 stay there.
- **Preschool** gains 9 before 3 (`order [9, 3, 4]`), so a new preschool player calls before she copies. Default; the developer may prefer `[3, 9, 4]` (decision 5).
- **Pre-K** gains 10 after 6; **school** gains 10, 11 and 12 after 6. A smooth pair at 6 steps up to 10, a judgment, rather than to a harder production level; that is the trade Sound Garden and Jelly Drums also made.

### Easier entries and levels between existing ones

Levels 7, 8 and 9 above are the entries and between-levels, placed by the built `order` and `entryLevels` support (Bubble Pop 15 and Pet Says 13 are the precedents; see [DESIGN](../DESIGN.md#minigame-contract)). The game's `levels` function must first become a `BandLevels` table with `order` for the bands that need one, and `PLANS` a table keyed by level ID, so an ID can land before the one below it; slice 1 does both before any entry is built. No saved level, pin or couch stop moves: the couch's stops stay 5 and 6, the pre-K and school minima stay 4 and 5. Remaining calls (the preschool placement of 9, a pre-K window for 11) are decisions below, with defaults the build uses.

### Left out on purpose

- **A sequence that grows until a mistake, a beat that speeds up, a timing grade, combos, elimination** (Simon, Bop It, Rhythm Heaven's medals, Patapon's fever): every one punishes or pressures.
- **A deadline for the first tap or for the round.** The island has no round-ending timers; the only clock is the relative judge, and the pause rule removes its one hard edge.
- **Voice identification** (*Which frog answered?* by ear): Sound Garden's dive leaves it to Animal Snack's *Who's Calling?* and Peekaboo Barn; the frogs are told apart by the picture.
- **Counting the knocks aloud** (answer with the same number): a counting game inside a rhythm game; Duck Pond and Monster Munch own quantity. *Knock a call* answers with the same count but never asks for it.
- **Melodic replies and pitch**: Jelly Drums and Song Maker. The frogs' two voices are two players, not two notes to compose with.
- **Notation, rhythm syllables, a score to read**: Beat Builder's grid is as far as the island goes; the hop and tick marks are pictures of a gap, not symbols to learn.
- **A microphone**: no clapping or voice detection.
- **Layered independent rhythms** (two parts at once held by one child): the audit's "larger design for a music slice" is the proposed Hat Band in the [music study](subjects/music-instruments.md), not this game.
- **Imported audio or a recorded birdsong**: everything stays synthesized.
