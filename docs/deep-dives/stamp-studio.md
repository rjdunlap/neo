# Stamp Studio deep dive

*ID `stamp-studio` · bands lap, toddler, preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–6 on 2026-10-10 · session `claude/roadmap-item-14-game-content-unyq86` ([PR 110](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/110))*

## The game today

A sheet of cream paper and a row of stamps. She chooses a stamp, taps the paper, and it is pressed there with a pop; the green arrow (top right) finishes the picture, the back arrow undoes the latest stamp. Nothing is checked: every picture is welcome, the arrow works after the first stamp, and a round ends with the ordinary sticker (always 0 misses, 0 hints). A finished picture can be kept on the treehouse picture board. It lives in Paint Pier (`rainbow-fingers`, `fluffy-salon`, `stamp-studio`, `pixel-pictures`). `skills`: composition, creative expression, spatial reasoning. It is the only open-ended game whose levels are tool unlocks and spoken invitations, not rules.

| Level | Plan (`PLANS` in `logic.ts`) | Bands whose window includes it | What she decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | stars: one stamp, big stars | lap | where to tap |
| 2 | friends: a cat or a fish | lap, toddler | which of two, and where |
| 3 | picture: star, flower, fish; four colors; drag to rearrange | toddler, preschool | stamp, color, and moving a stamp |
| 4 | sizes: adds the cat, a quarter-turn button and a "big" button | preschool, pre-K | the same plus how a stamp sits |
| 5 | garden: the same tools, "What grows in your imaginary garden?" | preschool, pre-K, school | what to make |
| 6 | story: the same tools, "two friends on an adventure" | pre-K, school | what to make |

Ranges (`levels` in `index.ts`): lap 1–2, toddler 2–3, preschool 3–5, pre-K 4–6, school 5–6.

**What a round draws from.** Four stamps (`star`, `flower`, `fish`, `cat`, `StampKind` in `logic.ts`, duplicated as `STAMP_KINDS` in `src/content/creations.ts`), four colors (`STAMP_COLORS`: purple, blue, green, pink), two sizes (1 and 1.3), four quarter turns, a 24-stamp limit (`LIMIT`, the same number as `PICTURE_MAX`), and one plain paper. Nothing is random: every round at a level is the same round. The saved sticker is a seeded star, flower, fish or cat in one of four colors (`sticker` in `index.ts`).

**How a picture is made.** `place`/`pixel` keep a stamp's position as fractions of the paper, clamped 70 units from every edge, so a picture survives resizing. At levels 1–2 a stamp cannot be touched again; from 3 a stamp is a draggable circle of 55 units radius (`draggable`), picking one selects it with a yellow ring, and a tap on a stamp never presses a new one beneath it. `finish` waits 0.9 s, then hands `{ kind: 'picture', stamps }` to `ctx.finish`, and the treehouse button after the sticker keeps it ([DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md#pet-treehouse)). The ghost finger is `stampDemo` in `logic.ts`: five presses, a turn on the third and a big stamp on the fourth where those buttons exist, one carry to the middle, then the arrow.

**Couplings.** How-to: one `goal`, five scoped steps, a finish line and a note, with a ghost finger (`touchDemo`). Voice: `game.stamp-studio`, `stamp.stars/friends/picture/sizes/garden/story/kind/full/done`, and the shared `color.*` names. Music `STYLES.paint`, not silenced. Browser: `CREATIVE_ONLY=stamps` in the `creative` suite plays levels 1–6 (three stamps, a drag, a turn and a grow, undo, finish) and `creations` keeps and swaps pictures; `fingerdemo` has all six. No couch entry, journal entry or picnic step. [IDEAS](../IDEAS.md#short-ladder-audit-2026-10-09) holds two rows: the audit's "leave: open-ended, nothing to climb", and round three's "stamp sets: sea, space, farm and a picnic".

**Known gaps** (read from the code; none has been seen on a device unless said).

1. **Undo after the arrow drops a stamp that is still on show.** The undo handler pops the last stamp before it tests `done` (`index.ts`, the `undo` button): during the 0.9 s wait the picture is kept without that stamp while the screen still shows it. Turn and big have no `done` test either, so the kept picture can differ from what was on the paper when the arrow was pressed.
2. **Levels 4, 5 and 6 are the same game.** Their tools, stamps and colors are identical; only the spoken prompt differs. So the ladder is really "toy, two stamps, colors and moving, then three prompts".
3. **Every round climbs.** `finish` always reports 0 misses and 0 hints, so two rounds in a row step her up one level ([`nextLevel`](../../src/progress/difficulty.ts)), whatever she did (or did not do). A lap child reaches 2 after two rounds, a toddler 3. Any new level on top will be reached the same way unless the accounting is defined (decision 2).
4. **Levels 1 and 2 show one thing in two colors.** The stamp buttons are drawn in orange (`stampArt(kind, 'orange', 33)`) but with no color row the stamp that lands is purple (`this.color` starts `'purple'`). A one-year-old is shown an orange star and gets a purple one.
5. **Every lap round is the same round.** One purple star, forever; the cat and fish of level 2 likewise. A child who plays often meets no variety until levels 3 and 4.
6. **Lap rounds end only on the green arrow.** A pat is a fine press (the sheet's `onTap` drops touches less than 120 ms apart, and a hand that lands as more than three pointers is ignored by `palmOnGlass`), but nothing ever finishes a lap round. After 24 stamps every touch says `stamp.full` again, one spoken line per tap.
7. **Three of the first colors are missing.** The row offers purple, blue, green and pink: no red, orange or yellow. The row sits at `220 + 125 i`, so it cannot simply grow (seven discs of 86 units at 108 apart are 756 wide, and the portrait paper is 418 wide).
8. **Words are raw ids.** `stamp.kind` speaks the id ("fish!", "cat!") on every choice; there is no name table, no article or plural, and a stamp has no sound of its own (only a pop whose pitch cycles). A new kind needs a spoken name.
9. **Only two sizes, and "small" is the normal one.** The big button toggles 1 ↔ 1.3, so "make it big or small" is a 30% change that the 0.46-scale board hardly shows.
10. **Undo takes back only the latest stamp.** There is no way to take away the one she picked (a stamp dropped under another one stays).
11. **Parallel lists.** `StampKind` (`logic.ts`) and `STAMP_KINDS` (`creations.ts`) must match by hand, and `cleanStamp` drops any kind it does not know. A kind added to the game only would be saved, then silently removed when the save is repaired. The sticker pool is a literal four-by-four array, so adding to it would redraw stickers she already owns.
12. **"Tell me about it" has no listener.** Levels 5–6 ask her to tell the story and nothing hears it (a co-play note, not a bug).
13. **One paper.** There is no backdrop, so a picture has no "sky" or "water" to place things in, and the kept picture has none either.
14. **Cards.** The how-to card was once taller than the screen (796 of 768) and its copy was trimmed; every new level adds a `LevelLine`.
15. **Person checks open:** whether a one-year-old pats and slaps or presses, and whether the pop rewards it; whether the 0.46-scale board reads at all with a 1.3 stamp; how the drag feels for a three-year-old.

## What makes sense here

The first thing the genre shows is that a stamp game grows by **content and by what can be made**, not by harder answers: Tux Paint by hundreds of stamps in categories, Storybook Weaver and the sticker-book apps by backgrounds and characters, Kid Pix by stamps that speak and animate, Mario Paint by turning the picture into other things (a song, a walk). The second is that the learning in a stamp game is **language and structure that she produces by placing**: a thing "up in the sky", "next to" another, a repeat that fits a border, the same on both sides. That is a good fit for a game that must never say "wrong".

So the plan keeps the audit's verdict where it is true (**nothing to climb in the sense of a harder answer**, and the floor stays a toy) and still adds a lot. It has four parts, cheapest first:

1. **Guards and words** (slice 1), because three of the gaps above are real defects and one is a save trap.
2. **Fresh content**, which is the core of this dive and what round three already asked for: six stamp **sets** with a backdrop each (thirty-six stamps, thirty-two new), a sound and a spoken name for every stamp, seven colors, three sizes, a wake-up when the picture is finished, and a picture that plays. These need no new level and make every existing level different every round.
3. **Five levels on top**, an **override of the audit's "leave"** that the developer decides (decision 1): each is an *invitation* ("Can you put the sun up in the sky?"), never a question with one right answer. An invitation she meets earns a sparkle; one she does not meet earns nothing, and the arrow still finishes. They teach one thing each: place words in a scene, relations between two stamps, a pattern of her own that fits a border, symmetry as a toy, and a repeating tile.
4. **Entries below level 1**, which wait on the level-order decisions.

**Arc by band.** Lap: a pat that sounds, a stamp that says its name, a picture that finishes itself. Toddler: choose a friend, hear it, color it, move it. Preschool: sets, colors, three sizes, and place words in a scene (level 7). Pre-K: relations (8) and her own pattern (9). School: patterns, a mirror toy, a repeating tile (10–11) and, later, a story told over a few pictures.

**Stays out** (see the last section): counting to a target, copying a model, scoring or grading a picture, unlocking or buying sets, typing a caption, and a free-drawing tool.

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

IDs, saves and the sticker do not change for any of this.

1. **Round variety at levels 1–3.** A seeded round picks its stamps and color: level 1 one stamp from the pool, level 2 two, level 3 three, each from one set. The stamp buttons and the stamp that lands share the same color (this fixes gap 4). Paper tint can vary by seed too (not saved). Test: every pick is from the pool and distinct, the button color equals the stamp color, and over 50 seeds each stamp in the pool appears.
2. **Six stamp sets**, each a backdrop and six code-drawn stamps (`SETS` in `logic.ts`, art in `art.ts`, a spoken name and a sound per stamp). The four existing stamps keep their art and join sets, so every kept picture still reads:

   | Set | Stamps (new in bold) | Backdrop |
   | --- | --- | --- |
   | Sea | fish, **whale, crab, starfish, shell, boat** | blue water below a sand strip |
   | Farm | cat, **cow, pig, duck, hen, barn** | green field, sky, a pond |
   | Garden | flower, **butterfly, ladybug, snail, sunflower, tree** | grass and a fence |
   | Space | star, **moon, rocket, planet, comet, ufo** | night sky |
   | Picnic | **apple, cupcake, basket, kite, balloon, ant** (check against the Windy Picnic's props) | a cloth on grass |
   | Town | **house, car, bus, bike, lamp, cloud** | a street and sky |

   Sets are open from the start; nothing unlocks. At levels 1–3 a round shows one seeded set; from level 4 a **sets button** (a round button with the set's first stamp on it) opens a row of six, choosing is never wrong, and a picture may mix stamps from several sets (the backdrop is the last set she chose). Test: each set has six distinct kinds, every kind has a name, a sound and art that reads at the board's 0.46 scale (a size check on the drawn bounds), and every kind is in `STAMP_KINDS`.
3. **A voice and a sound for every stamp.** Choosing a stamp says its name once (`STAMP_NAMES` with article and plural: "a cow", "cows"), and each press plays the stamp's own sound from what `sfx` already synthesizes: `sfx.animal` for cat, cow, pig, duck and dog, `splash` for sea things, a pitched bell or chime for stars, flowers and the moon, `clunk` for house and bus, `chirp` for hen and butterfly. Pitched sounds step the pentatonic scale from `notes.ts`. Optional (decision 4): at levels 1–3 the first ten presses are **counted aloud** ("one!", "two!"…), then stay quiet. Test: the name and sound tables cover every kind; the count says 1 to 10 once each and then nothing; no sound is pitched off the pentatonic set.
4. **A wake-up when the arrow is pressed** (Kid Pix's animated stamps, Beck and Bo's finish): the stamps bounce in the order they were placed, each with its sound, in at most 2.5 s however many there are, and then the round ends. This also closes gap 1, because the stamps are frozen first. Test: the schedule for 1 to 24 stamps ends within 2.5 s and visits each stamp once.
5. **Seven colors and three sizes.** Red, orange and yellow join (the six first colors in rainbow order, plus pink). The size button cycles small 0.7, middle 1, big 1.5 (inside `cleanStamp`'s 0.5–2 clamp, so no save change). The layout needs its own look (decision 5): seven 86-unit discs do not fit one row beside the arrow in portrait. Test: no two controls overlap at four views, each tap radius is at least 100 units across, and yellow on cream has a visible outline.
6. **Take this one away.** A bin button, visible when a stamp is selected (levels 3+), removes that stamp; the back arrow still removes the latest. Test: removal never leaves an orphan drag handle or ring; undo and bin cannot act after the arrow.
7. **Play the picture** (levels 3+, Mario Paint's composer, Chrome Music Lab's Kandinsky): a button scans the paper left to right and each stamp sounds, pitch by height on the pentatonic scale. Nothing is scored. It overlaps IDEAS' *Draw a Song*; this version is a button on a finished picture, not a drawing tool. Test: the scan visits stamps in x-order, every pitch is in the scale, and the whole scan lasts at most 6 s for 24 stamps.
8. **The pet tells it back** (levels 5–6 and the invitation levels): after the arrow, the pet says what it sees, built from the stamps ("I see a big pink cow and two stars!"). It only says what is true: the count of each kind, the biggest, and a place word only when the geometry shows it. Test: for random pictures every spoken fact is true of the data, plurals are right, and a picture of one stamp says one sentence. This is the listener gap 12 lacks. Device speech for composed sentences is unchecked.
9. **Co-play and off-screen.** Co-play: "Take turns: she stamps one, you stamp one, and say where it went ('your star is next to my cow')." Off-screen: sponge or potato stamping on scrap paper (already there), leaf printing, a bead or pasta necklace in her own pattern, a folded-and-painted butterfly (shared with Rainbow Fingers) and wrapping paper from a cut potato.

**None to add.** Stamp Studio already opens at lap level 1. What a lap child needs are the guards: a pat that sounds, a picture that finishes itself (slice 1), and a different stamp each round (fresh content 1).

### Left out on purpose

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Levels on top.** The audit's verdict is "leave: open-ended". Lean: override it for 7 Where does it go?, 8 Next to, between, above, 9 Make a pattern, 10 Butterfly paper and 11 Wrapping paper, as invitations she cannot fail, in preschool 3–7, pre-K 4–9 and school 5–11 (no lower bound moves). Or build only the fresh content and leave the ladder at 6.
2. **How an invitation level counts.** (a) Always 0 misses and 0 hints, as levels 1–6 do (every pair of rounds then climbs her, whatever she did). (b) Misses stay 0; an invitation not met when she presses the arrow counts as one hint, so two rounds with two or more unmet step her down one level, and all met is "smooth". Lean: (b) for 7–9, (a) for 10–11, which have nothing to meet. Never spoken as a failure either way.
3. **Sets.** Lean: six sets open from the start, seeded at levels 1–3, chosen from level 4; the kept picture gets an optional `scene`. An older cached build that loads a newer save drops stamps whose kind it does not know and strips `scene` on its next save, so the order is: ship the kind ids in `STAMP_KINDS` first, then the art.
4. **Counting aloud** at levels 1–3, one to ten. Lean: yes, once each, then quiet.
5. **Seven colors, three sizes** and their layout (two rows, or a pot that opens). Lean: seven colors and three sizes, layout in the slice, with a portrait screenshot.
6. **The pet tells it back.** Lean: yes, true-only sentences from the stamps; composed speech on a device is unchecked.
7. **Level order.** Level 7 (a scaffolded place word) is easier to meet than 5 and 6 (an open story). A preschooler reaches 7 only after 5 and 6. Accept it, or let the card's arrows and a grown-up pin lead there first.
8. **Mirror.** Lean: school only, with a `flip` field on the stamp, and one pure `mirror` function; do not teach it as a test.
9. **Entries below level 1** join the level-order decisions already listed.
10. **Sketches that need a decision first:** a multi-picture story (the flipbook-storage row), the pet as a stamp, a stamp from a pixel picture, and layers (behind and in front).
11. **Person checks to add:** whether a one-year-old pats or presses and what the pop teaches; whether "between" and "next to" mean something to a three- and a four-year-old; whether a 1.5-size stamp and a 0.7 stamp read on the treehouse board; whether the new stamp names, animal sounds and composed sentences read right on a device; and whether a six-year-old finds the repeating tile.
