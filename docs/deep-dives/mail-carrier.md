# Mail Carrier deep dive

*ID `mail-carrier` · bands lap, toddler, preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–8 on 2026-10-19 (`15fcd906`) · session `claude/dive-mail-carrier-finish` ([PR #620](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/620), resuming the claim in [PR #603](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/603))*

## The game today

A letter pops out of the bag at the top of the screen and she taps the house it belongs to. On the street (levels 1–5) the letter shows nothing, a door colour, a dice-face of dots or a numeral, and the mailboxes show the matching plate; the envelope flies to the box, the flag goes up and the neighbour in the window pops up and cheers. In Wonder Woods (6–8) the letter shows a neighbour's picture, Hazel's picture key says which sign is theirs, and the carrier walks Hazel's paths to the house. The learning purpose runs from cause and effect through colour and quantity matching, numeral reading and counting, to reading a map by its key, planning a route in order and comparing two path lengths. Home: Cozy Village, subject `cozy-village`; skills numerals, counting, colors, community, map reading, planning; music `STYLES.hub`; a ghost-finger bot (`touchDemo`, `autotouch` on both the street and the woods) for every mode; no couch entry. The Woodland Picnic's *invitations* step plays level 6 at the story level.

Windows: lap 1, toddler 1–2, preschool 2–4, pre-K 3–6, school 4–8. Two smooth rounds step up, two struggles step down, as everywhere.

**Content pools.** Six door colours (`DOOR_COLORS`); house numbers drawn from 1–`top` and sorted; six street neighbours (cat, dog, bear, bunny, pig, cow) dealt round the houses; one house drawing, one blue mailbox, one backdrop (`seed: 52`, so the same sky and hills every round). The woods: six neighbours without the dog (it reads as the bear at key size), five signs (acorn, mushroom, leaf, flower, star), one map graph of nine nodes and eight paths (`MAP_NODES`, `MAP_EDGES`) with one loop edge of four stones for level 8 (`SHORTER_EDGES`). Trips never send two letters in a row to one house, and a pair never sends both to one house (`makeTrips`).

**Accounting.** A wrong mailbox or house is a miss and the letter comes back; the second wrong in a letter glows the right one and counts a hint. On 7 a tap on a house that gets a later letter ("The bunny gets letter 2. Who gets letter one?") or no letter is a miss; taking a planned stop out is free. On 8 the longer path is a miss with the stone counts spoken, and the second glows the short one (a hint). A round is the whole bag; `ctx.finish` runs when the last letter lands.

**Journal.** None. **Couch.** None. **Story.** The Woodland Picnic plays level 6.

**Known gaps found in the audit** (for the build slices, not fixed here):

- **The how-to card is wrong at level 8.** The level 7 step is written `{ from: 7 }` with no `to`, so at level 8 the card shows both "tap the house for letter one, then letter two, then the green arrow" and the real level 8 step. The test for "later levels" wording does not catch an open range.
- **A lap touch off a mailbox does nothing.** Level 1 is the only lap rung and only a house answers; a touch on the road, the sky or the envelope is silent, and nothing invites the next touch when she stops.
- **No carrier on the street.** The game's name and GAMES.md say the pet delivers, but on levels 1–5 the envelope flies by itself; only the woods has a walking carrier. The cause is clear enough, but the pet is never seen doing the job.
- **One street.** The backdrop seed is fixed, every house is the same shape, and the mailbox is always blue; replay changes only colours, numbers and which neighbour peeks.
- **Six neighbours, and the duck is left off the street** while the dog is left out of the woods; both pools could be the same seven with the look-alike rule applied per level.
- **No spoken count at 3 and 5.** The right house says "Number 4!" but the dots are never counted aloud, so a child who tapped by shape alone hears no model of counting.
- **Nothing between 5 and 6.** The jump from a numbered street to a map with a key is the ladder's biggest step and pre-K lands on it after one level of counting.

## What makes sense here

Mail Carrier's identity is a letter that shows who it is for and a house that says so when it arrives. Every proposal keeps that: she reads something off the envelope (a picture, a number, a clue, a problem) and taps the house it names, and the neighbour tells her whether it is theirs. The ladder below fills the audit's holes: a helper's letter that asks *who can fix this* (the civics study's level 9, with a toddler picture-match rung), numbers past ten and a street with two sides (the step between a 1–9 street and the map), a clue in words on the map (locational language), a three-letter plan in her own order with the stones counted (the notebook's "compare routes" row, without a constraint maze), and making the key herself (the KS1 statement the current map levels only half meet).

The references' progression devices stay out: Paperboy's lost subscribers, crashes and week; the Post Box Game's race; a hub of unrelated minigames. Route constraints and a shortest-route puzzle with several blockers (the notebook's 8–10 row) stay out of this game: a blocked path is Relay Woods' *fallen log* and Robot Path's repair, and Mail Carrier's decision is always which house or which path, never which carrier (the [networks study's](subjects/networks-citizenship.md) overlap test). Dock routes for Maker Harbor wait for that land.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action.** Today it is a tap on a house with a letter in the air, which a one-year-old finds by accident. Slice 1 makes any touch at level 1 answer (a touch off a house makes the envelope wiggle and the nearest mailbox's flag wave, free) and brings an invitation after six seconds of nothing, so the first touch always does something and the second finds the box.
- **Acknowledgment and consequence.** Touch-down scales the house a little (slice 1); the envelope flies, the flag flips up with an `outBack` tween and the neighbour pops up and cheers; a wrong house says who lives there in the first person and hands the letter back. On the map the carrier walks the paths, so a plan becomes a visible walk. Slice 2 puts the pet on a small cart that rolls along the road to the chosen mailbox, so the street's delivery is seen as well as heard.
- **A concrete art and sound example.** *Who can help?* needs six tool pictures and six problem pictures, all `Graphics` in the palette: a wrench is two rounded rectangles and a notched circle in `wood.line`; a dripping tap is a short pipe, a bend and three falling blue drops, drawn on the envelope where the dots sit today; a watering can is a rounded trapezoid, a spout and three dotted drops. The sound: `sfx.pop` steps up a pentatonic note from `src/audio/notes.ts` per letter delivered, as the route level's `sfx.pop(6 + n)` does per planned stop, so the bag itself sounds like a scale filling in.
- **Variety on replay.** A seeded backdrop and house shapes (two roofs, a chimney, a window box), mailbox colours away from the door colour on level 2, seven neighbours on the street with the look-alike rule per level, a mirrored map per round seed (the graph is unchanged), six tools, nine clue shapes; never more letters per bag or smaller houses.
- **Reasoning versus precision.** Every level is a tap on a house (hit circle radius 110) or a key row; 7, 14 and 15 add taps on the same targets and one green button. No level asks for a drag, a precise drop or a quick tap, and the carrier's walk takes the time it takes.
- **Supported ending and replay.** A glow after two wrongs on every letter, the stone counts spoken on 8, the plan read back on 7 and 14; a round never ends in anything but a sticker; again replays the level with a fresh bag, next plays the one above.
- **Roles.** Solo stays the default. At 13 a grown-up can read the clue aloud a second time by tapping Hazel; at 14 two people can agree the order before the first tap (Buddy Delivery's cooperation without its wagon); at 15 one person can name a neighbour while the other finds the house.
- **Person or device checks.** Whether a one-year-old's touch lands on a house or on the envelope; whether a two-year-old reads a wrench on a sign as "the same picture" or as a thing; whether the dripping tap and the wilting flower read as problems at viewing distance (DEVICE-CHECKS already lists Mail Carrier's requests at viewing distance); whether eight houses in two rows are legible in portrait on an iPad; whether "between" and "nearest the post office" are understood from the pet's voice alone; whether a seven-year-old's second plan at 14 feels like a game or a correction.

### Left out on purpose

- **Blocked paths and route constraints** (the notebook's 8–10 row): a fallen log or a puddle that forces a re-route is Relay Woods' level and Robot Path's repair; here the decision would collapse to "the open one". Comparison stays at 8 and 14 without blockers.
- **Dock routes for Maker Harbor**: that land does not exist; the map code is reusable when it does.
- **A second woods map**: the mirrored map gives replay variety at no graph cost; a new graph would need new tests, stones and art for one level's worth of difference.
- **A wagon to steer** (Buddy Delivery): steering is Duckling Parade's; cooperation here is choosing the order together at 14.
- **Compass directions** on the picture map (KS1's other half): the map has no north, and left/right/between/nearest cover locational language at 13.
- **Odd and even by pairing** (2.OA.C.3's proof): level 11 uses parity as the street's rule the pet states, not as something she derives; a pairing activity belongs in a number game.
- **A couch entry**: a tap-the-house game gains nothing from a controller.
