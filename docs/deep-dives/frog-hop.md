# Frog Hop deep dive

*ID `frog-hop` · bands preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–7 on 2026-10-10 · session `claude/roadmap-item-14-game-content-kg2ryl` ([PR 116](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/116))*

## The game today

A frog sits on a row of lily pads, nine in view, each with a numeral under it. A sign says what to do (`4 + 3`, `7 − 3`, `3 → 8`), the pet reads it, and she taps a pad or a number card; the frog hops there, counted aloud, with a splash. Counting Cove (`lands.ts`, with Duck Pond, Monster Munch, Little Helpers, Pet Kitchen, Picture Graph, Lasso Loops and Fill and Dump). `skills`: number-line, adding, taking-away, counting-on. Music `STYLES.bubbles`. Frog Hop is also a **couch game** (team, school band). The ladder, level by level (`PLANS` in `logic.ts`, five questions each):

| Level | Mode and line | Bands whose window includes it | What she does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `find`, 0–8 | preschool, pre-K | "Hop to 5!": tap the pad with that numeral. The frog starts where it last landed |
| 2 | `next`, 0–8 | preschool, pre-K | "The frog is on 4. Hop to one more!" or one less: the pad next to it |
| 3 | `add`, 0–8, 1–4 hops | pre-K, school | `4 + 3`: tap where the frog lands |
| 4 | `back`, 0–8, 1–4 hops | pre-K, school | `7 − 3`: the same, going back |
| 5 | `gap`, 0–10, 1–5 hops | school | `3 → 8`: how many hops? Tap a number card (1–6). The pads are inert |
| 6 | `big`, 0–20, 2–6 hops either way | school | the same questions on a line to 20; the nine pads slide to keep the start and the answer in view |
| 7 | `make-ten`, starts 6–9, remainder 1–4 | school | `7 + ? = 10`: a card for the hops that reach 10, the frog hops there, then `10 + 3`: tap where the rest land |

Ranges (`LEVELS` in `index.ts`): preschool 1–2, pre-K 1–4, school 3–7. A wrong pad or card boings and the pet says what it is or "Count each hop from {n}"; after two misses (one hint per question, or per stage on level 7) levels 3–7 show the counted hop and the answer glows, levels 1–2 only glow. A right answer gives a spark, a cheer and "Ribbit!". The sticker is a lily pad with a seeded numeral 1–9 and the frog on it.

**What a round draws from.** Numbers only: no content pools. One backdrop (seed 61, sunny), one frog, one kind of pad. Questions come from `makeQuestions`, which never repeats an answer twice running and keeps start and answer inside one nine-pad window (`windowFor`; a trip is at most eight pads wide). Level 1 starts each question where the last one ended.

**Couplings.** How-to: a goal, seven step lines scoped by `LevelLine`, with level 7's open-ended (`{ from: 7 }`). A ghost finger (`touchDemo`, `autotouch()` through `answerSlot` in `logic.ts`). The couch entry (`COUCH_INFO['frog-hop']`): ring over pads or cards, `level: (stop) => [3, 3, 4, 5, 6, 6][stop]`, `demoLevel: 3`, one swapped line (`couch.hop.gap`). Browser: `BROWSER_SUITE=woods WOODS_ONLY=hop` loops levels `fromLevel…7` (a number in the script) with two wrong answers and the demonstration; `fingerdemo` plays the first and last level. No journal entry, picnic step or creation.

**Known gaps** (read from the code; none has been seen on a device).

1. **The numbers she lands on are never said.** `hopCount` speaks the hop number (`count` 1, 2, 3) while the frog crosses pads 5, 6, 7, and `right()` cheers without naming the landing pad. Counting on ("5, 6, 7") is what Siegler's board game had children say (see below), and the hint is the same "1, 2, 3" a child would already be using to count the hops.
2. **Level 7 hides the sum and tells the split.** The sign reads `8 + ? = 10`, then `10 + 3`; the whole `8 + 5` is never shown or spoken, and `hop.rest` hands over the remainder ("Hop 3 more"). She decides one piece (10 − 8) and the game does the rest of the splitting, which is the strategy itself. The make-ten rule test checks the pieces add up, not that she ever sees the sum.
3. **A pad tap on a card level is a bare squeak.** On levels 5 and 7's first stage `tapPad` plays `sfx.squeak()` and returns. The natural tap for `3 → 8` is the pad marked 8; she gets a squeak, no words and no count. It is correctly not a miss, but nothing says "tap a number card".
4. **Misses are named by size, not by kind.** `hop.wrong.find` names the numeral she tapped, which is good, but gives no direction ("further along" or "back"); at levels 3–4 a tap on the wrong side of the start (hopping the wrong way) and a tap one pad off (counting the pad the frog stands on) get the same "Not quite. Count each hop from {n}". The tick-mark-versus-interval slip is the classic number-line error.
5. **Level 7 can count two hints per question.** `wrongs` and `glowing` reset when the bridge finishes, so one question can add a hint at the card and another at the pad.
6. **Hard-coded top and caps.** `LEVELS.school.max` is 7 (not `PLANS.length`), `planFor` clamps so `describeLevel(8)` would return level 7's name, the browser loop's `7` is a number, level 7's how-to line is open-ended, `GAP_CARDS` ends at 6 (a difference of 7 has no card), `WINDOW` is 9 so a trip is at most 8 pads, and `answerSlot`, the pads and the window all assume one pad per number (`lo + slot`): a line of tens needs a stride.
7. **Sticker and icon share the pad.** `sticker()` and `HopIcon` both build `Pad` with a numeral 1–9 and the local `frogArt()`. Dots, flowers or a new frog on `Pad` would change every saved sticker, and nothing pins it (Duck Pond's [decision 6](duck-pond.md#decisions-for-the-developer) would also move `frogArt` to `src/art/`).
8. **One look.** A sunny pond with one backdrop, one pad, one frog; the same count voice every round. Nothing marks 5 or 10, though the research below calls them the benchmarks.
9. **The couch stops at 6.** `level(stop)` reaches 6, so level 7 is never played there although `control()` and `autoplay()` already handle its two stages. A trip's levels have to sit inside the band's range (`catalog.test.ts`).
10. **Level 7 has no recorded browser play.** Its [verification entry](../VERIFICATION.md) says the `woods` and `fingerdemo` plays at 7 "could not complete in this session's 31-second command window"; only typecheck and rule tests ran.
11. **Not a bug, checked:** in portrait the view is 1024 units wide and tall (`computeView`), so the nine pads at 104 spacing fit; only a phone held wide gets more room.

**Developmental and standards sources.**

- **A line is learned, not given.** Siegler's paper (**opened**) reports kindergartners placing numbers on a 0–100 line as if the line were logarithmic and second graders placing them linearly, and says counting experience alone does not produce a linear line a year or more after the counting is perfect. The multi-cue game does: bigger numbers are further along, the token moves more, more number names are said. Frog Hop already has all the cues; it has not asked for the one that tests it, **estimating where a number goes**.
- **Counting on has a path.** Children move from counting all, to counting on from the first number, to counting on from the larger (the "min" strategy), usually between about five and eight ([search summary of BSRLM and others](https://bsrlm.org.uk/wp-content/uploads/2016/02/BSRLM-IP-29-3-11.pdf), **search summary only**, ages vary by study). Saying the numerals while the hops are counted on fingers supports it.
- **Counting the marks, not the spaces.** Students often count the tick marks rather than the intervals, which shows up as being one off; the suggested move is to call the spaces "hops" ([an Ohio State paper](https://kb.osu.edu/server/api/core/bitstreams/49222dd7-185a-54ee-87a8-1c7bdf30f53d/content), **search summary only**; the page refused my fetch, 403). It is mostly documented for fractions and upper grades; the evidence for first-grade addition jumps is thin. It still names a miss worth a specific line.
- **Standards** (the pages I **opened** are marked ✓). K.CC.A.1 count by ones and tens ✓; K.CC.A.2 count forward from a given number ✓; K.CC.B.4 numbers and quantities ✓; K.CC.C.7 compare two numerals 1–10 ✓; 1.OA.C.5 relate counting to addition and subtraction ✓; 1.OA.C.6 add and subtract within 20, with strategies such as making ten ✓; 1.NBT.C.4 add within 100 ✓; 1.NBT.C.5 mentally ten more or ten less ✓; 1.NBT.C.6 subtract multiples of ten ✓; 2.NBT.A.2 skip-count by 5s, 10s and 100s ✓; 2.NBT.B.5 fluently add and subtract within 100 using place value ✓; 2.MD.B.6 represent whole numbers as lengths from 0 on a number line with equally spaced points, and sums and differences within 100 on it ✓. 1.OA.B.4 (subtraction as an unknown addend) I did not open (the page 404ed); I cite it from memory. Levels 1–7 cover K.CC.A.2, K.CC.C.7, 1.OA.C.5 and 1.OA.C.6; the number line to 100, ten more and ten less, the open line and estimating are not in the game.

## What makes sense here

**Frog Hop's own axis is the line itself:** order, distance and "how many hops", the picture that later holds counting, adding, taking away, tens and ones, fractions and negatives. Duck Pond owns the part and whole of a set; Frog Hop owns what a number *is on a line*. Its best hook comes from the research: a child who hops along a numbered line and says the numbers she lands on is doing what the board game did that raised Head Start children's number-line estimates (a spinner board game, not this one, so the effect is not claimed for Frog Hop).

Four points shape the plan.

- **Say the numbers.** The frog counts hops, never the numerals it lands on (gap 1). Counting on with the numerals is Siegler's active ingredient; counting hops is right at the levels where the *answer* is a hop count (5 and 7's bridge). Use each where it belongs.
- **Show the strategy before asking for it.** Level 7 teaches bridging by doing the split for her (gap 2). Back over ten and the stop at ten walk the same road one step at a time: the sum shown, then the split spoken, then a stop she may choose, then no help. A hint that leads somewhere is the demonstration itself.
- **Misses by kind.** The ladder is full of one-off and wrong-way slips; each deserves a line that names it, tested by a classification.
- **A school window of ten levels is fine, a two-digit line is the next picture, and an estimate is the check on it.** Tens (2.NBT, 1.NBT.C.5), tens and ones on an open line (the empty-number-line method) and estimating on 0–100 (2.MD.B.6) are school-band work that appears in no other island game's ladder. Fractions on the line (3.NF) and negatives (6.NS) are older than the school band and wait for the "does Wonder Woods grow" decision.

**Arc by band.** Lap: the frog hops where she taps and the notes rise (below level 1). Toddler: hop to the pad with the dots, 1 to 5 (below level 1). Preschool: find a number, then the next and the one before (levels 1–2). Pre-K: counting on and back, the missing number and who is further along (levels 3–4 and two entries). School: counting the hops, a line to 20, bridging through ten with help and then without, subtraction as the distance between two numbers, tens, tens and ones, and where a number goes.

**Overlaps, and who owns what** (the other write-ups agree).

- **Duck Pond** owns composing and decomposing sets, *ten and some more* and doubles; Frog Hop owns *crossing ten on a line* (it says so in its write-up). The frog is shared art (Duck Pond decision 6): moving `frogArt` to `src/art/` must leave this game's frog unchanged.
- **Bubble Pop** owns counting sequences (its levels 12–14: start at a number, count back, count by tens and fives) as popping in order. Frog Hop's tens level is **ten more and ten less as a jump** (1.NBT.C.5), a place-value fact, not a sequence recited. If Bubble Pop's count-by-tens is built first, level 11 is still a different decision (where does it land), but the two should share the spoken number words. Skip-hops of 2 and 5 stay a sketch because of this overlap and Little Helpers' equal groups.
- **Picture Graph** owns "how many more than" from a graph; Frog Hop's gap is the distance between two marks, never a comparison of two groups.
- **Market Stall** owns coins, **Pet Kitchen** owns fractions of food (its Thirds level is built), **Inchworm Measure** owns length with a ruler.

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

IDs, saves and the sticker do not change for any of this (the sticker is pinned first, see build slice 1). Every new picture is code-drawn and every sound synthesized.

1. **Say the numbers she lands on** (levels 3, 4 and 6). On a counted hop the frog says the pad's numeral ("5… 6… 7") and a small tally dot appears over the frog for each hop, so the hop count and the numerals are both seen. Levels 5, 7 and the new bridge levels keep the hop count ("1, 2, 3") because the answer is a count. On a right answer the frog names where it landed ("Seven!"). Test: the spoken numerals equal the pads crossed, in order, and end on the target; the tally equals the hops.
2. **Show the whole sum on level 7.** The sign reads `8 + 5`, the first stage asks "5 hops in all. How many reach 10?" and after the bridge "That is 2. 5 in all, so 3 more. Where does the frog land?" The split is modeled aloud, not hidden (gap 2); level 10 later puts it on her. Test: `toTen + remaining = hops`, and a voice line carries all three numbers.
3. **Misses named by kind** (a small classifier in `logic.ts`, `missKind`). Level 1: "That is 5. 7 is further along!" or "…back this way", with the frog looking toward it. Levels 3–4: a tap on the wrong side of the start ("The frog hops forward, to bigger numbers"), a tap one pad short or long ("Count the hops, not the pad the frog stands on"). Level 5: a card one more than the answer gets the same "count the hops, not the numbers", one fewer "you missed a hop". Test: for every question the classification of every wrong pad or card is defined, "off by one" is exactly an answer ±1, and a hint still comes after two misses.
4. **A pad on a card question is a nudge, not a squeak** (levels 5 and 7's first stage). The second inert tap says "Tap a number card!" and the cards bounce once. Not a miss. Test: an inert tap never changes `misses` or `hints`.
5. **One hint per question on level 7** (a rule): the bridge stage and the landing stage share one hint, as everywhere else (gap 5). Test: a question that earns hints in both stages reports one.
6. **Dots under the numerals on levels 1–2** (the numeral stays; the sign shows the dots too). Pre-K and preschool children who do not yet read "6" match the dots; fives are grouped. Drawn by a separate layer so the sticker and icon (gap 7) are untouched. Test: a pad's dots equal its numeral and fit inside the 104-unit pad.
7. **Landmark flowers on 5, 10, 15 and 20** from level 3: the benchmarks ([Number Frames](https://www.mathlearningcenter.org/apps/number-frames) uses 5 and 10) so a child can find 8 as "three after the flower". Never on 0. Test: only multiples of 5 above 0 carry one, none changes a hit area.
8. **New lines** (every level). A seeded scene per round: the lily pond, **stepping stones in a brook**, **a flower path** (the frog hops from bloom to bloom) and an **evening pond** (the night pond with fireflies Duck Pond plans is the same art and is built once). The pad's numeral and hit area are identical in every scene; the sticker stays the lily pad. Test: every scene lays out at four views with the pads, the sign and the cards clear of the pet.
9. **The frog's voice** on a landing (`sfx.croak()` exists) and a frog that hops with a little squash. No test needed beyond the existing ones.
10. **Co-play and off-screen** that match: "Say the numbers with the frog as it lands" for levels 3–4; "ask her which way the frog hops" for 3–4; "hop to ten first" for 7–10. Off-screen: a tape or chalk line (already), **hopscotch to 10 or 20 calling the squares**, stairs counted up and down, a ruler on the table with a counter hopping 3 more.

### Left out on purpose

- **A timer, speed scoring, a race, lives, falling in the water or a winner** (*Frog Jump*'s time pads, *Frogger*, the second player's race, the chutes in *Chutes and Ladders*). A wrong answer boings and teaches; the frog never drowns.
- **A spinner or a die.** A random number is not the learning; the generator deals a fair one.
- **Multiplication tables as hops** and **skip counting as a level** (Bubble Pop's sequences, Little Helpers' equal groups). Kept to a sketch above.
- **Negative numbers and fractions as levels**: older than the school band, and fractions of food are Pet Kitchen's.
- **Coins and money**: Market Stall. **Rulers and length in units**: Inchworm Measure.
- **A "how many more than" between two groups**: Picture Graph. **Bye-bye ducks and a countdown song**: Duck Pond's entry; the frog does not need a second song.
- **A journal entry, a picnic step or a kept creation.** A number line is not a discovery about the world.
- **A scored grown-up game on the child's island.** Closeness scores are a couch matter.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Levels on top and the school window.** Lean: build 8, 9, 10 and 11 (ready or nearly), define 12 and 13 after a child has played the rest, in a school window of 3–13 (no lower bound moves). The ladder has two inversions: 9 (count up, within 20) is easier than 8, and 11 (tens) is easier than 9 and 10 in arithmetic. Accept them (the how-to arrows reach any level), or fold them into the level-order decision.
2. **Counting on with the numerals.** Lean: say the landing numerals at levels 3, 4, 6 and 11, and the hop count where the answer is a hop count (5, 7, 8, 9, 10). Or say both on every hop ("one, five; two, six"), which doubles the counting time.
3. **The stop at ten at level 10.** Lean: only 10 is offered as a free stop; any other tap is a landing attempt. Or accept any pad as a waypoint (more freedom, a more complicated hint).
4. **Fixing level 7's hidden split** (fresh content 2) is a small fix I recommend building first, not a design choice; say if the told remainder is deliberate scaffolding to keep.
5. **Level 13's scoring.** Lean: unscored estimates plus one ticked-line placement per round. Or no counted round at all (then the level can never step her down).
6. **A grown-up window** for G1–G5 (hundreds, halves, below zero, a 0–1000 estimate and the couch duel): needs a `grownup` band or a couch-side plan table (as Jelly Drums decision 5). Lean: after Lands stage B, starting with G1.
7. **A shared frog.** The frog's art moves to `src/art/` with Duck Pond (its decision 6); lean yes, and this game's frog, sticker and icon must not change (pin them first).
8. **Level order.** The four entries below level 1 join the existing level-order rows.
9. **Person checks to add:** whether a six-year-old counts the pad the frog stands on (the off-by-one line); whether landing numerals aloud help or crowd the counting; whether the stop at ten is chosen; whether tens words ("forty, fifty") come out clearly with device speech; whether "100" reads on a pad; whether the open line's small hops are readable at 8 units each; whether a three-year-old uses the dots on levels 1–2; whether a brook and a flower path are calm or confusing.
