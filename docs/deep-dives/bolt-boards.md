# Bolt Boards deep dive

*ID `bolt-boards` · bands toddler, preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–7 on 2026-11-06 · session `claude/bolt-boards-dive` ([PR #1331](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1331))*

## The game today

Wooden boards hang on the barn's pegboard wall from big bolts, with a fruit or a peeking friend behind each one. She taps a free bolt and it spins out. A board with two bolts stays put, one with one bolt swings and hangs straight below it, and one with none slides down to the woodpile so its fruit hops into the basket. A bolt under a higher board is covered and waits. At levels 6–7 every bolt she takes out has to go into an open hole on the wall, so the order she chooses can leave her stuck, and a free undo takes it back. The learning purpose is cause and effect through a fastener, support (two, one or no bolts), and then taking things apart in order. It was built from its [definition](../definitions/bolt-boards.md) ([PR #681](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/681)). Every pose is computed exactly in `logic.ts`: there is no physics engine.

**Content pools.** A frozen, solver-checked list in `puzzles.ts` generated by `scripts/bolt-boards-puzzles.ts`: 10 boards per level (11 at level 6, with the authored first board), each mirrored by seed. Three fruits (apple, pear, plum), six board colors with a pattern each, and six friends from `CRITTERS` (cow, duck, pig, cat, dog, bunny; `bear` and `pip` are unused). Board shapes are always rectangles.

**Accounting today.** Covered taps are free at 1–3 and one miss from 4. A closed hole is free at 6 (lit) and one miss at 7. The two-miss glow counts one hint. At 6–7 a wander hint fires after `max(6, 2 × par)` removals, and "Stuck!" pulses undo without counting. Levels 1–5 can never dead-end, which a 200-sequence test asserts.

**Known gaps.**
- **Pre-K's jump.** Pre-K's window goes from level 5 (a toolbox with no limit) to level 6, which adds holes, two taps and dead ends at the same time. Nothing introduces the hole-parking action before the planning it carries.
- **Variety.** Ten boards per level and three fruits is the thinnest pool among the chunk-26 puzzles (Jar Sort and Ferry Jam carry more). At levels 1–2 a seed can repeat a board within a few rounds.
- **School tops out at 7**, and every level so far is taking things apart. The reverse, putting a thing back together (where the same "on top" rule has to be run backwards), is not in the game.
- **Person checks.** The [device check](../DEVICE-CHECKS.md) (whether a toddler connects the bolt to the drop, whether a partly covered head reads as covered, whether undo is found at level 6) is still open.
- **Couch.** None; the definition's later candidate (`src/couch/focus.ts` over bolts and holes) is unchanged.
- **IDEAS.** No rows; the IDEAS candidate was archived when the game was built.

**Learning anchors.** The definition's Willatts (1984) means-end study and the Tower of Hanoi summary (Klahr and Robinson 1981, via [Voelter and Call 2014](https://research-repository.st-andrews.ac.uk/bitstream/10023/8634/1/Voelter_and_Call_2014_Cognition_AM.pdf)) still set the ladder: one means for one end first, and dead ends only for pre-K and up. [NGSS 2-PS1-3](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/2-ps1-3-matter-and-its-interactions) ("an object made of a small set of pieces can be disassembled and made into a new object") is the school anchor for level 9: the second-grade expectation is both halves, and so far the game teaches only the first.

## What makes sense here

- **More boards and more fruit first.** The cheapest gain. Regenerate the frozen list to 20 boards per level, by appending boards from a second generator seed so the existing ten (and level 1's and level 6's authored first boards) stay first. Add three code-drawn fruits (orange, strawberry, banana) beside apple, pear and plum. Add the unused `bear` to the friends. The rules, accounting and bot are unchanged.
- **Teach the hole before the plan.** Pre-K meets holes, two taps and dead ends all at once at level 6. An in-between level where holes are plentiful (every reachable state leaves an open hole for every free bolt) teaches the lift-then-park action and the "a swing can cover a hole" idea with nothing to get stuck on. It is appended as level 8 and placed between 5 and 6 by `order`, as Ferry Jam's pre-K entries were.
- **Put it back together.** The physical take-apart toys and 2-PS1-3 both have a second half: build it again. Reversing the game's own rule makes a real school puzzle. Boards lie on the woodpile, a picture card shows the wall as it should look, and she bolts the boards back up. A board can only be bolted up while no board already up covers its bolt holes, so the bottom layer goes first. That is the "on top" rule run backwards, and the reverse of any removal order is a valid build order, so solvability comes from the existing solver. It is a new interaction (pick a board, then tap its glowing holes), so it needs its own definition before a build.
- **Left aside for now:** Screw Jam's keys and locks (a remembered rule rather than a visible one; a sketch for after level 9), ropes and chains, odd plate shapes (they would need polygon overlap in place of the exact rectangle test, and add no new idea), and moving or rotating platforms (motor timing).

The long arc: toddler taps a bolt and sees a board fall; preschool learns two-bolts-hold and on-top; pre-K learns which boards are in the way, then parks bolts in holes and finally plans around dead ends; school plans with holes for a friend, then builds the wall back from a picture.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action.** Level 1: tap any of three big bolt heads. Level 8: lift a bolt and tap any glowing hole, with no wrong hole while the glow is on. Level 9: tap the board on the woodpile that the picture shows at the back.
- **Touch and consequence.** Touch-down brightens the bolt head and starts its turn. The tap gives the pentatonic ratchet, then the board stays, swings with a creak or slides down with a soft thump. The return to readiness is the next free bolt head, which is never hidden. At level 9 the picked board lifts off the woodpile and its bolt holes glow on the wall. Each bolt goes in with the ratchet played downward, and the board settles into its pose.
- **Variety on replay.** Twenty boards per level, mirrored by seed. Six fruits, seven friends and six board colors. The rule never changes with the dressing.
- **Reasoning versus motor precision.** Every action is one tap on a 106-unit target. Nothing moves while she decides, and taps during a swing are ignored rather than counted. The reasoning is "what is on top" and "where will it hang", which are both visible. Nothing has to be remembered except at the key-and-lock sketch, which is why it is left aside.
- **Experiments stay welcome.** Taking down a board that was not in the way, putting a lifted bolt back, and undo all stay free. At level 9, a board bolted up out of order is not refused silently. It goes up, and the pet notes that the next board cannot reach its holes now ("The green board is covering its holes"). Undo is free, and the only miss is a tap on a covered hole, counted as at level 7.
- **Supported ending.** The glow after two misses and the wander hint lead to the solver's next move at every level. Each round ends with the cart (or, at level 9, the finished wall matching its picture with a little bell), then one sticker.
- **Co-play.** Built: a grown-up says which board is on top. At level 8, one player lifts and the other picks the hole. At level 9, one player reads the picture aloud ("the red one is at the back") and the other bolts.
- **Art and feel example (code-drawn).** The picture card at level 9 is the target wall drawn small with the same board `Graphics` at about a quarter scale, on a cream card with a pin at the top. As each board goes up in the right place, its tiny copy on the card gets a tick drawn as two rounded strokes, so progress shows without numbers. No new art system.
- **Needs a person or device.** The existing device check; whether a four-year-old finds the second tap of level 8 without the glow being explained; whether the picture card at level 9 is read as "make this" or as decoration on an iPad.

### Younger-band openings

None proposed now. Level 1 already plays in toddler with three big bolts. A lap entry (one board, one bolt as big as a hand, tap anywhere and the nearest bolt turns) is a sketch. It waits on the open device check of whether a toddler connects the bolt to the drop, and adding a lap band to the game is the developer's call.

### Easier entries and levels between existing ones

Level 8 is the in-between level: an appended ID placed by `order`, with no renumbering. Default windows: pre-K `{ min: 2, max: 8, order: [2, 3, 4, 5, 8, 6] }`; school `{ min: 4, max: 8, order: [4, 5, 8, 6, 7] }`, and `[4, 5, 8, 6, 7, 9]` once level 9 exists. `entryLevels` stays unset: a birthday from pre-K at 8 into school lands on 8, which school lists. Toddler and preschool are unchanged. Saved levels keep their meaning.

### Left out on purpose

Move limits, paid or earned extra holes, boosters and timers. The color-box sort. Ropes, chains, and key-and-lock bolts (a sketch after level 9; a remembered rule, not a visible one). Odd plate shapes and moving platforms. Physics plates that could fall unpredictably. Any visible par or star rating.
