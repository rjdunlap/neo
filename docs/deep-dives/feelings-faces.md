The [social-emotional subject study](subjects/social-emotional.md) (2026-10-17) already reviewed this game's place among the catalog's social-emotional play and asked for two things from its own dive: a prop-and-reaction play definition and an **Ask First** design that reviews the hug and event assumptions. ## The game today

**What she does.** The pet stands on a rug in a cozy room (`src/games/feelings-faces/index.ts`). At level 1 four feeling bubbles along the bottom change its face, with a sound (giggle, sigh, yawn, squeak), a spoken word and a small effect (confetti, a rain cloud, drifting rings, stars); a sad pet tapped gets a hug and cheers up. From level 2 each round asks four questions: match the pet's face, find a named face, choose what helps a need, watch a small happening and say how the pet feels, find the friend who feels a named feeling, or watch a happening and choose what would help. A wrong card boings and wobbles, the pet says what that card shows ("That face looks sleepy"), and after two misses the right card glows and "Try the glowing one!" plays. The big pet replaces the corner guide and repeats the instruction when tapped. One sticker per round, including with the glow.

**Learning purpose.** Emotion vocabulary (happy, sad, sleepy, surprised), reading a face, cause and feeling, and care: what helps. Skills `emotions`, `empathy`, `vocabulary`; land Cozy Village; music `STYLES.paint`.

**The ladder** (`FEELING_PLANS` in `logic.ts`; windows lap 1–2, toddler 2–4, preschool 3–6, pre-K 5–8, no `order` or `entryLevels` yet):

| Level | Mode | Bands whose window holds it | What she decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `play`, four bubbles | lap | Nothing is wrong: which feeling to see next. Ends after every feeling and six taps, or twelve taps (`playDone`) |
| 2 | `mirror`, happy or sad, two cards | lap, toddler | Which of two face cards matches the face the pet is making |
| 3 | `name`, happy/sad/sleepy, two cards | toddler, preschool | Which of two faces is the one named aloud |
| 4 | `name`, all four, three cards | toddler, preschool | Which of three faces is the one named |
| 5 | `help`, three cards | preschool, pre-K | Which helper answers the need shown: hug, pillow, apple or scarf |
| 6 | `why`, three cards | preschool, pre-K | After a happening (balloon flies off, a present, a jack-in-the-box, the moon rises), which face shows how the pet feels; the pet stays `calm` until answered so the happening carries the answer |
| 7 | `friends`, four friends | pre-K | Which of four friends, each wearing a different feeling, feels the one named |
| 8 | `care`, four cards | pre-K | After a happening, which helper would help |

**Content pools.** `FEELINGS` 4 (happy, sad, sleepy, surprised; the critter's `Mood` type also has `sing` and `calm`, used as the neutral face). `NEEDS` 4 with one `HELPER` each. `EVENTS` 4. `CARE_SCENARIOS` 2 (balloon and moon), so level 8's four questions show each happening twice. `FRIENDS` 6 of the 8 critters (`cat`, `bunny`, `bear`, `pig`, `dog`, `cow`; not `pip` or `duck`), four dealt per round. Question order comes in shuffled passes, never the same prompt twice in a row (`questions()`); the tests cover answer-once-among-distinct-options, friends all different, and the ghost finger's `choiceToTap` and `bubbleToTap` on every level and seed.

**Known gaps** (write-up findings, not fixes):

1. **Level 8 repeats itself.** Two scenarios over four questions: every round is balloon, moon, balloon, moon in some order. The present and jack-in-the-box end happy or surprised and rightly have no "help" question, so the fix is new scenarios, not reuse.
2. **Levels 5 and 6 are four of four.** With exactly four needs and four happenings, a round always shows all of them; replay varies only the order and the distractors.
3. **The hug is the only answer to sad.** `HELPERS.sad === 'hug'`, and `feel.hug` says "Hugs make it better" as a general truth. The subject study asks this dive to state it as the pet's own preference and to let an older level hear what a friend wants before helping.
4. **No anger, no fear.** Widen and Russell's sequence (below) has children labeling anger before surprise; the game teaches surprise and sleepy (a state, not an emotion) and skips the two feelings a two-year-old meets most. Adding them needs new critter brows and mouths and a tone review, so it is a decision.
5. **Lap plays a choosing level.** Lap's window is 1–2, and level 2 asks a one-year-old to compare two cards. A smoother first rung is a single big target.
6. **The how-to card's finish line** ("The round ends when the question is answered") understates four questions, and level 1's note is right. The pet's repeat-on-tap is undocumented on the card.
7. **No journal entries and no couch entry.** Nothing from a round comes home; fine today, and the rework's collection ([PET-INTEGRATION](../rework/PET-INTEGRATION.md)) is where that would change.
8. **Person checks never recorded:** whether a one-year-old reaches the bottom-row bubbles from a lap, whether the `calm` face reads as "waiting" rather than "nothing", whether the rain cloud beside a sad pet reads as sad or as weather.

Couch: none, and none proposed; this is a lap-to-table touch game.

**Developmental references.** Widen and Russell's model, as summarized by [Maassarani et al. 2014](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4044975) (opened): children's first emotion word is *happy*; at about 40 months they use two (happy plus sad or angry), then three (happy, sad, angry); fear or surprise arrives around 56 months, all five around 62 months and disgust last, around 66 months; early categories are broad and narrow with age. Their 5- and 6-year-olds were accurate for happy, angry and sad and much less so for fear, surprise and disgust. The [CDC 2-year checklist](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/digital-online-checklist/2-years.html) (opened) lists "Notices when others are hurt or upset, like pausing or looking sad when someone is crying" and "Looks at your face to see how to react in a new situation" as 24-month social milestones. Head Start's ELOF (search summaries of alignment documents, the official PDF not opened) frames infant goals as *expressing* a range of emotions and later *recognizing and interpreting* others'. A 2026 Essex mirror study ([summary](https://www.alphagalileo.org/en-gb/Item-Display/ItemId/274234), search) found four-month-olds given a mirror toy showed more sensorimotor response to others' expressions but no more copying: evidence that watching faces matters early, not that an app teaches a baby to read them.

What these say for the ladder: a one-year-old is in the *express and watch* stage, so lap is cause-and-effect with a face and a grown-up making it back; naming begins around three with happy and sad (toddler top, preschool); anger belongs before surprise if it is added; perspective (two people, one happening) and asking what someone wants are school-age reasoning, which supports the audit's school window.

## What makes sense here

**Fits.** A single big target at lap. Happenings as the content engine: each new prop with a reaction (Avokiddo) is one more level-6 question and, where it leaves a need, one more level-8 scenario. Answers that come from what a friend *says* (Breathe, Think, Do; Sago's disappointed friend), so the child is not guessing a universal rule. Several right ways to help, including quiet time and asking a grown-up. Perspective at school: two friends, one happening (the audit's row). A pet that is sad or tired and ends *calm* rather than always happy (the subject study's rule). The energy axis as a gentle school rung.

**Does not fit.** A camera or any image of the child. Breathing exercises or counting-to-calm instructions until a named reviewer clears them (Belly Breaths stays a proposal in [IDEAS](../IDEAS.md#candidates), gated in [WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER](../WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER.md)). Any record of how the child herself feels. Photos of people. Grading a feeling as correct for a person: on every level the answer is a face the pet is visibly making, a word that was spoken, a happening that was shown, or a preference the friend stated.

**Arc.** Lap pokes a face and hears the word; toddler matches and helps with two cards; preschool names, helps and watches why; pre-K finds the friend and joins a happening to a help; school hears two friends feel differently about one thing, asks before helping and works a happening out backwards. Within the rework, the friends on levels 7, 11 and 12 become her own creatures when the cast exists, and the feeling words she has met are a natural journal page; neither is needed for the slices below.

## Experience and presentation

**First unsupported action.** Tap the pet (proposed level 9) or a bubble (level 1): the face changes, a sound plays, the word is spoken, a small effect falls or rises. Nothing to read, nothing to choose correctly. The pleasure is the face: the critter's brows and mouth already change (`Critter.setMood`), the hop and poke animations react to the touch itself.

**Touch acknowledgment and consequence.** Every card already squashes on touch (`node.scale.set(0.88)` then `outBack`). Proposed: a tapped *happening prop* (balloon, present, moon) also wobbles and replays its sound, so a touch on the scenery is never silent (today it does nothing). The consequence of a right answer is bodily: the pet makes the face and the matching particle effect fires; a helper floats over and the need ends (snow stops, the bowl fills). Readiness returns when the cards redeal with their staggered pop-in.

**A concrete art and sound example.** The rain-shower happening for level 6: `puffs()` draws a cloud over the window (the existing `rainCloud` code already drizzles with `particles.burst` at gravity 500), the window's `swatch.blue.light` darkens with a 0.4 s tween, `sfx.whoosh()` then four `sfx.tick()`s as drops, and the pet's ears droop through `setMood('sad')` only after she answers. For level 11, two friends at 0.62 scale stand either side of the prop; the one who likes rain hops and `sfx.giggle()`s while the other sighs: two different faces from one cloud, drawn with nothing new.

**Variety on replay.** The pet is hers (`ctx.petSpec`), so the faces are her pet's. Friends shuffle from the whole cast. Happenings grow from four to eight, scenarios from two to four, so levels 6 and 8 draw four of a pool rather than all of it. Voice lines gain second variants. The sticker already picks a random friend and feeling.

**Reasoning versus reading, memory and motor.** No reading anywhere; the word is spoken and the pet repeats it on tap. Memory load is one happening, just shown, except level 13 (one happening ago). Motor demand is a tap on a 180×200 card or a 300×330 friend; the proposed lap rung is the whole pet.

**Supported ending and replay.** Every question ends with the glow after two misses; a round is four questions and a sticker; the celebration's green "again" replays the level. Level 9 and level 1 end on their own after a few taps and never fail.

**Co-play roles.** Lap and toddler: the grown-up makes the face back (the co-play hint). Level 11: one person speaks for each friend ("I liked the loud drum!" "I didn't"). Level 12: the grown-up can voice the friend's wish before the bubble appears. Solo play is always complete.

**Needs a person or device.** Whether a one-year-old on a lap reaches a bubble at y = view.h − 105 (the bottom row) or only the pet; whether the `calm` face reads as "waiting"; whether "sleepy" is understood as a feeling or a bedtime; whether the two reactions on level 11 read as *about the same thing*; how a child reacts to a friend who says "I want to sit by myself" (level 12) and whether the line needs softening.

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

- **Four more happenings** for level 6 (`EVENTS` 4 → 8), each one prop, one animation, one sound, one line: **rain shower** → sad (cloud at the window, drops, `whoosh` and `tick`); **a butterfly lands on the nose** → surprised (a small orange shape from `props.ts` flutters in, `squeak`); **a lullaby** → sleepy (three `bell` notes falling, the rug dims a little; no night overlay, that is the moon's); **a bouncing ball rolls in** → happy (a ball from `prop()` bounces across, `pop`). Pool test: every event maps to a `FEELING`, distinct feelings are not required (two events may both cause sad), but a round of four never shows the same event twice (already true of `questions()`).
- **Two more care scenarios** so level 8's four questions are four different stories (`CARE_SCENARIOS` 2 → 4): **the ice cream falls** → sad → hug; **snow starts** → cold → scarf. Both reuse existing needs and helpers; the hungry scenario (**the picnic basket is empty**) → apple is a third if the art budget allows. Rule test: the four scenarios cover four distinct helpers, so one round can show each helper as the answer once.
- **Friends from the whole cast** (`FRIENDS` 6 → 8, adding `pip` and `duck`). `duck` has a bill; the critter code already notes brows read on a bill.
- **The hug as the pet's own preference.** Reword `feel.hug` ("{pet} loves a hug!") and `feel.need-sad` ("{pet} feels sad. What helps {pet}?"), and let the level-8 helped line say "That is just what {pet} wanted." The mapping sad → hug stays, stated about this pet; no universal claim. Second variants for `feel.that`, `feel.helped`, `feel.how`.
- **Scenery that answers.** A tap on a happening prop wobbles it and replays its sound; a tap on the rug does nothing new. No miss is counted.
- **Card and lines.** Finish: "Four questions, then a sticker; free play ends by itself." Steps gain "Tap the pet to hear it again." These are strings true at every level.

### Younger-band openings

Lap already has the game. Level 9 above is the opening *within* lap: the one-year-old's first rung is the pet itself, not a row of bubbles. The grown-up's job is on the card: "Make the face back, and say the word."

### Easier entries and levels between existing ones

Level 9 (lap entry, `entryLevels`) and level 10 (toddler, between 2 and 3) above. Both use the built `order` support; the former level-order blocker does not apply. Toddler's top stays 4; a toddler who was at level 3 or 4 keeps it.

### Left out on purpose

- **Belly Breaths** (hold the tummy, the pet breathes): reviewer-gated per the subject study; stays in IDEAS, not in this ladder.
- **A camera photo booth**: no images of the child, ever.
- **Angry and scared as feelings** in levels 4, 6 and 11: developmentally earlier than surprise, but they need two new critter moods (brows, mouth, maybe a shake) and a tone review of the happenings that cause them (a knocked-over tower, a loud noise). Decision 2.
- **A Mood Meter rung** ("how big is the feeling?"): the energy axis is a real second dimension, but it asks the child to grade a feeling; keep as a later school idea, not a level.
- **Mixed feelings** (two at once, the Color Monster's tangle): no critter face for it; later.
- **Sharing or snack scenes**: Monster Munch, Pet Kitchen and Little Helpers hold those.
- **Couch play**: no controller version; the game is a lap game.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **A school window (levels 11–13).** The [roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#more-to-play-in-every-band) already lists "Feelings Faces to school" as a window still to give; the subject study marks Ask First's school window unapproved. **Default:** school `order [11, 12, 13]` ships with slice 4; pre-K ends at 11. Alternative: pre-K `[5, 6, 7, 8, 11, 12]` and no school band.
2. **Angry and scared.** New critter moods and the happenings that cause them. **Default:** not in slices 1–6; a later content slice after the subject study's review (its WAITING row covers the review).
3. **Level 12's words.** "Quiet spot", "a grown-up" (Hazel at the door) and a friend who ends calm rather than happy are proposals written for the fictional friend; **default:** build with these lines and the subject study's reviewer reads them before `main`, or they ship under the standing authorization if no reviewer is named by then.
4. **A feelings journal page** (the words she has met, filed as `RoundResult.discoveries`): **default:** wait for the rework's collection model (RW05); not in these slices.
