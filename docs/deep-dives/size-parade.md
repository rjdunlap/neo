# Size Parade deep dive

*ID `size-parade` · bands toddler–school · levels 1–8 on 2026-10-19 · session `claude/dive-size-parade-finish` ([PR #617](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/617)), finishing the claim in [PR #600](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/600)*

## The game today

A row of look-alike friends stands on a cream parade ground with their feet on one baseline, so only their height differs. In the **pick** levels (1–4) the pet asks for the biggest or the smallest friend and she taps it; a right tap chimes and the row re-deals, four questions to a round. In the **line** levels (5–8) three or five friends wait on the ground under a row of empty oval spots; she drags the next friend in size to the glowing spot until the line is full. The learning purpose is direct comparison of a measurable attribute and seriation (ordering a set by size), the kindergarten and first-grade measurement standards below. Subject `puzzle-peaks`, skills comparison, measurement and ordering, home land Puzzle Peaks; music is the hub style; the sticker and hub icon are three ducks in a size stair.

**The ladder** (`SIZE_PLANS` in `logic.ts`; windows toddler 1–4, preschool 3–6, pre-K 5–8, school 7–8; no `order` or `entryLevels`):

| Level | Plan | Bands whose window includes it | What she decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | Find the bigger of two friends | toddler | Which of two is bigger (the ratio is 2.4) |
| 2 | Find the smaller of two friends | toddler | Which of two is smaller |
| 3 | Find the biggest of three friends | toddler, preschool | The biggest of three (adjacent heights differ by about 37 units) |
| 4 | Find the smallest of four friends | toddler, preschool | The smallest of four (about 25 units apart) |
| 5 | Line up three friends, smallest first | preschool, pre-K | Which friend comes next, three times |
| 6 | Line up three friends, biggest first | preschool, pre-K | The same, descending |
| 7 | Line up five friends, smallest first | pre-K, school | Five in a row (about 18 units apart at game scale, the biggest 127 units tall) |
| 8 | Line up five friends, biggest first | pre-K, school | The same, descending |

**What a round draws from.** One animal per deal from a pool of three (`duck`, `bunny`, `cat`), the positions shuffled by the seeded RNG. Sizes are fixed fractions of the biggest (`sizeScale`: 0.42 up to 1.0 in equal steps), so every round at a level shows the same five heights in a different order. The pick levels show a cue of two circles and an arrow (small→big or big→small); the line levels show small arrows between the spots. A tap target is a fixed 154×190 rectangle around every friend, so the smallest friend (53 units tall) is as easy to touch as the biggest. Misses: a wrong friend boings, the pet names the size still waiting, and two wrongs in one question glow the right friend and count a hint. The ghost finger (`sizeTouch`) taps or carries the next friend in size.

**Known gaps** (from reading `index.ts`, `logic.ts`, the tests, the card and the voice lines; nothing was played):

1. **No parade in Size Parade.** A picked friend fades to half and the row re-deals 0.8 s later; a finished line sits still while the pet says "What a lovely parade!" Nobody marches. The pleasure of the action is the chime, not a consequence.
2. **Superlatives at two.** `size.pick` always says "biggest" or "smallest", so level 1 asks for "the biggest friend" of two. The how-to card promises "bigger, smaller, biggest or smallest, as asked", and K.MD.A.2 is comparative language for two objects.
3. **Pitch rises with size.** `sfx.bell(5 + rank)` makes the bigger friend chime higher. Infants map big to low and small to high (Walker et al. 2010, recalled, not opened), and Bubble Pop's dive already plans the opposite mapping; the two games would contradict each other.
4. **Silent touches.** A tap on the sky, the ground or the cue picture does nothing. In the line levels a right friend dropped on a later spot, or anywhere but the glowing spot, floats home without a word; only a wrong friend on the glowing spot speaks.
5. **Portrait overlap at five.** The friends spread between x = 165 and `view.w − 40` with a 175 gap; in portrait (`view.w` 768) five friends sit about 140 apart while each tap rectangle is 154 wide, so neighbouring targets overlap by about 14 units and the one drawn on top wins, the pitfall AGENTS.md names. A test pins nothing about spacing, and no portrait check is recorded.
6. **Nothing pins the size steps.** The rule test asserts only that bigger ranks are bigger; no test asserts the smallest adjacent difference (18 units at five) or the smallest friend's tap area.
7. **Three friends, one ground, one cue.** Replay at a level differs by animal (three) and order alone; the sticker is always three ducks whatever she lined up.
8. **No lap band, no journal page, no couch entry, no story use.** The ladder's lowest rung is a two-choice tap that already needs the word "bigger".
9. **Person checks never recorded:** whether a two-year-old reads the circle-and-arrow cue or ignores it, whether "the smallest friend still waiting" is heard as a hint, and whether the 40-unit drag lift (the friend rides above the finger) works for a first drag at level 5.

## What makes sense here

- **Keep size the only cue, and keep the baseline.** Friends share an appearance and stand on one line, which is the endpoint alignment the standards and Montessori both insist on. Mixed animals in one row would make "biggest" a question about ears.
- **More friends is not a new decision.** The ladder audit was right to leave the school window alone on quantity; the trajectory's ordering ceiling is six and five is already there. The new decisions are three-term (the middle one), fit (a size matched to a size), insertion, a hidden order found by pair tests, and an indirect comparison with a third object. Each is a different thought, not a bigger number.
- **The youngest rungs are a toy, then a question.** The nesting-cup evidence says a one-year-old pulls apart and combines before she orders; the first rung makes one friend big and little under her finger, the second asks for the big one of two with every touch answered.
- **Another attribute, same action.** Ordering five shades (IDEAS' Shade Lineup) is the drag-into-line interaction with the size cue swapped for lightness; Color Garden's dive names three-shade grading as its own ceiling and points sorting here, so the line belongs in Size Parade (decision 2).
- **The arc.** Lap: size words as cause and effect. Toddler: bigger or smaller of two, biggest of a few, two in a line. Preschool: lines of three, the middle one, who fits. Pre-K: lines of five, insertion, shades. School: hidden heights (sorting by pair tests) and the measuring ribbon (indirect comparison), then Inchworm Measure takes units and Seesaw Balance takes weight. The rework's [casting proof](../rework/PET-INTEGRATION.md#recommended-order) names Size Parade for her own creatures by their natural sizes; nothing here depends on it.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action.** Tap any friend: it squashes under the finger (`Critter.poke`), hops, and the pet says its size word. At level 1 the asked friend is 2.4 times the other, so the first right answer needs no adult.
- **The pleasure is the parade.** After a right pick the friend struts across the front of the ground past a flag and off, the row re-dealing as it goes (slice 2). After a finished line everyone marches off in order, each step a drum and a bell by size, the biggest lowest, so a correct line sounds like a scale and a bumpy one would not. Concrete art: the parade ground gets a flag on a pole (`props.ts`, a seeded palette colour) and each friend a ground shadow (an ellipse scaled with the friend), so size shows twice; the march is `Critter.hop` staggered by `ctx.tw.wait(0.15 * i)` with `sfx.bell(9 − rank)` and a low `sfx.tick` on landing. No new rendering path.
- **Touch, consequence, readiness.** Down: squash and tick. Right: chime, cheer, strut or slot. Wrong: boing, the friend shakes its head, the pet names what is still waiting; second wrong: the right friend glows. A tap on the ground makes every friend hop once and repeats the ask (no miss). The glowing next spot is the one-direction cue that makes a line easier than a free arrangement.
- **Variety on replay.** Eight friends instead of three, the flag's colour and the march's direction seeded per round, the asked word alternating (bigger, smaller), and at the pick levels the dealt sizes drawn from the five-step scale rather than always the extremes (two friends two or more steps apart, so the ratio stays at least 1.3). The rule never changes.
- **Reasoning, not reading, memory or precision.** Nothing is read. Nothing is remembered except in Hidden heights, where the state is always on screen (the swaps are visible) and the only hidden thing is height. Motor demands stay at a tap and a lifted drag; the smallest friend keeps a 154×190 target.
- **Supported ending.** After two wrongs the right friend glows until it is picked; a line always completes. One sticker. Replay is the same level with a new friend and order; the celebration's green again and orange next work as everywhere.
- **Co-play.** A grown-up says the size word as she taps (the card's tip); at Hidden heights one player names the pair to test and the other taps. Solo always works.
- **Person or device checks** (none can be decided by the bot): whether a two-year-old looks at the circle cue at all; whether the strut after a pick holds attention or delays the next question too long; whether "the middle one" is understood at three before it is at four; the shade steps on an iPad in daylight (level 15); whether a six-year-old understands that an unmoved pair means "already right" (level 13); the 40-unit lift on a first drag (level 17).

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

- **Comparative words at two.** `size.pick` takes the asked word: "bigger"/"smaller" when two friends are dealt, "biggest"/"smallest" from three; `size.wrong` the same. The card already says so.
- **The parade happens.** Strut after a pick; the march after a line, with the size scale (gap 1).
- **Pitch follows size, big is low.** `sfx.bell(9 − rank)` or equivalent on every right answer and in the march, matching Bubble Pop's plan (gap 3).
- **Every touch answered.** Ground or sky tap: all hop, the ask repeats, no miss. A right friend on the wrong spot: "This spot first!" and the glowing spot pulses, no miss; a wrong friend on the glowing spot stays a miss (gap 4).
- **Portrait spacing.** The tap rectangle narrows to the spacing when five friends share a portrait width, or the tap resolves to the nearest friend; a test lays five out at `view.w` 768 and asserts no overlap and every target at least 100 wide (gap 5).
- **Pinned steps.** A rule test asserts the smallest adjacent height difference at game scale (18 units at five) and that the smallest friend's target is at least 100 (gap 6).
- **Eight friends and a seeded ground.** `pip`, `cow`, `pig`, `bear` and `dog` join the pool; the flag and bunting colours come from the seed; the sticker shows the round's friend (gap 7).
- **Dealt sizes vary at the pick levels.** Two friends are drawn from the five-step scale at least two steps apart; three or four friends from four or five steps with the asked one always at an end. The question stays the same; the picture changes.

### Left out on purpose

- **Six or seven friends.** Quantity without a new decision (the audit's call); the trajectory's ordering ceiling is six.
- **Tower of Hanoi.** A planning puzzle whose only size content is one rule; it would be its own game (a notebook proposal, not a level).
- **Units, rulers and "how many worms long".** Inchworm Measure owns them; level 14 stops at taller or shorter.
- **Weight.** Seesaw Balance.
- **Mixed animals in one line.** A bunny's ears make "tallest" a trick question.
- **Width against height (the Brown Stair's dimension).** The critters scale as a whole; a non-uniform scale makes a different-looking friend, not a wider one. A prop-based version (hats, boxes) could come later as a decision.
- **A nesting or stacking mode.** Cups nest in one direction and would be a different action with new art; Fill and Dump and Block Tower already hold containers and stacks. Left as a notebook idea.
- **Speed, timers, counting the taps, a leaderboard of lines.**

## Decisions for the developer

Each has a default the build uses:

1. **The lap band and its accounting.** Default: `bands` gains `lap` with `order [16, 9]`; at 9 the first touch of the other friend in a question answers without a miss, the second glows the asked friend and counts a hint. Alternative: no lap band, 9 toddler-only with level-1 accounting.
2. **Shade Lineup's home.** Default: level 15 here (the same drag-into-line action with the cue swapped), Color Garden keeping its three-shade grading. Alternative: a Color Garden level, and this ladder ends at 14 for school.
3. **Hidden heights' feedback.** Default: a swap-only comparator, nothing revealed until the gate opens (as in the study). Alternative: a tapped pair peeks over the fence for a second (easier at five, but a memory load, which the rules keep out of younger modes).
4. **Her creatures in the parade.** Default: the eight friends. The rework's casting proof already names Size Parade; when RW05 lands, owned creatures by their natural sizes fall back to the friends. Not a new call, recorded so a build does not pre-empt it.
