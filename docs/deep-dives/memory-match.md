*ID `memory-match` · preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–12 on 2026-10-15 (research snapshot: 1–11) · `codex/memory-match-deep-dive` ([PR 153](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/153))*

## The game today

Research snapshot below; since then spoken support (slice 1) and three seeded picture families at levels 1–5 (slice 2, [PR #264](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/264)) and a fourth, vehicles (2026-10-14), are built, as are the dot arrangements at levels 6–7 (2026-10-14). Same time at stable ID 12 is built ([PR #373](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/373)); school order is [8, 12, 9, 10, 11]. Device/person checks remain open.

Tap two hidden cards; matching pairs stay face up until the whole board is finished. The fun is finding a remembered friend. The skill starts as visual matching and remembering locations, then becomes recognizing two representations of the same thing. One shell-awarded sticker finishes the board, including with help. Nothing is timed.

The source read for the original research session was `src/games/memory-match/index.ts`, `logic.ts` and `logic.test.ts`, the how-to, voice script, couch catalog, and the game's GAMES, IDEAS and roadmap entries. These are code-reading findings, not newly played rounds.

**Pools.** Picture levels take the first N entries of eight fixed color/shape combinations; circles and stars recur in different colors. Level 8 uses circle, star, square and heart, each in red and blue. Number levels always use 1–4 or 1–8; level 10 always uses all ten number words. Only the eight letters in level 9 are drawn from a larger pool (26 capitals, shuffled once per round). Level 11 always uses the same three fractions of a 64-unit square. `makeDeck` gives every pair exactly one card of each side and shuffles positions; it does not select content. Sticker and hub art are a fixed heart card and star-backed card, independent of the deck.

**Support and accounting.** The first card immediately joins `seen`; the second joins after the comparison and wait. An unsuccessful pair counts as one miss only if the first card's partner was already seen. Unseen exploration is free, although it still gets the generic boing and reminder. Two known mismatches since a match glow the last first card's partner; a match clears the glow and resets that counter. The hint line says its friend was seen. This rule should remain inspectable and deliberate, including its dependence on which card is chosen first. `nextCard` drives both bots from seen positions; tests cover completion across deck sizes and seeds without a penalized mismatch.

**Couch.** Catalog demo level 2, trip levels 4 then 5, band preschool. The shared face-off alternates after an unsuccessful pair; a match earns another turn and one pair point. Automatic hints are absent in face-off. `control()` and `autoplay()` move a grid ring. Appending island levels does not change this catalog, trip rules or course versions. The proposed family-table split-seat mode is a separate, decision-dependent slice.

### Known gaps and checks to plan

1. **Level 10 word legibility needs review.** Four columns and five rows yield `(768 - 170) / 5 - 16 = 103.6` logical units in the base view; the width formula at 1024 is 180, so it does not constrain them. The twenty-card board narrowly fits the target minimum. Preserve a computed minimum of 100 and verify all supported logical views before choosing a new layout or adding cards. Do not solve legibility by increasing the board. The larger issue is the effective word size: 38 × 103.6 / 140 is about 28 units.
2. **How-to leakage.** The number-word step is `{ from: 10 }`, so it also appears for fractions at 11. Bound it to 10 and the fraction step to 11 before appending another mode.
3. **Reading support is incomplete.** Only fraction cards speak their contents; number words and uppercase/lowercase do not. Tapping an already-open first card currently does nothing, so it cannot replay a label. Add free spoken labels and replay without turning a replay into another choice, miss or hint. Listening-supported matching is not independent reading.
4. **A hint can become stale.** After a glow, an unrelated exploratory pair can select a new glowing partner without charging another hint; the line can claim a partner was seen when this newly selected partner was never seen. Plan a rule test tying the hint message to the evidence, rather than counting exploration as a mistake.
5. **Touch-down behavior already exists.** `onTap` invokes the choice on pointerdown and the face becomes visible immediately. Preserve that response and palm rejection when adding replay; check double taps do not pick the same card twice.
6. **Current tests do not inspect plans or visual equivalence.** Extract plans and content selection to `logic.ts`; add fraction bounds/equal parts, unique identities, plan windows, layout minima, and support-selection properties. Do not claim the tests read here passed this session.
7. **People and device checks.** Letterforms such as I/i and font-dependent a/A, word legibility, actual number/fraction speech, comfortable mismatch viewing time, and the preschool sixteen-card load remain unjudged. Keep larger-board practice optional through the grown-up level picker; do not call more cards deeper learning.

## What makes sense here

Keep the two-card discovery and small satisfying pair reward. More quantity should mainly mean new code-drawn decks, different dot arrangements and selected vocabulary, with each board having exactly one partner per card. Existing picture levels remain available and their IDs and pins remain unchanged.

The arc is visible picture matching for toddlers, small hidden familiar decks for preschool, representation and two-attribute matching for pre-K, then time, quantities composed two ways and equal fractions for school. A later audio or changing-rule mode needs a separate definition before entering this linear ladder: it may be easier than its predecessor, and a broad attribute relation can leave unmatchable cards after a legal choice.

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

- **Picture decks, levels 1–5:** shapes, eight island friends (including Pip) and six fruits plus balloon/flower are built ([PR #264](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/264)); eight vehicles (car, bus, truck, train, boat, plane, rocket, tractor) are built as a fourth family (2026-10-14). Select N distinct identities before creating pairs. The two faces of a pair share identity, not a renderer index; code-drawn art only. Name each picture on reveal and replay. At level 8 keep the shape/color cross-product rule separate: a themed picture must not obscure either attribute.
- **Dots, levels 6–7:** scatter, rows and five-frame arrangements; paired numeral and dots must not have an answer-specific common color. Rotate among curated arrangements with adequate spacing. Quantities stay 1–4 and 1–8. Test actual count and positions within card bounds.
- **Letters, level 9:** keep the 26-letter pool and seeded eight-letter selection. First speak the letter name, including on lowercase; do not infer a sound from its appearance. Review letterform distinctions with a person; no new font or alphabet in this slice.
- **Number words, level 10:** show five pairs selected from 1–10 per board, instead of always twenty cards. This is a proposed change to quantity at an existing saved level, not a new ID. It needs the developer's choice below. Selection must reach all ten words across seeds; never use board position or color as the match. If ten pairs are retained, use a layout and text treatment that prove target and word-fit minima without scrolling.
- **Fractions, level 11:** keep the three values and word cards; vary the square's orientation and which equal parts are shaded. A half always has two equal parts here, a quarter and three quarters four. Circle sectors can follow as a later art slice after equal-area rendering is inspected. Each round uses one stated whole and no decorative fill that could be counted as part of it.
- **Support first:** bounded how-to, spoken contents and free replay, accurate hint wording, extracted plans and stable icon/sticker art. For a nonmatching unseen pair, explain the revealed cards without implying that exploring was wrong.

### Level 12 definition: Same time

**Defined 2026-10-15, docs only**, `codex/memory-clock-definition`, [PR #369](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/369); dispatch `memory-clock-definition-20261015T124701Z`. This completes the clock packet requested by the roadmap's [short ladders](../ROADMAP.md#short-ladders-in-order). Implemented separately in [PR #373](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/373), dispatch `memory-clock-build-20261015T135031Z`; the packet below records its acceptance criteria. School is 8–12 with order [8, 12, 9, 10, 11], other bands unchanged. The developer's 2026-10-12 approval covers the content lean, including the easier clock bridge and use of `order`; this packet does not choose a band/order table, resolve the remaining developer questions or authorize a different mode.

**Action and purpose.** Turn two cards to find the same time written in two ways, remembering where each was. The bridge practices recognizing hour/half-hour analog and digital representations with spoken support. It does not teach setting hands, elapsed time, independent reading, AM/PM or a daily routine; Clock Tower keeps its manipulation. The intended school extension retains stable ID 12, six hidden cards and three pairs. No timer, preview countdown, additional cards or score.

**One complete board.** The authored example is 2:00, 4:30 and 7:00, each appearing once as an analog face and once as digital text. All six start with identical backs. A first tap reveals and names the time immediately. A different second tap reveals and compares; either representation can be first. A correct pair stays visible with the existing pair reward. A nonmatch remains visible for the existing viewing interval, explains the two times, then turns back. Replay of the open first card is free and leaves that choice in place. Find all three pairs to finish once through the shell, including with help, for one sticker. No separate clock quiz or unassisted prerequisite follows the third pair.

**Seeded three-pair selection.** The finite pool is twelve displayed hours (1–12), each at :00 and :30: 24 normalized times. Select three distinct hours without replacement using `ctx.rng`. Choose one of the minute-patterns `[0, 0, 30]` and `[0, 30, 30]` with that RNG, shuffle the pattern and assign it to those hours. This guarantees both kinds on every board, avoids a same-hour :00/:30 discrimination in this first bridge, and makes all 24 times reachable across seeds. Then create one analog and one digital card for each selected time and shuffle all six positions. The example above is a test fixture, not a required first board or extra saved state. Times, representations, colors and placement must not have answer-specific shared decorations or a fixed analog/digital column. Choose the content before assigning pair IDs; the renderer, labels and match keys all read that same selection.

**Exact acceptable matches.** A time key is minutes on the twelve-hour cycle, `((hour % 12) * 60 + minute)`, with hour 1–12 and minute 0 or 30. Accept two different cards exactly when their keys agree and their representations differ. Thus analog 4:30 matches digital 4:30 in either selection order; 4:00, 5:30 and the same card tapped twice do not. Twelve o'clock is key 0 and 12:30 key 30; display them as `12:00` and `12:30`, never `0:00` or `0:30`. There is one partner per card and no AM/PM distinction, approximate hand-angle tolerance, text-input synonym or match by renderer index. Pair IDs may implement this equality only after tests prove they agree with normalized values and opposite sides.

**Readable code-drawn faces.** Inspect Clock Tower's `handsFor`, `spoken` and `miniClock` before implementation. Reuse the hour-motion rule; its dot-only hub/sticker face is not a finished learning card. Do not refactor shared drawing unless this implemented activity needs it.

- Draw with Pixi and the palette on a plain high-contrast face. Place all twelve upright numerals around the dial, 12 at the top; do not rotate or mirror the face. Omit a second hand, minute numerals, moving hands and decorative marks that resemble hands.
- Draw a short, broader hour hand and a longer, narrower minute hand, distinct by length/width as well as palette color, with one clear center. At :00 the long hand points to 12 and the short hand to the hour. At :30 the long hand points to 6 and the short hand halfway to the next hour, including 12:30 between 12 and 1. Overlap at 12:00 must still show the short hand's wider body.
- Measure clockwise angles from 12: minute `6 * minute` degrees; hour `30 * (hour % 12) + 0.5 * minute` degrees. At 4:30 these are 180 and 135 degrees. Neither snap the short hand back to 4 nor advance it to 5.
- For the full game, budget cards at least 140 logical units square, which exceeds the 100-unit target minimum; within them allow a dial diameter at least 112 and numeral text at least 14 logical units after scaling. Keep hands inside the numeral ring and all numerals/edges inside the card. Digital faces use centered `h:mm`, no leading hour zero, a clear colon and text at least 32 logical units after scaling. Treat these as implementation fit criteria, not established device legibility. If the six-card layout cannot meet them in a supported view, revise its spacing/layout rather than shrink the art.
- Keep the board, corner navigation and bottom-left pet clear in landscape and portrait. Inspect all faces in a temporary art view as well as during real play; a screenshot of hidden backs proves no clock legibility. The scaled how-to window must still visibly demonstrate the two representations; it accepts no touch and does not establish full-game target size.

**Speech, mistakes and supported finish.** Proposed instruction: “Find the clocks that show the same time.” Keep it in `voice-script.ts` via `ctx.instruct`, replayable from the pet. Each revealed face uses the same spoken time for its key: “two o'clock” or “half past four,” regardless of representation. A known mismatch names both, for example “Half past four and seven o'clock. Let's find the same time.” An unseen nonmatch names both neutrally, without treating discovery as failure. A match can say “Both show half past four.” Put any new lines in the voice script and bound the how-to steps/finish to level 12; do not leak number-word or fraction instructions into it. Proposed how-to steps: turn a card and listen; turn another showing the same time; tap the first open card to hear it again. Finish text: find all three analog/digital pairs.

Preserve the implemented accounting: charge one miss only when the first card's partner was already seen before comparison; the reversed selection order can therefore have different evidence. Exploration, repeated speech, selecting the first card, tapping matched cards and blocked input during comparison charge nothing. After two known mismatches since a match, glow a partner that has actually been revealed and count the one stable hint. Unrelated exploration must not move it or charge another hint; a match clears it. The hint can name that known time and explain the long hand at 12/6 and the short hand's position. Use the existing gentle boing for a known wrong choice; preserve neutral exploratory feedback. Keep retries available: remembering all six eventually makes every remaining partner known, and help leads to all three pairs without an automatic completion button. Assisted completion is welcome but does not establish independent clock understanding.

**Co-play and boundary.** A grown-up can say where a remembered time was and the child turns that card, or ask how the two displays show the same time. This requires no new seat, controller, attribution or save mechanism. No new off-screen clock instruction is needed. Keep the bridge-versus-duplicate question for observation once playable, as [already recorded](../WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER.md#waiting-on-the-developer). Level 10 quantity, levels 13–14, toddler/face-up openings and family split-seat decisions retain their separate scopes. An eventual implementation must apply the approved easier-level ordering while preserving old IDs/pins; this definition does not set ranges or settle the remaining choices.
