# Word Monsters deep dive

*ID `word-monsters` · bands lap, toddler, preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–8 on 2026-10-11 · session `claude/roadmap-item-14-game-content-msedsy` ([PR 120](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/120))*

## The game today

Every letter is a round furry monster with its letter on its tummy. In free play she taps one and it talks (its mouth opens, the device voice says the sound, a picture of a word it starts appears). In the question levels the instruction is spoken, she taps the right monster or drags letter monsters into slots to build a word, and the word then "comes alive": each placed monster says its sound, the voice blends the word, the picture bounces and a tada plays. Story Grove (`lands.ts`, with Letter Trails, Story Steps, Photo Safari, Goodnight Room, Rhyme Time, Opposites and Clap the Syllables). `skills`: letter-sounds, phonics, vocabulary. Music `STYLES.hub`. **Not a couch game, not in the journal, not in the picnic.** The ladder, level by level (`PLANS` in `logic.ts`):

| Level | Mode | Rounds · choices | Bands whose window includes it | What she does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `play` | 10 taps · 4 monsters | lap, toddler | Taps monsters to hear their sounds; both cases on the tummy (`Aa`). No questions, so no misses |
| 2 | `find` | 4 · 2 | toddler, preschool | "Find B!" The letter *name*, spoken from a capital. Pool of 14 letters (`abcdefghkmrstw`), both cases on the tummy |
| 3 | `who` | 4 · 3 | preschool, pre-K | "Who says mmm?" Any of the 26 letters; lowercase only from here on |
| 4 | `first` | 4 · 3 | preschool, pre-K | A picture (the Letter Trails picture for the capital) and "apple starts with aah. Who says aah?" 21 letters (no i, o, q, v, x) |
| 5 | `build` | 3 words · 3 | preschool, pre-K, school | The word's letters stand faint in three slots; she drags the matching monsters in, left to right. "Let us make cat! kuh, aah, tuh." |
| 6 | `spell` | 3 words · 4 | pre-K, school | The same, with no letters shown and one spare monster |
| 7 | `family` | 6 · 4 | school | The ending (at, og, ig, un, op) already stands in the last two slots; she drags in the first sound: hat, then cat, then bat. Two families of three |
| 8 | `end` | 3 · 3 | school | The first two sounds stand in place; she changes the last: cat, cap, can |

Bands (`LEVELS`): lap 1, toddler 1–2, preschool 2–5, pre-K 3–6, school 5–8. Mistakes teach: a wrong tap boings and the monster says "I'm B! I say buh"; a wrong drop boings and says "Next sound: buh"; two misses in a row glow the right monster (a hint, counted once while the glow stays). `misses` counts wrong taps and wrong drops; `hints` counts glows.

**What each round draws from.** 26 letters, each with a stand-in sound line (`sound.a` … `sound.z`, written as "aah", "buh", "kuh", "sss") for the device voice; 11 `monster.*` lines; eight fur colors shifted per round so neighbors differ; one backdrop. Pictures exist for **nine words**: cat, dog and pig (critters), sun (Letter Trails' sun), and cup, hat, box, cap and can drawn in `wordPicture`. Words to build: `WORDS` (cat dog pig sun cup hat box, level 5–6), `END_WORDS` (cat cap can, level 8), and five families of 4–6 words each (24 in all, level 7). The how-to card has a `LevelLine` per level and the ghost finger plays every level (`autotouch`, `nextMonster`).

**Known gaps, read from the code (nothing was played or run; this session has no installed dependencies).**

1. **Two letters share a sound on levels 3 and 4.** `SOUNDS.c` and `SOUNDS.k` are both "kuh". The `who` pool is all 26 letters and the `first` pool holds both c (cat) and k (kite); `pickLetters` rules out lookalikes (`bdpq`, `mnu`, `il`) but not letters that sound the same. "Who says kuh?" can offer C and K together, and the one that was not drawn is told off. The 200-seed test checks lookalikes, not sound twins. By hand it is a few percent of rounds on 3 and 4, but it is a wrong answer that is right. Q ("kwuh") and X ("ks") are also in the `who` pool; neither is a plain letter sound.
2. **Almost every prompt names the sound.** Level 3 asks for a sound, which is the point. But level 4 says "apple starts with *aah*. Who says *aah*?", level 6 reads out "kuh, aah, tuh", level 7 says "hat starts with *huh*", level 8 says "keep kuh and aah, change the end to *puh*", and a wrong drop says "Next sound: *tuh*". So levels 3, 4, 6, 7 and 8 are one decision, **match a spoken sound to a monster**; the picture on level 4 is decoration, and she is never asked to pick the first, last or middle sound out of a word herself (RF.K.2d and 2e below). This is the biggest gap in the ladder and the main design point of the plan.
3. **Level 8 is the same round every time.** `END_WORDS` is a fixed list (cat, cap, can) and the spares are always x and z (`pickLetters(..., ['x', 'z'])`), so she can pass by taking "the one that is not x or z" without listening, and a seed only shuffles the order. Level 7's decoys come from the family's other first sounds and are fine.
4. **Only four of level 7's 24 words are drawn.** `drawn` needs a word in `PICTURE_WORDS` (cat, hat, dog, pig; sun is deliberately hidden). Everything else (bat, mat, rat, sat, log, fog, hog, jog, dig, wig, big, fig, run, fun, bun, top, mop, hop, pop) has empty slots and a voice. The stated reason, that sun "is drawn as a big S", is stale: `letterPicture('S')` is a sun with rays and a face, and level 5 draws it as sun.
5. **No short-e word anywhere.** `WORDS` and the families use a (cat, hat, cap, can), o (dog, box), i (pig), u (sun, cup) and never e. The pictured-word set is nine words, so every new level is picture-limited.
6. **`box` does not fit a sound-per-slot game.** It has three letters and four sounds (b, o, k, s); RF.K.2d leaves out CVC words ending in /x/. `SOUNDS.o` is "aw", which suits dog but is not how most American voices say box, top, mop or hop (a short "ah").
7. **The letter strand stops early.** The tummy shows lowercase only after level 2, so she is never asked to match a capital to a small letter. `find` draws on 14 letters. Nothing uses her name.
8. **Bounded lines.** The how-to's level 7 step is `{ from: 7 }` with no `to`, so it also shows on level 8 (a test does not catch it). `LEVELS.school.max` is a literal 8 and `planFor` clamps silently.
9. **The sticker is not pinned.** `sticker()` picks a letter with `rng.pick('abcdefghjkmnoprstuwz')` and the fur from `FURS[charCode % 8]`; changing the `Monster` art or `FURS` would change every saved sticker.
10. **A hint can count twice in one word.** `hint()` glows the next slot's monster; after the word fills and the next slot is missed twice, it glows again (the same shape as Market Stall's gap 6).
11. **Device speech is the open risk.** The consonant stand-ins ("buh", "duh", "guh", "puh", "tuh", "kuh") are the schwa-added forms that pure-sounds phonics teaching warns against, because "kuh-aah-tuh" does not blend into "cat" as well as clipped sounds do ([Monster Phonics](https://monsterphonics.com/the-importance-of-pure-sounds-in-phonics/), search summary only). Whether the iPad voice says "aah", "eh", "ih" and "uh" so they can be told apart, and says a capital "A" in "Find A!" as a letter name, is the [open check in the roadmap](../DEVICE-CHECKS.md#needs-a-person-or-a-device) (and parent-recorded lines are an undecided proposal there). **No claim of phonics coverage is made here beyond what that check allows.**
12. **Not a bug, checked.** The answer is always offered, once; no lookalikes sit together; spare letters never look like a needed letter; a word builds only left to right (unlike the any-order spelling a reviewer faulted in Endless Alphabet); the bot never makes a wrong drop (all in `logic.test.ts`); the corner pet is clear of the slot row.

## What makes sense here

**Word Monsters' own axis is the letter as a sound, and a word as a row of sounds.** The ladder is already an Elkonin ladder (levels 5–8 are boxes per sound). What it lacks is the three things that make the method teach: **hearing a sound inside a word** (isolating), **running sounds together** (blending) and **saying a word slowly without being given the sounds** (segmenting). Today the voice does that work for her. Four points shape the plan.

- **Stop telling the sound, then add the skills it was hiding.** Level 3 asks for a sound; levels 4 to 8 should ask for a *word* and let the sound be what she works out. Whole words come out of device speech much better than isolated "kuh" and "aah", so a word-only prompt is also the safer one. The sound stays as the hint, and as what a wrong monster says about itself.
- **Slots count sounds, so letters can be teams.** Digraphs (sh, ch, th, ck) are one box with two letters on one monster. That is the Elkonin rule, Letters and Sounds' phase 3, and it solves `box` (x as one monster with two sounds is the odd case; a team is the clean one).
- **Reuse the art that exists.** Letter Trails already draws 26 pictures: fish (f-i-sh), duck (d-u-ck), nest (n-e-s-t), kite (k-i-t-e, a magic e), tree, moon, leaf, ball, house. They cost nothing and make the first digraph, cluster and magic-e rounds. Frog Hop's frog is a four-sound word. The existing nine pictured words already form a word ladder: hat → cat → can → cap → cup, each step one letter.
- **Her name is the best first word.** A name level uses `ctx.childName` and teaches letter names (Treiman). It overlaps Letter Trails level 8, which traces her name: Letter Trails owns how the letters are *drawn*, Word Monsters owns putting them in order by name.

**Who owns what.** *Rhyme Time* owns rhyme (hearing, not spelling); Word Monsters' families use the same rimes to *spell*. *Clap the Syllables* owns syllables; Word Monsters stays at one syllable until the grown-up window. *Letter Trails* owns stroke forms and the tracing of capitals and names. *Read & Do* (proposed) owns reading a printed word for meaning and sight words; Word Monsters' blend-it is hearing sounds blend into a picture, with no printed word to read. *Opposites* level 9 (planned un- words) and the grown-up window's word parts: one of the two should own un-; the decision is below. The couch-only *Word Search* finds words in a grid and the proposed *Word Code* deduces a word; a ladder builds a chain, so it is a third thing.

**Arc by band.** Lap: one big monster that talks (below level 1) and level 1. Toddler: "where is my letter?" with the first letter of her name, and find-by-name. Preschool: capital and small letters, first sounds, building a word with letters shown. Pre-K: hearing the first sound, then spelling a word from sounds, spelling her name. School: the sound of the last letter, blending, saying a word slowly, fixing the pet's word, two letters one sound, four sounds, the magic e, and a ladder of one-sound changes. Grown-up: ladders from a word list, word parts, and choosing a spelling.

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

Saves, IDs and the sticker do not change for any of this (the sticker is pinned first, slice 1). Every new picture is code-drawn and every sound synthesized or spoken.

1. **A sound-twin guard** (levels 3–4, and every build level's spares). No two offered monsters share a `SOUNDS` value, and q and x leave the `who` and `first` pools until they have a plain sound of their own. Test: for every plan and 200 seeds the offered sounds are pairwise distinct; for a build word no spare letter can spell a needed sound (k for c).
2. **Short-e words and a vowel balance** (levels 5–8). Add `hen`, `bed`, `net`, `pen` (e), `map`, `bag` (a), `mop`, `log` (o), `rug`, `bus` (u), `pin`, `lip` (i) and others, each with a drawn picture, so every vowel has at least three words. New families: `en` (hen, pen, ten, den), `et` (net, pet, wet, jet), `ug` (bug, rug, mug, hug). Test: every built word has a picture, the vowel counts are balanced, no word repeats in a round.
3. **Every word drawn** (level 7). Draw the 19 family words with no picture (and show the sun); the picture settles a word the voice might blur (wig, fig, fun). Test: every family word has a picture (a list check).
4. **Told, then asked** (levels 7 and 8, and 4 only if the developer chooses; decision 2). The first question of a round still says the sound as a worked example ("hat starts with huh"); the next questions say the word and nothing else ("What does *hat* start with?", "Make *hat*"), and a wrong tap or drop says the sound, as now. The picture and a free "say it again" tap on the picture help. Test: only the first question of a level carries the sound in its prompt line.
5. **Level 8 gets real decoys and more sets** (level 8). The spares are not always x and z: for each beginning a `DECOYS` table lists endings that make no real word (checked against a CVC word list in the test), so the right ending cannot be found by elimination; sets beyond cat/cap/can: `hat`, `dog`, `pig`, `cup` (ha_, do_, pi_, cu_), each decoy checked. Test: no spare makes a real word with the standing letters; the answer makes only the pictured word in the offered set.
6. **Case beside case** (levels 3–4). The tummy shows both cases (`Bb`) for the first two questions and lowercase afterward, or each monster shows both on level 3. Test: the glyph shown is a function of the plan.
7. **Pictures for the missing first sounds** (level 4). v (violin, `letterPicture('V')` exists) joins the pool now; i (igloo) and o (octopus) are **new drawings**. q stays out ("kwuh" is a near-twin of "kuh", fresh 1), so the pool grows from 21 to 24 letters. Test: every letter in the `first` pool has a drawn picture whose name starts with that letter's plain sound (not "ice cream", not "xylophone").
8. **Bounded how-to** (level 7 vs 8) and `LEVELS.school.max` that follows the plan length; a pinned sticker test (seeds 0–99).
9. **The pet reads it back** (levels 5–8): after the word blends, the corner pet says it once more ("{pet} says cat!"). Not a hint. This is the pet-as-learner idea from the idea notebook in its smallest form; the pet *mis-reading* a word is level 12.
10. **Co-play and off-screen that match each level.** Co-play: "make each monster's sound together, slowly: mmm, sss, then blend", "say the word and clap the sounds" (level 5–8), "you be the monster: say the last sound of *dog*". Off-screen: I-spy with sounds; sound boxes with three coins or buttons and a word said slowly; magnetic or cut-out letters on the fridge; a bag of small toys, "find one that ends with *p*".

### New levels on top

### Left out on purpose

- **Collectable rewards and persistent currencies** of the kind reading apps use. One sticker per completed round is the reward.
- **Letters spelled in any order** (*Endless Alphabet*): left to right is the rule, and it stays.
- **Gating letters in a taught order** (*Jolly Phonics*): any letter, any time; no lesson sequence, no locked letters. The Jolly Phonics order informs which letters the early picture words use, and nothing else.
- **A timer, a countdown, lives, a winner or a score in a child's save.** A word waits for her.
- **Reading a printed word for meaning, sight words and sentences.** That is *Read & Do*. Word Monsters has her hear sounds and put letters in order.
- **Spelling-bee and Wordle-style games on the child's island** (a hidden word, guess limits): a Word Code idea and the grown-up window's, not the child's.
- **Long vowel teams, silent letters and two-syllable words for the child's band** (ai, ee, oa, igh; lamb, knee): after magic e the spelling system needs a reviewed word list and recorded or reliable speech. The window above is where they live (G3).
- **Accented or non-English letters**, and a letter-sound claim for any language other than English.
- **A made-up word read aloud.** Pseudo-words ("cax") are used as wrong answers only in the data, never spoken as if they were words.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Levels on top and the school window.** Lean: build 9 to 11 first (they use the existing nine pictures, plus the pictures slice), define 12 to 16 as each is built, hold 17 until a child has played 9 to 11, in a school window of 5–16 (no lower bound moves). Small inversions: 9 is easier than 8, 10 may be easier than 6, 14 is easier than 13. Accept them, or fold them into the level-order decision.
2. **Tell the sound or ask for the word.** Lean: yes to *told, then asked* on levels 7 and 8 (the school band meets them after 5–6, where the sounds are read out), and leave level 4 as it is for preschool; levels 9 to 11 carry word-only from then on. The trade-off: it makes saved levels 7 and 8 harder, so a school child may step down. Or leave 4 to 8 alone and let only the new levels be word-only.
3. **Speech and recorded voices.** Everything phonics here waits on an iPad listen to all 26 `sound.*` lines and the letter names. Lean: do the check before slice 3, and decide then whether to respell the stand-ins (shorter, no schwa) or take on parent-recorded sounds (the roadmap's undecided proposal, which conflicts with the no-audio-files rule). A short `sound` page for the grown-up (play each line) could be a person-check helper.
4. **Team monsters (digraphs).** Lean: one wide monster wearing both letters (so the box count equals the sound count). Or two small monsters side by side that the child pairs first.
5. **Her name as a level.** Lean: yes, for pre-K, using letter *names*, with a cap of eight letters and the Letter Trails `PIP` fallback. The name is only ever shown on the board. Or never use the name here and leave it to Letter Trails.
6. **The pet as a learner (level 12).** Lean: yes. The pet makes a plain mistake and the child fixes it; nothing is lost or owed. Or leave "Fix" for later and move level 12's slot to magic e.
7. **Who owns un-.** *Opposites* level 9 plans un- words with a printed word; the grown-up window's word parts plan the same. Lean: Opposites keeps the meaning (un- as the opposite), and Word Monsters' word parts take the other affixes (re-, -ing, -ed, -s) and the doubling rule. Recorded 2026-10-22 ([vocabulary study](subjects/vocabulary-word-parts.md)): G2 owns re-, -ing, -ed and -s, Opposites 9 keeps un-, and compounds and -er/-ful/-less belong to the proposed Word Glue.
8. **A grown-up window** (G1 to G4) needs a `grownup` band or a couch-side plan table and a reviewed word list. Lean: after Lands stage B, starting with G1 because its minimum is exact.
9. **The new pictures.** About 40 small drawings across the plan (the short-e and stretchable sets, the 19 family words, digraph and cluster words, magic-e pairs). Lean: build them in tranches with the levels that use them, so a level never waits on art.
10. **Person checks to add:** whether device speech makes the vowel stand-ins distinguishable; whether "kuh-aah-tuh" blends to "cat" for a child (and whether continuous-start words blend better); whether "Find A!" and the capital letter names are spoken as names; whether a five-year-old can say a word slowly from a picture alone (level 11); whether a child sees that a final e changes a vowel without being told; whether a pre-K child recognizes the name monsters; and whether the biggest and smallest monsters stay at least 100 units across at every screen size.

**Decided 2026-10-23 (on the developer's delegation):** build 9–16 and define 17 in school 5–16 with `order` placing the inversions; "told, then asked" on 7–8; one wide team monster; her name as a pre-K level (letter names, eight letters, the `PIP` fallback); the pet as a learner on 12; Opposites keeps un-. Voice: no parent recordings; the `sound.*` stand-ins may be respelled and are spoken by build-time generated clips from `voice-script.ts` (device speech as fallback), so the iPad listen is a device check, not a gate, though no phonics skill is claimed until it passes. The grown-up window's word list is checked by the building session against cited sources.
