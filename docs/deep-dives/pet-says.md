# Pet Says deep dive

*ID `pet-says` · bands lap, toddler, preschool, pre-K · levels 1–5 on 2026-10-10 · session `claude/roadmap-item-14-game-content-ghbpi1` ([PR 112](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/112))*

## The game today

A big version of her pet stands in the middle of a meadow with two mitten hands riding on its body (the corner guide steps out). It does a move with a sound and says it; she copies it with her own body, away from the screen, and a grown-up taps the green arrow (bottom right) for the next one. Nothing is judged or detected: the game cannot see her, so no miss is possible and every round ends with the ordinary sticker (`finish({ misses: 0, hints: 0 })`). Tapping the big pet shows the move again. It lives in Cozy Village (`lands.ts`). `skills`: copying actions, body words, following directions, self-control. It is the island's only body game, and the only game whose on-screen child has nothing to decide: the child's own part happens off screen.

| Level | Plan (`PLANS` in `logic.ts`) | Bands whose window includes it | What she does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `copy`: six moves from eight (clap, stomp, wave, jump, spin, wiggle, up, down); no move twice in a row | lap, toddler | copy the move |
| 2 | `body`: the five body words (nose, tummy, ears, head, toes), shuffled; the mitten touches the part | lap, toddler, preschool | find the part on herself |
| 3 | `pairs`: four turns of two moves ("clap, then stomp") | toddler, preschool, pre-K | do two in order; the pet shows both |
| 4 | `says`: six turns, "{pet} says: clap!" or just "clap!"; the pet moves either way; two tricks, never first, never back to back; "Did you stay still?" | preschool, pre-K | move only after "{pet} says" |
| 5 | `freeze`: four dances of three to seven seconds (never the same twice), a 12-note marimba tune, then "Freeze!" and a silly pose until the arrow | pre-K | dance to the music, freeze when it stops |

Ranges (`LEVELS` in `index.ts`): lap 1–2, toddler 1–3, preschool 2–4, pre-K 3–5. Music `STYLES.quiet`, not silenced (the dance tune is the game's own `sfx.marimba`).

**What a round draws from.** `MOVES` (8) and `BODY` (5) are the only pools; `MOVE_WORDS` gives each of the 13 a spoken name, and each move also has a voice line `says.<move>` (the two tables are kept by hand). The sticker is a seeded pip in one of four colors in one of three poses (`sticker` in `index.ts`). Level 2 deals all five body words in a new order every round; level 5 has one tune; there is one backdrop. The "Pet says" level draws 6 from all 13 moves.

**Couplings.** How-to: a goal, four steps scoped by level, a finish and a note ("This is a body game; the screen is the guide"), with a ghost finger (`touchDemo`, `autotouch()` taps the arrow once it shows). Browser: `scripts/browser-check.mjs` plays levels 1–5 (`for level <= 5`, hardcoded), taps the pet once and every arrow, and asserts `[0, 0]`; it is in the `shortlist` suite (`SHORTLIST_ONLY=says`) and `fingerdemo` has levels 1–5. No couch entry, journal entry or picnic step. Voice: `game.pet-says`, `says.copy/body/pairs/rule/freeze`, 13 move lines, `pair/says/trick/tricked/dance/freeze-now/done`. [IDEAS](../IDEAS.md) holds two rows: round two's "more moves and body words" with "6, Opposite day", and the audit's "ends at pre-K by design".

**Known gaps** (read from the code; none has been seen on a device unless said).

1. **Every round climbs.** `finish` always reports 0 misses and 0 hints, so two rounds in a row step her up one level ([`nextLevel`](../../src/progress/difficulty.ts)) whatever she did. A lap child sits at 2 after two rounds, a toddler at 3 after four, a preschooler at 4 after four, a pre-K at 5 after four. The game cannot see her, so adaptation here is a round count, and any new level on top is reached the same way (decision 2).
2. **Level 2 is the same round every time.** `body` deals exactly five words into five turns, so the only variety is order.
3. **Move ids share a line namespace with system lines.** `say(\`says.${move}\`)` is a cast; a new move called `dance`, `freeze`, `pair`, `rule` or `done` would silently speak the wrong line. `MOVE_WORDS` and the `says.<move>` lines repeat each other by hand ("Turn around!" and "turn around").
4. **The mitten targets are hand-set numbers, checked on no critter.** The nose is (4, −112) and the ears (±96, −228). Against `critter.ts`: a `nose` snout is at (0, −106) and a pig's at (0, −100), which fit; a cow's muzzle spans −114 to −22, so the nose lands on its top edge; a duck's bill sits at −100 to −68 and the duck has **no ears at all** (its `ears` is unset), so "touch your ears" touches the air above a tuft; round ears are at (±72, −222), pointy ears reach −292, floppy ears hang at (±112, −190) and a bunny's start near −230, so the mitten is beside, above or below them depending on the pet. The saved companion can be any of the eight critters in any color. No test checks it; the browser loop takes one screenshot per level, but of the default pet only.
5. **Only the arrow continues, and nothing says so.** A grown-up is the clock. If nobody taps the green arrow, a one-year-old taps the pet and sees the same move again, forever; the arrow has no idle nudge. The how-to explains it, but the card is read once.
6. **The freeze level's `Turn.moves` is `['wiggle']` and unused**, and its four dances share one 12-note tune, one pose and one backdrop.
7. **Open-ended how-to line.** `{ from: 5 }` has no `to`; the first level above 5 would claim freeze-dance wording. `scripts/browser-check.mjs` loops to 5 by number.
8. **The sticker pool** (four colors, three poses) has no pinned test; any new pose would redraw stickers she owns.
9. **`wave` always raises the mitten on the right of the screen.** There are no left or right words anywhere, which is right, but the game states no convention: the pet faces her, so its screen-right hand is her left if she treats it as a person and her right if she treats it as a mirror.
10. **The "Pet says" tricks are always "no name".** A trick is only the move said without "{pet} says"; real Simon Says also tempts with the wrong name or with "do this / do that".
11. **Person checks open:** whether a one-year-old copies at all or just taps the pet; whether the mittens read as hands; whether the tune and freeze feel like a game or a wait; whether grown-ups find the arrow ([roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#needs-a-person-or-a-device): "Pet Says as co-play").

## What makes sense here

The first thing the genre shows is that **its challenge is the rule, not the movement**. Simon Says, the HTKS task and Red Light, Purple Light all start from an easy action and change what the cue *means* (only with the name, the opposite, the pairing switched). The second is that **on a screen nobody can check the body**, so a ladder here is made of rules that a *grown-up* can see being kept, plus a reveal ("like this!") so they know what the right thing was. That fits Puddle Island: no judging, no camera, no lives. The third is that the youngest players do not copy at all (the CDC lists waving and pat-a-cake at 12 months, not imitation on cue), so the floor is a **toy she acts on**, not a cue she follows.

The plan keeps the game co-play and adds, cheapest first:

1. **Guards and words** (slice 1), because three gaps are traps (the shared line namespace, mitten positions checked on no critter, an open-ended how-to line).
2. **Fresh content**: ten body words with a chant, eighteen moves in tiers (social, walking and animal moves), counts, five scenes, four tunes with dance styles and named statue poses, and richer tricks. These make every existing level different every round and add no level.
3. **Seven levels on top** (6–12), the HTKS-shaped backbone: *Opposite day*, *Quiet dance*, *More opposites*, *A walk in the woods*, *Sun or moon*, *Pet says, opposite*, and *This hand*. They are the researched progression made inspectable: no level asks the game to judge her.
4. **Entries below level 1**: *Pat the pet* (a lap toy: her tap, the pet's move) and *Your turn!* (she chooses the move).

**Arc by band.** Lap: a pet she can pat that waves bye-bye, plays pat-a-cake and peekaboo. Toddler: copy, then find the part, then two in order, then choose the move herself. Preschool: body words and "Pet says". Pre-K: dance, opposites, a story walk, a switched rule. School (a decision): the rules stacked or switched, and the mirror.

**Overlaps, and who owns what.**

- [Opposites](opposites.md) owns *knowing* antonym pairs on picture cards (its slice 2 is an "acting flip" where the card's picture moves). Pet Says owns *doing the opposite with her body*: the pet shows one move and she does the other. They share up and down, fast and slow, open and closed; Pet Says' table of pairs is its own (it adds loud and soft, head and toes, stomp and tiptoe) and a test must keep every Pet Says pair a *move* pair with a visible difference. Both write-ups now say so.
- [Stop and Go](../GAMES.md#stop-and-go) (wait for green, cars and a light) and *Quiet dance* (stop when the music plays) share "stop" as a rule; the object, the body and the cue differ.
- Rhythm Neighbors, Sound Garden and Clap the Syllables judge or count claps. Pet Says' claps are *counts and manner* (three claps, loud or soft), never a rhythm, and nothing is listened to.
- *Belly Breaths* ([IDEAS](../IDEAS.md)) owns breathing; no level here teaches it.
- *Bear Hunt Walk* ([IDEAS](../IDEAS.md)) is a proposed tap game about words for movement along a route; level 9 is the body version, with a story the pet performs.

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

IDs, saves and the sticker do not change for any of this. Every new move or word needs a spoken name, a line, a performance, a sound and a pose; all of it code-drawn and synthesized.

1. **Moves in tiers** (`MOVES` today has 8). *Base* (the 8 now), *social* (**peekaboo**, **pat-a-cake**), *walking* (**tiptoe**, **march**), *animal* (**hop like a frog**, **stretch like a cat**, **waddle like a duck**, **flap like a bird**), plus **sway like a tree** and **shake it out**. Level 1 draws six from base and social (a lap round never meets an animal move); levels 3, 4 and the walk draw from all. Performances reuse the mittens, `hop`, the stretch and the rotation: peekaboo is both mittens over the eyes then out ("peekaboo!"); pat-a-cake is the mittens meeting and crossing; tiptoe a small stretched hop with a hush; march alternating low hops with `sfx.drum`; frog a crouch, a leap and `sfx.croak`; cat a long stretch and `sfx.yawn`; duck a rock with `sfx.animal('quack')`; bird fast mitten flaps with `sfx.chirp`. Test: every move has a word, a line, a performance and a sound; the tiers partition the pool; level 1's pool is base plus social; no move twice in a row.
2. **Move ids stay clear of system lines.** The lines become `says.move.<id>` (or a test that no move id equals a system line id) and one table holds each move's word and line, so the two cannot drift. Test: ids and system line ids are disjoint; every move's line exists.
3. **Ten body words and a chant** (level 2). Add **eyes, mouth, cheeks, shoulders, chin** (hands crossed to hug) to the five. A round deals five of ten, consecutive words at least 70 units apart so the mitten shows which part. Not added: knees and elbows, which a legless blob cannot show (they read as toes). The **chant** is the "Head, shoulders, knees and toes" shape with the pet's own words ("Head, shoulders, tummy, toes"): three verses, each dropping a word and keeping the touch (omission rounds), then once more all the way through. It is original wording on a traditional pattern and uses the pet's spoken lines with a marimba beat, not a copied tune. Test: ten distinct targets; all lie on the drawn pet for all eight `CRITTERS` (a bounds check on each spec's shapes); consecutive words are 70 or more units apart; the chant's last verse has no spoken word; a round never repeats a word.
4. **Counts and manner** (level 3). A pair turn can carry a count of two or three ("stomp three times, then clap") with the pet counting aloud as it moves ("one, two, three"), or a manner ("a big jump, a little jump"). Counts never pass five; the spoken count is true of the performance. Test: the number of beats a move performs equals its spoken count; no count above 5; pair turns still have two moves.
5. **Richer tricks** (level 4). Today a trick is the move without the name. Add **a wrong name** ("Bunny says clap!"; stay still) and **"do this / do that"** (the pet says "Do this!" with a move and she copies; "Do that!" and she stays still), after the plain trick has been met. Two to three tricks in six to eight turns, still never first, never back to back, and the reveal is always kind ("Hee hee!"). Test: tricks are spaced two or more apart; trick kinds appear only after the first plain one; the first turn is never a trick; the count is two or three.
6. **Dance variety** (level 5). Four tunes of different feel (a bounce, a sway, a shimmy, a march) in the pentatonic steps from `notes.ts`; named dance styles shown by the pet (bounce, sway, twirl, shimmy, robot); the stop pose is a **named statue** ("Freeze like a tree!", "like an ice cream!", "like a flamingo!") the pet strikes and she can copy or not. The dance still lasts three to seven seconds (never the same twice), nothing is timed against her, and "freeze" is held until the arrow. Test: every tune step is in the pentatonic set; each statue has distinct mitten targets; two dances in a round have different tunes or styles; the existing length rule holds.
7. **Scenes.** Five backdrops (meadow, beach, living room, snowy hill, evening sky), one per round by seed, through the `Backdrop` parameters. Nothing depends on one. Test: all five lay out at four views; the pet's clearance is kept.
8. **An idle nudge, not a timer.** After about ten seconds with the arrow showing and nobody tapping, the arrow wiggles (as other games nudge); it never advances by itself and the round never ends on a clock. Test: the nudge starts only while `next.visible`, stops on any tap, and never fires `advance`.
9. **Co-play and off-screen.** Co-play: "She copies, you tap the arrow; now let her be Simon and you copy her." Off-screen: Simon Says with family names; musical statues to a real song; action songs she knows; a sticker or ribbon on one hand for "this hand"; a bedtime stretch to end the day.

### New levels on top

IDs are appended, never inserted, and a new level is easier or harder than the one below it only as the table says. **Accounting** (decision 2, lean): `misses` stay 0 everywhere because the game cannot see her; at the rule levels (4 and 6–12) `hints` count a turn the grown-up had to replay **twice** (the pet tap that shows the move again), so a round with several such turns is "struggling" and a clean one is "smooth". A replay is never spoken as a failure; levels 1–3 and 5 stay 0 and 0.

### Left out on purpose

- **A camera, a microphone or any body tracking.** The ground rules allow it only if a grown-up opts in; it needs new infrastructure and a device check, and the game's point is the grown-up watching. Not before a person has played the co-play version.
- **Scoring, ranks, gold moves, badges, prizes for counts of sessions,** and **elimination** (Just Dance, GoNoodle, Simon Says, musical chairs).
- **Speed as a challenge** (a faster and faster song, a beat she must hit). Tempo may be a choice, never a test.
- **Knees, elbows and other parts the pet cannot show.** Legs and elbows would need new art; the words wait.
- **Rhythm patterns to echo.** Sound Garden, Rhythm Neighbors and Clap the Syllables hold them.
- **A couch entry.** A body game does not suit a controller face-off, and the co-play line covers a family. A "sit-down Simon" with controller buttons would be a different game.
- **A journal source or a picnic step.** Nothing here is a discovery about the world.
- **Left and right before school.** Held to level 12.
- **Recorded narration or licensed songs.** The chant is original wording on a traditional pattern; parent recordings are a proposal.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Levels on top and the school window.** The audit says the ladder ends at pre-K by design. Lean: override it for 6 Opposite day, 7 Quiet dance, 8 More opposites, 9 A walk in the woods and 10 Sun or moon (pre-K 3–10), and open a school window at 10–12 with 11 Pet says, opposite and 12 This hand. Or build only 6–8. Or leave the school window out: a six-year-old may find a pet's body game young, and the HTKS research is for 4–8 only as a measure.
2. **How a rule level counts.** (a) Always 0 misses and 0 hints, as levels 1–5 do (every pair of rounds then climbs her). (b) Misses stay 0; a turn the grown-up replayed twice counts one hint, so two rounds with two or more such turns step her down. Lean: (b) for 4 and 6–12, (a) for 1–3 and 5; never spoken as a failure.
3. **A mirror convention.** If left and right are ever in the game, the pet's screen-right mitten is her right (lean) and the word "mirror" is said. Or leave level 12 out.
4. **Tricks with a wrong name and "do this / do that"** at level 4 (lean: yes, after the first plain trick).
5. **The chant.** Lean: yes, original wording on the traditional pattern. Device speech for the words is unchecked.
6. **Level order.** 7 is easier than 6, and 9 is easier than 8; and a preschooler never reaches the walk or dance levels without skipping. Accept, or take it with the level-order decisions ("May a band's range skip levels?").
7. **Entries below level 1** (*Pat the pet*, *Your turn!*) join the level-order decisions already listed.
8. **Make a dance** as a kept creation needs a new creation kind (bounded list, repair, migration, backup tests): decide with the treehouse owner.
9. **Person checks to add:** whether a one-year-old pats, copies or just watches; whether the new mitten targets read on a duck, a cow and a bunny; whether the pet's counted and named moves read right with device speech; whether "Quiet dance" is a game or a confusion to a three-year-old; and whether a four-year-old can do "the opposite".
