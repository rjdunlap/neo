# Market Stall deep dive

*ID `market-stall` · bands preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–7 on 2026-10-11 · session `claude/exciting-ride-je8lip` ([PR 118](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/118))*

## The game today

A fruit stall with a striped awning and a shopkeeper (a pig, bear, cat, dog or cow, seeded). A price tag shows the numeral and that many dots. She taps shell coins in the purse along the bottom; each lands on the green mat on the counter, and the voice counts the running total aloud. A tap on the mat sends the coin nearest the finger back; the bell takes the payment. Bigger coins are worth more and each shows its number. Cozy Village (`lands.ts`, with Clock Tower, Lemonade Stand, Scoop Shop, Mail Carrier and the other everyday games). `skills`: counting, money, adding, counting-on. Music `STYLES.stickers`. **Not a couch game, not in the journal, not in the picnic.** The ladder, level by level (`PLANS` in `logic.ts`; 3 or 4 orders each):

| Level | Mode, coins and prices | Bands whose window includes it | What she does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `count`, coin 1, price 1–4 | preschool | "The apple costs 3": tap three 1-coins, ring the bell. Count out *n* |
| 2 | `twos`, coins 1 and 2, price 2–6 | preschool, pre-K | The first coin worth more than one. Any exact payment buys |
| 3 | `mix`, coins 1, 2 and 5, price 3–9 | pre-K, school | Three coin sizes. The running total is said, so she hears 5, 7, 8 |
| 4 | `pair`, coins 1, 2 and 5, two prices of 1–5 (total 3–10) | pre-K, school | "A pear for 4 and a plum for 3": add the prices, then pay. Running totals are not said (the adding is the puzzle) |
| 5 | `ways`, coins 1, 2 and 5, price 4–10 | school | Pay, then buy another at the same price and pay with *different* coins. The same coins again is a miss |
| 6 | `change`, price 2–9, the customer pays a 10 | school | "They paid 10": give the change. The 10-coin sits on the counter as decoration |
| 7 | `fewest`, coins 1, 2 and 5, price 4–10 | school | Pay with as few coins as possible. An exact but longer payment stays on the mat with "can you use fewer shells?"; the second time is a miss |

Bands: preschool 1–2, pre-K 2–4, school 3–7 (`LEVELS`). Mistakes teach: too little or too much is a boing and a spoken comparison ("It costs 7"); two misses on an order show faint coins of the fewest payment (or a different second way) and say "Here is one way to make it." Rounds end with a tada. `misses` counts wrong payments, the repeated "fewer" nudge, and the same-way slip; `hints` counts the faint-coin help.

**What each round draws from.** Six fruits (`GOODS`: apple, orange, lemon, pear, grapes, blueberries; each item is picked by the seeded generator, and the two items of a level-4 order are different fruits), five shopkeepers, one backdrop, one tag shape, shell coins worth 1, 2 and 5 (`COIN_LOOK` also holds an unused 3 and the 10 that level 6 shows). Prices come from `makeOrders`: every target is 1–10 and never the same twice running. Voice: 13 `shop.*` lines in `voice-script.ts`; the how-to card has a `LevelLine` per level and a ghost-finger bot (`autotouch`, `paymentFor`, `payTouch`).

**Known gaps, read from the code (nothing was played or run).**

1. **Everything stops at ten.** `makeOrders` throws away any target over 10, so a price of 11 cannot exist. The mat holds twelve coins, the tag's dots end at the second row (the tag box ends at y = 44; price 11 would put a third row at y = 50, outside it), and the mat's rows of six are spaced 80 apart while the 10-coin is 88 across.
2. **`LEVELS.school.max` is a literal 7.** `planFor` clamps silently (`describeLevel(8)` would return level 7's name), the how-to's `{ from: 7 }` lines are open-ended, and the browser loop's `7` is a number.
3. **The first coin of each size arrives unnamed.** Level 2 brings a 2-coin and level 3 a 5-coin with only the number on the face and the running total heard afterward. Nothing says "this one is worth five", and nothing shows that a 5 is the same as 2, 2 and 1. Preschoolers cannot yet read 5.
4. **Misses are named by size, not by kind.** "Not enough yet" and "That is too much" cover the classic slips: counting each coin as one (price 7, four coins down), being one off, paying the price on the change level (she gives 4 back when the price was 4), or paying only one of the two prices on level 4. The change level's lines only say "the price and the change make 10."
5. **Change is spoken as coin values, not counted up.** On level 6 each tap says the coin's value ("5", "2"), while the shopkeeper's method (and the Year 2 and Year 3 giving-change objectives below) is to start at the price and count up to what they gave: "7… 8, 9, 10." The 10-coin is a prop that is never connected to the price.
6. **Level 5 can earn two hints per order.** `wrongs` and `hinted` reset after the first way, so one order can show faint coins twice. Everywhere else a hint counts once (Frog Hop's [gap 5](frog-hop.md#the-game-today) is the same shape).
7. **The sticker and `coinArt` are shared.** `sticker()` picks a fruit with `rng.pick(GOODS)` and draws `coinArt(1|2|5)`; `GOODS` is also the order's fruit table, so adding a stall's goods would change every saved sticker. Lemonade Stand imports `coinArt` from this game and draws it for its purse. Nothing pins either.
8. **Greedy `fewest` is only right for sets like 1, 2, 5 (and 10).** It is a true minimum for these coins; a 3 or a 4 would break it. The unused 3 in `COIN_LOOK` is a trap.
9. **A full counter is a bare boing.** The thirteenth coin only boings. No line says "the counter is full, use a bigger coin".
10. **Only fruit and one stall.** IDEAS names "fruit and toys to sell"; `props.ts` has only eight kinds (apple, orange, lemon, pear, blueberries, grapes, balloon, flower).
11. **No couch, journal or picnic hook.** There is nothing for a grown-up to play, and nothing the treehouse could keep.
12. **Not a bug, checked:** the purse spread (`spread(n, 220, view.w - 160, 170)`) fits three coins in the 1024-unit design width, and the faint hint coins stay on the counter: reading `anotherWay`, a second way for a price up to 10 is at most five coins (the fewest set, then twos and ones, then all ones only for a price of 2 to 4 whose first way was the other two). Level 7 is recorded: the `woods` shop loop pays too much, shows the hint and finishes (verification entry of 2026-10-10).

## What makes sense here

**Market Stall's own axis is what a coin is worth, and that a handful of coins is one amount.** A one-year-old taps and hears a count; a three-year-old counts out a number of identical shells (Give-N); a five-year-old finds that one big coin is the same as several small ones (value, then equivalence); a seven-year-old trades ten ones for a ten and gives change by counting up. Price, cost, profit and saving are **Lemonade Stand's** and the proposed **Maker Harbor's**; the ladder here never asks "is it worth it".

Four points shape the plan.

- **Say the value, not just the total.** The voice counts the running sum but never names a coin's worth, and every new coin arrives unnamed (gap 3). A coin's value is a small number of dots and a spoken word before it is a numeral.
- **Name the slip.** The classic errors are countable: coins counted as ones, one off, the price instead of the change, one of two prices. Each deserves its own line, from a small classifier in `logic.ts` that a test can pin (as Frog Hop's `missKind`).
- **Count up to give change.** The shopkeeper's method is also the unknown-addend idea (1.OA.B.4): the price is the start, ten the end, the coins fill the gap. Spoken that way, level 6 teaches subtraction without a minus sign.
- **Exchange is the bridge past ten.** Past ten, Market Stall adds a 10-coin and the teaching moment is *ten ones are one ten* (1.NBT.B.2a, the Montessori trade), not the subtraction or the number line.

**Arc by band.** Lap: a coin drops into a jar and a voice counts (below level 1). Toddler: "give me two" with the stall closing at the right number (below level 1). Preschool: count out 1 to 4, the first bigger coin (levels 1–2). Pre-K: three coins, adding two prices, and the entries that name a coin and compare two prices. School: the same price paid two ways, change from ten, fewest coins, then a purse counted, the ten-shell coin and prices to twenty, two baskets, change from twenty, whether she can afford it, and trading up.

**Overlaps, and who owns what** (the other write-ups agree).

- **Lemonade Stand** owns price, cost, profit, saving and the market week; Market Stall's top levels stay on **making amounts, exchange and change** and never ask what a good is worth to a customer (IDEAS keeps these separate on its Market Stall row). Maker Harbor's tickets are a different currency, never shell coins.
- **Duck Pond** owns *ten and some more* and doubles as parts of a set; **Frog Hop** owns crossing ten on a line (its 8–10). Market Stall's claim is one thing neither makes: **the ten as a coin**. Level 9 pays 11–20 with a 10 and some ones, and its hint never says "ten and some more", it shows the 10-coin and the rest.
- **Clock Tower** owns time; **Pet Kitchen** owns fractions of food; **Inchworm Measure** owns length; **Picture Graph** owns "how many more than" from a graph. Level 12's "can I buy it?" compares an amount to a price, which is a coin question, not a graph.
- **The coin art is shared with Lemonade Stand.** Pin it before anything changes (build slice 1).

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

Saves, IDs and the sticker do not change for any of this (the sticker and `coinArt` are pinned first, slice 1). Every new picture is code-drawn and every sound synthesized.

1. **Name the coin** (levels 2–3 first round, then never again in a round). When a coin that is new at this level (the 2 at level 2, the 5 at level 3) is first used, the pet says "That is a two. It is worth two." and the coin flashes its dots. Not a miss, not a hint. Test: a coin is named once per game start, only if its value is new at the level.
2. **Pips on the coins** (levels 1–3). A small row of pips equal to the value, in fives, drawn on a Market Stall layer only so `coinArt`, the icon and the sticker stay the same. A preschooler matches pips to the tag's dots. Test: pips equal the value and fit inside the coin; a 10-coin has ten (two rows of five).
3. **Misses named by kind** (a classifier `missKind` in `logic.ts`). *Counted coins:* the coin count equals the price but the total does not ("You put 4 coins, but a 5 is worth five. Count what each is worth."). *One off:* total = target ±1 ("One more shell"/"One too many"). *Paid the price, not the change* (level 6: the total equals the price, "That is the price. They gave you 10; give back what is left"). *Only one price* (level 4: the total equals one of the two). *Too big a coin* (the 5 pushed it over). A hint still follows two misses. Test: every wrong total has exactly one kind; "off by one" is exactly target ±1; the kinds are only reachable on the levels that can produce them.
4. **Count up the change** (levels 6 and 11). The shopkeeper's voice starts at the price and counts up as each coin lands ("7 … 8, 9, 10") instead of saying values; a small **ten strip** under the tag shows the price cells filled and the change cells filling. The value of each coin is still the first thing said on touch-down. Test: for a correct change, the spoken sequence strictly increases from the price to the amount given and ends exactly there.
5. **One hint per order on level 5** (a rule, gap 6). Test: an order that earns help in both ways reports one hint.
6. **A full counter says so** (gap 9): "The counter is full. Try a bigger coin." Not a miss. Test: it never changes `misses` or `hints`.
7. **A first hint that teaches, then the answer.** After two misses the pet says "Try the biggest coin that fits first" before the faint coins at the mix, ways and fewest levels; the second miss shows the coins as today. Hint accounting stays one per order. Test: a hint always leads to a legal payment.
8. **More stalls** (every level): a **flower stall** and a **balloon stall** from the existing `flower` and `balloon` props, seeded per round; the awning, goods and keeper change, the coins, tag and mat stay. A bakery or toy stall needs new props and waits (sketches). The stall is a new table beside `GOODS` so the sticker's fruit pick is unaffected. Test: every stall lays out clear of the pet at four views; the sticker for seeds 0–99 is unchanged.
9. **The shopkeeper says goodbye in her own words** (a pool of five thank-yous that include the item). Not a test beyond voice coverage.
10. **Co-play and off-screen that match each level:** "take turns being the shopkeeper" (already); "ask her which coin is worth the most" for levels 2–3; "can you pay a different way?" for 5; "count up from the price to give change" for 6; "ten ones for one ten" for 9. Off-screen: a pile of real buttons or shells and a few pretend prices; a piggy bank counted out loud; a snack-shop game at home with a cup of ones and a cup of tens.

### Left out on purpose

- **Profit, cost, advertising, haggling, restocking, speculation and anything that expires** (*Lemonade Stand*, *Neopets*, *Animal Crossing*): the Lemonade Stand and Maker Harbor lines, not this game's.
- **A purse that refills by magic, or a wallet she can run out of** (*Toca Store*). The endless purse is a manipulative, and a round says so in a co-play line; a *fixed* purse appears only in level 12, restored every round, never carried across rounds.
- **A timer, a queue of waiting customers, lives, a winner or a score in a child's save.** A customer waits as long as she needs.
- **Real money, purchases or ads.** Shell coins are a play currency; they are not the child's stickers and never feed the treehouse.
- **Written change as a column sum, and decimals.** Pounds-and-pence recording is Year 3 work; the island stays in whole shells.
- **A calculator that adds for her** (the toy register). The point is the adding.
- **A journal entry or picnic step** beyond the sketch above: nothing in this game is a discovery about the world until a level has one.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Levels on top and the school window.** Lean: build 8, 9, 10 and 11 (ready or nearly), define 12 and 13 after a child plays the rest, in a school window of 3–13 (no lower bound moves). The ladder has small inversions (8 is no harder than 7; 13 is easier than 12 but heavier). Accept them, or fold them into the level-order decision.
2. **Shell coins or real coins.** Lean: keep shell coins (bigger is worth more) for the whole island ladder; real-coin look and ¢ or $ marks only in the grown-up window (G4), and only after a currency decision (US cents, pounds, or none). Or never.
3. **The ten-shell shell and its tag.** Lean: a ten bar (one frame of ten cells) plus the ones on the tag from price 11, and the 10-coin joins the purse at level 9. Or keep the tag a numeral at 11–20 and rely on the voice.
4. **Counting up as the voice of change** (levels 6 and 11, fresh 4). Lean: yes, counted up from the price with a ten strip, coin values still said on touch-down. Or keep saying values.
5. **Level 13, Trade up.** Lean: define it with a child first (the multi-tap selection is a new interaction) and ship it unscored. Or drop it: level 7's tap-to-swap already teaches the trade for single coins.
6. **A grown-up window** (G1–G5): needs a `grownup` band or a couch-side plan table (as Jelly Drums decision 5 and Frog Hop decision 6). Lean: after Lands stage B, starting with G1, because it only needs a true-minimum `fewest` that gap 8 asks for anyway.
7. **Level 12's "too dear" tap.** Lean: a miss the first time, a nudge after; the first round is "tap what you can buy". Or never a miss.
8. **Level order.** The four entries below level 1 join the existing level-order rows.
9. **Person checks to add:** whether a six-year-old counts each coin as one (the counted-coins line); whether the pips help a three-year-old match a coin to the tag's dots; whether counting up from the price is understood or confusing next to the coin values said on touch-down; whether tens-and-ones words come out clearly with device speech; whether a child finds the 12-coin counter limit frustrating or a nudge toward bigger coins; whether a bigger-is-worth-more coin set confuses a child who has seen real coins; whether a toddler gives "two" and stops.
