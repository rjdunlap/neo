# Fluffy Salon deep dive

*ID `fluffy-salon` · bands lap, toddler, preschool, pre-K · levels 1–5 on 2026-10-19 · session `claude/dive-fluffy-salon-finish` ([PR #613](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/613), resuming the claim in [PR #591](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/591))*

Research and ideation only: nothing under `src/` changed. Fluffy Salon is the island's grooming game and its one "shape something with tools" activity (the [visual-art study](subjects/visual-art.md) calls it the nearest thing to sculpting). This dive keeps the free, satisfying fur play that Toca Hair Salon made the genre's standard and grows what the requests ask for: a single color word for a toddler, two customers, a comparison against a friend's hair, two colors at once, a customer who changes their mind, and two gentler rungs for the youngest hands. The creature-world rework's most concrete wish for this game, that the style **stays on the creature** when she goes home ([PET-INTEGRATION](../rework/PET-INTEGRATION.md#4-what-a-round-makes-comes-home-to-the-creature-rw06-rw07-rw10), its recommended step 3), is specified here and gated on the rework's collection model.

## The game today

**What she does.** Her own pet sits in the salon chair under a striped cape with a head of 26 strands of fur (`src/games/fluffy-salon/index.ts`, `logic.ts`). Tool buttons sit along the bottom (grow, snip, comb, curl) and five color bottles down the right (pink, blue, purple, yellow, green). A finger dragged through the fur works on every strand within 38 body units of it: grow lengthens each touched strand by 9 (to at most 220), snip cuts a strand back to the first point the finger reaches and sends the cut fluff tumbling as particles, comb and curl move a strand's curl by 0.06 a touch, a color recolors it. Grow rings a pentatonic bell, snip ticks, the rest squish. A round-button mirror at the top right ends free play, or checks a request. Request levels show a small card at the left with the asked-for style drawn on a little copy of the pet's head, and the pet speaks it ("Can you make my hair short and blue, please?"); tapping the card, or the pet's face in free play, repeats the line.

**Learning purpose.** Creativity and pretend play (skills `creativity`, `pretend-play`), descriptive words heard and acted on (`vocabulary`: long, short, curly, straight and the color names), and `fine-motor` control (sweeping strokes, keeping a snip where it is wanted). Land Paint Pier; music `STYLES.paint`; the corner guide steps out because the pet is in the chair, so the pet repeats the instruction itself.

**The ladder** (`PLANS` in `logic.ts`; windows lap 1–2, toddler 1–2, preschool 2–4, pre-K 3–5; no `order` or `entryLevels`):

| Level | Mode | Bands whose window holds it | What she decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `play`: grow and the five colors | lap, toddler | Nothing can be wrong: where to stroke and which color; the mirror ends it |
| 2 | `tools`: every tool | lap, toddler, preschool | Which tool to try, and when she is done |
| 3 | `ask`: three one-part requests ("make it long", "pink", "curly") | preschool, pre-K | Which tool does what was asked; the round moves on by itself when the fur is right |
| 4 | `two`: three two-part requests ("short and blue") | preschool, pre-K | Two things to do, in either order, and when to look in the mirror |
| 5 | `match`: two pictured styles (length, curl and color) | pre-K | Three things to read off a picture, with the words spoken too |

**Content pools.** Five hair colors (`HAIR_COLORS`); requests draw length from {short, long}, curl from {curly, straight} and a color, avoiding a repeat description inside the round. Every request starts from fur that is clearly not the asked style (long starts at 70, short at 130, curly at 0.1, straight at 0.75, and a different color), so no request is done before she touches it (rule-tested). Free play starts at length 55, curl 0.3 and a seeded color. The customer is always her own pet; the cape's stripe is purple (teal for a purple pet); the wall and checked floor never change. "Done" means an average length of at least 145 (long) or at most 50 (short), at least 80 % of strands in the asked color, and an average curl of at least 0.6 (curly) or at most 0.2 (straight).

**Mistakes and help.** At level 3 there is no mirror: the round moves on as soon as `needs()` is null, so nothing counts as a miss; after fifteen seconds without progress (a stroke with the tool that helps, or picking it) a tool button glows (one hint); wrong-tool strokes, off-fur pats and wrong picks do not reset the quiet. At 4 and 5 the mirror checks: looking too early is one miss with "Almost! I want it longer / pink / curlier", and the second miss glows the tool for the first thing still missing. When the missing thing is the colour and at least half the strands have it, the pet says "Almost! A little more {fix}, where it glows" and the old-colour strands glow. The ghost finger (`salonMove`, `strokePath`) tours the tools in free play and sweeps the roots with the right tool for a request, looking in the mirror only when nothing is left to do; a rule test proves every request finishes in a few sweeps even at the slowest frame rate.

**Known gaps** (write-up findings, not fixes):

1. **(Fixed in slice 1.) The hint timer is a clock, not an idle timer.** `sinceProgress` is reset only when a request starts (`index.ts:318`) and grows every frame, so at level 3 the tool glows fifteen seconds in whether or not she has been working the right tool slowly, and that glow counts as a hint. Two careful rounds can then read as struggling.
2. **(Fixed in slice 1.) Touches off the fur get nothing.** A pat on the pet's body, the cape or the chair, or anywhere outside the touch rectangle, is silent. At lap that is most of the first touches.
3. **(Fixed in slice 1; the self-finishing lap level is slice 3.) Free play has one exit and no invitation.** The mirror is the only way to finish levels 1–2; it appears after some work or 25 seconds but never says what it is for, and a lap child may never find a 54-unit button in the top-right corner. Stamp Studio's lap level finishes by itself after the eighth pat; nothing like that exists here.
4. **(Fixed in slice 1.) "I want it pink" when it is mostly pink.** The color rule asks for 80 % of strands; a child who has colored most of the head hears a line that sounds like the color is wrong, and nothing shows which strands are still the old color.
5. **One customer, one room.** The pet is the only head, the cape stripe is the only variation, and nothing from a round is kept: the style resets at the next visit, which the [integration review](../rework/PET-INTEGRATION.md#4-what-a-round-makes-comes-home-to-the-creature-rw06-rw07-rw10) calls the most Neopets-like single change the island could make.
6. **Six buttons at lap.** Level 1 shows grow plus five colors and the mirror for a child under two; the first lap rung should need no button at all.
7. **No journal, no couch, no picnic step.** None of these is wrong for a grooming game; recorded so the slices below do not assume them.

**Person checks open:** [DEVICE-CHECKS](../DEVICE-CHECKS.md) lists "Fluffy Salon and Sound Garden (satisfying free play, clear exits, guided modes that keep creative exploration)". The exits are gap 3.

**Learning references.** The descriptive words are the point for the youngest: *(recalled)* the Head Start Early Learning Outcomes Framework's infant-toddler language goals put "follows two-step directions that involve a familiar object or routine" at 36 months and single-step directions earlier, which places one-word color requests (*Pink, please*) in the toddler band and two-part requests (level 4) at preschool as built; the headstart.gov framework page returned 403 in this session, so the wording was not checked. For the comparison rung, Common Core [K.MD.A.2](https://tasks.illustrativemathematics.org/content-standards/K/MD/A/2) asks a child to "directly compare two objects with a measurable attribute in common, to see which object has 'more of'/'less of' the attribute, and describe the difference", with *Longer and Shorter* and *Which is Longer?* among its tasks; *Longer than Pip's* is that standard done with fur, side by side in the mirror. Measuring with units (1.MD.A.2) is the school rung this dive leaves as a decision.

## What makes sense here

- **Keep free play free, and give it an ending.** The references are unanimous that the grooming pleasure needs no goal; what Toca lacks and the island needs is a comfortable stop. The mirror becomes something the pet asks for, and the lap rung finishes by itself the way Stamp Studio's does.
- **Grow the request ladder by what a request can mean, not by its length.** One word for a toddler; two words (built); a picture (built); two customers (whose request is whose); a comparison (longer than a friend's); two colors at once (both must be there); a changed mind (undoing what was just done). Each is a new thing to understand, not more to remember.
- **The customer is who she cares about.** Friends from `CRITTERS` in the chair now; her own creatures once the rework's collection exists; and the style staying on the creature is the single most visible "comes home" the island can offer. That persistence is specified below but not built before the collection model, so the save changes once.
- **Tools reverse each other.** Grow and snip, curl and comb: *Changed my mind* makes that explicit, and it is why no haircut is ever ruined.
- **Not here:** timers, a mess that accrues, affection meters, customers who get cross, styles locked behind visit counts, and a second accessory system when the rework is defining wearable slots.

The long arc: lap pats fluff and sees it grow; toddler snips and answers a color word; preschool follows two words and serves two customers; pre-K reads a picture, compares, mixes two colors and revises; a school window, if the developer wants one, measures.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action.** A stroke through the fur: it grows under the finger with bells, and the fluff bends and falls. At lap today the stroke has to land on the fur and the fur alone (gap 2); *Pat the fluff* answers every touch in the touch rectangle, growing fur where there is fur and making the pet giggle and the fur wobble (`Critter.poke()`, which squashes everything attached) where there is not.
- **Touch-down feedback and consequence.** Every tool sounds only when it touches a strand, like the Play-Doh buzzer: the bell for growing, the tick and tumbling fluff for a snip, the squish for comb, curl and color. The consequence is drawn at once on the head. Return to readiness is immediate; nothing locks.
- **A concrete art and sound example.** A **floof shake**: when free play's fur passes a length, or a request is done, the pet shakes its head once: the hair `Graphics` is redrawn for half a second with each strand's `curl` term offset by a decaying `Math.sin(t * 18) * 0.3`, so the fluff ripples from the roots out, with `sfx.squish()` twice and a short `sfx.bell(8)` at the end. It uses the existing `drawHair`, `strandPoints` and `ctx.tw`; no new asset.
- **What varies on replay.** The customer (pet or a seeded friend), the start look, the cape's stripe and the wall's gradient pair (seeded from `palette.ts` pastels), which requests come and in what order, and, once the rework lands, the creature's last style as the starting point.
- **Reasoning versus reading, memory and motor.** A request's words are always spoken and pictured; no reading is needed at any level. Memory load stays at one request at a time (two cards stay on screen at *Two customers*; nothing is hidden). Motor precision is the color coverage rule (80 %) and keeping a snip where it belongs; the build slices soften the first with a glow on the strands still the old color and a "more pink" line, not by lowering the rule.
- **Supported completion and the comfortable stop.** A request level's hint lights the tool that fixes the first thing still missing; nothing times out and nothing is lost. Free play ends when she looks in the mirror; at lap the pet looks by itself after enough pats. Again replays the level; the orange next level plays the one above.
- **Co-play.** The card's line already makes the grown-up the customer ("long and pink, please!"). *Two customers* gives a second person a real role: one card each, and either order finishes. Solo stays complete.
- **Creative choice is never wrong.** Free play never counts a miss; at *Pink, please* a wrong color still paints (she sees what blue looks like) and is only named.
- **What a person or device must judge:** whether a one-year-old's pats land in the touch rectangle at all; whether the falling fluff reads as "cut" or as "broke it" to a toddler; whether "longer than Pip's" is understood with two heads side by side; whether the two-color mirror check feels fair when one side is thin; and whether the pet speaking for itself (no corner guide) is clear on an iPad's speech.

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

### Easier entries and levels between existing ones

Covered above: 6 and 7 enter below 1–2 and 8 sits between 2 and 3 for toddler and preschool. No decision about the level order blocks them: `order` and `entryLevels` are built (`src/progress/difficulty.ts`), and `landing()` sends a saved 6–8 carried into pre-K to its easiest level. Pre-K does not list 6–8.

### Left out on purpose

- **A tool bar with ten sets** (Toca 3): clutter; the island's rule is depth before clutter.
- **Cleanliness, affection or a mess that returns** (Nintendogs, Pokémon Refresh): neglect while away.
- **Styles unlocked by visit count** (Shampoodle): a streak.
- **A memory rung** (a pictured style shown then hidden): memory load without a new idea; the `match` card stays on screen.
- **Braids and ties**: a drag-ordering interaction that is its own game; not a tool here.
- **Washing and brushing teeth**: the [Splish Splash dive](splish-splash.md) already keeps grooming here and bathing there.
- **A second accessory system**: the rework's RW02 wearable slots with deliberate misfits are the accessory system; this game uses them when they exist (decision 2) rather than growing its own bows.

## Decisions for the developer

Each has a default below; the build proceeds on it.

1. **The lap round that ends by itself.** Default: at *Pat the fluff* the pet looks in the mirror by itself after twenty quiet seconds once the fur is long (no miss, no hint), as Stamp Studio's eighth pat does. Alternative: the mirror tap stays the only exit, and the invitation (wiggle and "Look in the mirror!") is all the help.
2. **The style comes home.** Default: gated on the rework's collection model (RW05) and the creature customization slice (RW06/RW07): a bounded `hairstyle` on the creature record (26 strands, each a length 8–220, a curl 0–1 quantized to twentieths and a color name), set when a round finishes on an owned creature, shown at home and used as the next visit's start; no save change before then. Alternative: a `creation` slot now in the existing picture frame, which the integration review argues is the wrong home for it.
3. **Accessories.** Default: none until RW02's wearable slots exist; then a bow or hat becomes a request part at 5 and 9 ("short, blue, with a bow") and a free-play item at 2. Alternative: a code-drawn bow now as a sixth tool (the 2026-10-09 audit's row).
4. **A school window.** Default: none; 12 is pre-K's top, as the audit's "ends at pre-K by design" says. Alternative: school `order [12, 13]` with 13 *Six beads long*: a bead ruler stands beside the chair and the request is a count of units ("make it six beads long"), 1.MD.A.2 with fur; the mirror lays the ruler against the longest strand.
5. **Who sits in the chair at 9 and 10.** Default: code-drawn friends from `CRITTERS` now, swapping to her owned creatures when RW05 casting lands (the integration review's open call 1). Alternative: wait for the creatures before building 9 and 10.
