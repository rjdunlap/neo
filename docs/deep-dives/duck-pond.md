# Duck Pond deep dive

*ID `duck-pond` · bands lap, toddler, preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–11 on 2026-10-10 · session `claude/roadmap-item-14-game-content-uh41js` ([PR 114](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/114))*

## The game today

Ducks hop onto a pond with a bounce, a splash and a quack, and every number is counted aloud. Counting Cove (`lands.ts`, with Monster Munch, Little Helpers, Pet Kitchen, Frog Hop, Picture Graph, Lasso Loops and Fill and Dump). `skills`: counting, number sense, adding. Music `STYLES.hub`. The ladder, level by level (`PLANS` in `logic.ts`):

| Level | Plan | Rounds | Bands whose window includes it | What she does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | `along` 3 ducks | 2 sets | lap, toddler | tap each duck on the bank; it hops in and is counted |
| 2 | `along` 5 ducks | 2 sets | lap, toddler | the same, five ducks |
| 3 | `make` 1–3, sign with dots | 4 | toddler, preschool | the sign says a number; tap ducks in until it is there |
| 4 | `make` 2–5, dots | 4 | toddler, preschool | the same |
| 5 | `make` 3–6, numeral only | 4 | toddler, preschool, pre-K | the same without dots |
| 6 | `howmany` 1–5 | 5 | preschool, pre-K | the ducks arrive by themselves; tap the lily pad with the number |
| 7 | `howmany` 2–8 | 5 | preschool, pre-K | the same |
| 8 | `add` up to 5 | 5 | pre-K, school | "3 ducks are swimming. 2 more come!" Then how many? |
| 9 | `add` up to 10 with "swim away" | 5 | pre-K, school | adding and taking away, a coin flip each round |
| 10 | `ten` starts 3–9 | 5 | school | some ducks swim; how many more make ten? The missing ducks then swim in and fill the ten places |
| 11 | `hide` totals 5–10 | 5 | school | the sign gives the whole; some ducks vanish under a bridge; how many hide? |

Ranges (`LEVELS` in `index.ts`): lap 1–2, toddler 1–5, preschool 3–7, pre-K 5–9, school 8–11. Pads (`padValues`): the answer and two neighbours within ±2, clipped to 1–10, shuffled. A wrong pad wobbles and counts the answer aloud (counting on from the visible ducks at levels 10 and 11); a right one is counted aloud and the ducks quack.

**What a round draws from.** There are no content pools beyond numbers: one kind of duck (yellow, `CRITTERS.duck`), one backdrop (seed 33), ten fixed pond places `SLOTS` that fill in the same order every round (so "three" is always the same three places), nine more for level 11's visible row and five under the bridge for the hidden ducks. Numbers are drawn uniformly from the plan's range, so the same number can repeat back to back (only level 10's starts are kept apart, by `tenStarts`). The sticker is a seeded sprinkle of one to three ducks on a ripple.

**Couplings.** How-to: a goal, one step line per mode with the last open-ended (`{ from: 11 }`), a finish line open-ended (`{ from: 6 }`) and a dots note for levels 3–4. A ghost finger (`touchDemo`, `autotouch()` taps ducks or the answer pad; the rule is `duckMove` in `logic.ts`). Browser: `BROWSER_SUITE=originals ORIGINALS_ONLY=ducks DUCK_LEVELS=…` (the loop runs `Array.from({ length: 11 })` by number and asserts `[0, 0]` for levels 1–5 and `[2, 1]` after two wrong pads on levels 6–11); `fingerdemo` plays all 11 (level 10 took 66 s and level 11 69 s, against the 75 s limit). No couch entry, journal entry or picnic step.

**Known gaps** (read from the code; none has been seen on a device unless said).

1. **The pad glows after one miss, and a hint counts on every miss from the second.** In `tapPad`'s wrong path, `glow(true)` runs on every miss, while AGENTS says the glow comes after two; `hints++` fires on the second miss and again on a third. The browser check taps two wrong pads, so it never sees the one-miss glow.
2. **Levels 1–5 can never be wrong, so they climb on round count alone.** `along` has no question; `make` rests the spare ducks the moment the sign's number is reached (`rest()`), so there is no overshoot. `finish` is always `[0, 0]`, two rounds in a row step her up, and nothing can step her down. The bank is deliberately one bigger than the sign (`bankSize`, with a test calling it "so stopping is a choice"), but the game makes the choice for her. The voice line `duck.extra` ("Oops, that's {count}! We need {n}.") is a leftover of the version where she could overshoot; nothing speaks it.
3. **The hidden ducks are never shown.** At level 11 the ducks beneath the bridge are real but invisible (`hideUnderBridge`), and on a right answer the check is speech only ("5 were hiding! 4 and 5 make 9!"). The part-whole is never seen together, and a child who answers by luck sees nothing.
4. **Hard caps of ten.** `padValues` clips to 1–10, `SLOTS` has ten entries, and tests pin both (`SLOTS.length` is 10 in a test). Teens, zero, or doubles past 5 + 5 cannot be built without a layout and pads change first.
5. **School's top is hard-coded.** `LEVELS.school.max` is 11 and does not follow `PLANS.length`, so appending a plan does not widen the range. `describeLevel` falls through to the add/subtract wording for any unknown mode, the how-to's `{ from: 11 }` and `{ from: 6 }` lines would describe a new level wrongly, and the browser loop's 11 and its portrait check at level 9 are numbers.
6. **No pinned sticker test.** The sticker draws `CRITTERS.duck` directly (one to three ducks), so a new duck skin, a frog or a night scene must not touch it, and nothing would catch it if it did.
7. **Fixed arrangements.** The same ten places fill in the same order, so a number is always the same picture (a child can learn "that shape is five" by memory of the layout, not by counting), and nothing is laid out as a ten-frame or in fives although the pond is already the "ten places" of level 10. Counting is never asked of scattered ducks, mixed kinds, or zero.
8. **Numerals only on the pads.** From level 6 the answers are bare numerals (62-point font, 74-unit hit circles at 190 spacing); a child who counts to five and does not yet read "5" can only guess, and the wrong-pad counting teaches by missing. The dots that sit beside the sign at levels 3–4 are gone by level 5.
9. **Taps on ducks overlap.** Bank ducks sit 104 apart with 72-unit hit circles; harmless today because any duck counts, but not for a game that makes ducks individually tappable.
10. **A long demonstration.** Level 10's full ten-place fill-ups and level 11's counting are 66 s and 69 s of the 75 s allowed, so any new level with more ducks needs a shorter path for the ghost finger.
11. **Person checks open** ([roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#needs-a-person-or-a-device): "Duck Pond 11 on an iPad", the bridge hides its ducks clearly and the pads are comfortable in both orientations). Also unchecked: whether a toddler can use level 5 (numeral only, three to six ducks), and device speech for the new lines.

**Developmental and standards sources.**

- **Counting principles.** Gelman and Gallistel's five principles: one-to-one correspondence, stable order, cardinality (the last number counted says how many), then abstraction (anything can be counted) and order-irrelevance ([Stanford summary](https://prek-math-te.stanford.edu/system/files/media/document/2017/The%20Principal%20Counting%20Principles.pdf), **search summary only**). The first three are what levels 1–5 already practice; the last two are never asked, since the ducks are always identical and always counted in the same order.
- **Giving N.** Wynn's Give-N task: children know "one", then "two" and "three", then work out the counting principle, about a year after "one" ([a review](https://jnc.psychopen.eu/index.php/jnc/article/download/10577/10577.html?inline=1), **search summary only**, ages uncertain). Level 3–5's "put N in" is a Give-N task, so the choice of when to stop is the skill.
- **Subitizing.** The IES progression (**opened**) gives perceptual subitizing, seeing up to four or five without counting, from about age 3, and **conceptual subitizing**, seeing a set as two smaller sets and knowing the whole (3 and 4 make 7), after it; Clements and Sarama recommend it through elementary school ([Springer chapter](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/978-3-030-00491-0_2), **search summary only**). The pond today asks nobody to subitize, and hiding ducks asks for the whole.
- **Standards** (opened). K.CC.B.5: count to answer "how many?" up to 10 scattered objects (and 20 in a line); K.CC.A.3: numerals to 20, and 0 means no objects; K.OA.A.3: break numbers up to 10 into two parts in more than one way; K.OA.A.4: the partner that makes 10; K.NBT.A.1: compose and decompose 11 to 19 into ten ones and some further ones; K.MD.B.3: classify objects into categories and count each; 1.OA.A.1: add and subtract word problems with the unknown in any position; 1.OA.C.6: make a ten as an addition strategy; 2.OA.B.2: fluently add and subtract within 20; 2.OA.C.3: odd or even by pairing up to 20 objects, and write an even number as a double. Levels 1–11 cover K.CC.A.1, K.CC.B.4–5, K.OA.A.2–A.4 and 1.OA's result-unknown and part-unknown problems; K.NBT.A.1, K.MD.B.3, the 0 case of K.CC.A.3 and 2.OA.C.3 are not in the game.

## What makes sense here

**Duck Pond's own axis is the part and whole of a set she can see**: counting it, building it to a number, and seeing how it splits into parts (some here, some hiding; ten and some more; the same on both sides). Its pond is already a ten-place frame (level 10 fills ten places and says "a full pond"), so the long arc is to make that frame explicit, five and five, and use it for teens and doubles.

The research makes three points. **First, the ladder lets her be right without choosing**: levels 1–5 cannot be missed and climb on round count (gap 2). The standard Give-N shape, with a real decision to stop, is what the game claims to test. **Second, counting is only asked of a tidy, identical, fixed set**: scatter, mixed kinds, zero and a number in more than one arrangement are what Counting Collections and the K.CC standards ask, and they are all fresh content that adds no level. **Third, quantity beyond ten and part-whole in more than one way are the gaps at the top**: teens as a ten and some more, doubles, and every way to split a number. The game's ground rules still hold: no timer, no loss, a wrong answer teaches, and a grown-up can count with her.

**Arc by band.** Lap: ducks hop in and are counted; a song where they wave and come back. Toddler: put in the number and decide when to stop. Preschool: how many, shown as dots as well as a numeral, with a zero. Pre-K: adding and taking away, with the sentence written after; scattered and mixed ponds. School: make ten and hiding ducks, then ten and some more (the teens), doubles, and finding every way to split a number.

**Overlaps, and who owns what** (the other write-ups agree).

- **Frog Hop** owns the number line and *crossing ten* (8 + 5 by hopping to 10 first; its [deep dive](frog-hop.md) adds back over ten, a free stop at ten and counting up to take away). Duck Pond's teens are *composition* (ten ducks and four more are fourteen), not a hop across ten, and its pond never shows a line.
- **Bubble Pop** owns count sequences and counting back as a skill (its levels 12–14). Duck Pond's "Bye-bye ducks" is a song, not a question.
- **Picture Graph** owns "how many more than" read from a graph. Duck Pond's comparison (below level 1, "Which pond has more?") is *more, fewer or the same* by matching one to one, not the difference. A "how many more" level was considered and left out for that reason.
- **Little Helpers** owns equal groups (2 × 3); **Monster Munch** owns sharing and leftovers; **Garden Grow** owns arrays; **Market Stall** owns coins. Doubles here is two equal parts of a whole; it stops at the double and the near double.
- **Bubble Pop's sketch of evens and odds** (pop numbers that pair up): ducks swim in pairs, which suits the idea better. Whichever is built first owns it (decision 4); Bubble Pop's write-up now points here.
- **Peekaboo Barn** owns hiding for memory; the bridge here hides one part of a known whole, so a count of a hidden number never needs memory.

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

IDs, saves and the sticker do not change for any of this. Every new picture is code-drawn and every new sound synthesized.

1. **Hidden ducks paddle out** (level 11). On a right answer the hidden ducks swim out from beneath the bridge one by one, counted aloud, so the whole is seen together (gap 3). Test: a hidden duck's reveal position is inside the pond and clear of the visible ducks'; the number revealed equals the answer.
2. **The sentence on the sign** (levels 8–11). After the answer the sign writes the number sentence ("3 + 2 = 5", "5 − 2 = 3", "7 + 3 = 10", "4 + 5 = 9") and the voice reads it, following Sum Swamp's idea without dice. A sentence is only ever shown after a right answer, so reading it is never required. Test: the sentence's value is the plan's answer for every seeded story.
3. **Real stopping** (levels 4–5; decision 2). The spare ducks stay on the bank (`bankSize` already makes one more than asked) and she decides: a green **"That's enough!"** check beside the sign ends the round. Too few or too many ducks is a gentle miss with a spoken count ("Oops, that's 6! We need 5." — the unused `duck.extra` line) and a duck tapped on the pond hops back to the bank, as a free undo (the fruit basket in Fill and Dump works this way). The ducks are still counted aloud as they go in. Level 3 (one to three) is left as it is: the round finishes by itself at the number. Test: a round ends on the check only when the pond holds exactly the sign's number; the undo returns exactly one duck and never goes below zero; a bot reaches the number and presses the check.
4. **Arrangements** (levels 6–7 and the starting ponds of 8–9). Instead of ten fixed places in a fixed order, a round deals one of three layouts by the number: a **dice pattern** (1–6), a **five-and-five** layout (two rows of five, filling the top row first), or a **scatter** (placed with at least 108 units between ducks). Level 6 uses dice patterns and rows, level 7 adds scatter. Counting aloud follows the layout's own order (left to right, then down), so no duck is counted twice. Test: for every number the layout has exactly that many distinct places inside the pond, at least 108 apart, and the spoken counting order visits each duck once.
5. **Zero** (levels 6–7, K.CC.A.3). The pond sometimes starts empty ("How many ducks? None!") and pads include 0 (the pad range becomes 0–10). At level 9, an "all swim away" story gives 0. At most one zero per round of five, never first. A wrong pad counts "nothing here". Test: a zero round has zero ducks; its pads include 0 and two neighbours from 1–2; no round has zero first.
6. **Dots on the pads** (levels 6–7, fading at 8). Each answer pad carries its dots, grouped as on the sign (fives first), so a child can match dots to ducks before she reads the numeral. The dots fade by level 8, as the sign's did by level 5. Test: each pad's dot count equals its numeral, and every pad is at least 100 units across with the dots inside.
7. **Mixed ponds** (levels 6–7, after the layout slice). Ducks and frogs on lily pads share the pond and the question is "How many frogs?" (K.MD.B.3 and the abstraction principle). The frog is the one Frog Hop draws, moved to `src/art/` so both use it (`frogArt` is a local function there today), with `sfx.croak()` that already exists. Test: both kinds are present in every mixed round, the asked kind's count is not the total, and no round asks about a kind with zero.
8. **More scenes** (every level). Today every round has one sunny backdrop. Add an evening pond, a frog pond (lily pads and reeds) and a **night pond with fireflies** as a skin; the night needs new art (`Backdrop` has no moon or stars; Owl Walk draws its own moon by hand). A seeded scene per round. The sticker stays the ducks. Test: all scenes lay out at four views with the pond, sign, bank and pads clear of the pet.
9. **Honest word choices.** Two ways to ask at level 6 ("How many ducks?", "How many are swimming?") and at 8 ("how many now?", "how many in all?"). Number words come from `count`; the spoken sentence for the sign says "plus", "minus" and "makes".
10. **Co-play and off-screen.** Co-play already says "count out loud and touch each duck"; add "tell her when to stop" for levels 3–5 and "cover some and ask how many are hiding" for 11. Off-screen: a bath with toy ducks (put two under a washcloth), counting spoons, a ten-frame egg carton with ten buttons and a cover, counting steps to the mailbox.

### Left out on purpose

- **Timers, countdowns, a race to the number, rivals, a fox or any loss** (*Potato Counter*, *Count Your Chickens*, *Hi Ho! Cherry-O*'s penalty spaces). A wrong answer counts with her; nothing is taken away.
- **Crossing ten on a line** (8 + 5 by hopping to ten first). Frog Hop owns it.
- **Counting back as a skill, skip counting by fives and tens, "make 20".** Bubble Pop's levels 12–14 hold counting sequences; its write-up already leaves make-20 to Duck Pond and Frog Hop, and teens here are composition, not arithmetic.
- **"How many more than" from two groups.** Picture Graph owns it.
- **Equal groups, sharing, arrays, coins and fractions.** Little Helpers, Monster Munch, Garden Grow, Market Stall and Pet Kitchen.
- **Numbers to 100 or addition within 20 as drills.** The game stays at composing, one story at a time.
- **A dice or spinner.** A random number is not the learning; the generator deals a fair one.
- **Multiplayer or couch.** A counting toy is better with a grown-up counting out loud; the co-play line covers it.
- **A journal or picnic step.** Nothing here is a discovery about the world.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Levels on top and the school window.** The plan of record is round two's 11 *Hiding ducks* (built) and round three's one level, **12 Doubles**, which the roadmap counts among its 37 new levels on top. Lean: keep 12 Doubles (its numbering and idea unchanged) and add **13 Ten and some more** and a to-define **14 Every way**, in a school window of 8–14 (the lower bound stays 8, so no saved level moves). That makes three levels where the plan counted one. Or build only 12; or stay at 11. **Level order:** 12's first rounds (totals to 10, a fixed shape) are easier than 10 and 11, and 13 (totals 11–19) is harder than 12, so 12 is an inversion like Pet Says' 7 and Peekaboo Barn's 9. Accept it, or fold it into the level-order decision; the grown-up's level arrows reach any of them.
2. **How levels 3–5 and the hint rule count.** (a) Leave as is: nothing can be missed, so levels 1–5 climb on round count, and a pad glows after one miss with a hint counted on every further miss. (b) Lean: make stopping a real decision at 4–5 (a "That's enough!" check, a free undo, a gentle miss for too few or too many) so these levels can step her down; glow only after two misses; count at most one hint per question. This changes how fast a toddler climbs and is the only way adaptation means something at 3–5; it lowers nothing already saved.
3. **Level 14's ordered pairs.** Do 3 + 2 and 2 + 3 count as two ways (lean, with the card saying "same ducks, swapped sides"), or one? And does 0 + 5 count? Lean: two non-empty sides, ordered; or leave level 14 out until a child plays 12 and 13.
4. **Who owns evens and odds.** Duck Pond (ducks swim in pairs; lean) or Bubble Pop (its sketch of pairing bubbles). One of them is built; the other drops its row.
5. **The toddler window's top.** Level 5 (numeral only, three to six ducks) may be beyond what two-year-olds do (Give-N children become cardinal-principle knowers about three and a half). Lean: leave 1–5, because stopping at 3 and 4 with dots is the toddler's work and level 5 is reached only after smooth rounds; add it to the person checks. Or end the toddler window at 4, which would pull a toddler saved at 5 down to 4.
6. **A shared frog.** Moving Frog Hop's `frogArt` into `src/art/` is shared infrastructure that two games use, so it is allowed (lean: yes); or draw a second frog for Duck Pond.
7. **Level order.** The three entries below level 1 join the existing level-order rows (the layout-and-pad slice is not blocked by them).
8. **Person checks to add:** whether a three-year-old stops at the number without a check; whether the dots on the pads are used or ignored; whether children read two-digit pads 11–19; whether the teen words (eleven, twelve, thirteen…) come out clearly with device speech; and whether a night pond is calm or confusing.
