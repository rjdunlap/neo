### What people can do, and what remains a hypothesis

- **L/T:** a present action has an immediate visible result. A fresh mark and its maker can remain side by side. The toddler's deeper choice is matching one obvious feature, not understanding millions of years or fossilization.
- **P/K:** propose comparing pictured features, recording a location and replaying a short process with spoken support. These are design placements informed by early-years practice, not demonstrated fossil expertise. A replay is a model, not an eyewitness account.
- **S:** NGSS supports using broad fossil types and relative order to justify a simple past-environment claim. Do not infer readiness for every 6-year-old from a grade-3 expectation.
- **U:** propose coordinating two incomplete records and noticing that several reconstructions can fit. Add evidence and alternatives before increasing board size.
- **M:** a 12-year-old might choose the Lost Layers mystery or a Trackway Tales case with two plausible reconstructions; a 13-year-old could enter the same kind of challenge through a separately defined `grownup` entry. That appeal is untested. M is an audience, not a new runtime band; 12 remains `school`, 13+ `grownup` under current policy.
- **G:** a cozy deduction case, an equal co-play role and a plain-HTML conversation card each serve a different purpose. A child need not wait for a parent to supply the correct answer.

## First game's proposed ladder: Fossil Dig, shared with earth-materials E5

**Fun action:** reveal an impression and make a little evidence record. **Learning goal:** a preserved feature plus context supports a comparison; the record does not reveal every property of the original organism. **First build proposal:** K only, one reviewed leaf-impression case, one round, one record. A new game is justified only if comparison/context changes the player's decision beyond Splish Splash and Puzzle Pals.

These are *proposed* ordered plans, not saved IDs or approved band ranges. Define P 1, K 1–2 and S 2–4 only after observation/layout review; U and older are separate follow-ups. Do not put level 5 in a child ladder just to reach parents.

| Plan | First round / new decision | Ending, help and meaningful rule proof |

| **1: A leaf left a clue** · K first, possible P | A broad impression already has a visible edge. Tap exposed/dusty regions to reveal; the location remains marked. | **2: Look more closely** · K/S | Two reference leaves share an outline but differ in a large, reviewed margin/vein feature visible in an enlarged inset; no microscopically small hit targets | Open the inset and make the comparison; the record names the feature that matters. | **3: A place, not just a picture** · S | Two simplified undisturbed layers have shown fossil observations; attach each record to its original site/layer before comparing before/after | Correct context pairing plus supported relative order ends a finite two-record case. | **4: What can this tell us?** · S/U follow-up | A reviewed marine-shell case compares two past-environment pictures with a provided evidence key. | **5: Another look changes the answer** · U follow-up | Start with two reference reconstructions that fit the visible clue. **First-round accounting:** all taps, reveals, reference selections, looking at clues and undo are exploration. An explicit Check against an incompatible reference is one miss, a gentle boing and a specific spoken clue. Check before enough evidence is visible repeats the instruction without a miss. Two incompatible checks on the same comparison create one persistent glow on a useful feature/reference and count one hint; do not recharge while it remains. Requested answer-leading support counts one hint. An “Explain and finish” path demonstrates the relation through those same controls, records supported completion and calls the normal shell finish. One sticker either way; no specimen is lost, damaged or withheld. This is a proposed contract to freeze in D1, not current behavior.

**Creative/uncertain modes:** Trace Prints has no wrong choices. In plan 5, plausible hypotheses before new evidence are not wrong answers. Do not silently turn zero-miss exploration into mastery: define a fixed exploratory entry or a reviewed adaptation policy before registration; no shared difficulty change is authorized by this study.

**Implementation shape/cost:** one authored leaf-impression silhouette and two clearly distinguishable reference drawings, a coarse reveal mask, a location stamp, reference selection, Check, replay/support, and a ghost-finger bot using actual input. ## Trackway Tales: one bounded replay case

Use invented animal footprints on a clearly labeled toy reconstruction card first; real track anatomy/gait needs paleontology review. Four visible successive marks are compared with two replay buttons: one proposed movement leaves alternating contacts, another leaves paired contacts. The player can scrub/replay either, then attach the one supported by the marks. Future cases vary direction or retain two explanations; do not infer species, chase, emotion, exact speed or social behavior from marks that cannot establish them.

Replays and draft placements are experiments. Explicit incompatible Check gets a clue to the first mismatching contact, with glow after two misses. A “we cannot tell from these marks” outcome is correct when more than one replay fits. Hint reveals an informative footprint segment; assisted finish plays it slowly and attaches the supported explanation. The future rule test stamps the same deterministic motion records as the preview, verifies each accepted replay reproduces the observed contacts under the disclosed tolerance, and proves a hint eliminates at least one incompatible replay. Avoid promising gait science from a toy rule. First-touch feedback and bot actions must show the input a person uses.

## Lost Layers: one older and parent case

**First concrete puzzle:** two columns are marked “undisturbed; layers stay in order.” Site A contains symbol observations X then Y then Z (bottom to top); Site B contains X, a visibly marked missing section, then Z. The pictured Y fossil is not observed at B. Two alignment cards place B's gap either across A's Y horizon or before it, with Y simply unobserved in B's preserved material. Both fit the initial observations. The first case supplies only these two candidate alignments and accepts both; it does not claim they exhaust every real geological history. In a later case, an independently established shared marker at A's Y horizon can constrain whether that horizon is preserved at B. Defining how the marker establishes that relationship is part of scientific review, not a property inferred from identical-looking fossils. Symbols represent an explicitly authored puzzle record, not a claim that all fossil species occur globally at once.

The meaningful action is connecting a record across sites while preserving its internal order. In the first case, finishing with both compatible alignments earns completion; the record explicitly cannot decide. A later case asks which third observation would separate the alternatives. “No fossil pictured here” is not proof of extinction; missing sediment, preservation and observation limits remain possible. Numerical ages, overturned strata, reworking, radiometric dating and real index-fossil reliability are outside the first model.

Generate by choosing a small authored history and masking observations, then enumerate all alignments allowed by the disclosed constraints. Accept all consistent solutions and never enforce a hidden master alignment. Help highlights a violated order/marker relation or offers an observation that distinguishes remaining alternatives. Draft moves and undo are free; only an explicit inconsistent Check counts a miss, with ordinary repeat help/support. Finish a finite case; optional compare-another is a fresh choice, not an optimization gate. A new game is contingent on a distinct interaction after the Outcrop Detective audit.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** a large touch clears a noticeable patch immediately; tool pulses/soft pentatonic feedback happen on touch-down. No instruction to read, precise brush stroke or tiny bone hunt. K can act before the explanation ends.
- **Layout:** reserve pet/home corners; keep leaf, location and references on one screen. Targets remain at least 100 logical units in child play after scaling, 64 only for grown-up-only cases. Add an enlarged clue view instead of shrinking features. Portrait/landscape and land fit remain unverified until implementation.
- **Speech:** put titles, invitations, comparisons and support in `voice-script.ts` during a build. `ctx.instruct` repeats the current goal; `ctx.say` describes the evidence. Dinosaur pronunciations and “before/after” wording need device checks. A study's suggested wording is not approved content.
- **Replay variety:** seeded draws from reviewed contrast pairs, altered reveal starting spots and different missing observations. Do not mirror away diagnostic features or procedurally invent anatomy. Changing color alone does not make a deeper science case.
- **Comfortable ending:** one small record/case, shell celebration, optional replay. No damaged fossil, failing excavation, countdown, rare drop, lost collection or duty to return.
- **Off-screen:** Trace Detectives uses large paper drawings and a conversation; a family may describe or watch. NPS kit descriptions inspire replicas and reference cards, but the island does not advise collecting fossils or disturbing sites. Review a release card before printing/speaking it.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Content reviewer:** name a paleontologist/museum educator for the leaf impression, reference distinctions, track model, relative-time assumptions, bird/dinosaur wording and any named organism. Content/model validation precedes building a real-species case.
2. **Shared ownership:** keep Fossil Dig as earth-materials E5's single seed; decide whether evidence/context earns a game after the Splish Splash/Puzzle Pals audit. Lost Layers likewise needs a concrete interaction comparison with Outcrop Detective.
3. **Extinction framing:** propose no disappearance spectacle below school; introduce factual “no longer living today” in reviewed school material when needed. Birds remain living dinosaurs. Zoo recovery framing does not prohibit discussing past extinct life, but wording needs an explicit decision; never imply collecting/rebuilding brings an extinct animal back.
4. **Range and placement:** freeze first K range and land capacity; choose `school`-top versus separately registered 13+ cases without inventing a middle band. Do not promise that an older case fits a younger game's adaptive ladder.
5. **Exploration and support:** settle creative/uncertain-mode adaptation, supported-finish accounting, and whether any later record is worth saving. First case requires no persistence changes.
6. **Human history:** human origins, archaeology, living cultural knowledge and fossil-land ownership are outside this first study's content packet. Research them with relevant community/expert sources before proposing scenes; dinosaurs and ancient human tools must not appear as a real shared period.
