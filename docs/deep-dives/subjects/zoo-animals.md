# Zoo animals: groups, tricky cases, keeper care and comebacks

**Subject:** `zoo-animals` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M, G · **Date:** 2026-10-16 · **Session:** `claude/zoo-animals-subject` · **Base:** `ee89b32` · [PR #462](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/462).

Research and proposals only, from [roadmap chunk 28](../../ROADMAP.md#chunks), the [subject procedure](../subjects.md#the-procedure-one-session-one-subject) and the [standards audit](standards-audit.md), which put this row first in wave E. No reference game was installed or played. No game, art, band, zone, save field or parent card was built.

**Scope.** What [`life-science`](life-science.md) (needs, life cycles, seeds) and [`ecosystems`](ecosystems.md) (food webs, changing habitats) leave out: **vertebrate groups and the tricky cases** (bat, penguin, whale, platypus), **body coverings**, **behavior and calls**, **what keepers do** (feeding, enrichment, check-ups), and **endangered species and recovery**. Ocean habitats, depth and tides stay with the not-started `oceans` row (see [boundaries](#overlap-boundaries)).

**Recommendation:** define **Who Am I?** first, for pre-K to upper school: one clue at a time ("I have feathers", "I can fly", "I feed my babies milk") and she turns over the friends it rules out until one is left. It teaches the groups by their features and makes the tricky cases a pleasant surprise rather than a trivia question. Its first three levels need **no new anatomy**: the cast is the eight `CRITTERS`, the owl (a `Critter` in Owl Walk) and four animals already drawn inside games (penguin, frog, turtle, fish). The bat is the first new species, chosen because it is the cheapest tricky case. Next define **Enrichment Day** (preschool to school: watch what a friend likes to do and choose something that lets it, with several good answers) on one habitat kit, and **Species Comeback** for grown-ups and the oldest children as a studbook-pairing puzzle, which is the part of recovery that the ecosystems study's Meadow Plans does not already cover. These are proposals, not a build queue.

### Overlap boundaries

- **Feeding** is Animal Snack's action; **check-ups** are Teddy Doctor's. By the rule "same action, extend", a keeper feeding round is an Animal Snack cast mode and a cooperative check-up a Teddy Doctor mode. Only the **enrichment choice** with several valid answers is a new decision, so only Enrichment Day is a new game.
- **Who Am I? and Critter Sort.** Critter Sort places critters by a stated or inferred rule. Who Am I? narrows a gallery to one by successive clues (elimination), adds real groups and tricky cases, and at its top lets her choose the questions. The action and the reasoning differ, so it is a new game; group signs inside Critter Sort stay its deep dive's decision.
- **Species Comeback and the ecosystems study's Meadow Plans (F7).** Meadow Plans already owns patches, sockets and food paths under scenarios. Species Comeback leaves habitat layout to it and owns what F7 does not: **choosing breeding pairs from a family tree** so the next generation keeps variety, then matching young animals to a release site by their needs. Corridors are left out.
- **Whale and `oceans`.** This row owns the whale as a *classification* case (a mammal that lives in water); `oceans` owns ocean habitats, depth zones and tides. One whale drawing serves both.
- **Habitat needs** stay with Habitat Helpers and `life-science`; **food webs** with `ecosystems`; **inheritance mechanisms** with the proposed `genetics` row.

## What fits

- **Fits:** elimination by features (Who Am I?), behavior-based enrichment with several valid answers (Enrichment Day), a family-tree pairing puzzle (Species Comeback), cast swaps that put zoo friends into games she already knows, a zoo-visit card.
- **Does not fit, and why:** a zoo manager or tycoon (money, guest pressure, an animal as stock); collecting species as a quota; quizzes of names (2-LS4-1 excludes names); showing extinction, poaching or a dying animal to young bands; a timer on feeding rounds; animals that get hungry or sad while she is away (the treehouse rule: nothing accrues or waits); real animal-care instructions without a reviewer.
- **Where it lives.** The developer decided on 2026-10-16 to dress existing lands with habitat kits rather than add a zoo land, so "zoo" here means a keeper's care for friends on the island, not a destination.
- **Captivity framing.** Keepers care for animals that live at the zoo; the island never sells, buys or catches them. How much to say about why zoos exist is a [decision](#decisions-for-the-developer).

## A first game's ladder: Who Am I?

**The board.** A gallery of three to eight friend cards (at least 100 units, spread clear of the pet). The pet gives one clue at a time, spoken and shown as a picture sign. She taps a card to turn it over (the friend waves and hides); the last friend left jumps out: "It's me!" Clues are always true of the answer, from a reviewed trait table.

| Level | Band | First round | What she decides | Mistakes and hints |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · One clue | pre-K (preschool entry a decision) | 3 cards, one picture clue ("I have feathers"); tap the one who fits | Which friend has the feature | Tapping a friend who does not fit: boing, "Cow has fur, not feathers"; two misses glow the answer |
| 2 · Turn them over | pre-K, school | 4 cards, two clues; after each, turn over every friend it rules out | Who the clue rules out, and who stays | Turning over a friend who fits is a miss with the reason, and the card flips back. Tapping a card she need not turn yet is a miss only if it fits |
| 3 · Groups by features | school | 6 cards from all five groups; three clues from coverings ("smooth wet skin"), eggs or milk, breathing | The same, with less familiar features | A wrong turn names the feature it has; after two, the cards the clue rules out shimmer |
| 4 · Surprises | school | Includes the penguin and the bat: "I have feathers" keeps the penguin, "I can fly" turns it over; "I feed my babies milk" keeps the bat | That the group goes by features, not by flying or swimming | After a surprise the pet explains it once ("Bat feeds its babies milk, so bat is a mammal that flies") |
| 5 · Which group? | school | The clue is a group ("I'm a bird"); she turns over everyone who is not | Using the features that define a group | The miss names the feature: "Penguin has feathers, so penguin is a bird" |
| 6 · You ask | school | A secret friend; three question cards to choose from; the pet answers yes or no, and she turns over the friends the answer rules out | Which question to ask | Asking is never a miss, however many questions; only turning over a friend the answer keeps is. The hint suggests a question that splits what is left |
| 7 · Make a riddle | school (upper) | She picks a friend and chooses clue cards until the pet can find it; the pet "guesses" aloud between the friends that still fit | Which clues make one friend the only answer | Not judged: an ambiguous riddle makes the pet ask for another clue |
| 8 · Platypus (later) | school (upper) | A friend that lays eggs and feeds milk | Holding two features at once | As level 4 |

**Finish.** A round finishes when one friend is left (levels 1–6) or the pet finds her friend (level 7); one sticker each round, with help. Free looking (tapping the clue sign to hear it again, peeking at a friend's card) is never a miss.

**Rule tests.** For every generated round: each clue is true of the answer by the trait table; each clue rules out at least one remaining card; applying the clues in order leaves exactly one card; level 4 rounds contain a surprise that a "flies" or "lives in water" shortcut gets wrong; at level 6 every secret friend can be reached with the offered questions and the hint question splits the remaining set; level 7's checker agrees with the table on which clue sets are unique. A rule test checks consistency with the table, **not biological truth**: the table needs a named reviewer.

**The trait table must be careful.** Draft cases a reviewer should settle: whales have a little hair, so "fur" is never a clue when the whale is in the gallery (use milk); "scales" for a turtle and for a fish are different things, so they get different signs or the clue is avoided; many animals can swim, so "swims" is never a clue; the cast's fish and reptile are egg-layers, but not every fish or reptile is, so the egg clue is about these friends, not the whole group, and the speech says "I lay eggs", never "all fish lay eggs"; the cast's bat is a fruit bat, speech never states what all bats eat (three vampire bat species do drink blood), and the blood myth is not raised for young bands.

**Cost.** Levels 1–3: one game (M) plus moving four drawings to shared art (S). Level 4: the bat (S–M). Level 8: the platypus (M). Voice lines grow with the trait table; how-to card per level, and a ghost-finger `autotouch()` (it taps the cards a clue rules out).
