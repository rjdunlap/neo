# Greece and Rome: a ring of stones, a jar of pebbles, water that runs downhill

**Subject:** `ancient-mediterranean` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M, G ([definitions](../subjects.md#the-audiences)) · **Date:** 2026-10-19 · **Session:** `claude/subject-ancient-mediterranean-finish`, [PR #607](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/607) (finishing the claim of [PR #590](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/590)).

Research and proposals only. **Define Water Road first:** close a ring of stones with its keystone and pull the wooden frame away, then lead water from a spring to a town fountain so that every stretch runs downhill, over a valley on a row of arches and, for the oldest, down and up again through a siphon. Its ladder follows what made Greek and Roman building and water work: a true arch stands only when the ring is closed and pushed back at both ends; an aqueduct is a gently falling channel, mostly underground, that crosses a valley on arcades or in a pressurized pipe; the town's fountains drink first. Each level's action *is* that structure, not a costume on a quiz. Voting by pebbles and by lot, the owl coin and the obol, the weighed oil jar, the mosaic border, the Roman clock face, the long jump and a nineteen-hundred-year-old birthday invitation extend games that already own counting votes, paying, weighing, patterns, clocks, measuring and letters. The youngest bands get stones that fly into an arch and a chime when the keystone lands, with no claim that an infant learns history. Older play adds the arcade, the siphon, who gets water when there is less, two sources that disagree and a grown-up water commissioner's puzzle. No runtime band, zone, game or system is added here.

| [Roman aqueduct](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roman_aqueduct) (first 100,000 characters) | Water moved "by gravity alone"; Vitruvius's minimum fall "not less than 1 in 4800", about 21 cm per km; the Pont du Gard's channel falls about 34 cm per km over 50 km and stands about 48.8 m above the river; most conduits ran "0.5 to 1 m below ground" following contours, so of Rome's roughly 780–800 km of conduit "only about 47 km stood above ground"; arcades crossed valleys and inverted siphons took deep ones (a header tank, pressurized lead pipes across a low bridge, a receiving tank "slightly lower than the start"); settling tanks and *castella* fed "public fountains … then public baths, then fee-paying private users". **Consequence:** the whole Water Road ladder: downhill every stretch (4), over the valley (5), mostly underground (6), the siphon (7), the fountains first (8) |

### Real-history boundaries

- **Living countries, living cultures.** Greece, Italy, Turkey, Egypt and the whole middle sea are home today. The island says *long ago, by the middle sea* and *in the owl city* or *the seven-hill town*; real names, dates and "Athens" and "Rome" appear in the M rung's spoken clues and on the grown-up card (decision 1, as the silk road study leans).
- **No war, no army, no arena.** The Persian and Peloponnesian wars, Alexander, Caesar's and Claudius's invasions, Hadrian's Wall as a frontier, Boudica, gladiators and the Colosseum, the race in armour, boxing, pankration and wrestling: out. KS2's "power of its army" is met by not depicting it; a reviewed grown-up line may say the roads and the wall were built by and for soldiers.
- **Exclusion and enslavement.** Athens's votes excluded women, enslaved people, freed people, children and resident foreigners; enslaved people quarried, built, rowed and served across both worlds; the Vindolanda game's plot is a search for an enslaved person. Younger rungs omit all of it and never say "everyone voted". The M *vote or lot* rung and the grown-up card state it in one reviewed sentence (decision 5); never "the Greeks invented democracy for everyone".
- **No religion and no myths as history.** Temples as buildings, Hera's races and the Pantheon's name are only what they are on screen (a roofed hall, a girls' race, a round room with a hole in the roof); gods, the origins of Christianity (California 6.7.5–6.7.7), Jewish history under Rome and the Olympic sacrifices stay out. Aesop, the Iliad and the Odyssey belong to the literature row, not here.
- **Athens and Sparta.** The comparison (6.4.6) is built on war and on Sparta's treatment of children and helots; out at every band.
- **The Parthenon Marbles.** A live ownership dispute between two countries; a grown-up card line ("people disagree about where they belong") at most, after review.
- **Every historical line spoken or printed needs a named reviewer** (a classicist or a KS2 educator who teaches the Greeks and Romans units). Rule tests check that an arch ring is closed, that water falls, that pebbles are counted and that a sum is right; they cannot check history.

## What fits, and what does not

- **Fits:** stones that close a ring and a frame pulled away; a prediction about which arch stands; a buttress where the push goes; piers each a little lower; arches side by side over a valley; round the hill or through it; a siphon that ends lower than it starts; fountains that drink first; pebbles counted; a lot that is fair because every ticket had the same chance; one coin worth six; the jar weighed empty and full; a border that repeats; a clock that writes IIII; a jump measured in worms; a letter nineteen centuries old. Each is a fun action whose consequence is the learning.
- **Fits as a mode or note:** pebbles in the jar, the lot machine and *vote or lot* (Village Vote, cross-listed with the civics study); owl and obols (Market Stall); weigh the jar (Seesaw Balance); the Roman clock (Clock Tower); mosaic border and mirrored emblem (Pixel Pictures); long jump (Inchworm Measure); a sea-leg line (Caravan Days); the bead frame's Roman cousin (Lasso Loops, one provenance line); the tablet line (Mail Carrier); two sources (Attic Finds or Household Casebook).
- **Does not fit:** armies, invasions, walls as frontiers, gladiators and the arena; ostracism as a game; emperors, generals and philosophers as answers; gods, temples as worship, the Pantheon's name; Athens versus Sparta; a timeline quiz of dates; a city builder with scarcity, debt or riots (Caesar and Zeus, *recalled*, not opened); dressing the pet as "a Roman"; any claim that one city "gave" the world democracy or that the key pattern is Greek alone.

**Tentative windows:** toddler 1; preschool 1–2; pre-K 2 and 4 (two whole channels); school 3–8; grown-up 9 (or The Water Commissioner registered separately). `school`'s upper rungs are selected by level, not a new U/M band. A first implementation can be 1–3 only. Never silently add a level below a saved one; use the `order` contract if a younger rung is appended later.

**First-round packet to define:** one frame drawn as a bent plank between two low walls, three to seven wedge stones at least 100 logical units after scaling, a chime for the keystone and a soft slide for loose stones, the frame as a drag or tap target off the pet, the pet's walk through as the ending, two seeded stone counts, voice lines, a how-to card per level and a ghost finger that taps the pile and pulls the frame. No water until level 4, no physics engine, no drawing.

**Mistakes and accounting:** level 1 counts nothing. From level 2 only a *commit* (the last asked prediction, a pull with a block placed, opening the spring, setting a tank) can count a miss; dragging, looking, lowering a pier and pulling a frame as an experiment are free. After two misses on one arch or road, one hint glows the useful thing and counts once; asked-for help counts at most once per road. Assisted completion still finishes for one sticker. At level 6 either road is right; at 8 any fountains-first order is right.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is tapping a pile of stones, not answering a question. Touch-down lifts the next stone at once; the keystone chimes; the frame slides with a wooden creak and the arch holds with a low hum; water runs with a trickle that stops dead in a pool. Palette colours for pale stone, a blue channel, green hillside and a terracotta town; no sepia filter. Stones are wedges a child can tell apart from blocks; the water road is one drawn line; piers are short and tall by eye; the fall budget is a column of drops. Portrait keeps the frame or road across the upper half, the pile and the tray below it, and the pet bottom-left; *Open the spring* is at least 100 units and off the pet. This is not a music game: pick an existing compatible island style in the definition.

Replay varies stone counts, which side is open, pier heights, the valley's width and the spring's units, never the rules. Nothing accrues; nothing is owed. Round completion uses `ctx.finish`; cleanup releases taps, drags and timers.

**Creature-world fit (proposals for the rework, not promises).** A keystone chime and an arch the pet walks through are a natural permanent mark on the creature home (RW05's collection): each arch finished could stand on the home's hill as a non-gating record. A mosaic floor from Pixel Pictures could become the treehouse's doorstep (customization through making). An RW07 adventure could ask *How will the water reach our garden?* and send a child to Water Road 4 for a bounded result. None of this is required for the first slice.

**Co-play:** frame-puller and pebble counter (above). **Off screen, proposed:** *An arch on the table* and *Our stadion* need the movement reviewer's one line on jumping safely; *Knucklebones* needs the small-parts line; *Rota* needs only its honesty line; *A birthday invitation* waits on a historian.

5. **Exclusion and enslavement depth.** Younger rungs omit them. Decide whether the M *vote or lot* rung and the grown-up card state in one reviewed sentence that women, enslaved people, freed people and foreigners could not vote in the owl city and that enslaved people built, rowed and served in both worlds, or defer. 8. **Religion and myths stay out.** Confirm that temples are shown only as buildings, that Hera's races are "the girls' races", and that Aesop, Homer and the gods belong to the literature row, not here.
9. **Numerals.** The Roman clock stops at XII (Year 3); numerals to C and M, the "no zero" lesson and the abacus belong to `number-structure` and the silk road's bead frame. Confirm, so no rung here teaches place value by the back door.
10. **Rota and the couch.** A two-seat Rota could be a small couch face-off (three pieces each, a bot, a course version); decide whether it is worth a registration or stays a family card. Lean: card now, couch later if a face-off slot wants a calm turn game.
