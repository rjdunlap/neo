The opened [IES/What Works Clearinghouse practice-guide page](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/WWC/PracticeGuide/15/Published) recommends developing informal sharing, treating fractions as numbers, explaining operations and using number lines centrally. Its evidence ratings differ: informal sharing is **minimal evidence**, number understanding and meaningful computation **moderate**. These recommendations do not validate our proposed game.

The opened [full guide](https://ies.ed.gov/ncee/wwc/docs/practiceguide/fractions_pg_093010.pdf), printed pp. 11–12 and Appendix D, reports that equal distribution develops in early childhood; dividing discrete objects is generally easier than dividing one continuous object. It also warns that successful sharing does not automatically establish fraction concepts. This supports a gradual bridge, not claiming preschool fraction mastery. The guide is from 2010; this session did not perform a systematic review of later interventions.

[NAEYC's practitioner account](https://www.naeyc.org/node/2439) supports mathematical noticing through meaningful play and adult conversation, not a specific fraction ladder. The Head Start math page failed to open; **no ELOF code is claimed as verified**. No opened evidence establishes independent fraction reasoning under three, a precise age for each proposed rung, or teen/parent demand. Standards are curriculum evidence; publisher descriptions are interaction evidence; neither establishes transfer from Puddle Island.

## First game's proposed ladder: Ribbon Workshop

**Core round:** a reference rail declares one whole. A pictured request shows a target length. Tap a broad piece card, then its broad join bay; a strip unrolls along the rail. Tap a placed segment's accessible row to return it, or Undo. Check compares the exact total with the request. After success the pieces become one ribbon around a code-drawn gift; no racing or requiring another round. First packet: one construction per round, one shell-owned sticker even with support. Later “another way” belongs in a separately authored level, never an unannounced condition.

This is construction of equivalent lengths; it is distinct from Inchworm Measure only if exchanges and fraction units remain central, and distinct from Pet Kitchen through joining/repartitioning lengths rather than serving friends. Recheck both before freezing identity.

IDs are proposals for a new game, not permission to change existing saved IDs. Suggested first launch is assessed levels 2–4 for `school`, with explicit `order`/`entryLevels` if an appended creative entry is later introduced. Decide actual band windows before implementation; do not accidentally launch younger players at symbolic tasks. No runtime band is added.

**Exact rules:** store numerator/positive denominator or integer ticks using the finite board's least common multiple; compare exact quantities, not pixel tolerances. A whole has a stable identity. Selecting a different reference frame changes the represented fraction, not the physical ribbon. Bounds cover tray count, maximum length and undo; enumeration accepts every stock-feasible solution, not just an authored answer. First packet needs no general constraint library.

**Misses/help:** edits, exchanges, previews, empty Check, replay and predictions are free. An explicit nonempty Check against a request counts one miss when unequal. After two such misses, one persistent useful-action glow counts one hint for that request; do not recharge the same help. Requested assistance counts according to the frozen packet. Solver help must remove an obstructing piece if no addition can finish. Supported completion uses ordinary `ctx.finish`; free creative play cannot provide evidence of independent mastery. Resolve neutral creative adaptation before registering level 1.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is tapping one broad piece card; it immediately brightens and curls at its end. The joining bay pulses; placing it visibly unrolls ribbon with a gentle pentatonic sound. Color is decorative, while length, partition marks and optional spoken labels communicate the mathematics. No new image/audio asset is needed: Pixi Graphics, palette colors, existing pet and synthesized sound suffice.

Keep each touch control at least 100 logical units after scaling. A short sixth-length drawing is not its own tiny button: use a full-width tray row and broad selectable segment rows linked to the rail. Avoid six adjacent narrow targets. Show one long rail or two stacked rails, with the pet and corner controls clear in portrait and landscape. Diagram scale may change on resize; logical quantities and the declared whole cannot. Use ordinary taps; optional drag later uses shared cancellation/cleanup rules. A ghost finger must perform the actual selection, exchange, undo and Check actions.

Seed arrangement, colors and valid piece inventories; variety must preserve target feasibility. Learning difficulty grows through exchange, unit choice and constraints, not wobbling targets or fine slicing. Replay uses the same problem on request, and a new round is optional. Co-play roles alternate requester and builder without hiding necessary information. A child, tween and parent still need to judge whether the ribbon action feels worth doing; iPad targets, speech and comprehension are untested.
