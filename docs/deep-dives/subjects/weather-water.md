**Scientific presentation checks.** The opened [USGS interactive water-cycle diagram](https://water.usgs.gov/edu/watercycle-kids-adv.html) and [condensation explanation](https://www.usgs.gov/water-science-school/science/condensation-and-water-cycle/) distinguish water's locations and processes. Vapor is invisible; visible clouds contain tiny droplets and/or ice. A drawn trail identifying a modeled water parcel is a viewing aid, not a visible stream of vapor. Water can remain in storage, take different routes, or revisit a location. Do not turn evaporation into boiling or imply every drop promptly returns to its starting pond. The [NOAA weather/climate explanation](https://www.noaa.gov/explainers/what-s-difference-between-climate-and-weather) distinguishes short-term conditions from long-term patterns, commonly characterized over 30 years. Neither a snowy day nor seven generated skies establishes climate.

**Runoff anchor and model limit:** the opened [EPA green-infrastructure guide](https://www.epa.gov/green-infrastructure/types-green-infrastructure) explains temporary collection and infiltration in rain gardens; [NOAA's watershed explanation](https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/watershed.html) connects land surfaces to downstream flow. These support the broad comparison, not Rain Garden's invented capacities or instantaneous steps. USGS runoff pages and its urban-flood fact sheet returned errors; their search excerpts were not used to calibrate anything. Retention, infiltration and later drainage are different processes; the first toy combines them into a visible storage counter and must say so. A more realistic model needs a separately sourced definition and science content review. No drainage-design, drinking-water or severe-weather advice is proposed.

## What people can do, and what is still an inference

The opened [CDC one-year guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/1-year.html) includes shared games and putting an object in a container; the [two-year guidance](https://www.cdc.gov/act-early/milestones/2-years.html) includes trying toy controls and combining toys. These observations suggest simple, immediate interactions to investigate; they do **not** establish touchscreen skill, screen benefit or water-cycle understanding. No developmental scoring or milestone claim belongs in the game.

[NAEYC's preschool STEAM guidance](https://www.naeyc.org/resources/pubs/tyc/feb2020/breaking-down-steam%20) describes weather charts and summaries, observation, discussion and playful material investigations. It supports starting with visible changes, not vocabulary recall. The school and middle-school rungs below are curriculum-informed design hypotheses; standards say what to teach, not what an individual child already understands. This session found no direct study validating these proposed games for tweens or parents. The [tracker's older-range caveats](../subjects.md#what-the-search-found-about-the-older-range-2026-10-14) remain in force.

## What fits

Keep the first model finite, inspectable and replayable. A few discrete water counters can animate a trial; this does not need a fluid engine, continuous weather simulation, network weather feed or a new save format. Every outlet must remain visible and every experiment reversible. The result should explain a cause before asking for an answer.

Do not combine all the subject into a large weather dashboard. Rain Garden investigates a bounded surface model; Drop's Journey investigates pathways/state; Forecast Detective investigates evidence. Full climate modeling, flood response, climate anxiety content and global water equity need separate subject/content definitions. Seasonal changes should acknowledge location and hemisphere; the reason for seasons belongs with the space study, not a misleading “closer to the Sun” animation.

## A first game's ladder: Rain Garden

Working ID `rain-garden`, **not registered**. The first definition must compare its actual decision with Water Paths/Puddle Pipes/Sand Table again. Its distinctive action is **change a surface, keep a trial comparable, inspect distribution**. Ground tiles and outcomes remain visible; no hidden-card memory puzzle.

### Smallest complete round and model

For the initial toddler round, a cloud releases four pictured **model water units** onto one large garden patch. Up to two remain in the patch; the rest travel along a visible downhill arrow into a pond tray. The units animate one at a time and settle. The child can replay the same shower from its starting state or deliberately finish after the first trial. A leaf badge marks completion of the experiment, not a correct answer. Nothing withers or floods, and leaving never creates a duty to return.

For comparisons, use exactly the same input and route from a fresh starting state, changing only the indicated material. Stone's toy capacity is 0 and garden soil's is 2 units per tile. Show occupied storage slots under the soil and remaining units in every outlet. Capacity is an invented teaching parameter, not liters, rainfall depth, a claim that all garden soil absorbs equally, or a plant-growth rate. A later already-wet trial preserves occupied slots **only within that experiment** and labels the changed starting condition.

For deeper boards, use at most six large sockets in two rows of three, with explicit fixed arrows descending between height terraces; all water routes form a directed acyclic graph. Each tile retains up to its remaining capacity and passes the rest to its shown downstream neighbor. Endpoints are accounted-for trays. No splitting, puddle pressure, evaporation or changing gravity in this kernel. A leaf marker identifies a bed receiving stored water; it is an outcome indicator, not instant botanical growth. Present sequence as a run, not real-time simulation of a storm.

At every step, **input + initial stored water = final stored water + all outlet water**. The trial never destroys overflow or quietly discards a blocked drop. Fixture generation and exhaustive small-state search must establish solvability before boards are used; having a proposed invariant here is not a passing test.

**Concrete level-4 check:** stone above garden lets the lower bed retain 2 of 4 units and sends 2 to the pond. Garden above stone sends 2 past the dry lower bed; the total still balances, but the bed's visible condition fails. The explanation points to *where the water stayed*, rather than “try the other one.” This distinguishes purposeful revision from memorizing the right tile.

**Accounting proposal:** levels 1–3 and 6 are exploration; `misses: 0`, experiments and replays unscored. At 4–5 and 7, a separate failed goal Check counts one miss; editing/running is not a wrong answer. Two failed checks glow the causal location; count one hint when that automatic guidance is shown, and one per requested worked step. At 8 a hypothesis unsupported by the visible evidence counts a miss only on a deliberate Check; a prediction before testing never does. Requested worked steps and assisted completion count as help. Passive replay and reading the rules do not. Assistance always leaves a complete finish available with the ordinary one sticker.

**Adaptation limit:** creative/comparison rounds with zero misses can step up under the current shell rule; that signals supported participation, not mastery. Preserve current adaptation and pins, describe this plainly in the grown-up card, and do not add a global adaptation redesign. If observation shows involuntary progression makes the toy less usable, define a separate correction before widening the audience.

**Proposed band windows:** toddler 1; preschool 1–2; pre-K 2–3; school 3–8; grown-up 7–8; no lap. U and M are planning audiences, not new bands. Older cases must be available through the ordinary how-to level selector/pins without forcing replay of the toddler rounds. These are proposed mappings for this new ID, not authority to renumber any saved ladder.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is tapping a cloud that visibly overhangs the patch. Touch-down squashes it immediately; four drops descend with quiet synthesized plinks and the patch's storage marks fill. Use Pixi Graphics and the shared palette: cream soil cutaway, dark outlines, differently textured ground and blue water; distinguish stored/traveling/pond water by position and symbols as well as color. No imported sprites, rain recordings, lightning flashes or startling thunder. Spoken labels and pet replay explain the result with sound off still playable through pictured traces.

Controls stay at least 100 logical units in child bands. Use tap-select/tap-place before precision dragging; any later object drag uses the shared helper. Six sockets and a comparison cannot all be squeezed onto one landscape row: the definition needs a portrait/landscape sketch with controls and pet clearance. Show one active trial and one compact pictured bookmark rather than two tiny interactive boards. A grown-up-only case can use the 64-unit minimum, but shared school layouts retain child sizes.

Replay variety comes from authored surface/route combinations and seeded solvable goals, not arbitrary tile clutter. Repeat preserves a board; New round belongs to the shell after Finish. Initial work does not store gardens or journal discoveries; later persistence needs a separate bounded model and migration slice. Every demonstration must act through the same touch controls and finish with both the first and top implemented level.

Co-play is observer/changer, then swap. The pet provides solo narration and a worked example; an adult is never required to supply a hidden fact. An off-screen connection can be a **window observation conversation** about what is visible and what is guessed, or an original paper water-parcel story with several possible stops. Do not issue real water-play, storm-watching, boiling, drainage or drinking instructions. A physical experiment card would require its own recognized-source and reviewer work before publication.

6. **Build level 6's two-shower experiment.** A fresh/wet start, carried storage, two-shower replay and explanation. New-interaction tier, with conservation/state reset checks and one portrait review.
