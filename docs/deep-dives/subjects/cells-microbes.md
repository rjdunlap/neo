# Cells and microbes: looking closer, cells and microscopic life

**Subject:** `cells-microbes` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M and G as [defined in the tracker](../subjects.md#the-audiences) · **Date:** 2026-10-17 · **Session:** `claude/subject-cells-microbes`, [PR #520](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/520). Scoped by the [further gap review](further-gaps.md), which asked for microscopy, cellular evidence and single-celled life without reducing microbes to enemies.

**Research and proposals only.** Recommend one new identity, **Puddle Lens**: turn a big lens dial over one fixed sample (a drop of puddle water, a leaf, an onion skin) and decide **how close you have to look to answer a question**. Its first rungs are a shared "look closer" toy. Its school rungs ask what the view can and cannot yet show: is anyone there, alive or can't tell yet, one cell or many. Everything else extends what is already proposed. Microscopic stops go to **Zoom Walk**. A lens look at fungi and tiny decomposers goes to **Leaf Litter Window** (Garden Grow). A drop-of-water scene goes to **Photo Safari** as content. Yeast and fermented food go to the cooking and food/fiber studies. Molecules stay in the chemistry study, which is in flight in PR #516. No game, band, zone, land registration or save format is added.

## What people can do, and what remains uncertain

- **Germs before cells.** Siegal, Fadda and Overton's review ([2011, *Phil. Trans. R. Soc. B*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3189351/)) reports these findings, cited there rather than opened here. Preschoolers propose invisible mechanisms such as germs to explain contamination (Legare et al.). Many 3- to 5-year-olds do not understand that illness takes time to develop (Kalish 1997). Children of 4 to 7 often treat germs as not alive: they may grow, but do not eat or reproduce (Solomon and Cassimatis 1999). The review calls the early physical theory, in which germs act like poisons, a **placeholder** for later biological understanding. Children's microbe ideas are thus dominated by contamination and illness. The review notes that "some bacteria are intrinsically 'good'" is mostly an advertising message, not something studied in children.
- **Seven to fourteen.** Byrne's study of children's mental models of micro-organisms ([*Int. J. Sci. Educ.* 33(14), 2011](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/73092); repository returned 403, so **known from search summaries only**) studied children aged 7, 11 and 14; a 2010 *J. Biol. Educ.* paper reportedly using the same sample describes 414 children in southern England. No finding from it is used here beyond its age range.
- **A lens makes it look bigger, not be bigger.** Ashbrook's twos-classroom note ([NSTA, 2010](https://www.nsta.org/blog/when-young-children-use-magnifiers)) records children "always intrigued" by magnifiers. They agreed a finger "just looked bigger" and touched it to check. That is a teacher's anecdote, not a study. It supports a toddler or preschool rung about appearance, the same convention as Far Lights' "looks brighter, not bigger" ([astronomy](astronomy.md#a-first-games-ladder-far-lights)).
- **Close, closer, closest.** *Science and Children* ([NSTA, February 2008](https://www.nsta.org/resources/close-closer-closest)) has young learners look at one object at three magnifications (hand lens, then a lit pocket microscope). Puddle Lens's stops copy that structure. The page gives no grade or outcome data.
- **Scale is hard at every age.** Tretter, Jones et al. (2006, *JRST*) and Jones and Taylor (2009, *JRST*) study students' and experts' sense of scale. They were **known from search summaries only**, with no findings opened. So the study claims nothing about when children can order microscopic sizes, and Puddle Lens avoids numeric scale before upper school.
- **Microbiology literacy.** Timmis, Timmis and Jebok ([2020, *Microbial Biotechnology*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7415351/)) argue that "children should learn the microbiology language of nature". They propose child-relevant topics: food, soil, skin and teeth, ponds and rock pools, microbes as part of the biosphere's life-support system, and microscopy of everyday microbes. This is an editorial argument, not evidence of learning. It supports a **roles-beyond-disease** framing.
- **Gaps.** No study opened here establishes what toddlers can learn about microscopic life, or when children reliably separate "moves" from "alive". No study covers tween or parent appeal for a microscopy game. These are gaps, not findings.

## What fits

- **Fits:** turning a large discrete dial (no pinch), and tapping big features once magnified. Every tappable feature is drawn at least 100 units at the stop that shows it. "Can't tell yet" is a real answer. Swimmers move gently and on their own, never as targets to chase.
- **Fits the rework:** the lens is a **tool** in the creature home, of the kind the [rework brief](../../rework/README.md#learning-belongs-in-the-story) puts in a home base. New **slides** for the tray are permanent unlocks earned by finishing a round or a project step, with supported completion earning the same slide. The journal files only what a round actually showed (the existing journal contract). A creature project could ask "Why is the puddle green?" and end with the creature wading happily once the child has looked. No currency, timer or care need. If a microbe ever becomes a collectible fictional creature, it is **labelled fiction**, separate from the faceless science drawings in the lens.
- **Does not fit:** germ-killing, cleaning or "beat the bacteria" play; any hygiene, handwashing, illness or diagnosis content; culturing, swabbing, collecting pond water or sampling anything from a body; live organisms; viruses or atoms in the same view as cells for young players; and faces on microbes in the science view (a decision below).

**Identity test.** Puddle Lens earns a separate game only if its central decision is **how much magnification a question needs**, on one sample that does not change. If a definition finds the rounds are only "slide and look", they become Zoom Walk content instead (decision 1).

## A first game's ladder: Puddle Lens

### The model, stated once

- **Stops, not numbers.** Four ordered powers: *eyes* (1), *hand lens* (2), *microscope* (3), *closer microscope* (4). Each sample has a feature table: the features visible at each stop. A feature appears at its first stop and stays until it is too large to fit (the "whole leaf" outline leaves at stop 3).
- **One fixed sample per question.** The slide does not change while the dial turns; only the view does.
- **"Looks bigger, is not bigger."** A reference (the pet's paw, a ruler bar) stays its real size beside the lens at levels 2–4.
- **Swimmers move by themselves.** Motion is gentle drift and turning; never a moving target, never steered.

### Proposed levels and bands

| Level | Band | Round | What she decides | Mistakes and hints |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 Look closer | lap | Tap the lens three times: leaf, drop, sand, each swelling with a chime | Nothing to get wrong; supported toy | None; three taps finish. Exploration accounting |
| 2 Lens over | toddler | Drag the big lens over three things; each looks bigger, the paw stays | Which to look at next | None; looking at all three finishes. Exploration accounting |
| 3 Anyone there? | preschool | A drop; turn the dial up until swimmers appear, tap one (or say "no swimmers" on an empty drop) | When to stop turning | Tapping the empty drop at stop 1 says "I can't see anyone yet"; not a miss. A wrong "no swimmers" is a miss with "look closer". Glow on the dial after two |
| 4 Just enough | pre-K | The pet asks for a view; set the dial, press Show | The stop that shows it: not too far, not too close | A wrong Show is a miss naming why (too small, too close). Turning is free. Two misses glow the accepted stops |
| 5 Alive or can't tell yet | school | Two moving specks; answer alive / not alive / can't tell yet, at any stop | Whether this stop is enough evidence | An answer before the deciding stop is a free prompt ("Could be. What would show it?"); only the final committed answer at a deciding stop can miss. Movement alone never counts as the evidence. Hint lights the stop that shows a boundary |
| 6 One cell or many | school | Sort four samples into one cell / many cells / not cells | Which stop shows the boundaries, then the sort | Each wrong drop is a miss naming what the view shows; a clump of single cells counts as one cell each |
| 7 Wall clue | upper school | Plant or animal cell, or can't tell at this power | Using the wall, not colour | Green-only reasoning gets "an onion cell has no green parts; look at the edge" |
| 8 Why green? | upper school / 12 | Two puddle drops; choose the view that separates "tiny green living things" from "dissolved colour" | The deciding view | A non-deciding view is free; only the final answer can miss |

Proposed windows: lap 1; toddler 1–2; preschool 2–3; pre-K 3–4; school 4–6, with 7–8 at school-top for 9–12. Grown-up Slide Files levels follow later. The exact `BandLevels` table is a definition task.

### A rule test

For every sample, the feature table is total over stops 1–4. For every question, the accepted stop set is non-empty, and the asked feature is invisible below its minimum stop. For level 5, "can't tell yet" is accepted exactly when the two candidates share every visible feature at the current stop. The level's top stop always separates them. For level 6, each sample's class is visible at some allowed stop. Every hint names a stop that adds at least one deciding feature. Seeded rounds pick sample pairs only from enumerated, reviewed combinations.

### Cost

M: one game folder, a dial control, code-drawn sample layers (leaf, drop, onion skin, sand, a ciliate, a green thread, a yeast-like clump), how-to card and ghost finger (turn dial, press Show). No new engine piece, save field or band.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** turning the dial and seeing something appear that was not there.
- **Touch-down feedback:** the dial clicks and the lens ring brightens on touch; each stop has a soft pentatonic step.
- **Replay variety:** seeded samples and positions, two or three swimmer shapes, and a drop that is sometimes empty.
- **Off-screen:** the reviewed Look Closer card. Nothing practical ships before review.
- **Presentation decision:** microbes in the lens are faceless, labelled science drawings. The companion creature reacts with delight beside them. Fiction stays with the creature, not the cell.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Identity:** confirm Puddle Lens as a new game (one sample, how close is enough) rather than Zoom Walk content. Lean: new game, if the level-4 "just enough" round survives definition; otherwise fold into Zoom Walk.
2. **Placement:** Bubble Beach lists 4 games and suits a puddle; the layout test decides, with no smaller targets. Lean: Bubble Beach, or the rework's Explore places when they exist.
3. **Review:** the existing [science and scale facts reviewer](../../WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER.md) row covers stop order and drawings; levels 5–8 and Slide Files need a biology/microbiology educator before claims ship. No reviewer is named.
4. **Accounting:** levels 1–2 are exploratory (completion earns the sticker but does not move the level as if mastered), as Space and Astronomy propose. At level 5 an early, possibly true claim is a free prompt, not a miss; confirm.
5. **Older placement:** levels 7–8 at the top of `school` for 9–12; Slide Files on `grownup` for 13+. No middle band.
6. **Microbe framing:** confirm faceless science drawings in the lens. Confirm that no germ, hygiene, illness or "bad microbe" content enters the island through this subject. Any later disease topic needs its own reviewed scope.
7. **Practical card:** any hand-lens, Foldscope or microscope page card needs a named reviewer. No culturing, swabbing, body samples, pond collection or sunlight-through-lens instructions are drafted.
