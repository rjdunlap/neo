## Gear Garden: first game's proposed ladder

**Fun action:** build a motion path and make an island flower spin. **Learning goal:** discover that contact transmits rotation, a mesh reverses direction, and gear ratios relate turns. First release should cover **1–3**, not the entire table. IDs and band windows are design proposals until definition/registry review: L/T 1; P 1–2; K 2–4; S 3–7; G 6–8. Higher modes add relationships, not speed. Preserve any subsequently claimed IDs and save mappings.

| Level | First round and new decision | Completion / proposed rule proof |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1: Turn a flower** | Two equal gears already touch. Tap anywhere on the broad machine face for a quarter input turn; the flower follows. Turn-back control is optional for T | Finish is available from the start; all exploration is valid. Empty/no-touch finish conveys no skill evidence. Prove one input increment causes equal opposite rotation and repeating/resetting is deterministic |
| **2: Bridge the gap** | Three equal-gear sockets in a line; input and output fixed, middle empty, one loose gear. Put it in and Turn | One connected output run completes; Show me installs the missing gear. Test disconnected output stays still and either direction works |
| **3: Two machines** | Choose one of two pictured machine panels; only the chosen board opens full size. One is a line of three equal gears, the other four; each has one gap and one loose gear | Either completed machine counts. Three gears produce the input direction at output; four reverse it. Enumerate contacts and fit each board separately; the selection cards and loose gear stay at least 100 units |
| **4: Turn this way** | Same machine choices, now a flower carries an arrow request; choose the machine and input direction | Accept every connected arrangement/direction satisfying the arrow. Trace the first discontinuity or show the actual output arrow; experimental tests remain free |
| **5: Big and small** | Choose one of two authored pairs, 12 driving 24 teeth or 24 driving 12; watch the output mark for one input turn | Compare slow/fast without a time challenge. Prove output turns are -1/2 and -2 respectively; compatible radii/center distances, equal input and a full readable replay are required |
| **6: Two flowers** | One input, two acyclic branches; request same or opposite output directions | At least two accepted assemblies per seed; enumerate the finite board. Help preserves work only when a satisfying completion still exists |
| **7: A turn recipe** | Two output requests, one full turn and half a turn per input turn; choose from authored compatible gear trains | Solve signed rational ratios exactly, not pixel angles. Show one-input-turn replay and both output traces; never imply an idler alone changes a simple train's overall ratio |
| **8: Your moving garden** | Choose two flower movements and build a working scene from a finite kit | Creative Finish always available, including unfinished experiments. Optional repair/compactness cards are separate opt-in goals; no minimum-part medal or correctness score on a creation |

**Rule model:** a mesh edge between external gears i and j imposes `turns[j] = -turns[i] * teeth[i] / teeth[j]`. This proposed rule is derived from equal tooth travel at a no-slip contact: one driving revolution passes its tooth count, so the driven gear makes that count divided by its own teeth in the opposite direction. It is an ideal kinematic model, not measured friction, force or load capacity. Teeth counts, turn fractions and expected directions stay exact; animation samples the result. A first board is an acyclic single-layer graph, with no belts, compound shafts, gear slip or torque simulation. Disconnected components do not move. Detect every physical contact, not just intended solution edges; reject authored boards with overlap or unintended locked loops. Later variable-size sockets must encode radius/spacing and phase-compatible tooth rendering, not merely draw circles that overlap.

The pictured machine choices are design seeds requiring geometry enumeration, not verified generated puzzles. Seeded variants change endpoint art, orientation and which gap is missing while preserving the checked rule graph. Reflection must transform direction cues consistently. Enumeration must accept all legal solutions, establish at least one completion from every offered starting task and generate help from the current state, including undoing an incompatible choice. A generic “add a gear” hint is insufficient if it creates a conflicting contact.

**Mistakes/support:** placing, turning and revising are experiments: zero misses. Unsupported drops return gently with a socket cue. A deliberate optional prediction is unscored. Offer Show me throughout; after two unchanged-goal trials, offer a glow/replay of the motion path rather than blame. Count requested or answer-revealing assistance in `hints`, including automatic placement; never count a replay as a wrong answer. Use the existing assisted finish/reward flow and one sticker per completed round. Adaptation remains the shell's band-clamped policy; a smooth toy finish is not evidence of independent understanding.

## Experience and presentation

The first action must be visible without reading: a large assembled handle/flower and immediate pressed state. A tap increment is the complete accessible input; optional circular cranking comes later, preserving palm rejection, cancellation, leaving the screen and cleanup. Use `onTap` and the shared drag helper for loose gears. Ghost-finger play must demonstrate the actual gesture at every implemented level.

Fit controls and loose pieces at 100 logical units after scaling; reserve the lower-left pet and top navigation. Small fixed gear teeth and pegs are decorations, not hidden precision targets. A board that cannot fit gets fewer pieces or a new authored layout; grown-up-only boards may use their 64-unit/zoom contract. Sound-off arrows and marked teeth carry direction/turn information; narration and SFX supplement them. All art is palette-based Pixi Graphics, all sound synthesized. No imported machine pictures/audio.

Reuse one scene with seeded flower/visitor variety and meaningful layout alternatives. Include a broad Turn, replay/reset, visible loose-piece tray and supported Finish. No lives, timed cranking, service queue, wear, energy currency or away-state care. No saving or journal discoveries in the first slice. Do not implement shared graph infrastructure until the activity actually needs it.

**Off-screen companion:** a future plain-HTML conversation card can ask a family to notice what turns when an adult chooses a familiar mechanism to show. Do not reproduce the automata construction guide as child-facing instructions. Material choice, pinch/entanglement risks, small parts and real tool use need a named reviewer for any practical card; a screen toy is not tool-safety instruction.
