## What people can do, and what remains uncertain

- **L/T:** [NAEYC's infant/toddler science guidance](https://www.naeyc.org/node/982) emphasizes making things happen, observing, comparing and sharing discoveries. It supports a large repeatable control and noticing a shape. It does not establish that a toddler understands a lunar cycle or that a screen model transfers to the sky.
- **P/K:** familiar matching, relative comparisons and narrated records are the proposed entry. No preschool-specific causal Moon study was opened. Treat these as design hypotheses, with individual variation; do not infer orbital understanding from matching a crescent.
- **S:** [Plummer and Small's university-hosted research abstract](https://pure.psu.edu/en/publications/using-a-planetarium-fieldtrip-to-engage-young-children-in-three-d/) reports improved lunar-pattern/representation learning in 46 children aged 6–7 after a planetarium-and-classroom intervention. This is an abstract, not a full-method review or a trial of independent tablet play. Borrow linked representations and evidence discussion; do not claim the app replicates the result.
- **U/M:** [Chastenay and Riopel's primary research abstract](https://journals.aps.org/prper/abstract/10.1103/PhysRevPhysEducRes.16.020107) describes a lunar-concept assessment tested with 296 students, approximately ages 10–14. It distinguishes facets of understanding rather than making recognition of phase names sufficient. This is assessment research, not evidence that tweens enjoy an educational game. Use viewpoint changes and evidence constraints as proposed challenge; test appeal separately.
- **G:** adjustable models and deduction games offer plausible parent play. None of the opened sources establishes that parents want these exact activities. Give a complete puzzle, a useful shared role and an optional page card, rather than only parent supervision.

**Age claims stay modest:** standards say what a curriculum invites, not what every child can do. No educational-game engagement study for 11–14 was established here. A twelve-year-old's proposed school-top puzzle and a thirteen-year-old's grown-up version use today's bands; no middle band is silently introduced.

## What fits

Observation, controlled replay, manipulating one cause, translating between views and explaining ambiguous evidence fit the island. New fun should come from opening a view, making a Moon appearance, shaping a probe's path or solving an observing mystery. Planet lists, rapid naming, attendance calendars and diagrams that can be solved by vocabulary alone do not earn a new game.

Keep the first models offline, deterministic, code-drawn and small. They need neither a generic 3D engine nor imported star maps, real dates, geolocation, camera access, telescopes or waiting for night. Sound is synthesized and speech replayable. Do not add constellation lore from living cultures without appropriate sources/review; invented patterns must be labeled invented.

## A first game's ladder: Moon Window

### Smallest round and explicit model

**First slice:** one broad framed Moon view, a large Later control, three authored observations, replayable narration and one sticker through the shell after three presses. Pressing causes a soft immediate frame dip/light response, then the image changes. No next-night wording: the observations are separated snapshots, not three consecutive nights, and the app never reads the device date. A replay ribbon stays on screen; nothing tests memory.

**Causal model, introduced separately at level 5:** Earth at the origin, distant sunlight from the right, Moon center on a circular path. The phase plate is a fixed north-up reference diagram, not a locally accurate horizon view. Eight phase stops are samples of a smooth cycle, roughly three-to-four days apart, not eight equally long named phases or eight nights. A step advances the sample; the Sun stays fixed in this simplified relative cycle. Sizes/distances and motion timing are schematic. Explain the compressed model in the how-to and pet's replay.

For inspectable rules let the Moon angle `theta` be its counterclockwise angle from the Sun direction. The visible lit fraction is `(1 - cos(theta)) / 2`: Sunward position gives new, a quarter-turn gives half-lit, opposite gives full. This is the distant-light, sphere/observer geometry used by the toy, not a high-precision ephemeris. Draw a sphere's projected illuminated region, not a straight pie wedge. At quarter stops, half the visible disk is lit; the whole sphere still has an illuminated side.

A fixed model-up marker preserves waxing/waning orientation across the diagram and phase cards; it does not claim that everyone sees the Moon with the same tilt. No Earth-shadow wedge is drawn. Eclipses require additional geometry and are out of scope. At new Moon the ordinary phase view is empty; only an explicitly toggled model overlay may outline the unseen sphere. The framed view supplies no claim of a particular time or visibility above a local horizon; a future literal sky scene needs separate rise/set/observer rules. This avoids making every phase appear high in the same night sky.

For example, a half-lit-area clue alone permits the two quarter positions. Reveal whether the preceding observation was more or less lit; the cycle direction determines which remains. Do not silently require the player to interpret a hemisphere-specific crescent orientation.

**Accounting and finish:** each level has a finite authored round (three reveals at 1, two matches/comparisons at 2–3, one completed record/model case at 4–7). Instructions, naming, evidence replay and uncommitted edits are free. After two failed checks on the same task, show/count one actionable hint; subsequent failed checks remain misses without repeatedly charging the same glow. Offer the supported move and completion; if explicit solution help is used, count a hint once for that help. Help never prevents the shell's sticker. Exploration and initial predictions alone are not evidence of mastery or struggle: any adaptation policy for those modes must be specified before registration, rather than treating 0/0 as proof of conceptual learning.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** one framed view and one large pictured control. Touch-down changes the frame immediately, before speech finishes. The pet notices the view; it never needs a child to read “waxing.” At older levels the target appearance and model-up marker are always present.
- **Layout:** use palette/Pixi geometry, a quiet drawn sky without misleading clock cues, and pentatonic synthesized feedback. Keep the bottom-left pet clear; at least 100 logical units per child control in both orientations. Put the orbit model and phase plate beside one another in landscape and stacked in portrait; use three large selectable stops at once before an eight-stop control. If eight distinct targets cannot fit, step/select with large buttons; do not shrink them.
- **Replay:** vary starting sample, question direction and legal arrangements with seeded RNG. Art variety is secondary to changed evidence; never shuffle the model's laws. On resize the evidence remains readable and selection survives. A touch ghost finger must operate the same public actions as the player, including Later, replay, confirmation and supported finish.
- **Speech/support:** `ctx.instruct` supplies the repeatable instruction and `ctx.say` explains observations. Touch cards preview names without making a selection; an explicit pictured check commits where needed. Any large pet repeats the instruction. No added journal/creation data is needed for a complete first round.
- **Co-play:** observer and model-maker take turns; all evidence stays available, and solo play works with the pet. No two simultaneous touches or hidden device is needed.
- **Off-screen bridge:** a proposed page conversation compares a remembered Moon shape with a replayed model; “not seen” is an acceptable observation. A later reviewer may adapt the NASA physical model for the family's ages. This document does not supply equipment handling, direct Sun viewing, eclipse viewing or solar-filter instructions. No nightly attendance, stored observation obligation or weather-dependent reward.
