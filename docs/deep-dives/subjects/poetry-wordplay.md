# Poetry and wordplay: a rhyme to finish, a riddle with two clues, a word with two meanings

*Subject `poetry-wordplay` · 2026-10-22 · `claude/research-poetry-wordplay` · [PR #843](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/843). Research and proposals only, for L/T/P/K/S/U/M and grown-ups. No game was played or implemented.*

**Recommendation:** rhyme, syllables and songs are built (Rhyme Time, Clap the Syllables, Sing-Along Barn). What is missing is the playful use of sound and meaning *together*:

- **A rhyme she finishes** (the pet sings a line and pauses before the rhyming word).
- **A riddle that needs two clues**, one by sound and one by meaning.
- **A word with two meanings**, shown as two pictures, for the age that starts to enjoy that (about 7).

**Define first a Rhyme Time mode, *Rhyme Riddles* (levels 5–7).** The pet gives two clues about one of three pictures: "it rhymes with *bear*" and "you sit on it". The rhyme clue leaves two pictures, the meaning clue picks one, so neither clue alone is enough. This reuses Rhyme Time's twelve reviewed rhyme families, its art and its voice, so it needs no new game, no new land and no new content review, and a rule test can prove that both clues are needed. Tongue twisters and jokes are real but smaller: the island has no microphone, so a twister can only be listened to, and a pun is a school-age pleasure that needs a reviewed word list first.

**Evidence limits.** Nothing opened shows that a tablet rhyme game improves a child's reading, or that tongue twisters or puns do. The island claims only that a child can hear, choose and produce a rhyme, and use two clues together, with spoken support. A completed round is not evidence of independent phonological awareness. Pronunciation differs by accent (cot and caught, and many word pairs across English varieties) and between languages, so rhyme tables are authored and rule-tested, never generated, and a family-language edition needs its own table (see the open family-languages decision).

## What people can do by age

- **Under 2:** rhyme is heard, not judged. A grown-up sings, stresses the rhyming word and pauses; the child fills the gap with a sound or a gesture (Nemours, practitioner guidance). Nothing to *choose*.
- **About 2:** finishes the last word of a well-known rhyme. Rhyme awareness "emerges" in the 24–30-month range on one secondary chart.
- **3–4:** recognises that two words rhyme with prompting, enjoys alliteration and tries to copy a twister (NC progressions).
- **5–6:** produces a rhyme and finds the odd one out; reads a poem as a kind of text (RL.K.5).
- **7–8:** begins to enjoy double-meaning jokes and riddles (McGhee, approximate). Idioms and "literal versus meant" come after, L.5.5.
- **9–12 and teens:** word ladders, homographs, idioms and writing their own verse; the evidence on what a 11–14-year-old chooses to play for wordplay is **absent in these sources** (a gap, not a finding). Grown-ups already do this for pleasure (crosswords, cryptic clues).

## What fits

- **Fits as modes:** the pause-and-finish line (Sing-Along Barn, lap and toddler); Rhyme Riddles and a "you say a rhyme" rung (Rhyme Time); *same first sound* (a sibling mode for Word Monsters or Rhyme Time, owner to decide).
- **Fits as a small new game, later:** *Two Meanings* (school, 7+), after a reviewed word list.
- **Does not fit:** *recording and scoring a tongue twister* (no microphone, and speech-sound development varies); jokes below about 7 (a child repeats the shape without the pleasure); reading a poem aloud to a score; rhymes by auto-generation.
- **Needs review:** traditional rhymes carry dated or contested content. Use only verses a person has reviewed, and leave out any rhyme whose meaning is violent or demeaning. Humour is subjective: no joke is ranked, a groan is welcome, and nobody is a loser.

## A first game's ladder: Rhyme Riddles (Rhyme Time 5–7)

Rhyme Time currently stands at pre-K 1–3 and school 2–4. These are new plans after level 4, kept at pre-K 5 and school 5–7. A definition may renumber them.

| Level | Round | What she decides | Notes |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 5 | The pet says "It rhymes with *bear*. You sit on it." Three pictures: *chair*, *pear*, *hat*. The rhyme clue keeps *chair* and *pear*; the meaning clue keeps *chair*. | Which picture fits **both** | Pictures are Rhyme Time's own; all spoken, replayable |
| 6 | Same, but the meaning clue comes first ("You can eat it. It rhymes with *bear*"), and a distractor fits the meaning but not the rhyme | Hold one clue while hearing the other | Memory is two phrases, not a list |
| 7 | **Her turn:** she taps a picture, then chooses one of two rhyme partners and one of two meaning chips; the pet guesses and is right or fun-wrong ("A pear? No, a *chair*!") | Build a riddle that works | Production (RF.K.2); the pet's wrong guess is a feature, not a miss |

**Accounting.** A miss is picking a picture that fails one clue; the spoken hint names the clue it fails ("A pear rhymes with bear, but you can't sit on it"). The first miss repeats both clues; two misses glow the answer. Level 7 has no misses. Asking for a replay is free and never a hint.

**Rule tests.** For every generated round: exactly one picture satisfies both clues; the rhyme clue alone leaves at least two; the meaning clue alone leaves at least two; every rhyme word is in the same reviewed family as its target; every picture is distinct. At level 7 the chosen pair always yields a unique guess. These are checked against the existing family table, so no new word enters without a test.

**Cost.** Small to medium: new plans and voice lines, a meaning tag per picture (about two short tags each, reviewed once), no new art, a ghost-finger bot for the new levels. Placement: stays in Rhyme Time's land.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** touch a picture. It lifts and says its word on touch-down; the clue is repeatable by a music-note button, as Rhyme Time does.
- **Replay variety:** the target, the distractors and which clue is first come from the seed.
- **The payoff:** the chosen picture does what the clue said (the chair is sat on by the pet).
- **Off-screen:** the three cards above. A grown-up in another language can supply their own rhyme pairs (a family-language table is a separate decision).

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Rhyme Riddles as Rhyme Time levels 5–7** (lean: yes), or a separate game.
2. **Meaning tags and the rhyme verses** need an ordinary content review; confirm who reviews, and confirm the rule that no verse whose meaning is violent or demeaning ships.
3. **Two Meanings (PW4):** a new game or a mode, and where it lives. Not before the word list is reviewed.
4. **Tongue twisters:** confirm that they stay listen-and-imitate and never graded.
5. **Alliteration (PW3) owner:** Word Monsters or Rhyme Time.
6. **Grown-up puns:** is Pun Match wanted at all, given humour is subjective?
7. **Family languages:** rhyme does not translate, so each language needs its own table. Order and reviewers remain open.
