# Making media: pictures, sound and moving pictures made for an audience

**Subject:** `making-media` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M and G as [defined in the tracker](../subjects.md#the-audiences) · **Date:** 2026-10-17 · **Session:** `claude/subject-making-media`, [PR #531](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/531). The tracker row names "a flipbook, stop-motion, a recorded song" and "a shared family project".

**Research and proposals only.** The island already lets her make things: paintings, stamp pictures, pixel designs and songs, each of which can be kept in the treehouse ([GAMES](../../GAMES.md#paint-pier-art)). What no game asks is the media-arts question: **what will someone watching this understand?** Recommend one new identity, **Movie Night** (working name): her creature friends sit in a little cinema, and she makes a short movie for them on a strip of three or four big shot slots. At first she drops sounds onto shots that are already filmed. Next she chooses which shots go in and in what order. Later she points a camera frame at a busy scene herself. Then the premiere plays, and the audience reacts to exactly what the movie showed them, and to nothing it left out. A friend who missed the clue asks the question the missing shot would have answered ("Where did the hat go?"). The rest extends what exists: the frame-by-frame flipbook stays the notebook's **Pixel Flipbook** seed (Pixel Pictures). The picture-book story stays **Story Weaver**. A "recorded song" is already Song Maker's kept tune. Stop-motion with real objects becomes a family card, because the island has no camera or microphone. No game, band, zone, land registration or save format is added.

## What people can do, and what remains uncertain

- **Older children and effects.** Breyer, *Children's comprehension of video effects: understanding meaning, mood & message* ([MIT S.M. thesis, 2002](https://dspace.mit.edu/handle/1721.1/61125); abstract opened) worked with 9- to 14-year-olds in video workshops: "Initially, children described features or instances of effects, but not their context or purpose." After the workshop they talked more about effects on the viewer, and used effects more in planned stories. A single workshop thesis, but it supports the U/M rung: **ask what the audience will feel or know, not what the effect is called**.
- **Young children and edited film.** Search summaries only, and pointing in different directions, so **no rung's age is set from them**: Smith, Anderson and Fischer (1985), "Young children's comprehension of montage", *Child Development* 56, 962–971 (citation only); Kirkorian and Anderson in *PNAS* ([doi 10.1073/pnas.1611606114](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1611606114); publisher page 403), reported to compare four-year-olds and adults watching montage in normal and shuffled order; a Portuguese preschool study (Canelhas and Vicente; repository page blocked by a bot wall) reported to find edited videos no worse than unedited ones when key information stayed centered; and German-language work by Pittorf ([Merz author page](https://www.merz-zeitschrift.de/autorinnen/details/pittorf/), search summary) reported that pans challenge four-year-olds and that long-shot-to-close-up cuts are not reliably understood even by fourth graders. **Implication, as a hypothesis to observe:** keep the key thing centered and large in young rungs; leave framing, cuts and "what the viewer cannot see" to school and above.
- **Animation.** Reused from the [notebook's evidence](../../IDEAS.md): a UK school found children must move objects only a little between frames, and teaches flipbooks before stop motion; onion skinning (a faint previous frame) is the common aid. [Stop Motion Studio](https://www.madisonpubliclibrary.org/kids/apps/stop-motion-studio) (library listing, opened) is listed for grades K–5 with an adjustable onion skin.
- **Gaps.** Nothing opened establishes when a child can reason about what *another viewer* knows from a film (as opposed to a live scene), tween or parent appeal for movie-making puzzles, or whether making media transfers to reading film. These are hypotheses for observation.

## What fits

- **Fits:** a strip of big slots (three or four, at least 100 units each in both orientations); sounds and shots as draggable cards (`src/engine/drag.ts`); a premiere that plays the strip with the audience reacting; a free Watch at any time; an audience model that is a finite set of facts, so rule tests can enumerate accepted strips. Creatures are cast and audience, which suits the [rework](../../rework/README.md): a welcome movie for a new friend fits the "How can we welcome a new friend?" project, and new sets and sound pads can unlock permanently through play, with no currency.
- **Never judged:** mood, music choice, style, "a good movie". Any sound in a free slot and any music is creative. Only facts the audience must know (and, for a surprise, the order in which they learn them) are checked.
- **Does not fit:** recording her voice or filming with a camera (no camera or microphone), sharing or publishing outside the device, a timeline scrubbed in real time, frame-rate or lip-sync timing, speed, or a viewer rating. A "views" count or applause meter that varies with quality would turn taste into a score.
- **Needs new infrastructure:** the strip and audience-fact model inside the game's `logic.ts`; a viewfinder (a large draggable frame with two sizes) for framing levels. No save change until kept movies are decided.

## A first game's ladder: Movie Night

### The model, stated once

- **Shots.** A shot is a pre-drawn little scene on one set (a pond, a garden, the creature home), played for about two seconds with a small loop of motion. Each shot carries a finite set of **facts** the audience learns from it: `who` (Pip), `where` (the garden), `thing` (the hat), `event` (lands, opens, finds), `detail` (the key on the hook), `feeling` (Pip smiles). Facts are authored per shot, never computed from art.
- **Sounds.** A sound pad is a synthesized effect from `src/audio/sfx.ts` (splash, boing, giggle, whoosh, clunk, bell and animal voices exist today; a knock or a snore would be new synthesized effects). An **event sound** matches one fact (`splash` ↔ `event: lands-in-water`); a free sound matches nothing and is always welcome.
- **The brief.** A pictured request from an audience member (no reading): a set of facts the audience must know by the end, and optionally an ordering constraint (`thing` revealed only after `event: opens`, for a surprise), or a fact that must **not** be known before the last shot.
- **The premiere.** Plays the strip in order and updates the audience's known facts shot by shot. Each audience member reacts: a happy cheer if their brief is met; otherwise they ask the one question for the **first** unmet requirement ("Where was the hat?", "That splash came before the jump!", "We already knew it was a kite!"). The reaction is computed from the same rule the tests use.
- **Framing (levels 6+).** The viewfinder is a frame placed over a wide scene in two sizes. A shot's facts are those of the objects whose centers lie inside the frame; a `detail` fact counts only in the close size. Positions snap to a coarse grid, so accepted frames are enumerable.

Level 1 is the creative way in; levels 2–3 keep one mechanic (sound on a shot). Levels 4–5 replace sound with shot choice on the same strip; 6–9 add the viewfinder. Each group is its own definition packet, so a build never carries two unproven interactions.

### Mistakes and hints

- **Watch is always free** (a preview of the current strip, no audience judgement). A **premiere** is the check.
- **Accounting (decision, lean):** the Story Steps 11 precedent: the first unmet premiere is free exploration; each repeated unmet premiere counts a miss; the second unmet premiere glows the slot (or shot card) that the asked question points at and counts one hint for the round. The alternative is the Welcome Ways precedent: a premiere is an observation; the same unmet requirement twice glows and counts a hint, and misses come only from hints. Record one before building.
- Level 1 and every free sound or music choice never miss. "Show me" from the lightbulb plays a fitting strip and counts one hint.
- The asked question always names **the first** unmet requirement, so a hint leads somewhere useful (rule-tested).

### A rule test

For every authored round: at least one accepted strip exists; every accepted strip satisfies every requirement in the brief; the number of accepted strips matches an enumerated list (so an unexpected alternative fails the test until it is reviewed); every non-accepted strip produces a question; filling the slot that question names, with some shot or sound, moves toward an accepted strip; in framing levels, every accepted frame lies on the grid and gives each `detail` fact only in the close size; the Story Steps overlap test, that no level's accepted set is exactly "the shots in time order".

### Cost

Levels 1–3: one session to define, one to build (strip, sound pads, a premiere with three audience critters, how-to card, ghost finger dragging a pad). Levels 4–5: one definition, one build (the fact model and briefs). Levels 6–9: a definition and a build for the viewfinder, then one for the cases. Placement: Paint Pier (four games), checked by the layout test, with the open question of Paint Pier's name (recorded under [Lands: navigation and discovery](../../ROADMAP.md#lands-navigation-and-discovery)).

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** at level 1, the pet drags one pad onto a shot and the audience laughs; then the pads are hers.
- **Touch-down feedback:** a picked pad plays its sound immediately; a slot glows while a card is over it; Watch and Premiere are big buttons with the pet's spoken label.
- **Replay variety:** several sets and casts per level, seeded; the audience mix changes; sound pads rotate among the synthesized effects. New sets and pads unlock permanently through play (rework direction); nothing expires.
- **The premiere:** lights dim, the strip plays large, the audience reacts, then the lights come up. No ratings, applause meter or "views".
- **Off-screen:** the **Make a movie at home** card; a family premiere co-play line on the how-to.
- **Music:** the island loop is silenced (`silenced(STYLES.quiet)`) so her sounds are heard.
