# Writing and composition: say it so a reader who cannot ask you still understands

*Subject `writing-composition` · 2026-10-22 · `ccr-62156535-nl3r1r` · [PR #819](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/819). Research and proposals only, for L/T/P/K/S/U/M and grown-ups. No game was played or implemented.*

**Recommendation:** the island cannot read what she writes, and should not try (no handwriting recognition, no free-text scoring, no microphone). Composition is still teachable on glass if the *writer's job* is kept and the *marking* is replaced by a reader: **a friend who can only do what the note says.** Writing differs from talking in one way the island can show: the reader cannot ask "which one?" ([speaking-listening](speaking-listening.md) owns the spoken version, where the pet asks). **Define first a new game, *Leave a Note* (WC1)**: she builds a note from picture chips (later word tiles), leaves it, and the friend does exactly what it says. A note that fits two things sends the friend to the wrong one; she sees why, repairs the note and sends it again. The rules are solvable and inspectable (a note is enough when it picks out exactly one thing), every sufficient note is accepted, and nothing needs reading at the first levels. Younger bands keep the roots of writing in existing games (marks and pictures in Stamp Studio, a grown-up writing down her words), not a fake "writing" game. Whether this is a new game or a mode is decision 1. A wider set of modes (a sign for a purpose, a story passed back and forth, a comic, an invitation, a scribe bird) is listed under Candidates; most are modes of this one engine or of Stamp Studio and Story Weaver, not new games.

**Evidence limits.** The ELOF and Puranik and Lonigan are opened; the 2023 Head Start classroom study stays a search summary. No study was found of screen-based sentence or note composition for children under 6, of dictation as a scaffold, or of middle-school engagement with writing games (a search for dictation research returned nothing on it). The research is therefore about *where the skills sit*, not about whether our rounds transfer to paper. Nothing here is evidence that a child can write; completing a round shows she chose and ordered pieces.

## On the island today

- **Story Steps** (toddler–school): ordering four pictures; the order is her meaning, not her words. Level 8 *Choose the cause* writes a "because" for her (it narrates it). Nothing yet has her *make* a "because".
- **Story Theater** (Story Steps 12–13, school): choose a cast, weather and prop, watch a narrated picnic, and at level 13 change exactly one thing and compare. This is revision of a *plan*, and the nearest built relative of WC1 level 3. Nothing is saved.
- **Stamp Studio, Painted Canyon, Pixel Pictures, Song Maker**: the pictures she makes and keeps (bounded slots in `src/content/creations.ts`). A picture is the root of a message.
- **Letter Trails** (letter formation), **Word Monsters** (letters to words, word families), **Read & Do** (printed sign to a picture, free replay): reading and encoding, not composing. Spelling and sentence mechanics are `spelling-sentences`.
- **Photo Safari** levels 10–11 (queued, speaking-listening SL1–2): she tells the pet which friend to photograph with picture chips; a description that fits two makes the pet *ask*. WC1 is the written twin: the reader here **cannot ask**.
- **Story Weaver** (notebook seed): a three-page picture story the pet narrates, on creation storage. [making-media](making-media.md) owns it and does not redefine it; this study treats it as the home for *kept* stories (WC3).
- **Gap:** she never makes a message for someone else who then acts on it, never repairs a message after it fails, and never gives a reason for a choice.

**What does not fit:** free typing or handwriting scored on glass (the island cannot judge it; handwriting is Letter Trails, keyboarding is its own study); "write a story and get a grade"; sharing her writing off the device (no accounts); streaks or word counts; a timer on composing. **Existing-game route first:** Story Theater (a one-change revision) already shows revising a plan; WC1 differs by making the *message* the thing she composes and by having the reader fail. A new game is warranted because the interaction (compose, hand off, reader acts, repair) is none of Story Steps, Photo Safari or Mail Carrier.

## First game's proposed ladder: Leave a Note

Home: Story Grove (capacity to be checked by the definition). Bands: preschool 1, pre-K 1–2, school 2–3 (the rules below put revision at level 3).

| Level | Scene and rounds | What she decides | Rule |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 One chip** | Bunny waits at the gate; she taps one picture chip (apple, ball, hat) and drops it into the note slot; Bunny trots off and brings that thing. Three rounds | Which chip | Any chip is a valid note; the friend fetches it. No miss is possible |
| **2 Who and what** | Two friends are waiting (Bunny, Bear), and each wants a different thing. A note needs a *who* chip and a *what* chip | Both chips | Valid only if the friend named fetches the item named. Wrong item or friend: the friend fetches it, looks puzzled, one miss |
| **3 The note that failed** | A shelf holds two things of one kind that differ by colour or size (two hats). First note: *what* only. The friend takes the first one that fits and the pet shows the other: "I wanted the red one." She adds a *which* chip and sends it again | Which detail to add | First note failing is the lesson, not a miss: it is the round's designed first try. Sending a note that still fits two things is a miss. Two misses glow the missing chip type; the hint counts as one hint |

**Rule test (`logic.ts`):** a note is *sufficient* when the chips it holds fit exactly one object in the scene; every sufficient note is accepted whatever chips it uses (colour or size or position); every round has at least one sufficient note; no round's items are lookalikes the chips cannot tell apart; the glow hint names a chip kind that, once added, makes the note sufficient; the seeded RNG repeats a scene. Level 3's first-draft failure is built into the scene, not decided by her. **Spoken support:** the pet speaks the finished note and the friend's choice; chips are pictures; word tiles arrive only at school (level 3 may add them, with replay). **Cost:** medium, a new-game tier build; the how-to card and a ghost-finger bot follow the card's own taps.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** a drop onto the note slot after a spoken invitation; no reading.
- **Touch-down feedback:** a chip lifts and the friend's ears turn toward it. A drop that does not fit a slot slides back; nothing flashes red.
- **Replay variety:** seeded scenes change the pair of things and the failing note's twin object; the friend's route differs.
- **Kind consequences:** the failed note leaves the friend with the other hat, holding it politely, never "wrong". The pet narrates what the note said and what the friend did, so the cause is audible.
- **Off-screen:** a grown-up scribes a sentence she says (*"I made this for…"*), reads it back, asks one question, and writes the new detail; or two people play the barrier game with a hidden picture and a note. Both are plain HTML cards (WC5). The cards give no developmental promise.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **A new game or a mode?** Lean: a new game *Leave a Note* in Story Grove, since no existing game has a reader who acts on a note. The alternative is Story Theater levels 14+ for school only, which gives up the preschool and pre-K rungs.
2. **Name and identity** (spoken title, hub icon, land); capacity of Story Grove to be checked.
3. **Kept notes?** Lean: no. Nothing is saved at WC1; WC3 stays with Story Weaver and creation storage.
4. **Where do tweens play?** WC2's top levels reach 12 at `school`; whether M needs a band is the subjects plan's decision, not this one.
5. **Free text:** confirm that the island never takes typed or handwritten text as a *composition* the app judges; typed text, if any, belongs to `keyboarding-editing`.
6. **Reviewer:** none for invented friends and things. A reviewer is needed only if a card tells a grown-up how to teach writing or speaks to a child's own personal story; this study ships neither.
