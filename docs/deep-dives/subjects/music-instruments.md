# Music and instruments: lead, arrange and shape a sound

**Subject:** `music-instruments` · **Audiences:** L, T, P/K, S, U, M and G · **Date:** 2026-10-14 · **Session:** [PR #340](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/340), `codex/music-instruments-subject-research`. Research and proposals only; no game implementation.

**Recommendation:** develop the existing **Little Conductor** seed first: let the player change how a band performs, with an unhurried tap alternative to the baton. Define **Hat Band** next for arranging independent musical parts, with an older constraint mode inside that game. Keep melodic question/answer in **Song Maker**. **Sound Workshop**, making the character of one synthesized note, is a third, later candidate. These choices add musical decisions beyond copying, longer sequences or faster tapping. They do not replace earlier subject priorities or create another execution queue.

## Instrument families and composers: scope of this study

The original subject includes the orchestra and composers as well as rhythm. For instruments, pair a pictured sound-producing action with its player: bow/pluck a string, strike a drum, or show an air-driven instrument. The opened Carnegie Hall material supports comparing actions and listening, not sorting every wooden instrument into woodwind. A piano or a synthesized patch should not be assigned a family just by its material or pitch. In the first games use clearly labeled **toy voices**; realistic instrument recognition is deferred because its acoustic evidence is missing under the synthesis-only contract. A reviewed visual instrument/action mode could extend Sound Garden if its listening/action remains the same; it does not warrant another quiz game.

For composers, the [LA Philharmonic's program note on Britten's *Young Person’s Guide*](https://www.laphil.com/works/the-young-persons-guide-to-the-orchestra-op-34) was opened. It describes a theme from Purcell passed among orchestral sections, followed by contrasting variations and a recombination of instruments. The design lesson is **recognize an idea after its presentation changes**, not memorize a composer's birthday. No score or recording was imported or auditioned.

A later Hat Band variation could hold an original four-note theme constant while changing who plays it; an older player then changes one musical feature and compares what remains recognizable. For preschool the first choice is simply who plays the same tune; school can hear/see the unchanged contour, while upper-school/parent play can preserve an explicit feature across two different arrangements. These are possible depths inside Hat Band/Song Maker, not another roadmap priority. A contextual composer/story card would need a separately sourced selection spanning living traditions as well as historical concert music, rights checks and content review. This session researches the musical mechanism and its fit; a biography or repertoire collection remains outside the first definitions.

## First game's proposed ladder: Little Conductor

These are **proposed level IDs**, not appended to any existing save. First definition M1 covers only 1–3; 4–8 need their own packets. Suggested windows: toddler 1–2, preschool 1–3, pre-K 2–5, school 3–8. Validate readiness, layout and adaptation before registration. Lap stays with existing games; no grown-up window is justified just by turning on more controls.

**First screen:** three friends on a wooden bandstand play one original four-beat pentatonic motif. Two pace pads, a turtle and a rabbit with moving beat marks, sit below them. Tapping either gives an immediate glow/preview; the band changes pace at the next beat, with a visible pending marker. Provisional 60/90 bpm values are contrasting playback choices, not tap-performance targets. The notes remain fixed. Listen again and Done are large, spoken/pictured controls. The pet repeats the instruction; friends never act disappointed.

| Level | First round / decision | Completion, mistakes and support |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1: lead a little band** | Choose either pace, hear the four-beat motif; change freely | Done after one complete phrase at the chosen setting since the last edit. Attempts and preferences never miss; an early Done explains/replays without penalty. One performance, one sticker |
| **2: two ways to play** | Hear the same motif at both paces, then choose the version to finish with | Both contrasted, final chosen version heard, then Done. Hearing marks show what remains; no preference is wrong. Replay and a modeled tap remain available |
| **3: two pictured requests** | A friend requests turtle pace for the first phrase and rabbit pace for the second. Each boundary waits for a choice; Play auditions it, Check submits it | Two correct submitted phrases finish. Wrong Check gently boings and names the requested pace; first mismatch is visible, two misses glow the wanted pad. Help demonstrates that choice and counts once for that phrase; all exploration/replay is free |
| **4: try a baton** | Optionally drag a broad baton area up/down to choose pace; tap pads do the same work | One chosen performance. Smooth/quantize gesture estimates with hysteresis; no deadline or accuracy score. Cancellation returns to a stable pace. Never infer tempo from touch pressure |
| **5: strong or gentle** | Keep tempo fixed; choose two capped gain settings, then contrast them | Same creative listening/Done rule. Show beat spacing unchanged. Stronger is relative, never permission to override device volume; no pressure sensing or moralized mood mapping |
| **6: invite a player** | Cue one part in/out over a fixed accompaniment; leave space for a solo | Every arrangement finishes after audition. A boundary can wait for the cue; no missed entrance. More elaborate parts/sections belong in Hat Band |
| **7: shape a performance** | Two phrases with independent pace and strength: make one contrast, keep the other property fixed | Any arrangement satisfying the displayed property rule works; aesthetic choice is free. Check names only the actual mismatch; undo/replay and helped completion remain |
| **8: revise for a listener** | Hear two versions, choose what to change, then replay the revision | A deliberate audible change and heard result complete, with optional explanation to a person. No app score for beauty, emotion or verbal fluency |

**Accounting:** creative 1/2/4/5/6/8 use no misses for choices; listening/revision gates are guidance. In request/constraint 3/7 only deliberate incorrect Check counts; show the failing property, preserve other choices and allow another attempt. Count requested or automatic revealed help once per phrase; never count replay as a hint. A help-led success earns the ordinary sticker. The current adaptation system still steps smooth creative rounds upward: the definition must explain that as interface progression, not evidence of music mastery, and must not silently alter global difficulty rules.

**Inspectable rules:** use authored event beats/pentatonic steps, pace IDs, gain IDs and active-part IDs. Quantize changes without skipping/repeating score events. Rules can enumerate every request/setting combination, prove both pace choices preserve pitch/order, verify a four-beat heard gate resets on relevant edits, and show help always names a satisfiable next action. Check cancellation/resize and no continuation after destruction. Acoustic timing and whether strong/gentle are distinguishable require a person/device, not a unit assertion.

**Cost/fit:** M1 freezes these rules, speech, supported finish, bot actions and layouts; M2 builds 1–2 with tap controls; M3 adds the two-request Check interaction. A baton is optional later work, not a dependency on a child making a rhythmic gesture. Use Pixi/palette art and original synthesis. Keep `silenced(STYLES.x)` running for the clock; never stop it. Inspect the engine's tempo/playhead semantics before choosing a local score scheduler; do not globally change other games' music speed. All clocks, tweens, held input and sound nodes must release with the scene.

## Hat Band: one older-family case

**First creative slice:** two friends, two removable hats, two authored four-beat loops (one drum, one pentatonic melody). Put on a hat, listen, remove it, add the other. Tap-select then tap-place is a full alternative to drag. Any heard nonempty mix can finish; an empty band simply invites a player. Creative changes never miss. Pre-K can try both separate and combined before choosing, without being asked to name the sound.

**Older arrangement brief:** three roles (drum D, melody M, bass B) across three pictured four-beat phrases. Arrange a melody solo at the beginning, all three together at the end, and let bass enter exactly once and remain through the ending. Every role must sound somewhere. Each phrase is a nonempty subset of {D,M,B}: at most 7³ = 343 arrangements before constraints, small enough to exhaustively accept alternatives and provide a useful repair. For example **{M}, {M,B}, {D,M,B}** and **{M}, {D,M}, {D,M,B}** both work. The second arrangement introduces bass only at the end; neither case requires an arbitrary middle texture. Auditioning/editing is free; only Check counts a mismatch, and help points to one local constraint without discarding the mix.

This is an arrangement puzzle about musical roles and space. It earns a place beside Beat Builder only if those different parts, auditions and audible contrast work. Reuse its section concepts where useful; do not duplicate a sequencer engine by default. Define a single selected-phrase editor with 100-unit child targets rather than nine shrinking boxes. Older grown-up controls may use the 64-unit minimum only in a grown-up-only registration. For a parent the optional question is “can you satisfy the same brief another way?”, never a leaderboard or mandatory minimal solution. No couch scoring, extra saves or public sharing in this packet.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is a pace tap or a hat placement with an immediate visible and audible consequence. A glowing queued icon explains the short wait before a beat-boundary change; a static fallback shows the pending/active distinction without relying on motion or color. Sound-on play is intrinsic to listening discrimination. Visual score/property assistance and a supported finish keep participation possible with sound off, but cannot be counted as evidence of hearing a musical distinction.

Little Conductor's friends sway at the selected pace without treating fast as better. Hats change what the character actually plays, with a one-part replay so cause and effect is visible. Rotate a small authored motif/loop set with seeded selection; variety changes the material while the first screen remains familiar. Repeated listening is welcome. Music and speech need their own room: pause/duck the phrase for an instruction, then replay it from a clear boundary. Compare perceived loudness of synthesized timbres on a real device before a timbre task; equal numeric gain is not evidence they sound equally loud.

Every card is level-scoped and has a ghost-finger demonstration through the actual tap/drag controls; the bot must not set hidden answers. Both layouts preserve bottom-left pet clearance and 100-unit on-screen controls, including selected-phrase editing. Finish returns through the normal celebration/origin route with one sticker. No saved score/mix means no migration or new room object; later kept arrangements need their own bounded schema, repair, backup and playback definition.

**Off-screen invitation, to define after review:** one person chooses a familiar family song, another signals a musical contrast, then swap leader/listener. A person may listen, describe or decline moving/singing. No cameras, recordings, practice obligations or child-built small-parts shakers. This is a proposed card concept, not ready-to-print practical instruction.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Identity and first audience:** define Little Conductor 1–3 now, then check its live performance control earns the identity beside Sound Garden. Start toddler with taps; gesture readiness remains unobserved. Hat Band must add distinct musical roles beyond Beat Builder. Sound Workshop waits behind these two and the energy/waves overlap check.
2. **Placement:** audit Music Mountain capacity for the child candidates and school/grownup registration/destinations for older arrangements; no new land or middle band is assumed. The 12/13 boundary follows existing bands.
3. **Claims and material:** recommend a music educator review of score/voice terminology, instrument depictions and musical-property isolation before educational claims. The no-imported-audio/pentatonic contracts stand. Authentic instrumental recognition, diatonic harmony, composer portraits or culturally named repertoire need separate sourced definitions; nothing here authorizes an exception.
4. **Participation and persistence:** freeze creative/request accounting before implementation; no inference of independent mastery from heard/assisted endings. Keep first performances transient. Any kept Hat Band mix needs a separate save contract. Name the existing required reviewer before practical family cards or physical demonstrations ship; that does not block screen-only definitions.

These are scoped implementation questions, not a request to suspend research or a new approval gate for ordinary reversible work. Record device/enjoyment gaps in [DEVICE-CHECKS](../../DEVICE-CHECKS.md#needs-a-person-or-a-device).
