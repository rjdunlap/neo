# Nature and sustainability: second chances, gardens, seasons and care

**Subject:** `nature-sustainability` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M, G · **Date:** 2026-10-17 · **Session:** `claude/subject-nature-sustainability` · [PR #542](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/542).

This is research and proposals only, from [roadmap chunk 28](../../ROADMAP.md#chunks) and the [subject procedure](../subjects.md#the-procedure-one-session-one-subject). Executive function was already claimed (PR #539), so this session took the next unclaimed row in the sprint order. No reference game was installed or played. No game, band, land, save field or parent card was built.

**Recommendation:** define **Second Chance Shed** first. It grows the notebook's [Trash Sort](../../IDEAS.md#atari-2600) seed into a game where each used thing goes to its next life on the island, and where the choice visibly makes something:
- a peel fed to the worm bin becomes soil, and a sprout appears;
- a whole jar on the reuse shelf becomes a friend's planter;
- a loose-wheeled cart on the mending table comes back rolling;
- a can in the island's Remake machine comes out as a new thing.

From school age, a friend's request decides between two valid places, which is a "reuse first" decision rather than matching an item to a bin. Its older case, **Picnic Loop**, schedules cups across picnics so that few reach the Last Heap within a washing limit, under the island's stated rules. That is a monitor-and-minimize puzzle with several valid plans.

The rest of the subject already has homes:
- **gardening:** Garden Grow and its Under the Soil and Leaf Litter Window proposals;
- **seasons:** Day by Day, weather/water and astronomy;
- **habitats and tidying a habitat:** Habitat Helpers' Patch Change;
- **caring for animals:** Enrichment Day, Habitat Helpers and the rework's creature home, which never decays.

## First game's proposed ladder: Second Chance Shed

A small shed yard. Used things arrive one at a time on a little cart at the top. The places along the bottom each show their pictured key above them:
- the **worm bin** (a friendly worm peeks out; a pot beside it);
- the **reuse shelf** (two or three slots);
- the **mending table** (a needle and a little hammer);
- the **Remake machine** (a hopper with a chute);
- from level 7, the **wash tub** and the **Last Heap**, a neat, neutral pile.

At school age a **friend's request bubble** (a picture: a planter, a drum, a pencil cup) sits by the friend at the side of the yard.

Items and places are at least 100 logical units after scaling. The pet sits clear at the bottom left. In portrait the places stack as two rows of two rather than shrinking.

**Fictional island rules, to freeze and review before building:**

| Place | Takes (first content set) | From level 5, also accepted | Consequence shown |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Worm bin | Banana peel, apple core, carrot top, a dry leaf | — | The worm munches, the soil darkens, a sprout pops in the pot |
| Reuse shelf | A sturdy box, a whole jar, a tin with a lid | — | Used on the spot, at every level: Bunny hops into the box for a nap, a seedling is planted in the jar. At school age the use is a friend's request instead |
| Mending table | A teddy with a loose button, a cart missing a wheel, a kite with a torn tail | — | One beat of mending, then the toy comes back working and the pet plays with it |
| Remake machine | A flattened can, a sheet of drawn-on paper, a newspaper | The tin with a lid (it is metal, like the can) | It chugs and drops a new thing (a drawing pad, a little can-lid medal) |
| Last Heap (level 7 on) | One-use picnic cups | — | They stack neatly; a counter beside the heap shows how many, with no sad face or smell |

No glass that could look broken (the jar is drawn as a sturdy, unbroken pot-jar, and review may replace it with a tub), no meat, dairy or oily food, no pizza box, glossy paper, paper cup or compostable cup, and no battery. Each item has exactly one island place before level 5; from level 5 the tin with a lid is the item with two valid places. Paper never appears with the worm bin in the same round. Every spoken line says "on our island".

**First three rounds.**
1. Level 1: a peel waits on the cart and the worm bin wiggles. Any drop into the bin munches. A drop anywhere else floats the peel back to the cart with a soft pop, and no miss is counted at toddler level.
2. Level 2: a jar comes next. Dropped in the bin, the worm says (from the voice script) "I only munch food bits. Where can a whole jar go?" and the shelf glints. Wrong places count as misses from this level, with a gentle boing, and after two misses the right place glows.
3. Level 3: a teddy with a loose button arrives. The mending table's needle bobs. When it comes back mended, the pet hugs it.

**Scientific and moral boundary.** The game demonstrates *the island's* rules, not the rules of a real town. Reuse first holds at level 5 only because a friend asked, and the hierarchy is a preference, not a law (EPA: no single approach suits every material in every circumstance). The Last Heap is a neutral, tidy place with no bad smell or sad animals, and sending something there is never called bad. Picnic Loop's target is a puzzle constraint. The game must not say the Last Heap is harmful, or that the child is responsible for the environment.

### Rules, mistakes, help and proof to define

- **Finite rules.** An authored item table (item, island place, request tags), request cards and place capacities live in `logic.ts`. The seeded RNG chooses the order of items and which reviewed items appear. It never invents an item's place. Levels 5–8 are checked as a whole plan: an assignment satisfies every request, every capacity and the target.
- **Accounting note for Picnic Loop.** The answer is a schedule (which cup is used at which picnic, which are washed), checked by the bell. Enumeration over the small cup set proves the target can be met and lists every accepted plan.
- **Exploration and accounting.** Levels 1–4 judge each drop at once, and each correct drop plays its place's consequence. Level 1 counts nothing; from level 2 a drop in a wrong place is one miss. Levels 5–8 accept and rearrange placements freely, and **only the cart bell** is a claim: one miss per unsuccessful ring, never one per wrong item. The friend or the place first unmet says what is still missing (for example, "I still need something for my flower"). Undo is free.
- **Help.** After two misses, glow the right place for the current item (levels 2–4) or the first unmet request and one item that fills it (levels 5–8). **Show me one** places one item correctly and counts one hint, and never moves a correct item. A supported finish stays available and counts its assistance.
- **Completion.** A successful bell or the last placement plays the consequences in turn, then calls `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })` once. One sticker even with help. No timer, lives, score or optimality rating.
- **Rule tests to write only when building:**
  - each authored item has exactly one place at levels 1–4;
  - every generated round fits its capacities;
  - every level 5–8 case has at least one solution;
  - level 5 always contains an item that Remake would take but the request needs;
  - level 6 needs planning (a greedy first pairing can fail);
  - levels 7–8 have several accepted plans and at least one tempting plan that fails;
  - hints reduce unfinished work and keep correct placements;
  - a repeated bell cannot finish twice;
  - seeded layouts keep every target in bounds.

  Tests prove the rules are consistent, not real-world truth.
- **Delivery contract.**
  - level-scoped how-to steps in `src/content/howto.ts`;
  - `ctx.instruct` lines in `src/content/voice-script.ts`, repeated by the pet;
  - a ghost-finger `autotouch()` that drags each item to its place, and at levels 5–8 fills requests and then rings the bell;
  - shared object drags, `onTap` for the bell, synthesized pentatonic consequence sounds (the worm munch, the Remake chug as a rising three-note figure), and no imported audio or art.

## Fit with the creature-world rework

This section is proposals only. It reflects the [rework brief](../../rework/README.md), which says games report a bounded result and an adventure applies it.

- **Permanent unlock through play.** An RW07-style adventure, *"What can we make from the shed?"*, could ask for Shed level 5 and turn the friend's filled request into a home decoration that stays (a jar planter on the creature's windowsill, a can-lid mobile). The decoration is the adventure's outcome. The Shed itself records nothing new and has no save field.
- **Customization.** Remade things are candidates for RW06 home items. A child-chosen color for the drawing pad, or a pattern for the planter, would come through existing creations only if a later definition asks.
- **Creatures.** A worm-bin creature (a soil-dwelling friend) is a possible collection family for RW05/RW10. Its care must never decay, and its lore is labeled fiction (worms do not literally make toys).
- **Never:** losing a decoration, a shed that fills up while she is away, or a "clean the island" chore with consequences for not doing it.

## Experience and presentation

Touch-down lifts the item with a wobble and makes every place breathe slightly. A valid drop plays its own consequence at once, and each place has a distinct sound and animation, so a non-reader learns the places by what they do. The first unsupported action is the toddler's drag into the one open worm bin. Variety comes from the reviewed item set, the order of items and the requests, not from speed or clutter.

Co-play is the shed keeper and courier described above. Off-screen, the family can do a kitchen sort with their own local rules, and later the Our Tree visits. Both need review. A real compost or worm bin is mentioned only as something a grown-up may choose, never assigned.

None of this has been observed: child appeal, whether consequences teach the places, device speech, or any transfer to home habits.
