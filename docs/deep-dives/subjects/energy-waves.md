## What people can do, and what the island already offers

Developmental guidance supports a progression from consequence to comparison and then an explicit model. The exact age windows below are proposals. Reading skill, fine motor skill, understanding and preference can differ; older content must earn its depth through choices, not more tiny pieces.

- **L/T:** repeat a pleasant effect and share attention with a person. Existing Sound Garden, Jelly Drums and other touch toys already do that. No need for an infant circuit editor or formal energy vocabulary.
- **P/K:** compare a change, connect a pictured gap and try an on/off switch. NAEYC's account supports trying this with help; it does not establish independent use. Start Circuit Garden at pre-K/school, considering a preschool entry only after observation.
- **S:** build and repair a short connection, keep one comparison property fixed, explain a result by pointing/replay. Circuit Garden should expose the return connection; a wire merely reaching a lamp is insufficient.
- **U/M:** test a design across all disclosed switch states, distinguish possible explanations by a chosen experiment, compare model/trace and repair. Standards support these content directions, not whether a twelve-year-old will choose this game.
- **G:** offer a complete design puzzle, a shared observer/builder role and a later reviewed page companion, each with a finite ending.

## First game's proposed ladder: Circuit Garden

New game's **proposed** IDs, not changes to any saved ladder. Define levels 1–3 first, initially pre-K 1–3 and school 1–6; preschool 1–2 is an observation-dependent possibility. Upper-school and grown-up 4–6 require separate scope/registration decisions. Register only built IDs: if the workshop design (proposed 5) precedes contrast (4), explicitly use `BandLevels.order` to skip the absent ID, or freeze a different order before the first registration; never expose missing plans or renumber a saved level. These ranges are not approvals and do not imply an older player must repeat a preschool entry.

**First round:** a toy flower lantern sits beside a visibly two-terminal battery. Wires already connect one side of the battery through the lamp; the return wire has one large gap. A broad connector waits in the tray. Touch-down lifts/glows it; dragging or tap-select/tap-place bridges the two pictured sockets. Both lamp terminals and both battery terminals remain visible. Closing the connection lights the lantern immediately; a static complete-loop outline and speech explain the result. The pet is clear bottom-left and repeats the instruction. After seeing the lit state, the player can choose Done or keep experimenting; guided Finish can place the missing piece with counted help. There is no wait for an invisible event or deadline.

| Proposed level | Complete round and decision | Mistakes, support and completion |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1: a way back** | One gap in a single lamp loop; fit the connector and see the lantern light | Placing/retrying is free exploration; an invalid placement returns to tray with a nudge. Done after a lit state was observed; an early Done offers guidance, not a miss. Ordinary sticker |
| **2: a switch changes it** | Already complete wiring, one switch; observe both on and off, then choose the final state | Neither preference is wrong. Two large observed-state marks; Done after both were seen. Help demonstrates an unseen state and counts once; the family can finish with help |
| **3: mend the lantern** | A finite two-gap board and a pictured “lamp on” request; place/undo pieces, audition freely, then Check | Only deliberate incorrect Check is a miss: show both gaps or the remaining break, gentle boing and spoken repair. Two misses glow a useful next socket. Help places one useful piece/counts once per reveal, never double-counts a persistent glow; supported finish remains |
| **4: two ways to switch** | Contrast prebuilt series and parallel switch branches controlling one lamp; try all four states | Predictions/test runs are free. Choose a pictured behavior after seeing evidence; deliberate incorrect Check counts. Explicitly show where the branch splits/rejoins; no brightness comparison |
| **5: build for either friend** | Construct a switch network so either switch alone can light the lamp, while both open leave it off | Evaluate the whole four-state brief, accept every legal equivalent routing. Check reports one counterexample; help shows one productive local change, undo/replay preserve work |
| **6: find a useful test** | Compare two disclosed candidate networks: switches in series versus parallel; choose a state, test, then identify the one in a covered box | Reveal the complete result and allow another test; uninformative tests are free. Only a submitted identification contradicted by accumulated evidence misses. A=closed/B=open distinguishes the candidates; both closed does not. Support selects a distinguishing test then explains the result |

**Bounded electrical model:** one ideal source, fixed wires, one nonpolar lamp and switches; no arbitrary shorts or bare battery bypasses are placeable in 1–3. A closed conducting route must join the source terminals **through the lamp**. Conductors connect two terminals; a switch opens/removes its edge. Reversing an ordinary connector does not change connectivity. Evaluate the whole state before showing a result; do not animate a supply droplet travelling outward and arriving before anything else turns on. A steady loop highlight is a model cue, not a literal electron-speed picture. The battery supplies energy; the lamp transfers it to light and surroundings, while charge is not consumed. Use plain, reviewed wording, not a fake quantitative energy ledger. Initial brightness stays fixed; series/parallel **switches** at 4–6 do not claim a rule for two lamps, resistance or source loading. Multiple sources, numerical voltage/current, shorts and brightness need a different model packet.

**Rules worth testing later:** enumerate each board's legal placements/switch states; assert starting boards are unsolved where required and every board has a finish; accept all equivalent terminal graphs, not one wire arrangement. Permuting wire positions/orientations must preserve electrical behavior when connectivity is equal. Verify both lamp terminals/return path, open switch interruption, the four-state series/parallel tables, a guaranteed useful repair hint and an informative test for each mystery pair. Distinguish observed-state gates from current lamp state. A proof that the game’s Boolean rule is consistent is not a proof of physical realism or conceptual transfer.

**Accounting/adaptation:** no miss for testing, prediction, preference, replay, undo or an ineffective test. Only Check/identification submissions at 3–6 count mismatches; each has a truthful local explanation and assisted completion. Requested/revealed help counts once for that reveal, including automatic help; replay does not. Creative levels still step up under the existing global adaptation rule: that is interface progression, not an inference of independent science mastery. Freeze this in the definition rather than altering shared difficulty code. One ordinary sticker per finished round, even helped; no rewards from a test or each lamp activation.

## Lantern workshop: one older-family case

**Brief:** place two labeled switches A and B in a finite socket board with one battery and one lamp. With both switches open the lamp must be off. With A alone closed, B alone closed, or both closed, it must be on. The four pictured target rows stay available with spoken replay. The legal kit admits series and parallel alternatives; only the parallel behavior fulfills every row. At least two spatial routings should realize it, and acceptance compares connectivity/results, not an authored picture. The switch branches join before the lamp so the source has no bare bypass.

All four observations can be tried manually; Check also evaluates all four and reveals the first failing row deterministically. For series wiring it can show A closed/B open and trace the break at B. Undo and a next-connection hint repair locally. An optional “another layout?” invitation is never a condition of finishing. The counterfactual is the enjoyable decision: a route that lights once may fail a friend's other switch. This earns older play through generalization, not more small wire ends or more rapid toggling. A later mystery can use level 6's disclosed two-network hypothesis set; hidden faults without discriminating tests are out.

## Wave Ribbon: a distinct second game

A drawn elastic ribbon has a driver on the left and a visible marker at a fixed horizontal position. Tap Send to launch one finite pulse; watch the pulse reach a pennant while the marker rises/returns at the same horizontal spot. Replay and step views show the same run. First free round: send, observe, choose Done. First comparison: two finite amplitude presets, same pulse shape/speed, replay both before choosing a pictured trace. A single pulse has a width/duration, not a repetition frequency; introduce the frequency control only with a repeating driver in a later round.

For older players, hold medium/speed constant and compare two repeating frequencies using the same elapsed-time window: higher frequency makes shorter wavelength, not a faster crossing. Let players choose the next contrasting test; show crest travel and marker motion separately. A later medium or reflection case needs its own reviewed boundary conditions. The analytic model can sample a prescribed displacement `y(x,t) = A g(t - x/v)` for a pulse, and a repeating driver for steady waves; it is a deliberately ideal, one-way teaching model, not a numerical string solver. It should not claim stiffness, energy dissipation or finite-end reflection until those rules exist. At a later fixed-frequency energy comparison, relative energy can depend on amplitude squared only after specifying the same medium, frequency and observation interval; it cannot become a universal rule for every wave.

**One older case to define:** two pictured hypotheses differ only in whether changing amplitude changes propagation speed. Two amplitudes on the same ribbon, equal distance and frozen time marks, provide a comparison. Explore freely, then Check the matching claim; a mismatch replays the comparison and points to equal arrivals. Alternatives can choose either run first; no verbal explanation is required. A later frequency test distinguishes “more wiggles” from “faster travel.” Enumerate preset pairs and ensure the displayed traces actually distinguish the hypotheses; no invented noise or hidden answer. Choosing tests rather than only matching a trace is the older depth.

Sound is a later connection: air compression oscillates along travel; this ribbon oscillates across it. Sound Garden/Sound Workshop remain the musical homes, and all pitched feedback must use the island’s pentatonic steps. A slow visual ribbon frequency is not automatically an audible oscillator frequency. Do not bend the music contract to teach hertz; a later sound-wave packet must define its visual/time mapping and claims explicitly. Sound-off ribbon investigation remains complete.

## Experience and presentation

Use Pixi Graphics and the island palette; no imported sprites/audio. The first unsupported action is a connector placement or Send tap, with immediate touch-down feedback. A lamp's on/off state uses an outlined icon and shape/contrast change as well as color; no flashing signal task is needed. Show an entire small circuit so its return remains legible. Fixed sockets avoid thin wire-end targets. Use tap-select/tap-place alongside `drag.ts`, `onTap`, 100-unit screen targets in both orientations and pet clearance. A grown-up-only registration may use 64-unit targets; an older mode inside a child registration retains 100. Do not shrink a dense diagram until it fits.

Seeded variation changes gap location, legal wire routing and lantern decoration without reversing the science or relying on color. Hints reveal a useful next action; free tests stay enjoyable even before a player knows what to predict. The how-to card must describe this level and demonstrate real placements/toggles with the ghost finger. Controls, trace sampling and completion must survive resize, cancellation and leaving; scene-owned animation/speech and cleanup follow the shell. Finish uses `ctx.finish`, normal celebration and origin return. A first slice keeps nothing beyond ordinary progress/stickers; saved circuits/experiment journals would need a separate bounded save and discovery definition.

Human observation later must establish whether the wire really appears to have a way back, the marker stays put perceptually, and a person wants another test. Matching a highlighted answer or watching a bot cannot establish that. Co-play alternates chooser/observer with no speed contest; an off-screen card is optional and separately reviewed.

## Decisions for the developer

- **Initial scope and placement:** recommend pre-K/school Circuit Garden 1–3 first, preschool only after observation; define the older workshop at the same time so the model supports it. Confirm Tinker Lab capacity and how grown-up-only levels are registered/destined without introducing a band or assuming a proposed zone.
- **Model claims:** recommend a science educator review the two-terminal lamp/battery drawings, charge-versus-energy language, magnetic materials and ribbon/sound distinction before instructional claims. This is a recommendation for model review, not a newly imposed permission gate on screen-only documentation.
- **Practical companion:** the repository already requires a named reviewer before real electrical, magnet or string instructions/cards ship. No practical advice has been authored here; that dependency does not block definitions/builds of a bounded screen toy.
- **Accounting:** freeze creative/observed gates versus Check misses and helped completion; do not equate smooth exploration with mastery or change the shared adaptation policy silently.
- **Magnet ownership:** prefer a materials experiment within the future observation/fishing activity rather than a second stand-alone fishing game. Confirm which owner when that slice is selected. Do not postpone Circuit Garden for it.
- **Later depth:** numerical circuits, wave boundaries/interference, saved designs and signal coding each need separate definitions. Wave Ribbon and Sound Workshop have distinct models; neither is a dependency for the other's first slice.
