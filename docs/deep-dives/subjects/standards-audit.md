# Standards audit: which subjects still need a research session

*Survey, not a subject study. Requested by the developer, 2026-10-16: "review Common Core and other popular learning objectives and standards to see if more subjects would make sense to have deep research sessions … feel free to push the envelope (zoo animals, for example)." Docs only; nothing here is built, scheduled or promised.*

The [subject tracker](../subjects.md) holds science, history, technology and life-skill rows. This audit lays the main standards frameworks over that tracker and over the [game deep dives](../README.md#tracker), names what no row or game covers, and proposes new tracker rows. Each new row is **not started** and follows the tracker's [procedure](../subjects.md#the-procedure-one-session-one-subject): a session opens the standards at their source, so the codes below are a starting list, not a citation.

## The headline finding

**The subject tracker has no mathematics or language-and-literacy rows at all, and those are the two halves of Common Core.** The early rungs are covered by built games and their game deep dives (counting, adding, place value, sharing, money, clocks, length, letters, sounds, rhyme, syllables, sentences). What nobody has researched is the *later* Common Core domains, which sit exactly at the island's thinnest audiences (U, about 9–12; M, 11–14; and grown-up puzzles): fractions as numbers, ratio and proportion, negative numbers, expressions and equations, area and volume, statistics and chance; and in language, vocabulary and word parts, informational text, opinion writing and speaking and listening. Those are the strongest new rows.

The second finding is that several popular frameworks name whole areas the tracker has never listed: **world languages**, **sign language**, **theatre and dance**, **animal diversity** (the zoo), **oceans**, **philosophy for children**, **media literacy** and **strategy games**. Most of the remaining framework areas map onto rows that already exist.

## Ideas a session might start from (proposals only)

So the rows have something concrete, a few sketches. Each would need its own session's research before any definition.

- **Zookeeper Rounds** (`zoo-animals`, T–S): a morning round of the zoo where each friend needs something (a fish for the penguin, a puzzle feeder for the bear, shade for the hippo); later levels add an enrichment choice with more than one good answer. Extends the Animal Snack action.
- **Who Am I?** (`zoo-animals`, P–U): clues one at a time ("I have fur", "I fly", "I drink milk") narrow a gallery to the one animal: a bat. Critter Sort's rule-guessing with real animals and tricky cases.
- **Species Comeback** (`zoo-animals`, G): a cozy planning puzzle with habitat patches, corridors and a population that recovers when the plan works; no failure, only a better plan.
- **Below the Waves** (`integers` × `oceans`, U–M): a diver and a lighthouse share one vertical number line; "go down 7, up 3" across sea level.
- **Paint Mixer** (`ratio-proportion`, U–M): make the exact color of a sample by keeping red to yellow in the same ratio while making more.
- **Spinner Fair** (`chance-statistics`, K–M): predict which spinner the pet should pick, run it many times, and watch the bars settle; unfair spinners are an investigation, not a trick.
- **Shell Sowing** (`strategy-games`, P–G): mancala on the beach with shells, counting as the move; a parent plays alongside or the pet plays gently.
- **Riddle Room** (`poetry-wordplay`, P–G): a spoken riddle and three pictures; grown-up level is puns and word ladders.

## Order (non-binding)

1. **`zoo-animals`** first: asked for by name, close to built games, and every audience has a fun action. Plan the cast cost before candidates.
2. **`fractions`, `ratio-proportion`, `chance-statistics`**: the clearest Common Core gaps at U–M, where the catalog is thinnest.
3. **`speaking-listening`, `vocabulary-word-parts`, `poetry-wordplay`**: language beyond phonics, from lap upward.
4. **`strategy-games`, `estimation`, `algebra-balance`, `area-volume-space`, `integers`, `oceans`, `informational-reading`, `writing-composition`, `theatre-puppetry`, `philosophy-reasoning`, `media-literacy`** in any order.
5. **`world-languages`, `sign-language`** wait for the developer's decisions below.

## Decisions for the developer

- **Which second language, if any?** World languages depend on the device's speech voices and on what the family speaks; a session cannot choose. Sign language needs a Deaf reviewer and accurate hand drawings.
- **How far up does math go?** The ratio, integer and algebra rows sit at 11–14; whether that belongs to `school`'s top levels or a future band is the tracker's open [middle-band decision](../subjects.md#decisions-for-the-developer).
- **A zoo or habitat land?** Zoo animals could dress existing lands with habitat kits or need a destination of their own; the [zone concepts](../../ROADMAP.md#zone-concepts) are the place to decide.

## Not run

No standards PDF was read end to end; ELOF, EYFS, IB, Core Arts, C3 and Jump$tart came from search summaries, and every session must open its anchors at the source before relying on them.
