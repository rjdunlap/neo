# Traits, inheritance and variation

**Subject:** `genetics` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M and G ([definitions](../subjects.md#the-audiences)) · **Date:** 2026-10-16 · **Session:** `codex/genetics-subject-research`, [PR #476](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/476).

Research and ideation only. No game, level, family tool or scientific claim has shipped from this study. Ages below are design hypotheses, not demonstrated readiness. `school` remains 6–12; 13+ remains `grownup`; U and M are planning audiences, not new bands.

**Recommendation:** define **Sprout Shuffle** first, a small inheritance modeling toy for the older end of school: move one information token from each parent plant into a possible offspring, then unfold all possible combinations. Define **Hidden Sprouts**, its older-family investigation, next. Younger audiences get observational comparisons inside familiar games; a new toddler genetics editor would add abstraction without a useful new action. **Patchwork Conservatory** is a later parent design puzzle, inside the same model if it earns its depth.

## First game's proposed ladder: Sprout Shuffle

**Model to freeze in definition G1:** one invented diploid plant, one locus, two alleles represented by distinctly shaped tiles as well as letters `A` and `a`. Each parent has two slots. `AA` and `Aa` display a spotted petal, `aa` a plain petal. This is an explicitly declared complete-dominance toy rule, not a claim about real flower markings. “Dominant” means visible with one copy in this model, never stronger, better or more common. Each parent contributes one of its two copies with equal chance; enumerate four equally likely slot routes with replacement between offspring. Duplicate routes remain distinct even if they produce the same genotype or appearance.

A player manually choosing contributions is **building one possible model outcome**, not controlling which offspring reproduction produces. The fan shows all four routes; an optional labeled **Try one random sprout** button samples after the model is built. Replaying a sample repeats that trial; starting a new sample advances the seeded RNG. No sampled result gates Finish. Four routes do not mean a family of four must contain one of each. Numerical percentages and actual meiosis wait for separate definitions.

**First complete round:** two large plants with `AA` and `aa` trays, a sprout between them and two empty contribution slots. Tap one tile in each tray; it slides along a source-colored path into the sprout, which opens spotted. Open the fan to see all four possible slot routes; finish with a spoken “each route can make a spotted sprout.” No text-only navigation. A visual preview and instruction repeat are available before any answer.

IDs are proposals for a new game only. Do not renumber any existing game. Do not declare broad school support from this table: G1 must decide the exact band range/entry level and older registration arrangement. The older modes need not block the first school slice.

**Accounting:** a free prediction, sampled result, assembly edit, replay or absent answer is never a miss. A nonempty incorrect explicit Check is one miss with a gentle boing and specific spoken counterexample; after two misses on a task, keep its useful highlight. Proposed help accounting: charge one hint once per task when Show me begins or the automatic guided step appears; subsequent replays are free, and help leaves a supported solution available. Because exploration level 1 has no wrong answer, mark its completion as assisted/exploratory through an agreed policy rather than allow two exploration rounds to establish independent mastery. The definition must resolve this against the shell's existing `misses`/`hints` adaptation contract; this study does not change that contract or invent saved flags.

Finish one short authored case through `ctx.finish`, for one ordinary sticker even with help. The table and final sprouts can fold into a temporary postcard; no new creation/journal format or persistent breeding tree initially. Returning/replaying resets the authored round without loss or obligations.

## Hidden Sprouts: one older-family case

A complete definition can start with exactly two candidate genotypes, `AA` and `Aa`, both visibly spotted, and a known `aa` tester. The learner knows the model, not the hidden answer. Two supplied records demonstrate the distinction:

1. **Three spotted offspring:** both candidates remain possible. Under `AA × aa`, that record has probability 1; under `Aa × aa`, it has probability 1/8 (independent offspring in this model). “Both still possible” is accepted; no forced unique answer or numerical-probability question.
2. **Two spotted and one plain offspring:** only `Aa` remains possible, because `AA × aa` cannot produce plain in this model. The record order can vary; the plain observation remains the useful clue.

The first case asks the player to retain every compatible explanation, then choose a tester for a further investigation. A known `AA` tester cannot distinguish the candidates by offspring appearance; `aa` could, but another spotted result would not settle it. An unlimited sample button is optional exploration; the two authored records guarantee the teachable contrast without grinding. Supported Finish can replay the conflicting route and fill the candidate set.

Use exact enumeration for each record; do not infer genotype from an absence of a rare observation. An explicit “evidence does not yet decide” card must be as prominent as the genotype cards. No claim of statistical confidence, diagnosis or real breeding advice. A second-locus, incomplete-dominance, linked-gene or mutation case needs its own reviewed model; changing a rule mid-round is not difficulty progression.

## Patchwork Conservatory: later parent puzzle

A bounded screen-only case, not an ongoing garden: two independent toy loci, `A/a` for spots and `B/b` for a petal rim. A pictured brief asks for a cross guaranteed to produce **both** visible traits. Three available plants are `AAbb`, `aaBB` and `AaBb`; crossing the first two guarantees `AaBb`, while pairs involving the third can fail at least one requested trait. Two slots choose a pair of distinct plants; all legal pairings are inspectable and reversible. A later brief can include more than one valid pairing; accept all.

Display one locus's four routes at a time, then combine the supports; never squeeze sixteen child-sized targets onto one screen. Independence is an explicit invented-model assumption, not the claim that genes generally assort independently. The puzzle is about a guarantee over possible outcomes, not making a more valuable organism. If its only new work is solving a product of two one-locus tables, keep it as a Sprout Shuffle mode; a separate title needs a distinct implemented action.

## Experience and presentation

**First unsupported action:** a tile pulses inside a large parent tray. Pressing it immediately depresses/highlights it; tapping the empty offspring slot transfers it, with a drag alternative using the shared drag helper. Paths retain the source identity after placement. A big open-flower control previews the resulting appearance; the pet can repeat the instruction without supplying the answer.

**Art/feel example:** draw two soft green parent stems and cream petals with Pixi Graphics and palette colors. Spotted petals have three dark oval marks; plain petals retain an outlined empty-petal icon, so color is redundant. On reveal the new sprout slowly uncurls and both contribution paths briefly glow. Pentatonic synthesized plucks acknowledge placement, with quiet background music; no imported assets. Toy allele tiles and plants are new art, not biological remixes of the saved pet or Bug Builder's wings.

**Variety without extra load:** seed plant positions, benign petal colors and which fan route is missing; phenotype rules and copy probabilities stay fixed. Use authored cross families to guarantee the intended contrast, then seed presentation. Swapping two identical allele copies must not count as a new discovery. A small case ends after one complete inspection/check, not a quota of successful random births.

**Layout:** two parent inspectors and one offspring inspector can share the screen, but only the selected inspector exposes interactive tiles. Every active child control remains at least 100 logical screen units in portrait/landscape; the four-route fan may page, with stable positions and replayable source arrows. Keep the bottom-left guide clear. Cancellation, resize and exiting cancel a pending drag; timer/tween/listener cleanup follows the existing scene contract.

**Co-play:** one person predicts by placing a possible-outcome card; the other reveals a contribution path and checks whether the card is possible. Swap roles without recording performance by seat. Watching or asking for the completed fan remains a valid way to finish.

**Off-screen:** older families can draw two parent panels and four contribution routes together, then compare alternative sprouts. This is a future reviewed plain-HTML conversation card, not a prompt to experiment with real breeding, collect genetic information or compare family members. For younger children, describing a pictured leaf suffices; no loose tokens required.

Device speech clarity for `A/a`, tile identity without letters/color, target comfort, whether manual combinations mislead about controlling reproduction, independent interpretation of “possible/guaranteed,” tween/parent appeal and any learning transfer require person/device observation. None is established by this research.
