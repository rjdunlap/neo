## First game's proposed ladder: Food Web Workshop

A meadow is drawn as three to six large organism stations. An observation button at each station replays what was eaten, with a pictured food source. Carry that source's **food badge** to its eater to place a directed ribbon; a tap-source/tap-eater alternative can be considered in the definition. The ribbon animates food/energy transfer toward the eater, never the animal's travel route. Stations and badges remain at least 100 logical units after scaling. The pet sits clear of the bottom-left; portrait rearranges stations rather than shrinking them. Graph edges are decorative and cannot intercept input.

**First three rounds.** These are illustrative authored meadow records, not a reviewed species/diet list. Freeze and review exact organisms before implementation.

1. Three stations (a plant, a plant-eater and an insect-eater). Two visible replay cards show the two feeding events. Make either supported ribbon, then the other, and tap **Show our web**. The first free action is replaying either observation; no blind test of diet knowledge.
2. The same small web arrives with one ribbon reversed. Replay its observation, remove/undo that ribbon and replace it in the evidenced direction. Keep the other correct ribbon. The task is model repair, not naming animals.
3. A shared plant source feeds two different consumers. Replay both records and branch to both consumers. The diagram must allow one source to support two links without implying one token can feed infinitely many real animals.

| Proposed level | Starting band | Decision added | Completion |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · Make the picture | school | Translate two visible observations into directed links | Show our web matches both records |
| 2 · Repair the ribbon | school | Distinguish arrow direction from mere connection | Correct reversed link; preserve the other |
| 3 · Shared source | school | One source can branch to different eaters | Include both witnessed links |
| 4 · Another food | school | One consumer has two observed food sources; a web is not only a chain | Include all provided records; either link order accepted |
| 5 · What remains connected? | school, upper range | Temporarily mask one source and trace a remaining documented food path | Highlight any valid remaining path, or the explicit no-path answer when appropriate |
| 6 · Two changes, one plan | school top; grownup registration unresolved | Arrange supplied patches to retain requested paths under each scenario | Meet both graph criteria; all valid plans accepted |

**Scientific boundary.** Levels 1–4 reconstruct only the displayed observations: an unrecorded link is *not established by these cards*, not universally impossible. Level 5 says **a shown food path remains**, not that a population survives or stays constant. Level 6's rule is network reachability, not proof of ecosystem resilience. We must say these are simplified models. Feeding arrows carry matter and energy; if a later decomposer view adds matter recycling, use a separate view/key. Never loop sunlight/energy back into the Sun or imply plants obtain their food from soil. Decomposition, mutualism, population feedback and exact time scales are not implemented by the first feeding graph.

### Rules, mistakes, help and proof to define

- **Finite rules.** Authored nodes, observed edges, initial diagram and goal predicates. Seeded RNG changes station order and reviewed case selection, never invents diets. Levels 1–4 compare with the displayed record set; level 5 checks any acceptable directed path; level 6 checks every specified consumer/scenario pair. No continuous ecological simulation, external data or persistent ecosystem.
- **Exploration is free.** Replays, tentative ribbons, undo, masking a source and testing a plan count no misses. **Show our web** is the deliberate claim at levels 1–4: one unsupported/missing/reversed relationship counts one miss per check, with a gentle boing and the relevant observation replayed. Do not cascade one check into several misses. At level 5 predictions and retracing remain unpenalized; assisted Finish explains the trace. Level 6 only the deliberate final Check can count a miss. All modes need an explicit supported ending.
- **Help.** After two failed checks, glow the relevant evidence and affected endpoints; a replay by itself is not a hint. Explicit **Show one connection** installs or repairs one evidenced link and counts one hint; never remove correct work. A supported Finish remains available and counts assistance. Define how a free exploration round affects adaptation: zero recorded misses alone must not be claimed as independent mastery. Resolve this before registering level 5 or an observation-only mode.
- **Completion.** A valid final check replays the web and calls the shell's finish once; one sticker even with help. No manual reward/save/celebration layer, lives, timers or optimality score. A grow-up may stop at the normal home control. No persistence of unfinished worlds, new journal facts or saved creations in the first slice.
- **Rule tests to write only when building.** Every authored case has a solution; first-round graph is acyclic and each edge has visible evidence; reversed-link repair has exactly one faulty edge; branch and alternative cases actually require branching/alternatives; graph path rules accept every legal path; level-6 cases have multiple valid plans and at least one tempting single-scenario plan that fails the other; hints strictly reduce unresolved work and preserve correct edges; duplicate checks cannot award twice; seeded layouts retain all target bounds. Tests prove model consistency, not biology.
- **Delivery contract.** A level-scoped how-to, replayable spoken observation/goal lines, `ctx.instruct` repeated by the pet, and ghost-finger `autotouch()` showing badge drags and deliberate Check. Use `onTap`, shared object drags, palette Graphics and synthesized pentatonic feedback. Destroy drag handles and any animation resources. No microphone/camera or imported animal art/audio.

## Bounded follow-ups

**Patch Change.** Start with a prepared Bunny garden that already works. Preview a single scenario where one resource patch is unavailable; invite/inspect freely, restore that need from two pictured options, invite again, Finish. Do not copy built level 6's two-visitor garden as a new mode. Append an ID and preserve levels/pins. Proposed P/K entry needs an observed usability check, not a promise that three-year-olds infer the effect. The exact unavailable-patch story must avoid animal distress or a duty to maintain the garden while away.

**Pollinator Post.** First freely visit two compatible flowers with a bee and watch a pollen mark transfer; next choose a destination using a visible compatibility key; later compare two visit plans that reach different flower groups. Nectar collection and pollen transfer must be visibly different; fruit/seed formation is a later compressed outcome, never instantaneous proof of guaranteed reproduction. No blanket claim that every plant needs bees. A bot can choose a legal flower-state transition. If the packet turns into steering along a route with no pollen/flower-state decision, reuse Seed Travelers or a route game. Species compatibility, own-flower transfer, actual reproductive wording and any fruit outcome need botanical review.

**Leaf Litter Window.** Merge into Under the Soil: one tap reveals a leaf, fungi and tiny decomposer activity; pictured snapshots advance compressed time and replay. No picking up fungi, feeding worms, live compost advice or obligation to return. Any explanatory sequence needs review; this study does not establish its time scale. Exploration Finish must not count chosen order as an error.

**Meadow Mysteries.** Extend Photo Safari's Field Notes Detective. A fictional record says fewer visits were seen at a flower patch. Two explanations remain possible: fewer flowers were open, or observations were made at a different time. A paired record at the same time but contrasting flower state can help distinguish them. Include **we do not know yet** when records are insufficient; one photograph is not population evidence. Enumerate surviving hypotheses after each observation and ensure at least one informative next action. No arbitrary negative feedback for opening an unhelpful clue. Review whether the case is experimental control or mere observational association before writing causal speech.

**Meadow Plans, older family case.** Four patch sockets, six supplied patch cards, fixed documented feeding links and two visible masks (one patch unavailable; one food source unavailable). Each requested consumer must retain a plant-to-consumer path in both scenarios. Free Try reveals the first missing path; Undo and Show one useful patch preserve supported completion. A complete definition must exhibit two valid arrangements and one plan that passes only one scenario; a solver enumerates every arrangement. No claim that connected organisms necessarily survive, all predators are harmful, or species counts alone measure ecosystem health. This is a candidate for a parent's own quiet puzzle, with untested appeal.

## Experience and presentation

Touch-down lifts a food badge and pulses its source; a valid docking produces a soft wood click and a ribbon ripple. Feeding records can show a small food symbol entering a mouth, with no chase, injury or disappearance of the depicted organism. Source and destination remain visible during replay. Replay variety comes from reviewed observation sets and seeded station arrangements, not faster animals, more clutter or obscure species names.

Co-play uses observer/connector roles and spoken observations, never requires an adult to know the diet in advance. Off-screen, the first proposed activity is a picture-card web on a table. A future nature card can ask who uses a familiar place, after reviewer approval; it must permit simply watching without touching, collecting or changing anything. Real field use, child understanding of arrows and actual device speech/touch remain untested.
