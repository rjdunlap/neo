# Off-screen companions: the line, the card and the hand-off

**Subject:** `off-screen-companions` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M, G · **Date:** 2026-10-14 · **Session:** `claude/off-screen-companions-research`, [PR #322](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/322).

Research and proposals only, following the [subject procedure](../subjects.md#the-procedure-one-session-one-subject). Catalog read at `d840a77`. No code, test or asset was changed. This is the last subject in suggested wave A.

**The finding that comes first.** Every game already has one `offScreen` line (93 of 94; the exception is grown-up-only Pond Conga), and the grown-ups' page shows two of them. The lines were written as ideas for the whole age range of a game, so a game that supports lap shows the same line to a one-year-old's parent as to a nine-year-old's. **At least five lines in games a lap or toddler parent sees name small loose objects** (a marble, coins, buttons, dry beans, a bean). The [audit](#on-the-island-today) lists them. They are the first build slice because a removal-only swap makes the page safer and needs no new advice. Separately, one line prints a literal `{name}` on the page. Neither is a design for the future; both are present state.

**Recommend, after that:** make the line **per band** (so a lap parent and a school parent each see one that fits), show **three** on a small "At home" card with a "what to notice" and a "something to say" under each, and add a "things from around the house" list derived from the lines. All of it is plain HTML on the grown-ups' page. Nothing is tracked, nothing is due. [Roadmap recommendations](RECOMMENDATIONS.md#off-screen-companions-recommendations).

**Gaps, recorded.** There is no outcome evidence that an in-app off-screen suggestion changes what families do. Nothing found says what a 11–14-year-old wants from one. The study therefore specifies cards that cost the family nothing to ignore.

## What people can do

Only the points that bear on a card; the [movement](movement-exercise.md#what-people-can-do-evidence-and-limits), [routines](routines-self-care.md#anchors-and-evidence-limits) and [social-emotional](social-emotional.md) studies hold the age detail.

- **Under 1 to 2.** Content is repeated actions with a body or an object: roll, bang, fill and dump, hide and find. A mouthing child puts things in her mouth, so **the object is part of the instruction**.
- **3 to 5.** Pretend play, simple rules, sorting, counting with touch. She can lead: "you be the customer".
- **About 6 to 8.** Wants a task with a result: build it, measure it, beat your own try. Can write a few words on a card.
- **About 9 to 12.** Can run a small experiment, keep a table, teach a younger child. A card works when it hands her a *question*, not a recipe.
- **Early teens (the M audience).** No study found. A tween will not do a game's "off-screen idea" because an app said so; the evidence-light bet is a **challenge she can take to a friend or sibling** (a build with a measurable result) and leave if she does not want it.
- **The grown-up.** The AAP and Zero to Three pages above put the adult's *attention* first. Time and materials are scarce on a weekday evening; a card needs one object from the kitchen drawer and under a minute to start.

## On the island today

Counted from `src/games/*/index.ts` at `d840a77`; the page code is `src/parent/panel.ts` (`weekSummary`).

- `GameModule.offScreen?: string` ("A real-world activity that carries the same skill off the screen, for the parent zone") and `coplayHint?: string` (a tip on the game's own screen for the early bands, switchable). **93 of 94 games have an `offScreen`**; 88 have a `coplayHint`. Lines run 50 to 140 characters, median 87. Ten of the eleven grown-up games have one.
- **The page shows at most two** `offScreen` lines (`panel.ts:371`): those of the games she played in the last seven days, or, if she played none, the first two games her band lists, in catalog order. So a family sees the same two lines repeatedly, and the other ninety are effectively unreachable.
- **It is one line per game, not per band.** Fifty-one games reach lap or toddler; their parents and the parents of a nine-year-old see the same sentence when the game spans both.
- **`{name}` is not replaced.** The panel prints `esc(g.offScreen!)` and nothing else, but Weather Wardrobe's line reads "let {name} pick one thing to wear". The page shows the braces. (The co-play tip does substitute.) **A bug, not research.**
- There is **no "why this" and no "what to notice"**: the line says what to do, not what to look or ask for. The island's [rules](../../../AGENTS.md#rules-for-every-activity) already say "Include co-play and an off-screen activity where useful; grown-up UI stays plain HTML".
- Nothing is tracked and nothing expires. That is correct and the study keeps it.

The lines for most games are fine: rolling a ball, clapping syllables, a pots-and-spoons band, stuffed animals, a sock sort. About 19 of the 93 use only talk, song or movement. The lines are, in the Zero to Three sense, mostly *the same action in the real room*.

**What the catalog lacks:** a *different* line for a school child (a build with a number to beat), a conversation line ("what do you think will happen?") that stretches the moment, and a plain list of the dozen household things most lines use.

**Household things the lines use (rough keyword counts over 93 lines; a line can match several):** paper, card, pens or chalk 30 · stuffed animals and toys 30 · blocks, cups, boxes, bowls and trays 24 · clothes and socks 15 · pots, pans and spoons 12 · small loose items 15 · food 8 · a ball 6 · water or bath 6. So a "things around the house" list of **paper and pens, stuffed animals, blocks or stacking cups, a bowl or tray, a few socks, a pot and a spoon** covers most of it, **without** the small-loose-item row.

## What fits

- **Fits.** A per-band line; a three-line "At home" card; a "what to notice" and a "something to say" per line; a kit list; a rule test that fences small objects out of lap and toddler lines; the page keeping its current plain-HTML form.
- **Fits as a mode of what exists.** All of it extends `offScreen` and `weekSummary`; no new game, no new contract, no save field (the card has nothing to remember).
- **Does not fit.** A "mark as done" or streak of at-home activities (obligation, the AGENTS.md rule); push reminders; a calendar of weeks (Camp Khan's shape); a camera or photo upload (the island has none); a purchase list; any line that instructs about food, bath, sun or medicine without a reviewer; rewards on the island for doing the off-screen line.
- **Infrastructure.** A pure function `offScreenFor(mod, band, childName)` (with the `{name}` replacement) and a small test list; no engine or save work.

## A first card's ladder

The ladder is the card's, not a game's. One line has: **try** (verb first, one household thing), **notice** (what to look for), **say** (one sentence).

| Band | Try | Notice | Say |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Lap | Roll a large soft ball back and forth on the floor | Does she watch it, reach, push it back? | "Roll!" "Again!" |
| Toddler | Fill a bowl with stacking cups and tip it out | Does she tip or pass? | "In. Out. Big cup. Little cup." |
| Preschool / pre-K | Pretend shop: price three toys on paper; she is the customer | Does she count or point to what she wants? | "How many do you want?" |
| School | Build a ramp from a book; predict first, then measure how far the ball rolls | Did the second try change in the way she said? | "What will happen if you tilt it more?" |

These examples are proposals to be reviewed. They contain no small object, no food and no water; their only reviewed-for-safety claim is that those three were avoided.

**Rule tests (all pure):** for lap and toddler, no line names an item from a small-object word list (marble, coin, button, bean, bead, pebble, rubber band, battery, balloon, small ball, dry); no line contains `{`, a number of minutes, "every day" or "must"; each line fits a length cap; each game that reaches lap has a line valid at lap; the card builder returns the same three lines for the same inputs; a line may not mention buying anything.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** the grown-up opens the gear, sees "At home" with one line to try.
- **Touch-down feedback:** plain HTML; nothing to press except a closed `<details>` for "why this".
- **Replay variety:** lines already vary by game; the new variety is the band. No rotation, because nothing the family has not done is marked.
- **No tracking:** no checkbox, no 'done', no count. A parent who ignores it loses nothing.
- **Off-screen twin of the card itself:** a printed list on the fridge, if a reviewer approves. Until then, the plain page only.

## Decisions and open questions

1. **Does a removal-only swap of small objects (large soft items, cups, blocks) need the named reviewer first?** *Lean: no*, because it removes a risk and adds no instruction; **any new line** about bath, food, water or sun does need the reviewer. Waiting on the developer.
2. **Who is the reviewer** for practical lines (a pediatric nurse, early-years educator, a food-safety reviewer for the food lines)? Already open in [Waiting on the developer](../../WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER.md).
3. **One line per band, or per game plus an optional extra for school?** *Lean: per band, only where the bands differ.*
4. **Card on the grown-ups' page only, or also on the how-to intro?** *Lean: page only*; the intro is already full.
5. **Three lines or two?** *Lean: three, selected by recent play first, then by her band, in catalog order beyond that*; if she played none, show one from each of three lands rather than the first two games.
6. **M surface?** None added; wait for the middle-band decision.
7. **Printable cards?** Held until the reviewer.
