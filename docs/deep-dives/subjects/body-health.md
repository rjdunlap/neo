**Recommendation:** define **Sense Detective** first: choose a way to investigate a mystery toy, reveal a clue, then decide what the evidence supports. **Developmental reading:** Head Start supports concrete exploration and supported routines for the youngest; the preschool guide supports real physical exploration, not touch-screen equivalence. The school/older rungs below are design hypotheses informed by curriculum complexity. No opened empirical study establishes independent sensory-test selection at age three, a particular older puzzle difficulty, teen appeal, learning transfer or therapeutic benefit. Do not call the game a sensory assessment, brain training or treatment. An iPad conveys pictures and sound; it does not convey texture, smell or taste. Illustrated clues about these properties must be labeled as a character's report, never presented as sensations the player experienced. Vision/hearing differences are not errors; provide a depicted clue/replay route and keep everyone's own perception ungraded.

## First game's proposed ladder: Sense Detective

Levels 1–2 are [defined](../../definitions/sense-detective.md) (2026-10-22) and built (2026-10-23, Tinker Lab); levels 3–5 await B3.

**Small complete experience:** a toy parcel belongs at the puppet museum. Choose an investigation, inspect its outcome, select every possible toy and press the large **Check** card. One task at the entry, two tasks at deeper levels, one shell-owned sticker at completion, including with help. No real people or health states are classified. Controls must remain at least 100 logical units in landscape and portrait; limit to three candidate cards and two investigation buttons on the first definitions. Tap acknowledgment squishes the button immediately; result playback can repeat without submitting.

**Concrete finite pool:** bell, rattle, drum; view observations are `round` for bell/drum and `handled` for rattle; sound observations are `ring`, `shake`, `beat`. Each toy's original drawing visibly matches its view description. This is an authored toy identification model, not a universal acoustic rule about real objects. Sound cards include replay plus an equivalent pictured event (bell swinging, pellets moving, drum struck); the player can use it without hearing. No smell/taste/texture emulation. Define additional pools only after the first pool works.

| Proposed level / window | First round and new decision | Experiments, support, future rule proof / cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 1 · P trial/K | Bell/rattle; open the clear view to see a handled outline, then choose rattle | Window opening/replay free. Wrong confirmed choice gets a gentle boing and comparison; after two misses glow rattle. Assert each entry has one visible matching card. S content/UI |
| 2 · K | Bell/drum; round view is shared. Choose the sound investigation to distinguish them | Either investigation is free, including an uninformative one; Check only judges the explicit answer. Hint points to sound, then its consistent card. Assert a useful available observation always exists. M new source-selection action |
| 3 · K/S | Three toys; sound withheld for this task, view shows round. Select **both bell and drum** | No forced guess or secretly unique target: task asks what the view permits. Incorrect confirmed set gets a specific counterexample; two misses glow one add/remove edit toward the exact set. Assert exactly the two consistent toys and no leaked sound. M multiple-selection |
| 4 · S | Start with the same round clue, then choose another investigation and reduce the set after ring | Records remain visible; observing never costs a miss/hint. Check accepts the updated consistent set. Assert filtering is monotone, fresh evidence is consistent, and sound distinguishes the pair. M evidence strip |
| 5 · S upper trial | The sound channel is unavailable; answer the **can we tell which one?** card and choose the evidence we would need | Correct answer may remain “not enough clues.” Unavailable sound is a property of the authored exhibit, not a disabled visitor. Hint demonstrates two consistent toys. Assert ambiguity is genuinely retained and suggested next evidence would distinguish. M explanation selection |
| 6 · U horizon, definition after overlap check | Construct a two-clue exhibit for a partner with either one possible toy or an explicit shared pair | All valid creations accepted; explicit Show exhibit ends without grading aesthetics. Verify the generated answer set exactly; assess whether this should instead be an older Field Notes mode. M authorship, no persistence |

The level numbers above are provisional for a new proposal; they do not reserve or change any saved ID. Proposed initial registration would be K/S after a layout/observation check; preschool remains a trial and does not silently join. L/T keep existing games. Do not route the older cases into an unapproved band.

**Rules to freeze in the definition:** for candidate universe C and revealed observations O, accept exactly `{c in C: c agrees with every observation in O}`. No scoring against a hidden identity when the observations permit several. Select/deselect and investigation choices stay free; Check of an incorrect final set is one miss. In incomplete selections, identify one missing compatible card or one incompatible chosen card. After two Check misses on a task, show that useful edit and count at most one automatic hint for the task; explicit Show me uses the same assisted path. A deliberate supported continuation can reveal the full valid set. Resubmitting unchanged state only repeats guidance, without repeated penalties. Exploration is not mastery evidence: levels whose task is only revealing a view must not promote independent inference from zero misses. Resolve that adaptation policy before registration.

**Future meaningful tests, not run now:** all authored clue tables are internally consistent; every task has a nonempty valid set; unique tasks are uniquely identified by available evidence; ambiguity tasks have at least two solutions; every suggested investigation reduces the set when reduction is promised; hints strictly improve a wrong set; answer positions/identity vary with seeded bags, not reroll exhaustion; no hidden data reaches a masked view; bot and accessible depicted clues lead to the same supported ending. Include counterexamples where hidden identity differs but revealed clues match, so an evaluator cannot reward a guess as knowledge.

## Older and systems follow-ups

**Body Connections first case:** a neutral original human teaching diagram, separate from the customized animal pet. Reorder three explanatory panels for a sound event: air vibration reaches an ear, the ear converts the event into signals, and signals reach the brain. Replay the whole model and compare which representation belongs at each boundary. This is model assembly, not surgery or repairing a “broken person.” A later digestion case distinguishes movement, breakdown, absorption and delivery; a subsequent interacting-systems case joins a reviewed respiratory/circulatory diagram. Accept every order allowed by an explicit partial-order model where processes overlap; do not claim all bodily events form a single sequence. If the first case is merely ordering pictures, put it in Story Steps and defer the new game until cross-system interaction earns it. Physiology educator review of the model/art/speech is needed before scientific claims ship.

**Perception Studio first case:** two equal horizontal rails, with a movable tiled surround. Slide the surround through three authored offsets; tap a guide to overlay straight reference lines; compare or compose a scene and press Show. A person may see a distortion or none. Neither response is wrong. Geometry tests prove the rails never rotate or change length and guides coincide; the payoff is discovering that changed context can change appearance while a measurement stays fixed. Further cases may compare surrounding size or occlusion, one variable per case, after review. No flashing, timed fixation, required afterimages, eye tracking or claims about a player's senses. The Exploratorium source motivates the experiment; the island geometry and artwork would be original.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is a large window latch with a toy silhouette; opening it reveals a satisfying little mechanical performance. Use Pixi Graphics, palette colors and existing prop shapes: bell rim, handled rattle and drum skin, with shape-coded evidence cards. Render the view as a picture of the available evidence, not a camera pretending to measure the user. A warm wooden exhibit and a curtain lift make investigating pleasurable; art and clear consequences carry the fun rather than a quiz badge.

Seed the exhibit toy, card order and original display decoration; clue rules remain stable and replays preserve the same task. Keep clues visible, allow pause/replay/undo, separate clue controls from Check, and reserve the bottom-left pet and shell corners. A ghost-finger demo must show investigation then selection/Check; it cannot skip to the answer. Speech belongs in voice-script, instruction replay on the guide, code-drawn art and synthesized pentatonic sound only. Sound-producing investigations should not compete with the music loop; choose an appropriate silenced style while preserving its clock.

Co-play alternates investigator and explainer; a parent can ask what is still possible without hiding evidence or supplying the answer. Future off-screen Notice Together can borrow the *roles* from the PBS spinner, with original cards and reviewed real-object prompts; no practical activity is approved here. Person/device checks remain: preschool inference, portrait reach/100-unit targets, actual speech, usefulness of depicted sound alternatives, comfort with internal diagrams, teen/parent choice to replay. Browser automation cannot establish those or learning transfer.

## Decisions for the developer

1. Confirm whether Sense Detective's choice of information channel earns a new game; consolidate with Photo Safari if the final action is photography/visual clue collection. Define K/S first; preschool is an observation trial.
2. Choose a science/health educator to review body-system models, accessible sense language and human teaching-diagram scope. Existing Teddy Doctor pairings are pretend behavior, not newly verified medical instructions.
3. Decide initial band ranges, subject/land destination and capacity; school-top at 11–12 and grownup at 13+ can serve older proposals without a new band. Cozy Village, Tinker Lab and Lantern Lake all require actual capacity checks; proposed Discovery Marsh is not available infrastructure.
4. Freeze adaptation for free reveals, uncertainty and unjudged creation before registering anything. Supported completion earns the ordinary sticker; it does not prove independent mastery.
5. Name a qualified reviewer before any real care, food, sleep, physical, body-safety or diagnostic advice/card is drafted for release. Those content areas are open research gaps, not dependencies of the screen-only toy definition.
