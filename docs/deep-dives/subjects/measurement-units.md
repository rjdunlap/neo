# Measurement and units: choosing the tool, choosing the unit, saying what changed

*Subject `measurement-units` · 2026-10-21 · `claude/measurement-units-research` · [PR #764](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/764). Research and proposals only, for L/T/P/K/S/U/M and grown-ups. Base: `1f769164`. Opened from the [further-gaps review](further-gaps.md#two-additional-research-sessions). No game was played or implemented.*

**Recommendation:** the island already has a measurement ladder in pieces: length in Inchworm Measure, mass in Seesaw Balance, capacity in Fill and Dump and clock time in Clock Tower, and most of their next rungs are already planned in those games' deep dives. This study joins them instead of adding another. **Define first, inside Inchworm Measure, *Big worms, little worms***: she measures the same thing twice with two worm sizes and says who used the smaller worms. It is the one Common Core length idea (2.MD.A.2) that no built or planned rung covers, it reuses Inchworm's built drag-and-snap action, and it needs no new game, land or decision. A cross-attribute **Measuring Post** game (which tool answers this question? whose count used the smaller unit?) and a grown-up weights puzzle are later proposals, each behind a placement decision.

**Development.** The opened abstract of Sarama, Clements, Barrett, van Dine and McDonel, *Evaluation of a learning trajectory for length in the early years* ([ZDM 43(5), 2011](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/evaluation-of-a-learning-trajectory-for-length-in-the-early-years/)), reports that children largely moved through the trajectory's levels in order, that one level lays units end to end or tries to repeat one unit and sometimes leaves gaps, and that indirect comparison's place needed revising. Search summaries (not opened) name the levels from length recognition through direct and indirect comparison, serial ordering, end-to-end measuring and *unit relater and repeater*; a later extension describes consistent measurement in upper grades. A study of 40 six- and eight-year-olds (redalyc PDF, **search summary only**; the server failed) reportedly found the inverse link between unit size and count better understood than how to choose a unit for an object, with measurement sense differing between length, mass and volume. The Head Start ELOF page refused the fetch: **no ELOF code is claimed**. Search summaries place preschool measurement under a mathematics subdomain about comparing two objects by a shared attribute; an alignment page names it P-MATH 8, unverified.

No opened evidence sets a precise age for any rung below, establishes that a tap game teaches unit relations, or measures teen or parent appeal. Standards are curriculum evidence; developmental work supports the order; neither establishes transfer from Puddle Island.

## First game's proposed ladder: Big worms, little worms (Inchworm Measure)

One new level appended to Inchworm Measure with a stable ID (the next free ID after checking newer work and Inchworm's queued dive); its order places it after level 2 for school and as pre-K's top. These numbers are this study's; the definition freezes them.

| Step | What she sees and does | What she decides | Mistakes and help |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| a. Big worms | A stick and a bucket of **big** worms (two of Inchworm's usual worm lengths, visibly fatter and a different colour). She lays them end to end, as at level 1 | Where each worm goes; when it is done | As level 1: a worm hanging off the end is the existing gentle miss |
| b. Little worms | The same stick moves to a second row; the bucket now holds **little** worms (Inchworm's usual length) | The same | As above |
| c. Which count? | A two-row card shows both rows of worms against the same stick, with each count said aloud ("3 big worms. 6 little worms.") "Who measured with the little worms?" She taps the row | That the bigger count belongs to the smaller unit | A wrong tap is one miss; the pet says the stick did not change, and the little worms are smaller, so it took more of them. Two misses glow the little-worm row and lay one big worm over two little ones |

Four sticks a round, lengths chosen so the big-worm count is a whole number (2–3 big, so 4–6 little, inside the six-worm maximum the built levels 2–3 already fit on screen; `src/games/inchworm/logic.ts`), seeded. From the third stick, step c sometimes asks the reverse ("Which count used the big worms?"). A rule test checks every stick: both counts are whole, the little count is exactly twice the big, and the two rows end at the same mark. Optional later variant, for the definition to keep or drop: a third unit three times the little worm, so the ratio is not always two. Ghost finger: the existing Inchworm bot lays worms and taps the right row. Cost: new-level tier on an existing interaction, with the row-tap card as the one new input.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** laying the first big worm, the same drag she already knows. The new question waits until both rows exist.
- **Touch-down feedback:** worms already lift on touch; each row on the card highlights on touch-down and is at least 100 units tall.
- **Replay variety:** stick, leaf, snake and pencil (Inchworm's objects), seeded lengths, the reverse question.
- **Off-screen:** the Measure it twice card; co-play: guess before the little worms go down which count will be bigger.
- **Only a person can judge:** whether a five-year-old reads big versus little worms at iPad size, and whether laying two rows feels like work.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Measuring Post as a new game, and where.** Lean: yes, as a school game in Tinker Lab or Cozy Village after a capacity check, because tool choice crosses attributes and no existing game holds all three. Alternative: spread its levels over Inchworm, Seesaw and Fill and Dump and drop the cross-attribute question.
2. **Standard units, and which system.** Lean: island units (worms, blocks, cups) through the first packet; at school top levels, metric (cm, kg, l), which matches England's programme and the family's languages. Alternative: US customary (2.MD.A.3 names inches and feet), or both.
3. **Weights Case's home.** Lean: Lantern Lake after its capacity test, defined after Box Balance so the two puzzles do not repeat each other. Alternative: a level of Box Balance.

Nothing in M1 waits on these.
