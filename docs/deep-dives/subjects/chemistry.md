# Chemistry: the same pieces, rearranged

**Subject:** `chemistry` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M and G, as defined in the [subject tracker](../subjects.md#the-audiences) · **Requested:** 2026-10-16 (with SpaceChem as a reference) · **Researched:** 2026-10-17 · **Session:** `claude/subject-chemistry`, [PR #516](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/516).

**Research and proposals only. Nothing built.** This follows the completed [Matter study](matter.md) and does not repeat it: phase changes, dissolving and particle dots stay in Melt and Freeze, Stir and See and Particle Lens.

**Recommendation.** Define **Bond Beads** levels 1–4 first: a new construction game whose fun is taking a few beads apart and building something else from exactly the same beads, until the tray is empty. Each bead has a fixed number of hands; a build is valid when every hand is held. Its spine is conservation through rearrangement (MS-PS1-5) and composition (MS-PS1-1), at the ages where those standards sit (upper school and middle); its `school` (6–8) opening is honestly a hand-holding puzzle that claims no chemistry. The grown-ups' chemistry puzzle should be a reviewed **molecule sea pack for Island Bridges**, not a new Lantern Lake game: Island Bridges already is Hashiwokakero, a bond-count puzzle. Visible evidence of reactions (fizz in a sealed bag, a new solid) extends Matter's Weigh It and Stir and See after the science review Matter already requires. SpaceChem's programming half belongs to Machine Line and Parcel Programs. Lap through pre-K get no atom content.

## Anchors opened at their source

Standards are curriculum destinations, not gates, and none shows that a game teaches them.

| [MS-PS1-5](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ps1-5-matter-and-its-interactions) (opened) | "Develop and use a model to describe how the total number of atoms does not change in a chemical reaction and thus mass is conserved"; emphasis on physical models or drawings, including digital ones, that represent atoms | Excludes "the use of atomic masses, balancing symbolic equations, or intermolecular forces." | [MS-PS1-6](https://www.nextgenscience.org/pe/ms-ps1-6-matter-and-its-interactions) (opened) | A design project for a device that releases or absorbs heat by a chemical process (dissolving ammonium or calcium chloride) | **Out of scope:** a real-chemical engineering task. **The learning progression.** The National Research Council's [Taking Science to School, Appendix A](https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/11625/chapter/14), *Overview of Learning Progressions for Matter and the Atomic-Molecular Theory* (opened; its table's grade columns did not come through cleanly, so these placements are a reading of the layout), builds macroscopic foundations first. K–2: objects are made of materials with observable properties, and matter is conserved through reshaping and breaking. 3–5: "there can be invisible pieces of matter", atoms "too small to see" with common tools. Only 6–8: "All matter is made of a limited number of different kinds of atoms", and atoms "are neither created nor destroyed". That is the main reason the island's atom content starts at U/M.

## What people can do, and what remains uncertain

- **Sourced:** the standards and progression above put atoms and molecules at about 11–14. Below that, the island's job is macroscopic: observable properties, reversible and irreversible change, conservation (Matter).
- **Sourced (secondary):** misconceptions persist after instruction. Nakhleh's *Why Some Students Don't Learn Chemistry* (J. Chem. Educ. 69(3), 1992; bibliographic details and argument from [search summaries](https://journals.unam.mx/index.php/req/article/download/66458/58343/193668), not opened) argues many students build inappropriate particulate ideas from the start. Ben-Zvi, Eylon and Silberstein's *Is an atom of copper malleable?* (1986), cited through an [IOP diagnostic worksheet](https://spark.iop.org/sites/default/files/media/documents/BEST%20Diagnostic%20-%20Properties%20of%20copper.doc) (not opened), found students treating an atom as a small piece of the substance carrying its properties. **Design consequence:** beads are never coloured or textured *like the substance* (a water molecule is not blue and wet), and no line says a bead "is" sticky, wet or hot.
- **Sourced (practitioner):** Jasper Green's [RSC Education article](https://edu.rsc.org/cpd/conservation-of-mass/4011856.article) (opened; 2020) reports, citing Johnston and Scott (1991), that "only 40% of 12–13 year olds" predicted mass is conserved when sugar dissolves, and Kind's finding that some students think gases have no mass. It recommends accounting for the **whole system** (including escaping gas) and colour that shows atoms being rearranged. The Weigh It sealed-bag case and Bond Beads' tray tally take that advice; neither underlying study was opened.
- **Inference, not evidence:** that a 6–8-year-old enjoys a hand-holding puzzle; that a 10–13-year-old reads an emptied tray as "nothing was lost"; that parents enjoy molecule seas. Device and person checks.
- **Not established:** that building toy structures produces correct chemical understanding. A successful construction puzzle alone does not show it (the brief's warning). Rule tests can prove toy consistency, not chemical truth.

## What fits

- **Fits as a new game: Bond Beads.** The action (break links, rebuild from the same pieces until the tray is empty) is unique on the island, and the learning is specific: what something is made of, and that nothing is added or lost by rearranging. It passes the brief's "construction alone proves nothing" test only if the **tally and empty tray** are the point, not the shape.
- **Fits as modes:** Island Bridges molecule seas (G); a sealed-bag fizz in Weigh It (U–M); a "something new?" evidence round in Stir and See (U–M). The last two are review-gated.
- **Does not fit:** a periodic-table quiz (recall); equation balancing (outside MS-PS1-5's boundary); acids and bases, practical or pictured, until a chemistry educator reviews (no household mixing in any form); flames or burning art; any SpaceChem-style programming; a thermal device design (MS-PS1-6) needing real chemicals.

### The model, stated once

Beads are a toy *model* of atoms, and the game says what it leaves out.

- **Kinds:** a small enumerated set, H (1 hand), O (2), N (3), C (4), each with a fixed colour, a **letter** and a distinct shape mark so colour is never the only cue (white H, red O, blue N, dark-grey C after the common ball-and-stick convention; recalled, not opened).
- **Links:** joining two free hands is one link. At the M levels two links between the same pair are a double hold (O₂, CO₂); no triple hold in the first game.
- **Valid build:** every bead's hands are all held and the build is in one piece.
- **Real molecule:** only builds that match an **enumerated, reviewed list** are named (H₂, O₂, H₂O, CO₂, CH₄, NH₃), matched independently of drawn layout. A valid build *not* on the list gets a neutral line ("Every hand is held") and never a claim that the molecule exists.
- **Left out, and said so on the how-to card from level 5:** 3D shape and bond angles (the board is flat); electrons (hands are not electrons); charges and ions (no sodium chloride); extended structures (no diamond); energy, reaction conditions and speed; atoms are not coloured or textured like the substance.
- **Conservation:** the tally of each bead kind is the same before and after, and the tray empties only when every bead is used.

## A first game's ladder: Bond Beads

Working ID `bond-beads`, **not registered**. The decision is **which links to break and which to make** so the requested shapes use every bead. Overlap checked against Island Bridges (counts on a grid, single solution, grown-up), Mix & Make (recipes) and Machine Line (programs): only Bond Beads has free placement, rearrangement and conservation.

**Interaction.** Beads at least 100 units across. Drag from one bead to another to link one free hand on each: the line snaps with a soft pentatonic click. Tap a link to break it (a pop; free). Beads drag freely to tidy. **Undo**, **Start again** and **Check** buttons at least 100 units. A tally strip shows *before* and *now* as rows of small bead pictures per kind (numerals beside them from U). A request card pictures the target shapes. **Bead counts capped at 6 for S, 7 for U and 9 for M** so one board fits at 100 units in portrait with the pet clear; the definition audits this with `view` in both orientations, and if 9 does not fit, M cases drop to 7 or move to the grown-up seas.

| Level | Band | Board / request | What she decides | Misses |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | school | 3–4 loose 1- and 2-hand beads. "Hold every hand." Any valid shape finishes | Which hands to join | None: free building; every hand held ends the round |
| 2 | school | 4–5 loose beads (adds a 4-hand bead) and a pictured shape | Copy the arrangement | A Check that doesn't match counts one miss |
| 3 | school (U) | Two built shapes and a request for two different ones from the same beads; the tray must empty | What to break, what to rebuild | Same; a leftover bead is shown, not scolded |
| 4 | school (U) | One set of beads, two requested shapes that use them differently; either order accepted | Rearrangement, not addition | Same |
| 5 | school top (M) | Named kit: build water, then ammonia, then methane from small trays; the word "atom" now, and the model-limits line on the how-to card | Composition | Same; free builds before Check never count |
| 6 | school top (M) | Double holds: oxygen and carbon dioxide | When two links join the same pair | Same |
| 7 | school top (M) | **Reaction as rearrangement:** hydrogen and oxygen molecules become two waters; the tally proves every atom is still there | Break, then rebuild; conservation | Same |
| 8 | school top (M) | A larger rearrangement (methane and oxygen become carbon dioxide and water, 9 beads, if the layout fits); the "before / after" pair is kept as the result | Planning which links to break first | Same. No flame or "burning" art; the voice says "the atoms rearranged" |

**Accounting and help.** Building, breaking, undo and tidying are exploration and never count. Only a **Check** that doesn't match the request counts, once per Check. After a miss a spoken hint names one fact ("This red bead has a hand free."); after two, the bead with a free hand, or the link to break, glows; a supported finish then plays the remaining links as a ghost and the round completes with its sticker. Assisted completion is not mastery evidence.

**Rule tests a definition must name** (by enumeration): every authored request is solvable from its tray using every bead; every accepted build has every hand held and is connected; matching is independent of drawing (canonical graph form); each named molecule matches the reviewed list exactly; before and after multisets are equal at every level; all alternative solutions are accepted; every hint points to a bead or link that some solution changes. These prove toy consistency, not chemistry; the molecule list and wording need a chemistry educator.

**Cost.** One M game across three build slices; no new engine, physics or persistence beyond journal entries.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is level 1's drag from one bead to another; touch-down lifts and brightens the bead at once. The ghost finger is feasible: the same drag and tap gestures, with links supplied by the solver. Replay variety comes from seeded trays drawn from an authored pool per level, with requests constant within a level. Sounds: a click on link, a pop on break, a chime when a molecule is recognized; the island's loop plays. All instructions go through `ctx.instruct`; naming is spoken, and letters on beads are labels, not required reading. The pet watches from the bottom left. Off-screen: the conversation card "What changed? What stayed the same?" (any model-kit or kitchen card waits for the reviewer).
