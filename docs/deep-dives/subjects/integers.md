# Integers: below zero

**Subject:** `integers` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M and G, as defined in the [subject tracker](../subjects.md#the-audiences) · **Requested:** 2026-10-16 by the [standards audit](standards-audit.md) (the tracker's mathematics rows) · **Researched:** 2026-10-22 · **Session:** `claude/subject-integers`, [PR #785](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/785).

**Research and proposals only. Nothing built.** This study owns numbers **below zero**: a zero that means something, above and below it, order across it, distance across it, opposites, and opposite moves that cancel. It leaves alone its neighbours. The unstarted `oceans` study owns real depth zones and sea animals, so every depth here is fictional. The unstarted `area-volume-space` study owns the coordinate grid, so four quadrants (6.NS.C.6.b, 6.NS.C.8) are left to it. Money stays inside a round everywhere on the island, so owing is left out below the grown-ups.

**Recommendation.** Define **Sea Lift** levels 1–3 first: a new game in which she runs a glass lift on a pier that rises into the sky and sinks below the water, taking island friends to their stops. The water line is zero, and it is the one context here whose zero means the same thing to every family (see [the meaning of zero](#the-meaning-of-zero-stated-once)). The lift and its stops are drawn to scale on a **vertical** line, so the documented mistake (thinking −5 is above −2) is visibly wrong: the friend she skipped is still deeper than the lift. Sea Lift is a new identity rather than Frog Hop's G3 *Below zero*, because its action (sending a lift to a friend's stop, and comparing where friends are) and its picture (a line with a meaningful zero, standing up) differ from hopping a frog along a line to do arithmetic. Decision 1 asks whether Frog Hop's G3 sketch then retires.

## What people can do, and what remains uncertain

- **Sourced (abstract):** [Bofferding and Hoffman 2019, *Children's integer understanding and the effects of linear board games*](https://cadrek12.org/resources/childrens-integer-understanding-and-effects-linear-board-games-look-two-measures) (Journal of Mathematical Behavior; abstract through CADRE, the paper not opened). Kindergarteners (45) and first graders (48) played a numerical linear board game that included negative integers; order and value-comparison tasks showed the game group's improvement, especially for children who began with a whole-number mental number line, while a number-placement task mostly showed whether children used the endpoints. The authors recommend "better incorporation of integer values in linear board games". **This is the evidence for a younger rung**: order with negatives is learnable by 5–7-year-olds in a game. It is also a caution: placing a number on a line is a weak test of understanding; *comparing* is the better one, which is what Sea Lift asks.
- **Sourced (abstract, via search only):** Bofferding 2014, *Negative integer understanding: characterizing first graders' mental models* (JRME 45(2), 194–245; the NCTM page returned 402). Sixty-one first graders first reasoned with whole-number ideas, and a significant number later developed formal mental models after instruction.
- **Sourced (opened):** [Gullick and Wolford 2013, *Understanding less than nothing*](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/psychology/articles/10.3389/fpsyg.2013.00584/full) (Frontiers in Psychology). Fifth graders (about 10–11) showed "some ability to sequence and work with negative numbers even before formal school experience"; negatives are introduced "between fourth" and sixth grade; children may "order the negative end of the number line backwards" (citing Widjaja and colleagues); and, citing Varma and Schwartz, sixth graders after two years of instruction compare negatives as adults do. Its account of Borba and Nunes: after a short introduction to the minus sign's meanings, children used negatives but explained them idiosyncratically and preferred speech to manipulatives.
- **Sourced (search summary only):** the Absolute Blast! study (Millenium, 2023; the journal returned 503): 29 Portuguese sixth graders; the board-game group started weaker on integer addition and caught up with a puzzle-task group over three sessions, and talked through its reasoning more.
- **The documented mistake** is ordering the negative end backwards (−5 "bigger" than −2, because 5 is bigger than 2), and its cousin, reading the magnitude and dropping the sign. Sea Lift's design answer is that order is *height*: the lift and the friends are drawn at their stops, so "lower" is a thing she can see, not a rule she has to remember.
- **Not established:** that a lift game builds integer understanding that transfers to temperature, money or algebra. A correct stop proves a correct stop inside the toy. How the iPad's speech says "−3" (minus, negative, or a dash read aloud) is unknown and a device check.

## The meaning of zero, stated once

6.NS.C.5 asks for "the meaning of 0 in each situation". Three of the tracker's contexts have a zero that changes with the family:

- **Temperature.** 0 °C is freezing; 0 °F is far below it. A US family and the family languages (Portuguese, French, Spanish, Arabic) would read the same thermometer differently. Kept out of the first game; decision 3.
- **Floors.** In the US the ground floor is 1 and there is no floor 0; in much of Europe the ground floor is 0 and the first basement −1. A lift that numbers building floors teaches one convention as the rule. Sea Lift's stops are **stops above and below the water**, never floors of a building.
- **Owing and having.** Debt is real money and real worry; the island keeps money inside a round. Out of every child band; a grown-up puzzle may use it (decision 5).

**Sea level** is the one zero every family shares, it is already the island's own water line, and the standard names it. Depths and heights are fictional ("three stops below the water"), never metres, feet or real sea zones.

## What fits

- **Fits as a new game: Sea Lift.** The action (send a lift to a friend's stop, pick friends up in order, find a twin stop, bring the lift home) exists nowhere on the island. The learning is specific and visible: *the water is zero; lower is less even when the number looks bigger; how far from the water is not the same as how high*.
- **Fits elsewhere:** a family conversation card (where's zero?) and a taped-line floor game on the grown-ups' page; a grown-up delivery puzzle (Lift Rounds) after the Lantern Lake capacity test.
- **Does not fit:** sign-rule drills ("a minus times a minus"); written expressions with brackets; temperature numbers in Weather Wardrobe; a negative purse, overdraft or debt in a child's game; real ocean depths or animals' real depths (`oceans`); four quadrants (`area-volume-space`); timed rides.

## A first game's ladder: Sea Lift

Working ID `sea-lift`, registered 2026-10-22 with levels 1–3. The decision is **which stop** (or, from level 4, which number card), read from where friends are on a line whose zero is the water.

**Interaction.** A tall glass lift on a pier at the right of the screen, its shaft a vertical line of stops from +5 (sky, cliff ledge) to −5 (under the water), spacing at least 100 logical units in landscape and the line scrolling a little in portrait so no target shrinks (a layout audit in both orientations is part of the definition). The water line crosses the shaft at 0, drawn as the island's own sea. Each stop has a large button-shaped landing (at least 100 units) with its numeral; stops below the water carry the minus sign **and** a bubble mark, so the sign is never the only cue. Friends wait on landings (island critters above; fictional sea friends below, from the existing animal art). Tap a landing: touch-down squashes it at once, the lift glides there stop by stop with a soft pentatonic tick per stop (rising going up, falling going down; a splash sound as it passes the water), and the friend says where they are. The pet keeps the bottom left; the friends' requests appear as a speech bubble with an arrow and dots.

| Level | Band | First round | What she decides | Misses and help |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 *Up or down?* | school (pre-K after a person check, decision 4) | The lift waits at the water. A gull says "Take me three up!", a crab "Three below the water!" (arrow and three dots in the bubble). Requests 1–5 either way | Which stop is that far from the water, which way | A wrong stop: the lift goes there, the friend counts the stops from the water aloud ("one, two below; I need three below"), one miss. Two misses glow the right landing |
| 2 *Through the water* | school | The lift starts at a friend's stop above the water (+1 to +4); "Five down, please": it ends below the water. Mixed with rides that start below and go up past it | Where a ride that crosses zero ends (count back through 0) | As 1; the hint replays the ride with the stops counted aloud, pausing at the water ("zero: the water") |
| 3 *Deepest first* | school (U) | Three friends wait, at least two below the water (e.g. −5, −2, +3). "Pick up the deepest friend first," then the next deepest. Some rounds ask "highest first" | Order across zero; −5 is lower than −2 | Tapping −2 before −5 sends the lift there, and the friend at −5 is visibly still **under** the lift; the crab says "I'm deeper, down here!" One miss; two glow the next friend |
| 4 *How far?* | school (U) | Friends at −3 and +4. "How many stops from the crab to the gull?" Three big number cards (7, 1, 4) | The interval across 0 (England Year 6) | A wrong card (1 is the 4 − 3 mistake) plays the ride with stops counted, the water named halfway ("three to the water, four more"); one miss |
| 5 *Twin stops* | school (U) | A gull 4 above the water: "My twin lives just as far below." Tap the twin's stop. Some rounds the twin is above; one case is a friend *at* the water ("my twin is right here") | Opposites (6.NS.C.6.a); zero is its own opposite | A wrong stop names its distance from the water against the gull's; two misses draw two equal brackets from the water |
| 6 *Lower, or closer to the water?* | school top (M) | Two friends at −6 and +2. Alternating questions: "Who is lower?" then "Who is closer to the water?" | Order versus absolute value (6.NS.C.7.c–d) | A wrong friend shows both brackets from the water (distance) or the height difference (order), whichever the question asked |
| 7 *Back to the water* | school top (M) | Ride cards: the lift rides +5 then −3; then three cards, "which ride brings it home to the water?" (−2, +2, −8). Later rounds: "where does it end?" for two rides | Opposite moves combine to 0; p + q as a distance from p (7.NS.A.1.a–b) | A wrong card plays its ride and stops where it really ends; one miss |

**Accounting and help.** Only a wrong stop or card counts, once per answer; watching a ride replay, the counting voice and tapping a friend to hear where they are never count. After two misses the right landing or card glows; a supported finish then sends the lift by itself and the round completes with its sticker. A round is five rides at levels 1–2, three pick-up orders at 3, and five questions at 4–7. Levels 1–3 never show an expression; 4 and 7 show only the number cards; no equals sign at any level.

**Rule tests a definition must name** (by enumeration over every authored or seeded case): every requested stop is on the shaft; every level-2 ride crosses zero; every level-3 case has at least two friends below the water whose numerals' magnitudes order the opposite way from their heights; every level-4 card set holds the right interval, the "difference of magnitudes" mistake and one other, with exactly one correct; every level-5 twin is the opposite and in range; every level-6 pair has a different answer for *lower* and for *closer to the water*; every level-7 case has exactly one card returning to 0; each hint points at the right landing or card.

**Speech.** Every line is in `voice-script.ts` with the words spelled out; whether "−3" is spoken as "three below the water", "minus three" or "negative three" is decision 6 and a device check (the iPad's speech must never read a bare sign). Levels 1–3 lean on "below the water"; the mathematical name joins from level 4 if the decision allows.

**Cost.** One M game across three build slices; no new engine, physics or persistence beyond journal entries if the rework takes them. A ghost-finger bot taps the solver's landing or card.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is level 1's tap on a landing; touch-down squashes it and the lift starts moving before the friend speaks. Replay variety comes from seeded friends, stops and directions within each level's authored ranges, from the cast of friends, and from day, dusk and night skies over the same sea. The sounds are the pentatonic tick per stop and a splash at the water, so a ride across zero has its own sound. An off-screen activity: tape a line on the floor with a zero in the middle and take turns calling "two below" and jumping there.

## Fit with the creature-world rework

The friends at the stops can be her creatures: a sky friend, a cliff friend, a shore friend and deep friends, each meeting filing a journal entry for what a round actually showed. The lift's habitats (sky, cliff, shore, shallows, deep) dress Bubble Beach, as the [habitat kits](zoo-animals.md#habitat-kits-order-lands-and-cast-swaps) prefer dressing existing lands over a new one. Finishing a level never takes a friend away or makes one wait; nothing accrues. The [rework brief](../../rework/README.md) decides whether stop-friends join a collection.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Sea Lift as a new game, or Frog Hop's G3?** Lean new game: a vertical line with a meaningful zero, comparing friends' positions, is a different action from hopping to an arithmetic answer, and Frog Hop's whole-number ladder stays whole. G3 then retires, or stays as later Frog Hop arithmetic once Sea Lift lands.
2. **Where levels 6–7 live** until a band exists: `school`'s top levels (lean) or 13+ only.
3. **Temperature's zero.** Lean: no numbers below zero on any thermometer until the family's unit (°C, °F or both) is chosen; Weather Wardrobe keeps its picture thermometer (its dive's decision 3 answered: the integers study does not ask for numbers there).
4. **A pre-K window at level 1**, after a person watches a five-year-old with a grown-up. Default: school only.
5. **Owing.** Lean: no debt in any child band; Lift Rounds uses rides, not money. Allow an owing-and-having puzzle at 13+ only if the grown-ups want it.
6. **Words for below zero.** "Three below the water" at levels 1–3, then "negative three" (US school word) or "minus three" (England's word) from level 4; check the device's speech for each.
7. **Land:** Bubble Beach (nine games; the sea is its feel) or Counting Cove (twelve). Lean Bubble Beach, after the `landLayout` capacity test.
8. **Reviewer:** a mathematics educator for the level-4 card distractors and the level-6/7 wording before they are defined.
