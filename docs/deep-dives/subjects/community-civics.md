# Community and civics: helpers, rules, votes and shared things in a village

**Subject:** `community-civics` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M, G · **Date:** 2026-10-17 · **Session:** `claude/subject-community-civics` · [PR #556](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/556).

This is research and proposals only, from [roadmap chunk 28](../../ROADMAP.md#chunks) and the [subject procedure](../subjects.md#the-procedure-one-session-one-subject). No reference game was installed or played. No game, level, band, land, save field or parent card was built.

The tracker row asks for community helpers, jobs, rules, voting, how a community runs and local history. The [money study](money-economy.md#on-the-island-today-and-overlap-decisions) routed jobs, shared costs, taxes and public goods here, so this study takes those too. Local history is shared with `time-change` and `daily-life-long-ago`; this study claims only the village's own *then and now* and sends the rest back.

**Recommendation:** define **Village Vote** first. The creatures of Cozy Village decide together what to build on the square: each friend stands by the picture it wants, the child counts the paws, the bigger group's choice is built, and everyone uses it. The ladder grows from counting two groups (preschool) to a three-way vote with a run-off (school), a fairness rung where the losers of a vote cannot use what won until a fix is added (upper school), and chipping in shells for the shared thing (school top). Majority rule becomes an action the child performs, not a word; the island has no game whose action is a group decision. For grown-ups and teens, **Count the Ballots** is a cozy tally puzzle in which the same ballots elect different winners under two counting rules.

## On the island today and overlap decisions

- **Cozy Village is already the community.** Hazel the squirrel hosts it; Mail Carrier, Market Stall, Scoop Shop, Teddy Doctor, Lemonade Stand, Clock Tower and Stop and Go are its jobs and rules. The tracker guessed Storybook Square; that zone is proposed and library-shaped. The square's vote belongs beside Hazel's post office. Cozy Village holds twelve games, so a thirteenth needs the `landLayout` capacity test at every band and view before placement (the money study's Swap Meet has the same condition); the fallback is Maker Harbor or Storybook Square when either exists.
- **Little Helpers** owns quantities (how many helpers lift a fruit) and its [dive](../little-helpers.md#proposed-ladder) proposes levels 7–9. A "jobs" mode there would change its action; nothing is proposed for it.
- **Mail Carrier** owns delivering to the right neighbor through a picture key (levels 6–8). *Who can help?* keeps that action exactly and changes what the letter shows; it is a level, not a game. Mail Carrier has no deep dive yet; the definition goes in one.
- **Market Stall** owns coins on a mat; its [dive](../market-stall.md#new-levels-on-top) proposes 8–13 including 12 *Can I buy it?* The money study's *Pick one* is opportunity cost. *Chip in* reuses the mat's tap-a-coin action for a shared purchase but belongs to Village Vote's story (vote, then pay for what won), so it is proposed as Village Vote 6 with a note that the mat code is the same.
- **Lemonade Stand** owns earning by selling. Jobs and pay types (Earning 4-1/4-3, routed here) do not become a game: a wage is a number without a decision for a child. Jobs appear as *roles the helpers hold*, not as income.
- **Stop and Go** owns following a traffic rule; **Pet Says** owns following and resisting instructions; **Rule Parade** (notebook) owns reading a rule tile and testing who crosses. The civics case for rules goes to Rule Parade, as one case, not a new game.
- **Two Viewpoints, Common Ground and A Place for You** (social-emotional) own perspective and negotiation. Village Vote never asks the child to persuade or to say how a friend feels; it counts.
- **Timeline Trail and Attic Finds** own then and now. One village case ("the square long ago had a well; the villagers chose a fountain") is routed to `time-change` as content.
- **Swap Meet, Harbor Orders** (money) own trade and production. **Village Planner** (routines) owns scheduling jobs with one cart; it is upper-school logistics, not civics, and stays there.
- **No mayor.** Animal Crossing's mayor and iCivics' county official put one player in charge. The island's rules say nothing gates and the pet never commands; the village decides together and Hazel only reads out the result. This is also why no "approval rating" exists anywhere below.

## What fits

- **A decision whose result is built and used.** The rework brief wants the educational action to cause the visible result. A vote that places a swing on the square, which the creatures then swing on, is that loop in one round.
- **Counting is the preschool civic act.** Village Vote 1–3 is counting two or three groups and comparing; the voice counts along. The island's rule that mistakes teach applies cleanly: naming the smaller group as the winner is a miss, and voting is never a miss.
- **Roles by tool and action.** *Who can help?* and *Which door?* show a wrench, a stethoscope, a watering can, a letter bag; the job word is spoken, never required.
- **Rules as a fix with a side effect.** The civics case for Rule Parade asks for a rule that clears a jam without stopping play; "nobody on the slide" clears the jam and the parade sits down sadly. That is D2.Civ.12.3-5 and 13.6-8 as a test, not a lecture.
- **Shared cost without free-rider shame.** In *Chip in* the jar fills from six purses and the child moves the shells; a friend with one shell gives one, and the voice says the jar is full, not who gave least.

What does not fit, and why:

- **Elections with winners who rule.** No mayor, president, campaign, approval rating or term. The village has no leader to elect.
- **Votes about facts or feelings.** Preschoolers side with a majority on what is true (Corriveau and Harris); the island votes only on what to build or play.
- **Police, courts, punishment and emergencies.** KS1 names police officers and road safety; the `safety` tracker row needs a reviewer and this study does not pre-empt it. Helpers here are a plumber, a doctor, a gardener, a postkeeper, a lifeguard at the pond and a baker. Whether a fire creature appears is a decision below.
- **Taxes as a word.** *Chip in* is a shared cost the child can see; "tax" is a grown-up card word at most. Real money amounts, allowances and brands stay out (money study, decision 5).
- **Timers, re-election, budgets that fail, purchasable locations.** Out by the island's rules.

## First game's proposed ladder: Village Vote

Cozy Village's square has a notice board. Hazel reads the question; the friends walk to the picture they want and raise a paw. The child is the counter. Nothing is timed, nothing is lost: the thing that wins is built on the square and everyone, including the friends who wanted the other thing, plays on it.

| Level | Name | Band window | First round | What she decides | Mistakes and hints | A rule test asserts |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | **Two choices** | preschool, pre-K | A slide and a swing on the board; four friends walk to one or the other (3 and 1 at first); tapping a raised paw counts it aloud ("one, two, three"); tap the picture with more paws | Which group is bigger | Tapping the smaller picture: a boing and "Let's count again", with the count-along replayed; after two misses the bigger group glows. Tapping paws is free | Groups never tie; the winner is the larger group; the hint names the larger group |
| 2 | **Your pet votes too** | preschool, pre-K | Hazel asks the pet what it wants; the child taps a picture and the pet walks there with a paw up; then she counts | What her pet wants (free, never wrong), then which group is bigger | As level 1. When her side loses: "More friends wanted the swing. Let's all try it!" and the pet swings first | Her vote is counted; either side can win over the seed; no round is a loss |
| 3 | **Three choices** | pre-K, school | Three pictures, seven friends in a split with one clear top (such as 4, 2, 1); a tally board shows a mark per counted paw and the numeral | Which of three is biggest, reading the tally | A wrong picture: the voice compares the two tallies ("4 is more than 2"); glow after two | A unique maximum; the tally equals the count; the comparison line is true |
| 4 | **A tie** | school | Three pictures, eight friends, 3–3–2. "It's a tie! The slide had fewest. Where would those two friends go next?" Each of the two holds a small second-choice picture; she taps each friend and it walks to its second choice; she counts again and taps the winner | Where the dropped friends go, then the new winner | Sending a friend to the wrong second choice: it shakes its head and shows the picture; declaring before moving both: a nudge. Declaring the wrong winner: a miss as before | The seed ties the top two and never the run-off; moving both dropped friends always resolves it; the result equals an instant run-off |
| 5 | **Everyone can use it** | school top (U) | The tall slide won 5–3. The three who voted against are the smallest friends, and when the slide is built they stand at the bottom unable to climb. "Who can't use it?" Tap them. Then two fix cards: a step, or a lower slide. Either is accepted; the fix is built and all eight play | Who is left out, then which fix | Tapping a friend who is using the slide: "She's at the top!"; after two misses the stranded friends glow. The fix is a free choice; the voice says what each would have cost | The excluded set is exactly the friends whose size exceeds the slide's reach; every fix card makes the set empty |
| 6 | **Chip in** | school top (U) | The chosen thing costs 12 shells; six friends' purses (2 each at first); she taps shells from purses into the jar until it is full; everyone cheers | How to fill the jar; later, who gives what when one purse holds 1 and another 3 | A jar that stays short: "Still 2 to go"; no miss while shells remain. Taking a shell from a friend who has none is impossible (the purse is empty, not wrong). Any filling plan is accepted; the voice names it ("everyone gave the same", "some gave more") | Total purses ≥ cost in every seed; at least one equal-shares plan at the first round and at least two distinct plans at the second; nothing is said about who gave least |

**Windows (lean).** Preschool 1–2, pre-K 1–3, school 3–6. `order` is `min..max`. Levels 5 and 6 are upper-school by content; they live at `school`'s top as the band rule requires. Misses are counted only on declaring a winner, sending a run-off friend wrong or tapping a friend who is not stranded; voting, counting paws and choosing a fix are free.

**Cost.** A new game of effort M: a notice board, six to eight critters with a raised-paw pose (`attach()` and moods exist), four buildable square things drawn with `Graphics`, a tally board, Market Stall's coin-tap for level 6, voice lines, a how-to card and a ghost-finger bot that counts and taps. A seeded generator in `logic.ts` picks crowd sizes, the pictures and second choices.

### Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** tap a raised paw and hear "one"; the paw bounces and a mark appears on the board. A child who taps every paw has already done the round's work.
- **Touch-down feedback:** paws bounce and chime on touch; a picture on the board lifts; the jar's shells clink.
- **Replay variety:** the seed chooses the pictures (slide, swing, sandpit, fountain, bench, seesaw), the crowd and who wants what; at level 2 her own pick changes the outcome.
- **Off-screen activity:** the family meeting card: tonight's choice (which story, which walk) by a show of hands, counted aloud by the child.
- **Co-play:** a grown-up counts while she taps, then they swap; or the grown-up is the latecomer whose vote she asks for at level 4.
- **Portrait and landscape:** the board at the top, the two or three picture spots spread with `spread()`, the crowd in the lower half clear of the pet's corner; eight friends at 100 units need the layout test before any art.

## Count the Ballots (G and M), in brief

A cozy Lantern Lake puzzle: nine ballots, three pictured candidates (which ride to put on the fairground: a wheel, a carousel, a boat swing), each ballot a column of first, second and third choice pictures. Tap a ballot into the pile of its first choice; when no pile holds five, the smallest pile dims and its ballots are moved to their next choice still standing; repeat until a pile holds a majority. Level 2 asks, after the run-off winner is found, "Count only first choices: who wins?" and shows the plurality winner beside it; the deal is seeded so the two differ (a rule test asserts it, and that every deal reaches a majority without a tie at the elimination step). Level 3 adds a fourth candidate and twelve ballots. No clock, no score beyond the stickers Lantern Lake already gives; targets at 64 logical units with scrolling if a deal cannot fit. A 12-year-old reaches it on `school`'s top through the lake's signpost. The decision below asks whether this belongs in Lantern Lake (12 games today) or waits for capacity.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Where Village Vote lives.** Cozy Village is right by theme and has Hazel, but holds twelve games; the capacity test decides. Fallback: Maker Harbor or Storybook Square once either exists.
2. **May the pet's choice lose?** The ladder says yes from level 2, cheerfully, because losing a vote and then using the winner is the lesson. A grown-up might prefer the pet always wins at preschool; the study recommends against it.
3. **Count the Ballots' home.** Lantern Lake (grown-up) with the signpost for 11–12, or a school-top registration in Puzzle Peaks; the study recommends Lantern Lake.
4. **Equal shares or bigger purses give more at level 6.** Both are accepted and named; whether the voice ever calls one "fair" is a values call. The study recommends naming without judging.
5. **Which helpers appear.** Plumber, doctor, gardener, postkeeper, lifeguard, baker are proposed. A fire creature is plausible and KS1 names police officers; both border the `safety` row, which needs a reviewer. The study proposes no police and leaves fire to this decision.
6. **A persistent square.** Whether voted things stay on the square between rounds (a bounded list in the save) is a rework question; the first slice needs nothing persistent.
7. **The family meeting card's reviewer.** It is a conversation prompt, not advice, but it is printed text a parent will follow; name who reads it before it ships.
