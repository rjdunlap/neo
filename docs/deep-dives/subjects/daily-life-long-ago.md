# Everyday life long ago: objects, work, homes and play

**Subject:** `daily-life-long-ago` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M, G ([definitions](../subjects.md#the-audiences)) · **Date:** 2026-10-16 · **Session:** `codex/daily-life-long-ago-subject-research`, [PR #477](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/477).

Research and proposals only. **Define Attic Finds first**, developing the existing [time-and-change proposal](time-change.md#attic-finds-a-bounded-follow-up): inspect an unfamiliar object, try its moving part, and watch what it does. Its deeper question is *what evidence supports that use?* Real history follows through small, located object cases, never an undifferentiated “olden days.” Youngest play is object exploration, not a claim that an infant understands history. Older play adds context, continuity, conflicting accounts and honest uncertainty. No runtime band, zone, game or system is added here.

### Material culture: sources and boundaries

- **[NPS, What Is That Used For?](https://www.nps.gov/teachers/classrooms/what-is-that-used-for.htm)** supplies an artifact investigation using household objects from the 1800s associated with Herbert Hoover sites. Its useful move is comparing function with a familiar object. Its classroom worksheet and timed lesson are not our interaction. A particular US collection cannot stand for all households or countries.
- **[V&A, Toys and games from the past](https://media.vam.ac.uk/media/documents/legacy_documents/file_upload/17580_file.pdf)** compares materials, mechanisms and continuing forms of play. It is a **legacy** museum guide: do not reuse its gallery directions or broad claims about gender, class or game origins as a reviewed script. Borrow observation and comparison; re-source each historical claim before implementation.
- **[Te Papa, Growing up: toys, treasures and TV](https://www.tepapa.govt.nz/digital-museum/explore-digital-museum/growing-toys-treasures-and-tv)** presents a situated collection of childhood objects in Aotearoa New Zealand, including toys collected across 1940–2000s. One collection is not every child's experience.
- **[Te Papa, Pipi Mā dolls, ME024201](https://collections.tepapa.govt.nz/object/1605770)** records Punarau Media's 2016 talking dolls and their language-revitalization context. **Design inference:** an object associated with a living culture can be contemporary and technological. Do not turn Māori objects into a “long ago” bin. This is context for future review, not permission to reproduce designs, language or voices; creator/community consultation would precede such a case.
- **Access gap:** a Smithsonian [washboard record](https://www.americanhistory.si.edu/collections/object/nmah_2044691) returned an error. No object-level claim or date below relies on its search excerpt. Broader coverage of homes, food and textiles outside these collections remains research to do.

Real cases need a source packet recording object ID, maker where known, place, date/range, use, source uncertainty, who is represented and missing, and a historian/educator's review. Use community-authored sources and a reviewer from the community for living or Indigenous cultural content. Do not invent a universal technological sequence. No imported collection photographs or sounds: any eventual activity uses original Pixi drawings and synthesized sound, with attribution and rights reviewed separately.

## What people can do: an answer for every audience

These are invitations to try, not developmental cutoffs. Instructions are spoken and replayable; the game never needs a child to explain aloud or read.

| Audience | First independent action | Deeper decision and limit |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **L**, under 2 | Tap the large lid; a toy rises and moves immediately | Reopen it to make the same event happen. A grown-up names the motion. No history objective or scored identification |
| **T**, about 2 | Tap a large handle to make an invented toy bird bob | Try another visible moving part and see what changes. Not an “old or new?” task; no inferred date |
| **P**, about 3 | Inspect an invented object and try two pictured uses | Match a visible result to a need, such as carrying water. The pet demonstrates unfamiliar actions; all predictions are free |
| **K**, about 4–5 | Use one clue to choose how a fictional object works | Compare two tools meeting the same need; accept more than one workable answer. Real historical identification remains a later reviewed packet |
| **S**, about 6–8 | Choose the pictured clue supporting a proposed use | Compare a located past/current pair, noting one change and one continuity; labels are spoken. Familiarity with a tool is never assumed |
| **U**, about 9–12 | Reconstruct a daily task from two complementary records | Spot what a surviving object cannot tell us; choose another source that would help. Top `school` cases, without a new band |
| **M**, about 11–14 | Curate a tiny exhibit whose claims fit the sources | Repair an overconfident caption or request missing evidence. Hypothesis for voluntary appeal: a mystery with several defensible interpretations and player-chosen investigation order. Ages 11–12 use `school` top levels; 13+ use `grownup` if registered there; no claim of a new band |
| **G: own play** | Open a **Household Casebook** and connect object, account and pictured use | Resolve three bounded claims with a constraint solver accepting every supported interpretation; this is an Attic Finds case mode, not another Island Archive timeline |
| **G: co-play** | One person operates the object; the other chooses a clue to inspect | Swap investigator/curator roles after each object. All evidence remains visible to both, with a complete solo path; no seat-credit system required |
| **G: page tool** | Optional **One Object, Two Stories** plain-HTML prompt | Ask a willing adult how a familiar object was used, then distinguish memory from inference. An inclusive reviewed card, no uploads, recordings, family-tree requirement or activity tracking |

## First game's proposed ladder: Attic Finds

A new activity is justified only if inspection and experimentation are central. A choose-one-picture quiz alone fails this test. IDs and ranges below are **planning labels**, not saved level assignments.

| Step / audience | First round and new decision | Ending, support and meaningful rule check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **A: Lid and Lever** · L–T | One giant crank toy. Tap the handle; its rod raises the bird, a second tap lowers it. Later reveal the rod behind a lid | After three voluntary activations, show a large Finish picture while free play continues. Any taps welcomed, misses/hints 0. Future check: finish can always be reached; cancel/exit leaves no stuck motion |
| **1: Try a use** · P–K | Inspect a fictional crank toy's handle, rod and bird. Two big pictured experiments: make the bird bob or make it travel along a path. Try either, see the actual effect, then choose the shown action | Two objects in a round. Wrong predictions never count; pet names the observed effect. Both action cards stay retryable. Check: every object's effect is visible and matches its authored model |
| **2: Show the clue** · K–S | Same toy, now choose a clue supporting “the crank makes the bird bob.” Linked rod versus unrelated painted star; trial remains available | Two objects, each with a supported clue connection. An unsupported submitted link gets an explanation and one miss; after two misses on that claim, one counted hint glows the rod. Check: every accepted clue entails the stated claim within the model |
| **3: Same need** · K–S | Carry a pictured dry leaf bundle with either a handled basket or a fictional tray. Both work; inspect the grip and support | Compare one change and one shared job, with picture tokens. Never penalize choosing either working object. Check: enumerate **all** tools satisfying the shown need; no hidden preferred answer |
| **4: Context changes the answer** · S–U | Two island task scenes: carry dry leaves / carry loose water. The open basket works in the first, not the second; a watertight cup works in both at this toy scale | Two requests; one Check per evidence link, free trials/editing. Hint points to holes and shows the water consequence. Check: constraints come from the scene, not age/style/color. This is functional reasoning until real cases are reviewed |
| **5: Two sources** · U | A fictional storeroom object has an inventory picture and a drawing of use. Match which claim each supports: presence in a place versus observed action | Two claims; source replay is free. An inventory cannot prove that every family used the object. Check: source-purpose table accepts every sufficient combination and rejects unjustified universals |
| **6: What can we tell?** · U–G | One object record supports presence in one island home; a second home has no surviving record. Choose “unknown” rather than “absent” for the second | One case, three claims, including an unknown. Inspect all clues and revise freely. Check: removing evidence enlarges the consistent possibilities; absence of a record never becomes proof of absence |
| **7: Mend the exhibit** · M–G | A shop advert calls a fictional tool “everyone's favorite”; a household picture shows one use. Repair the caption to “advertised for…” / “shown in this home…” and choose a source that could answer a remaining question | Three repaired claims and one useful next-source selection finish. Captions are pictures with replayable speech, not required typed prose. Check: all supported captions accepted; distinguish promotional claims from independent evidence |

**Tentative windows:** lap/toddler A; preschool 1; pre-K 1–3; school 2–7; grown-up 6–7. `school`'s older cases are selected by level, not a new U/M runtime band. Preschool, grown-up registration and exact ranges require definition; never silently add A below a saved level. If appended later, use the existing `order` contract. A first implementation can be P/K only, followed by younger and older slices; every band need not launch together.

**First-round packet to define:** one fictional crank toy shown from two authored sides, two inspection controls, three clue areas presented in one enlarged detail window, two use trials, the visible rod/bird response, two seeded placements, and a second object using the same motion with different decoration. No free camera rotation, general physics or reusable museum engine. Inspecting the back uses a broad Turn button, not a precision gesture. Each action gives immediate feedback; the causal animation can be replayed without waiting for a long narration.

**Mistakes and accounting:** predictions, physical trials, selecting an object to inspect, free arrangements, reopening sources, undo and “unknown” when justified are exploration. Only an explicit **evidence Check** in steps 2–7 can count a miss. Empty checks and repeated taps during feedback do nothing. After two failed checks on one claim, glow a useful clue and count one hint for that claim, never again on replay. Requested help likewise counts at most once per claim. Assisted checks remain finishable for one shell-awarded sticker. These rules must be resolved in the definition before adaptation is implemented; observation-only steps cannot be presented as evidence of independent historical mastery.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is touching a conspicuous handle, not answering a question. A lid lifts slightly on touch-down; the rod moves immediately; bird motion and a synthesized wooden click explain the consequence. Use palette colors in **both** past and present scenes: no sepia-equals-old classifier. This is not a music-making game; select an existing compatible island style in the definition.

One large object occupies the center. A single enlarged clue view avoids three tiny hotspots; Turn, Try and Finish controls must each remain at least 100 logical units after scaling, with no pet/corner overlap in portrait. A future ghost finger turns the object, examines a clue, tries one unsupported prediction for free, then follows the same visible controls to finish. Large-pet layouts must repeat instructions when the pet is tapped; otherwise use the corner guide. All speech goes through the voice script and `ctx.instruct`/`ctx.say`.

Replay varies which object is presented, its orientation and clue positions, not the causal rules. Older cases vary relevant source combinations; any decorative changes must leave evidence equally legible. Nothing accrues while away. Round completion uses `ctx.finish`; saving a museum, a new journal taxonomy or furniture collection is outside the first slice. Cleanup must release interactions and scene callbacks.

**Co-play:** operator and clue finder exchange jobs; either can see and use every control. **Off screen, proposed for review:** invite a willing grown-up to tell a story about a familiar, safe object they already use. “I don't remember” is welcome. Do not ask families to handle antiques, dismantle appliances or reproduce historical labor. Family structure, access to heirlooms and willingness to share are never prerequisites.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Approve the fictional scope and younger finish:** crank toys/carriers first; A is cause/effect, not history mastery. Decide whether such rounds should drive adaptive stepping before registering them.
2. **Choose placement and windows:** Story Grove capacity, exact appended levels/ranges and whether the older case also serves `grownup`. Storybook Square stays proposed. No U/M band migration here.
3. **Name a historian/educator for real cases:** object provenance, mechanism, everyday work and who the collection excludes. Fictional models still need clear, internally consistent consequences and comprehensible art.
4. **Select a first located real-object packet, then its community review:** a toy-use comparison is narrower than a tour of global homes/food/clothes. Te Papa's contemporary dolls are a warning against “culture means ancient,” not a ready content license. Additional community-authored research remains open.
5. **Decide the age-specific treatment of labor and harm:** real household histories can involve slavery, coerced labor, colonial extraction and gendered restrictions. Do not recast these as cheerful role-play or imply everyone freely chose a job. Early fictional rounds omit them; older real cases require truthful reviewed context or are deferred. No violence as the fun.
6. **Review the family card separately:** inclusive optional wording and object safety before printing or speaking any practical prompt. No card implementation in this session.
