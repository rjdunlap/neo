# Estimation: guess first, then check, and say what a sensible guess is

*Subject `estimation` · 2026-10-22 · `claude/estimation-research` · [PR #799](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/799). Research and proposals only, for L/T/P/K/S/U/M and grown-ups. Base: `033a79f7`. No game was played or implemented.*

**Recommendation:** the island already asks for a guess in three places, each the same shape: a free, never-marked guess, then evidence, then an ordinary judged answer (Fill and Dump 7 *Which holds more?*, built; Seesaw Balance 15 *Guess the pumpkin* and Duck Pond's *Count the Peekers*, planned). Number-line estimation, the best-evidenced estimation skill, is already Frog Hop level 13 *Where does it go?* in [its dive](../frog-hop.md#new-levels-on-top), waiting on its scoring decision. This study does not re-propose any of those. **Define first, inside Lasso Loops, level 6 *About how many?***: a meadow of 20–39 fireflies, a free guess among three far-apart cards, one jar of ten looped as a benchmark, a chance to change the guess, then the built tens-and-ones loop and count as the check, with the pet comparing the guess to the count aloud. It is the one estimation habit no built or planned rung covers (a benchmark set used to adjust a guess about a collection), it reuses Lasso Loops' built loop, jars of ten and number cards, and it needs no new game, land or developer decision.

## Anchors and evidence limits

Opened 2026-10-22 (Common Core pages through thecorestandards.org). Grades guide content, never access.

| Opened source / codes | What it asks | Design implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [CCSS K.CC.B.5, C.6](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/K/CC/) | Count to answer "how many?" for up to 20 in a line or array and 10 scattered; say whether one group has more, fewer or the same, by matching or counting | **No kindergarten counting standard mentions estimating** (checked on the page). Below school the island's honest rung is *more or fewer*, and "a few or lots", not a number guess |
| [CCSS 2.MD.A.3](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/2/MD/) | Estimate lengths in inches, feet, centimetres and metres | Standard units wait on the measurement study's decision 2; island units (worms, blocks, cups) carry the guess until then |
| [CCSS 3.MD.A.2](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/3/MD/) | Measure **and estimate** liquid volumes and masses in g, kg and l | Seesaw 15 and Fill and Dump 9 (planned) already guess in pretend units |
| [CCSS 3.NBT.A.1](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/3/NBT/), [3.OA.D.8](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/3/OA/) | Round to the nearest 10 or 100; "assess the reasonableness of answers using mental computation and estimation strategies including rounding" | The upper-school rung is *is that answer sensible?*, a check on a computed total, not a guessing game |
| [Standards for Mathematical Practice](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Practice/) MP4, MP5, MP8 | Make "approximations to simplify a complicated situation" (MP4); "detect possible errors by strategically using estimation" (MP5); "evaluate the reasonableness of their intermediate results" (MP8) | Estimation is a habit used *inside* other mathematics. The tracker's MP1 and MP6 codes were not found to mention estimation on the opened page |

**Development.** Number-line estimation: Sasanguie, Verschaffel, Reynvoet and Luwel ([*Psychologica Belgica* 2016](https://psychologicabelgica.com/articles/276), **opened**) summarise Siegler, Thompson and Opfer (2009): children's placements on a 0–100 line shift from compressed (logarithmic) to linear between kindergarten and second grade, on 0–1000 between second and fourth grade, and on 0–100,000 between third and sixth. The interpretation is contested (Matthews and Chesney 2011 produced a similar shift in adults; **search summary**). Siegler and Ramani 2008 ([*Developmental Science* 11(5)](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/18801120/); the page refused, **search summary of the abstract**) report that four 15-minute sessions of a *numbered* linear board game closed low-income preschoolers' gap in numerical estimation, and a colour version did not; the Frog Hop dive already opened Siegler's 2009 board-game paper. Approximate number sense: **search summaries only** report that non-verbal number acuity sharpens from 3 to 6 and keeps improving into adulthood (Halberda and Feigenson 2008), and correlates with earlier maths scores in 14-year-olds (Halberda, Mazzocco and Feigenson 2008, a non-concurrent design a later reviewer questioned). Clements and Sarama's estimation trajectory sits in their *comparing, ordering and estimating* chapter; **its levels were not found or opened**, so no age is claimed for a "number estimator" level.

No opened evidence shows that guessing a jar's count on a tablet improves estimation, that a benchmark ten transfers to new collections, or what teens and parents enjoy. Standards are curriculum evidence; the number-line work supports Frog Hop 13 more than anything below; neither establishes transfer from Puddle Island.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** tap a guess card; any card is welcome and spoken back.
- **Touch-down feedback:** the card dips and chimes on touch, as the built answer cards do; the benchmark jar fills with the built loop sound.
- **Replay variety:** the count 20–39 and the scatter come from the seed; the guess cards follow the count; the comparison line varies with how close she was.
- **The reveal is the reward:** the jars line up beside the sign holding her guess, and the pet says both numbers. Nothing is ranked, and a far guess gets the same warm "Let's see!".
- **Off-screen activity:** the Jar guess card (E7), and the game's existing off-screen line (bundle straws into tens) with "guess first".

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Lasso Loops' window.** Lean: append 6 (and later 7) to school (3–7); no saved level moves. A grown-up can reach either through the how-to arrows.
2. **Level 7's miss rule.** Lean: tapping away a possible guess is one miss with a shown reason, because the range is now the thing being learned; or keep 7 unscored like 6 and judge only the count. Define 7 only after a child has played 6.
3. **Jar Clues' home.** A Lantern Lake puzzle after its capacity test (twelve games today), or a couch-only grown-up game. Lean: Lantern Lake, defined after Weights Case and Box Balance so the grown-up shelf's number puzzles do not repeat.
4. **Person checks to add:** whether a six-year-old changes a guess after seeing the benchmark jar; whether "about 30" is understood when spoken; whether the guess sign stays readable in portrait.

None of these blocks E1's definition: decision 1's lean is the default, and E1 adds no scored guess.
