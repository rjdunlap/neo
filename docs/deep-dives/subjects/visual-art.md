# Visual art: weaving, looking closely, styles and making

*Subject `visual-art` · audiences L, T, P/K, S, U, M, G · 2026-10-17 · subject session on `claude/subject-visual-art` ([PR #547](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/547)).*

**Research and proposals only.** The island already paints, stamps, mixes color, builds pictures from shapes and solves pixel pictures ([Paint Pier](../../GAMES.md#paint-pier-art)), and those games have researched ladders of their own ([Rainbow Fingers](../rainbow-fingers.md), [Stamp Studio](../stamp-studio.md), [Tangram Town](../tangram-town.md), [Puzzle Pals](../puzzle-pals.md)). This study looks for what is still missing. It finds two gaps. **Textiles:** no game, notebook row or study weaves. **Responding to art:** looking closely, giving the evidence for what a picture shows, and noticing how a picture was made. The standards give these as much weight as making, and the island has nothing for them.

It recommends one new identity, **Little Loom**: press a pedal, throw the shuttle, and watch over-and-under turn single threads into cloth. The ladder runs from a lap-and-toddler toy to pattern cards and color-and-weave puzzles for the upper years, and the finished cloth is a natural **child-made pattern** for a creature to wear (the rework brief names child-made patterns in the collection). The second recommendation is a cheap family card, **Picture Talk**: three museum questions to ask about any picture. The third develops the notebook's existing JumpStart seed **Gallery Walk** (hang code-drawn pictures in the portrait, landscape and still-life rooms; sort by style) into a later looking game that also answers "what do you see that makes you say that?" by tapping evidence in original island pictures. Everything else extends a built game or a proposal that already exists. Nothing is built: no game, band, land, registration or save change.

## A first game's ladder: Little Loom

**Fun action.** A wooden loom with big colored warp threads. Press a pedal: half the threads lift with a creak. Throw the shuttle: it zips across, a ribbon of yarn tucks over and under, and a beater thumps it into place. The cloth grows row by row and, at the end, is cut off the loom and becomes a scarf or blanket for a friend. **Learning.** Weaving is interlacing: alternate lifts make cloth that holds; the visible pattern comes from which thread is on top; a plan (the pedal order, the lift cards, the yarn colors) produces a predictable cloth, and revising the plan revises the cloth.

**The model (inspectable, exact).** A cloth is `cols × rows` (at most 8 × 8). Warp colors `warp[c]`, weft colors `weft[r]`, lifts `lift[r][c]` (true means the warp thread is on top). The visible cell is `lift ? warp[c] : weft[r]`. That drawdown is true of a real loom by construction (NPS: harnesses lift threads; plain weave needs two, twill three or more). **Island rule, stated as the island's:** a row holds only if it lifts at least one thread and leaves at least one down, no thread floats over more than three neighbors in a row or column, and a row never repeats the lift of the row just before it (on a real loom the same shed twice lets the new pick pull out at the edge: **recalled, not opened**; the island makes it a simple rule). A row that breaks the rule does not lock in: the ribbon slides loose and the shuttle returns, with a spoken reason ("Every thread went the same way, so the yarn slipped out").

**Layout.** Pedals are separate buttons, never the threads, so the warp can be drawn thin. Pedals are at least 120 across and 100 apart; up to four fit at the bottom right, clear of the pet. Lift-card holes (Loom 6) are at least 100 units apart, so a card has at most six holes and Loom 6 uses six warp threads. Every yarn has a mark (dot, stripe, star, heart, swirl, ring, as Rainbow Fingers' pots) and the palette pairs light and dark yarns, so no target depends on hue alone; a rule test checks value contrast between any two yarns a target sets side by side.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** any touch at level 1 throws the shuttle; at level 3 the pedal is the only new thing.
- **Touch-down feedback:** a pedal sinks and the threads lift on touch-down; the shuttle zips on release; a beater thump and a soft pentatonic pluck per row (pitch rising as the cloth grows; synthesized, `src/audio/notes.ts`).
- **Replay variety:** seeded warp colors, friends who receive the cloth (a scarf for Bunny, a blanket for Duck, a sail for the ferry), motif pools at 6–7.
- **Mistakes teach:** a loose row shows *why* (every thread went the same way); a wrong color shows *where* (the sample row pulses). Undo row is always free.
- **Off-screen:** a paper-strip or cardboard loom with a grown-up (the Paper Weaving card, after review); "find a stripe and a check on your clothes".
- **Comfortable stopping:** no timer, no thread that runs out, nothing that unravels while away.

## Fit with the creature-world rework

The brief's collection includes "child-made patterns" and asks that an educational action cause a visible result. A woven cloth that a creature then wears, or that warms the home, is that loop: plan, weave, give. It needs no currency and no unlock beyond the cloth itself. A future project ("How can we keep the new friend warm?") could request a Loom mode and receive the cloth as its typed result (RW07's adventure contract; proposal). Picture Talk and Gallery Walk fit the brief's "Can we tell what happened from these clues?" investigation, as a picture-reading step. Nothing here touches RW02b art, RW04 or saves.
