# Ancient river civilizations: rivers, records and the first cities

**Subject:** `ancient-river-civilizations` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M, G ([definitions](../subjects.md#the-audiences)) · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Session:** `claude/subject-ancient-river-civilizations`, [PR #587](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/587).

Research and proposals only. **Define River Tablets first:** count real things, keep a record of them in clay, then use the record to check what arrives. Its ladder follows the order in which writing itself grew out of accounting (tokens, marked envelopes, tablets, number signs, then pictures used for their sounds), so each level's action *is* the history rather than a costume on a quiz. Farming along the river (flood, silt, lifting water, canals) extends games that already own water, levers and planting. Cities appear as storehouses, rations and the people who kept the records. The youngest bands get cause and effect with clay and water, with no claim that an infant learns history. Older play adds reading a record, two records that disagree, the rebus step, and a grown-up decipherment puzzle. No runtime band, zone, game or system is added here.

### Real-history boundaries

- **Two living regions.** Egypt and Iraq are countries today; Kush is Sudan's heritage. The island says *long ago, by the Nile* and *long ago, between two rivers*, never "Egypt is pyramids." The flood is past tense (it ended in 1970).
- **Labor and enslavement.** Both civilizations used corvée and held enslaved people; the Giza evidence corrects "slaves built the pyramids" without erasing slavery elsewhere. Younger rungs omit it. The U/M case teaches what a bakery and a tomb can and cannot show, and must not conclude "there was no slavery." Depth is a developer decision with a reviewer.
- **Religion, kings and Hammurabi** (California 6.2.3, 6.2.4, 6.2.7) are outside the island's scope: no gods, no afterlife, no mummies as a game, no laws-of-a-king quiz. Senet's later afterlife meaning is not depicted.
- **No real scripts on the child's screen.** The island's tablet marks and picture-signs are invented island signs "like the scribes' marks." Real cuneiform and hieroglyphs appear, if at all, on a reviewed grown-ups' card with the museum links above. Fabricius' own "not academically correct" label is the standard to copy.
- **Every historical line spoken or printed needs a named reviewer** (an Assyriologist or Egyptologist, or a history educator who teaches the KS2 depth study) before it ships. Rule tests check counting, consistency and solvability; they cannot check history.

## What fits, and what does not

- **Fits:** record-keeping as the reason for writing (count, mark, check); the river's year as a sequence; a lever that lifts water; gates that share a flood; a repeating pattern from a rolled seal; pictures read for their sounds; two records that disagree; a bilingual puzzle for grown-ups. Each is a fun action whose consequence is the learning.
- **Fits as a note only:** mudbrick walls (Block Tower), loaves shared (Pet Kitchen), grain for pots (Swap Meet), the strike (civics), "how long ago" (Timeline Trail), silt (Sand Table).
- **Does not fit:** pyramids as a building race; mummification; gods, tombs and the afterlife; kings and named battles; a tomb-raiding or treasure-hunt framing (the ground is someone's heritage); a civilization dress-up quiz; dates as answers; any real script shown to a child as something she "wrote." No conquering, no armies, no points for empire.

## First game's proposed ladder: River Tablets

A new activity is justified only if *making and using a record* is central. If a definition finds the levels are counting alone, the rungs go to Lasso Loops and Story Steps instead. IDs and ranges below are **planning labels**, not saved level assignments.

| Step / audience | First round and new decision | Ending, support and meaningful rule check |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1: One token each** · P | Sheep walk into a pen one at a time; after each, tap the big jar and a clay token drops in with a clink. The pet tips the jar: the tokens and the sheep are counted together | Three to five sheep. Every tap is welcome: a tap before the next sheep rolls a token back out, no miss. Finish when the jar is tipped. Check: tokens never exceed sheep; finish always reachable |
| **2: Marks on the envelope** · P–K | The tokens are sealed in a clay ball. Press one mark on its outside for each token she counted (the tokens are shown first, then hidden). Crack it open and compare marks to tokens | Two envelopes, three to six tokens. Too many or too few marks is a miss, named by the pet while the tokens line up under the marks; after two misses on one envelope the next empty mark cell glows and one hint counts. Check: marks are placed in cells, so a count is exact; the hint leads to the right count |
| **3: Which row?** · K | A tablet with a sheep sign and a jar sign heading two rows. Goods arrive mixed; press each mark in the row of its kind | Two tablets; totals to six. A mark in the wrong row is a miss ("that was a jar"); it moves to the right row after the second miss with one hint. Check: every seeded delivery has both kinds; the tablet's rows read back to the delivery |
| **4: A ten is a different mark** · S | Twelve to twenty-nine baskets; a big tens-mark and a small ones-mark. Press the fewest marks that say the count. Additive: order does not matter | Two tablets. A reading that does not equal the count is a miss; an equal reading with extra ones (ten ones for a ten) is accepted and the pet shows the shorter way. Check: `value(marks) === count`; the shortest form is unique |
| **5: Check the delivery** · S | A tablet already written says what should arrive. The cart comes; count it against the tablet and tap *short*, *right* or *extra*, then how many | Two carts, differences of 0–3. A wrong verdict is a miss with the two counts spoken side by side; two misses glow the uncounted goods. A ration row ("each worker gets two bowls") is one cart. Check: the verdict and difference follow from the seeded tablet and cart |
| **6: Two tablets disagree** · S–U | The sender's tablet and the receiver's tablet record one cart; they differ. Count the cart: which tablet is right? A third case: neither matches, and *we can't tell which* is a right answer | Two cases, one of them unresolvable. A wrong pick is a miss; choosing "can't tell" when one tablet does match is also a miss, explained. Check: cases enumerate every consistent reading; the unresolvable case accepts only "can't tell" |
| **7: Say it with pictures** · U | The scribe must write something that is not a thing: "I see a deer." Pick the picture-signs whose *names sound like* the words (eye, sea, deer) from six | Two sentences from an authored list (I/eye, see/sea, be/bee, dear/deer, knot/not, sun/son, two/shoe…). A wrong sign is a miss with both names spoken. Check: each sentence has exactly one valid sign sequence; distractors never rhyme-match |
| **8: Which one do I mean?** · U–M | The same sounds, two meanings: add a mute meaning-sign (a sky, a person) so "sun" and "son" read differently in the spoken sentence | Two sentences. A wrong meaning-sign is a miss with the sentence re-spoken. Check: every sentence has one authored determinative |
| **9: Two Tablets** · M–G | A short line in island signs beside the same line as pictures; one sign is unknown. Deduce it from the crib and read a new line | One case with a unique reading. A wrong value is a miss; the hint names the matching position in the crib. Check: solvable from the crib alone; unique; the hint reduces the candidate set |

**Tentative windows:** preschool 1–2; pre-K 2–3; school 3–8; grown-up 9 (or Two Tablets registered separately). `school`'s upper rungs are selected by level, not a new U/M band. A first implementation can be 1–3 only. Never silently add a level below a saved one; use the `order` contract if a younger rung is appended later.

**First-round packet to define:** one pen, one jar, one goods kind (sheep), three to five arrivals, a tablet of large cells (at least 100 logical units after scaling), the mark that appears on touch-down with a soft "thup," the tip-and-count ending, two seeded layouts, a second goods kind (jars) for level 3, voice lines, a how-to card per level and a ghost finger that taps the jar per sheep and presses the cells. No free drawing, no physics, no reusable museum engine.

**Mistakes and accounting:** level 1 counts nothing. From level 2 only a *commit* (crack the envelope, hand in the tablet, give the verdict, read the line) can count a miss; pressing, erasing and re-counting are free. After two misses on one tablet, one hint glows the next cell or the uncounted goods and counts once; asked-for help counts at most once per tablet. Assisted completion still finishes for one shell-awarded sticker. A disagreeing-tablets case answered "can't tell" when it is the right answer is a correct answer, never a dodge.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is tapping a jar, not answering a question. Touch-down shows the mark or token immediately; the pet counts aloud as the jar tips. Palette colors for clay, river and grain; no sepia filter. The invented marks are a thick short wedge and a corner wedge, big enough to read at arm's length; cells are a grid, so a press can only land in a cell. The reed stylus is her finger; a drawn stylus would add a precision tool a small hand does not need. Portrait keeps the tablet centred, the pen and cart above it and the pet bottom-left; the Finish/commit control is at least 100 units and off the pet. This is not a music game: pick an existing compatible island style in the definition.

Replay varies counts, goods kinds, tablet shape and which tablet is wrong, never the rules. Nothing accrues; nothing is owed. Round completion uses `ctx.finish`; a kept tablet in the treehouse is a later creations question, not part of the first slice. Cleanup releases taps and timers.

**Co-play:** counter and scribe (above). **Off screen, proposed for review:** a play-dough tablet and a lolly stick, one mark for each spoon on the table, then count them back; a paper Ur board with coins. Both cards wait on practical review (small parts, play-dough, under-threes) and on the Ur rules' provenance line.
