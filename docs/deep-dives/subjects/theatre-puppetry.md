# Theatre and puppetry: a puppet on a stick, a voice, and someone watching

**Subject:** `theatre-puppetry` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M and G, as defined in the [subject tracker](../subjects.md#the-audiences) · **Requested:** the [tracker](../subjects.md#languages-arts-thinking-and-the-natural-world) row (pretend, character voices, puppet shows, acting a story for an audience) · **Researched:** 2026-10-27 · **Session:** `claude/subject-theatre-puppetry`, [PR #1025](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1025).

**Research and proposals. Puppet Stage levels 1–3 (TP1) were built 2026-10-30 ([GAMES.md](../../GAMES.md#puppet-stage)); everything else here is still proposed.** This study sits beside four neighbours and takes nothing from them: [Story Theater](../../GAMES.md#story-steps) (Story Steps 12–13) owns *choosing* a story's cast, weather and prop and watching the result; [Movie Night](../../GAMES.md#movie-night) owns putting sounds on filmed footage and a premiere whose audience reacts only to what it heard; [Cue Studio](../../GAMES.md#cue-studio) owns a tiny executable show of action cards; the [social-emotional study](social-emotional.md#candidates-and-fit) owns *Two Viewpoints* (one mishap, two accounts) and the proposed [Playdate Workshop](../../IDEAS.md#candidates). This study owns what none of them has: **her hand moves the character and chooses its voice, and an audience watches her do it.** Theatre here is performing, not authoring.

**Recommendation.** Define **Puppet Stage** levels 1–3 first: a new game whose fun is a stick puppet that pops up from under a little stage when she touches the floor, follows her finger across it, and talks in its own voice when she bobs it, while the companion sits in the front row and claps. The lap and toddler rounds judge nothing; preschool asks for a *named* puppet (identify the character); pre-K asks her to *say it like* a sleepy, grumpy or tiny puppet (voice shows who a character is and how it feels); school acts a three-scene story card for three creature friends who react only to what she showed, then takes a bow. The 9–12 rung directs the show (cues in order, a prop that stands for something else); the 11–14 rung is a *Yes, and* scene with the companion as a scene partner, after the improv evidence below. The grown-ups' forms are the **director's chair** co-play role (the grown-up narrates, she performs), a **puppet-show card** on the grown-ups' page (a sock or paper-bag puppet and a three-scene show at home), and a cozy **Shadow Play** silhouette puzzle in Lantern Lake, sketched only.

## What people can do, and what remains uncertain

- **Sourced, and a caution:** [Lillard et al. 2013, *The impact of pretend play on children's development: a review of the evidence*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC4662258) (Psychological Bulletin 139(1); abstract read through search summaries, the paper not opened). The review tested whether pretend play is *crucially causal* for development against two alternatives, that it is one of many routes (equifinality) or an epiphenomenon of other factors. For language, narrative and emotion regulation "the evidence fits all three positions"; for executive function and social skills it "leans against the crucial causal position"; for reasoning equifinality is supported. **The island must not claim that puppet play develops language, feelings or self-control.** It can claim a fun action in which a character is voiced and a story acted.
- **Sourced:** [Råde 2021, *Theories Supporting the Use of Puppets as Pedagogical Tool with Young Children*](https://www.hrpub.org/journals/article_info.php?aid=11009) (Universal Journal of Educational Research 9(7); the page did not load this session, the search summary only): a systematic review of 37 articles on the theoretical basis for puppets with young children. A 2022 [Nordic brief](https://nordiskbarnehageforskning.no/index.php/nbf/article/download/222/265/2919) by the same author (not opened) names four areas of opportunity, social skills, communication, subject learning and play, and two of challenge, pedagogical and contextual. A Croatian paper found in the same search suggests children "see the puppet as a peer" and talk more freely when "the puppet, rather than the child, experiences the emotion". All of this is teacher-with-puppet evidence, mostly small and qualitative; none is about a screen puppet.
- **Sourced:** [Hainselin, Aubry and Bourdin 2018, *Improving Teenagers' Divergent Thinking With Improvisational Theater*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6167459/) (Frontiers in Psychology; opened). Thirty seventh-graders (mean age about 11.5), 18 in eleven lunchtime improv sessions (co-construction, emotions, listening, imagination) and 12 in a sports control, were **not randomized**. After the programme the improv group gave more original and flexible answers and fewer prototypical ones on the Alternative Uses Task; fluency rose in both groups. The authors call it "preliminary" and ask for larger samples. [Sowden et al. 2015](https://eprints.chi.ac.uk/id/eprint/4475/) (not opened) report the same direction in primary-school children against matched control games. This is the only tween evidence in this study, and it is about improv games with people, not a tablet.
- **Sourced (standards as proxies):** Core Arts puts "identify characters" at Pre-K, "voice and sound" and "identify characters and setting" at kindergarten, "alter voice and body" and "multiple representations of a single object" at grade 2, and reflection on *why* choices were made from grade 3. EYFS expects a four- to five-year-old to "perform … stories with others".
- **Inference, not evidence:** that a one-year-old is delighted by a puppet popping up where she touched (cause and effect, as in Peekaboo Barn); that a three-year-old hears "the sleepy voice" and picks the slow low one; that a six-year-old enjoys acting a story card for a row of creatures more than watching the pet act it; that a twelve-year-old chooses a *Yes, and* scene at all. Device and person checks.
- **Not established:** that a chosen synthetic voice counts as "using voice" in the kindergarten sense. It is the nearest a microphone-free app can come, and the grown-ups' card carries the real thing.

## What fits

- **A puppet under her finger, on a stage, speaking in its own voice, with someone watching** fits every rule: Pixi `Graphics` puppets on sticks (the picnic's Bunny and Bear heads, a duck, a cat), a curtain, a front row of creature friends, synthesized voices through the existing engine, large targets (a puppet is at least 100 units tall), immediate feedback (it rises and bobs on touch-down), no timers, one sticker at the bow.
- **A named request** ("make Bunny talk") is a fair question with one answer and gentle misses. **A voice request** ("say it the sleepy way") is fair once the voices are distinct enough on the device (device check). **A story card** ("Bunny lost her hat: she looks, she asks Bear, Bear finds it") is acted scene by scene with one required action each, so the audience can judge what it saw without judging *how well* she acted.
- **Free performance stays free.** Which puppet, where it walks, how many times it bobs and what prop stands for the horse are never counted. Only a named-request level counts misses.
- **Does not fit:** a microphone, lip-sync, recording or sharing a show (no microphone, no camera, no accounts); scoring acting; applause meters; a show that must be finished in one go (a Take a bow is always available after one complete scene); timed scenes; a competitive talent show.
- **Belongs elsewhere:** choosing story variables and watching (Story Theater); sounds on footage (Movie Night); action sequences the pet executes (Cue Studio); naming feelings (Feelings Faces); the body (Pet Says, Animal Charades); authoring the story (Story Weaver); two accounts of one event (social-emotional); the mechanism that moves a puppet (Puppet Workshop); the fairness of two players' turns (Playdate Workshop).

## A first game's ladder: Puppet Stage

**Identity.** A new game, `puppet-stage`, spoken "Puppet Stage"; region (subject) `arts`; lap–school; music `silenced(STYLES.hub)` while a puppet speaks or sings, since the puppets' sung lines use pentatonic steps; a fanfare at the bow. Nothing kept (decision 4).

**The stage.** A proscenium with a red curtain, a floor with a long slot, a painted backdrop (meadow by day; a tassel at the right changes it to night, free at every level), and a front row of three seats below the stage where the companion sits at levels 1–3 and two more creature friends join at 4–6. Four stick puppets live under the floor: Bunny, Bear, Duck and Cat, each a code-drawn critter head on a flat body with a stick. Each has a voice: a `pitch`/`rate` pair for device speech and a default sung note.

**What she decides.** At 1, where the puppet comes up and which; at 2, which puppet the name or voice means; at 3, which voice fits the asked mood; at 4–6, where each character goes and what it does, in order, and (free) how many times it bobs, which backdrop, whether it sings.

**Mistakes and hints.** Only named-request and ordered-scene levels count misses; a wrong puppet, bubble or scene order is one miss with a spoken reason, two in a row glow the answer and count one hint. Free bobs, walks, backdrop changes and extra lines are never counted. Assisted completion is supported completion, never evidence of independent mastery.

**Rule tests (`logic.ts`).** The ask at level 2 names a puppet that is on stage; the three voice presets at level 3 are pairwise distinct in pitch and rate by at least a fixed margin; each story card's scenes have exactly one required (puppet, place, action) each, and the required place is on the stage; `premiere(show)` returns a reaction per friend that depends only on the performed scenes (Movie Night's property, restated); every level's plan is reachable by the ghost finger (the bot pops, taps the named puppet, taps the asked bubble, drags to the card's place and bobs); the lap plan has zero countable actions.

**Cost.** New-game tier, medium: the stage and four puppets (one session), the voice presets (small, shared), three story cards (authored, with their spoken lines in `voice-script.ts`), the how-to card with an `autotouch()` demonstration, the sticker, rule tests. Two batches: 1–3 and 4–6.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** touching the floor; a puppet rises there within one frame and says hello. No reading, no menu, the curtain already open.
- **Touch-down feedback:** the puppet lifts and tilts on touch-down, bobs on release; a bubble inflates; the curtain swings.
- **Replay variety:** which puppet rises nearest, the backdrop, the ask order and the story card draw are seeded; the voice presets are fixed so "sleepy" always sounds the same.
- **Off-screen activity:** the puppet-show card (TP5); the `coplayHint` for levels 4–6 is the director's chair ("read the card aloud while she does it").
- **Spoken support:** every ask through `ctx.instruct`; puppet lines through `ctx.say` with the puppet's voice preset. The guide pet never speaks in a puppet voice.

## Fit with the creature-world rework

The companion sits in the front row and claps, and can be one of the puppets at levels 1–3 if the [collection packet's](../../rework/CREATURE-COLLECTION.md) rig supports a stick pose; nothing is required of the rework for the first ladder. ## Decisions for the developer

Under the [October 23 standing rules](../../archive/DECISIONS-2026-10-23.md#standing-rules) the building session adopts each lean; only the device checks wait on a person. The leans were adopted on 2026-10-27 in the [Puppet Stage definition](../../definitions/puppet-stage.md).

1. **Identity.** A new game, Puppet Stage, rather than a Story Steps level: Story Steps 1–13 are ordering and choosing; a puppet under the finger is a different interaction with a different lap entry. **Lean: new game.**
2. **Land.** Music Mountain (six games; room for seven more) for a stage whose kindergarten core is voice and sound; Story Grove already pages at sixteen. **Lean: Music Mountain**, with Story Grove recorded as the subject-row alternative.
3. **Puppet voices.** Each puppet's lines use device speech with its own `pitch`/`rate` (the engine already sets `u.pitch = 1.15` for the pet); the single-voice rendered clips are not used for puppet lines, so the pet's own instructions keep their clips. **Lean: yes**, with a device check that the iPad's chosen voice honours pitch and rate audibly and pleasantly (a novelty voice is never used, as `voice-choice.ts` already rules).
4. **Nothing kept.** The show is gone at the bow; a sticker is the reward. **Lean: nothing kept** for TP1–TP2; a saved show is a treehouse-shelf question later.
5. **Director's chair versus Cue Studio.** TP3 is defined only after TP1–TP2 are played, with a written diff against Cue Studio (cards are stage directions tied to a story and she performs the show herself afterwards). **Lean: define after play.**
6. **The puppet-show card.** A craft card with scissors is practical advice; the card says the grown-up cuts and cites a library or museum source for the puppet. **Lean: write it at TP5 with sources on the card**; no outside reviewer under S1.

**Device and person checks (DEVICE-CHECKS):** the three voice presets are distinct and pleasant on the iPad's selected voice; a one-year-old's reaction to a puppet popping up where she touched; whether a six-year-old prefers performing a card to watching Story Theater.
