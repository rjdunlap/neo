# Philosophy and reasoning: "Is that always true?", a reason you can point to, and disagreeing kindly

**Subject:** `philosophy-reasoning` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M and G, as defined in the [subject tracker](../subjects.md#the-audiences) · **Requested:** the [tracker](../subjects.md#languages-arts-thinking-and-the-natural-world) row (Is it fair? What if? Reasons, counterexamples, disagreeing kindly) · **Researched:** 2026-10-31 · **Session:** `claude/research-philosophy-reasoning`, [PR #1051](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1051).

**Research and proposals only; nothing here is built.** Philosophy with children has three strands the tracker names: *reasons and counterexamples* (is that claim always true? what shows it?), *what if* (imagining a change and following it), and *is it fair?* (values). The island already owns much of the second and third: [Village Vote](../../GAMES.md#village-vote) owns majority rule and, in its proposed level 5, fairness to the losers ([community-civics](community-civics.md)); [Pet Kitchen](../../GAMES.md#pet-kitchen) and Little Helpers own equal shares; [Feelings Faces](../../GAMES.md#feelings-faces) 11 and the social-emotional study's *Two Viewpoints* own two friends seeing one happening differently; [Ramp Race](inquiry.md) and the proposed Trial Shelf own fair tests and "what can we tell?"; [Forecast Detective](../../definitions/forecast-detective.md) owns *Can't tell yet*; [Rule Machine](../../definitions/rule-machine.md) owns inferring a hidden rule from inputs and outputs; [Sorting Machine](../../GAMES.md#sorting-machine) owns building a sensor/gate rule; [Critter Sort](../../GAMES.md#critter-sort) owns sorting by a pictured attribute and, at its level 4, picking the rule that explains a finished sort. What no game has is the first strand as an action: **a friend says something sweeping about the cards in front of her, and she tests the claim, finds the one that breaks it, and helps the friend say it better.** That is the gap this study fills.

**Recommendation.** Define **Not Always** levels 1–3 first: a new game in which a creature friend stands on a little crate beside a row of picture cards and announces a claim about *those cards* ("All of these are red!", "Every one with wings can fly!"). Her fun action is to tap the card that proves it wrong: the card flips up and glows, the friend gasps, and the claim sign visibly changes (*all* becomes *most*, or a word is crossed out and replaced). Sometimes the claim is true of every card, and the answer is a big *Every one!* stamp. Later levels ask her to pick the mended claim, to see that a card the claim does not talk about cannot break it ("every red one is round": a blue square is not a counterexample), to choose the card that is a *reason* rather than a friend just sounding sure, and, at the top of school, to decide whether two friends' claims can both be true. Every claim is **closed-world**: it is about the pictured cards on screen, so the evidence is always visible, a rule test can check every claim's truth, and no line depends on real-world facts. Values (*is it fair?*) and imagination (*what if?*) are never scored: they go to the grown-ups' **question card** and the **"But what about…?" co-play role**, and to an ungraded talk moment after a round (decision 3).

## What fits

- **Fits, as a new game:** testing a spoken claim against visible cards. The action is on *the claim and its speaker*: the claim sign changes when she finds the counterexample, and later she picks the mended claim and the supporting reason. No existing game has a stated claim to break; a level of Critter Sort or Sorting Machine would turn their sorter or machine into a soapbox, which is a different scene and a different act (decision 1).
- **Fits, ungraded:** *Is it fair?* and *What if?* as talk. After a round the friend may ask an open question with two or three picture answers that are all accepted and named ("You think the big piece goes to Bear because he's hungriest. That's a reason!"); this is a talk moment, never a miss (decision 3).
- **Fits, off-screen:** the grown-ups' **question card** (a stimulus, one question, two follow-ups) and the **"But what about…?"** co-play role.
- **Does not fit:** a game that rules on values (which share is fair, which choice is kind) as right or wrong; debate with a winner and a loser; reading-heavy argument; real-world generalizations at first ("all birds fly") because the evidence is not on screen and the facts need a source check (they may come later, decision 4); voting on questions (Village Vote owns voting); *Can't tell yet* (Forecast Detective's).

## A first game's ladder: Not Always

The scene: a row of picture cards on a low table; a creature friend (from the current cast) on a little crate with a sign above it that pictures the claim (an *all* hand sweeping over a red blob, for "all of these are red"). The friend says the claim aloud; tapping the friend repeats it, free. Every claim is about the cards on screen and nothing else.

| Level | First round | What she decides | Mistakes and hints | Rule test |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Not all of them** · pre-K 1, school 1 (preschool by pin) | Four cards, three share one pictured attribute (colour, hat, wings, spots), one does not. "All of these have hats!" Tap the one without. Three claims a round | Which card breaks the claim | Tapping a card that fits: the card nods and shows the attribute ("This one has a hat"), one miss. Two misses: the breaking card glows (one hint). Success: the card flips up, the friend gasps, the sign's *all* becomes *not all* and the friend says "Not all of them!" | Every claim has exactly one counterexample among the cards; attributes are visually distinct at sticker size |
| **2 · Every one?** · pre-K 2, school 2 | Five cards; one of the three claims is true of every card. An *Every one!* stamp sits beside the row | Find a counterexample, or check them all and stamp *Every one!* | Stamping when a card breaks the claim: the friend says "Look again", one miss. Tapping a fitting card: one miss, as level 1. Every card tap is an answer; tapping the friend repeats the claim, free (decision 6) | Each round mixes true and false claims; the stamp is right exactly when no card breaks the claim; no claim depends on "some" versus "most" |
| **3 · Say it better** · school 3 | After she breaks a claim, three mended claims appear (pictured and spoken): exactly one is literally true of every card ("All of these have wings", "All of these can fly", "None of these have wings") | Which mended claim fits every card | A false mended claim: the friend points to the card it breaks (one miss). Two misses: the true one glows | Exactly one option is true over the cards; each false option has a nameable counterexample |
| **4 · Only the red ones count** · school 4 | "Every red one is round"; cards include blue squares (do not count), red circles (fit) and one red square | The card that is red *and* not round, or *Every one!* | A blue square: "That one isn't red, so the claim doesn't talk about it", one miss | Exactly one counterexample; at least one distractor that fails the "if" part |
| **5 · Show me why** · school 5, about 10 and up | Two friends disagree about a pictured scene; three cards: a picture that settles it, the friend "being very sure", the friend repeating itself | The card that is a reason | Choosing confidence or repetition: "Being sure isn't a reason. | **6 · Both can be true** · school 6, about 10 and up | Two claims about one row; *Both true* stamp or tap the card that breaks one; a kind or rude reply card for the friend who was wrong | Whether two claims are consistent with the cards; how to say so | The reply is never counted (decision 5); only the claim check counts | Generated rows have the intended truth values for both claims |

Levels 1–3 are the first slice. **Cost:** M. A claim model (`every`, `none`, `every … that …`), a small attribute vocabulary drawn from existing critter and prop art, the crate, the sign and the stamp. No new save field; nothing kept. A bot finds the counterexample from the model.

## Experience and presentation

- **The first unsupported action** is a tap on the card that is different, guided by the spoken claim. Touch-down: the card lifts and the friend leans to look at once.
- **The payoff** is the friend's reaction and the sign changing, not a score: *all* becomes *not all*, a crossed-out word is replaced, the friend laughs at itself ("I said that too fast!"). The friend is never embarrassed or punished.
- **Replay variety:** seeded rows and claims over the attribute vocabulary; the friend and the table props rotate.
- **Talk moment (ungraded, decision 3):** at most once a round, an open "Is it fair?" or "What if?" question with two picture answers, both accepted and named; skipped by a tap anywhere.
- **Off-screen:** the question card and the claim strips for "But what about…?"

## Fit with the creature-world rework

The claimant is a creature friend from the current cast, and the cards can show her collected creatures by attribute later; nothing here depends on rework infrastructure, and the game keeps nothing (decision 7).

## Decisions for the developer

Under the [October 23 standing rules](../../archive/DECISIONS-2026-10-23.md#standing-rules) the defining session adopts each lean; only the person checks wait.

1. **Identity.** A new game, Not Always, rather than a Critter Sort or Sorting Machine level: neither has a speaker whose claim she tests and mends. **Lean: new game.**
2. **Land.** Story Grove (the tracker's row; sixteen games, so a seventeenth pages, never shrinks) or Puzzle Peaks (thirteen today, Rule Machine queued as its fourteenth). **Lean: Story Grove**, for a game about what people say; the defining session checks `landLayout` either way.
3. **Values are never scored.** *Is it fair?* and *What if?* answers are all accepted and named, never a miss or hint, after the Count the Ballots precedent ([community-civics decision 4](community-civics.md#decisions-for-the-developer): name without judging). **Lean: yes**, at most one talk moment a round, skippable.
4. **Real-world claims.** Claims stay about the cards on screen for levels 1–6. A later rung with real generalizations ("all birds fly", the penguin) needs the building session's source check (S1) and the evidence still on screen. **Lean: closed-world only for 1–6.**
5. **Kind replies.** At level 6 the rude reply is never counted; the friend just looks sad and the kind reply appears again. **Lean: uncounted.**
6. **Inspection.** Every card tap is an answer; to look closer she taps the friend to hear the claim, and cards are large enough to read at a glance. **Lean: card taps are answers**; the defining session confirms card size and attribute legibility.
7. **Nothing kept.** No saved claims or journal entries for 1–3. **Lean: nothing kept**; a "claims I broke" journal page is a later treehouse question.

**Device and person checks (for DEVICE-CHECKS once built):** does a four-year-old find the counterexample from the spoken claim alone, or does she tap the odd one out without listening (watch the *Every one!* rounds); does a grown-up use the question card, and does the conversation last more than one exchange (SL.K.1.b)?
