## First game's proposed ladder

**Ready for Adventure** is a working title. Each round is one parade preparation and one parade playback, with one shell-owned sticker even after help. Art and sound remain code-drawn/synthesized. Levels below are proposed new IDs for an unregistered game; they do not change any saved game's levels.

| Level / proposed band | First round and decision | Mistake, support and finish | Future rule proof / cost |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1 · Pack the flag** · T/P | A finished flag and a ball sit beside a bag with a flag request. Choose the flag; it appears in the bag and the pet waves it in the parade | Wrong prop stays available; gentle boing names what the request shows. Two misses glow the flag and add one hint. Request replay is free. The correct pack completes the round | Request is visible, present and uniquely identified; every generated scene finishes in one pack. S |
| **2 · Make it ready** · P/K | A bare pole, a ribbon and the goal flag picture. Tap/drag the ribbon onto the pole, then put the finished flag in the bag | Packing early keeps the pole on the bench: explain the pictured missing ribbon; boing and one miss for a deliberate pack attempt, not for dragging past the bag. Two misses glow the next needed action, one hint per unresolved job. Parade uses the assembled flag | Pack legal iff attached; attachments cannot be lost or duplicated; hint is a legal action leading to completion. M: stateful prop art |
| **3 · Two useful jobs** · K | Attach ribbon then pack flag; place a beater with the drum then pack drum. Four action pictures remain visible | Any interleaving preserving the two dependencies is valid. Wrong target explains the relevant missing part; same support rule as 2. Finish when both are packed, then both appear in the parade | Enumerate all valid action orders, accept all six interleavings, and reject prerequisite violations. M: second prop |
| **4 · The request changed** · S | After packing two finished props, the pictured request changes from flag + drum to flag + bell. Unpack drum and pack the already-ready bell | All editing and exploratory choices are free. Check compares request and bag, names a remaining difference, adds one miss only for an incomplete deliberate Check. Two misses glow a useful edit, one hint for that request. Check success starts the revised parade | Any bag matching the request is accepted, surplus removal and replacement commute, and no request change can make the scene unsolvable. M |
| **5 · One workbench** · S | Two unprepared props, each needs two pictured assembly actions. One bench holds one prop. Either can be completed and packed first | Trying an occupied bench gives a free explanation and retains both objects; no move is irreversible. Check and hints as level 4. Replay shows why a proposed job waits; finish any complete plan | Finite resource state, reachable finish from every legal reachable state, alternate orders accepted, explanation names actual blocker. M; define separately after 1–3 |

**First implementation scope:** levels 1–3 only, proposed T 1–1, P 1–2, K 2–3; no school/grown-up claim in that first build. Levels 4–5 are later packets. Village Planner has its own older visual brief and rule definition; it is not an automatic age extension of this ladder.

Accounting needs deliberate implementation: request replay, undo, rearrangement, occupied-bench nudges and free planning are not hints/misses. Explicit help or the two-miss reveal records one hint for that unresolved job/request; retries must not repeatedly add hints for the same reveal. An assisted action may complete the round; the result records support, never independent mastery. The definition must spell out per-job reset boundaries before coding.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is putting the requested flag into the bag. The goal picture stays visible beside it, and tapping it speaks the request without giving away a dependency. Touch-down lifts the prop slightly, opens the bag lip and plays a soft pentatonic acknowledgment. A canceled drag returns the prop to its last legal place. A tap-select/tap-place alternative can remove precision demands if defined with the first interaction.

Use the customized pet beside a broad bench, ribbon drawn as a curved strip, a distinct flag silhouette, and a drum with the beater visibly clipped to it. A prepared prop visibly changes; a completed action picture moves to a finished rail. The parade uses exactly the packed props in a short skippable scene, so preparation changes something pleasurable. No health dirt meter or increasingly distressed pet. Keep 100-unit controls, corner-guide clearance and both layouts in the eventual definition; reduce objects rather than shrink them.

Seeded variation changes flag shapes/patterns, prop positions and parade backdrops while preserving identifiable requests and the same causal rule. Do not make colors the only cue or increase the object count to manufacture difficulty. Undo, narrated replay and assisted completion remain available. Stop/Home is always comfortable; nothing remains neglected between visits.

An optional off-screen conversation is “Which toy would you bring to an imaginary parade, and why?” A family may point, describe, enact or watch. This is a design prompt, not a physical-care instruction or a requirement to own particular objects. Actual routine cards are a later reviewed deliverable.

- **Activity ownership:** Toy Drawer needs a concrete Color Garden/Size Parade comparison before choosing mode versus new identity. Ready for Adventure must retain stateful preparation and visible use; a static order-card variant belongs in Story Steps.
- **Human checks:** after a prototype, ask whether a child sees why the prop is unfinished, whether free independent order feels meaningful, and whether a parent/tween wants to replay Village Planner. Device speech, drag comfort and family appeal are unestablished. This research does not claim a new performed device check.
