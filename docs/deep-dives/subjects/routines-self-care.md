## Toy Drawer follow-up: Make it Fit

**Developer direction, 2026-10-14:** could Toy Drawer, or another game, use Diablo-style “inventory Tetris,” making differently sized things fit? **Recommendation:** use this as Toy Drawer's lead mechanic. “Find it Again” remains a possible later mode. A backpack or expedition case can be another pictured container in the same packing game; it does not need another game ID just for a theme change. This is a proposal/definition direction, not an implementation claim.

Three additional first-party descriptions were opened for this follow-up: [Blizzard's Diablo II item guide](https://classic.battle.net/DIABLO2EXP/ITEMS/basics.shtml) describes limited inventory space and item-management choices; [Backpack Hero's developer page](https://thejaspel.itch.io/backpack-hero) describes bag organization and explicitly lists rotating held items; [Save Room's publisher listing](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1955330/Save_Room__Organization_Puzzle/) presents inventory organization as a stand-alone puzzle. None was played. These support the mechanic references, not child-development claims. Rotation is a proposed Puddle Island rule, not a claim that Diablo II rotates items. Borrow spatial decisions; leave the references' combat, consumables, loot rarity and ongoing inventory economy outside this candidate.

**First complete round to define:** an open **4×3 drawer**, a toy train occupying **3×1**, a bear occupying **2×2**, and a ball occupying **1×1**. All are required. Drag or tap-select/place them onto the grid; a faint footprint previews the cells before release. They fit without rotation, with four cells spare. For example: train across the first three top cells, bear in the bottom-left four, ball beside the bear. Accept every non-overlapping arrangement inside the drawer; empty cells are allowed. Once all three fit, an explicit large Close drawer control plays a little toy celebration and completes the round through the shell.

| Proposed rung | New decision, rather than extra speed |
| --- | --- |
| **Roomy packing** · preschool entry to test | Place a few already-oriented rectangles anywhere they fit; no printed destination outlines, sorting rule or demand to fill every cell |
| **Turn it to fit** · pre-K/school proposal | A generous quarter-turn button changes a selected object's footprint; there are different valid layouts |
| **Make room for one more** · school | A requested toy arrives after an initial arrangement. Move previously packed toys to fit it; the case is guaranteed solvable and every toy can return to the tray |
| **Awkward shapes / compartments** · older school | Introduce an L-shaped footprint or a visible divider, one new rule at a time, on a board that retains large cells |
| **Pack for a pictured outing** · upper school/parent horizon | Choose between equivalent item sets meeting visible needs, then fit and use them. The [Pharoway Caravan follow-up](#pharoway-caravan-follow-up-choose-the-cargo-then-fit-it) develops this as **Pack the Wagon**, with a manifest and multiple feasible cargo sets |

These are planning rungs, not approved level IDs, band windows or a difficulty claim. Lap keeps the existing cause-and-effect games. Toddler could get free put-in/take-out play, but independent fitting is not established for that audience. Older appeal needs a person to try it.

**Mistakes and support:** turning, moving, taking out and an unsuccessful fit are spatial experiments, with no miss. An overlapping or off-board drop returns to its last legal position with a gentle bump and a visible occupied-cell explanation. Nothing is discarded. Undo/Reset and a tray remain available; the round has no timer or loss. Help must follow a real solution: if the current arrangement blocks completion, show a useful relocation or an undo, rather than glow an impossible empty space. Assisted packing can finish normally and records help. The definition must specify how repeated help for one target is counted; free exploration must not be presented as independent mastery.

**Why it belongs here:** Color Garden and Size Parade organize attributes; Tangram Town fills authored picture parts/silhouettes; Block Garden proposes placing blocks and clearing lines. Toy Drawer packs a finite set of identifiable objects into shared capacity, keeps placed objects movable and accepts different layouts with spare space. That is a distinct puzzle worth a separate definition while retaining the existing Toy Drawer identity. Ready for Adventure's order-of-preparation rules stay separate from geometric fitting.

**Definition exit:** freeze cell/shape data, selection/drag cancellation, rotation pivot, overlap and board-edge behavior, tray capacity, completion/close/replay, help accounting, original art and both layouts. Keep every interactive cell at least 100 logical units on screen for child bands; cap the board rather than shrinking it. Future pure-rule tests must prove a solution exists, all valid placements are accepted, rotation preserves occupied area, undo restores state, and hints lead to a complete packing even after a blocking legal arrangement. No new physics, saves, persistent possessions or generalized inventory system is needed for this finite scene. Use the existing new-game verification tier only when it is built.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is putting the requested flag into the bag. The goal picture stays visible beside it, and tapping it speaks the request without giving away a dependency. Touch-down lifts the prop slightly, opens the bag lip and plays a soft pentatonic acknowledgment. A canceled drag returns the prop to its last legal place. A tap-select/tap-place alternative can remove precision demands if defined with the first interaction.

Use the customized pet beside a broad bench, ribbon drawn as a curved strip, a distinct flag silhouette, and a drum with the beater visibly clipped to it. A prepared prop visibly changes; a completed action picture moves to a finished rail. The parade uses exactly the packed props in a short skippable scene, so preparation changes something pleasurable. No health dirt meter or increasingly distressed pet. Keep 100-unit controls, corner-guide clearance and both layouts in the eventual definition; reduce objects rather than shrink them.

Seeded variation changes flag shapes/patterns, prop positions and parade backdrops while preserving identifiable requests and the same causal rule. Do not make colors the only cue or increase the object count to manufacture difficulty. Undo, narrated replay and assisted completion remain available. Stop/Home is always comfortable; nothing remains neglected between visits.

An optional off-screen conversation is “Which toy would you bring to an imaginary parade, and why?” A family may point, describe, enact or watch. This is a design prompt, not a physical-care instruction or a requirement to own particular objects. Actual routine cards are a later reviewed deliverable.
