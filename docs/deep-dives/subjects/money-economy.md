# Money and economy: choices, trades and saving inside a round

**Subject:** `money-economy` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M, G · **Date:** 2026-10-17 · **Session:** `claude/subject-money-economy` · [PR #548](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/548).

This is research and proposals only, from [roadmap chunk 28](../../ROADMAP.md#chunks) and the [subject procedure](../subjects.md#the-procedure-one-session-one-subject). No reference game was installed or played. No game, level, band, land, save field or parent card was built.

**The tracker asked for "a check of what Lemonade Stand and Maker Harbor already cover", so this study begins as an overlap audit.** The island already has three money plans:

- **Market Stall**, built for levels 1–7. Its [deep dive](../market-stall.md) proposes levels 8–13 and owns coin value, totals, change and exchange.
- **Lemonade Stand**, built for levels 1–4: weather, price and a five-day purse.
- **The roadmap's [older-child progression](../../ROADMAP.md#older-child-progression-a-market-week-not-an-island-grind)**, which proposes Maker Harbor tickets, a fair board, the Steady and Surprising wheels and a Harbor Bank.

This study proposes new work only for the gaps that remain. Every amount in it stays **inside one round**, as Lemonade Stand's purse already does. It does not add a third economy.

**Recommendation:** define **Swap Meet** first. Creatures trade the things they hold, each for the thing it wants. The ladder grows in four steps:

- **Toddler:** give the friend the pictured thing.
- **Preschool and pre-K:** make two-way swaps.
- **School:** plan a chain of trades when no direct swap works (economists call this missing a "double coincidence of wants").
- **School top:** shells arrive. The same goal then takes fewer steps, which is C3's "role of money in making exchange easier" (D2.Eco.5.3-5) as an action rather than a definition.

The collecting world already gives creatures things to hold and want, and the island has no game whose action is a trade.

## What people can do, and an answer for every audience

**By age (cautious).**

- **Lap (under 2):** no money concept. Give-and-take turn games are social play.
- **Toddler (2):** can hand over a pictured thing on request. A swap is an exchange of turns, not of value.
- **3–5:** can count identical coins and learn that a bigger coin can be worth more (Market Stall). Abstract finance is "usually too young" (CFPB). Choosing one treat and giving up another is understood when both are visible. Waiting is hard, and the 2018 replication warns against reading it as destiny.
- **6–8:** sharing becomes egalitarian (Fehr et al.). Saving toward a visible goal and comparing two prices are within reach. A shop is still more ritual than profit; reviews of Jahoda place that understanding later.
- **9–12:** profit, specialization, a plan across days, simple interest as an amount ("10 becomes 11"). Percentages are later (England KS3).
- **11–14:** budgets out of a given amount, unit pricing, compound interest as a table, and comparative advantage as a puzzle. The tracker's tween evidence favours real challenge, choice and play with friends.

## First game's proposed ladder: Swap Meet

**The island rule (fiction, stated as the island's):** each friend holds one thing and shows a thought bubble of the one thing it wants. A friend trades only for the thing in its bubble; it then gives back what it held and is happy. From level 4, she is the trader with a basket: she can trade what she carries with any friend who wants it, and take that friend's thing. Trading back is an undo: the same two friends swap back the things they just traded, so no state is a dead end (ME1 settles the exact rule).

| Level | Band | First round | What she decides | Mistakes and help |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 *Give it* | toddler | Bunny pictures a ball; her basket holds a ball and a leaf. Drag the ball to Bunny; Bunny hands back a carrot, which goes to the basket. Three friends, one at a time | Which thing matches the bubble | A wrong thing: Bunny shakes her head and the bubble pulses ("Bunny wants the ball"). **No misses counted at toddler** |
| 2 *Two friends* | toddler–preschool | Two friends and two things; each friend gets the one it pictures | Which friend gets which thing | A wrong offer is a miss (gentle boing, spoken want); glow on the right thing after two |
| 3 *Swap!* | preschool–pre-K | Otter holds a berry and wants a shell; Duck holds a shell and wants a berry. Drag Otter's berry to Duck: both things cross | Which two friends want each other's things (two pairs among four friends) | A drag to a friend who does not want it is a miss; hint glows a matching pair |
| 4 *Chain of two* | pre-K–school | She holds a leaf; her pet wants a kite. Duck trades a shell for a leaf; Frog trades the kite for a shell | The order of trades | An offer of a thing nobody in the bubble wants is a miss. A legal trade off the path is exploration, never a miss. Hint: the next useful trade glows |
| 5 *Chain of three, a side trade* | school | A three-step chain plus one tempting legal trade that leads away | Plan before trading; recover by trading back | As 4; the hint names the friend who has the goal ("Frog has the kite. What does Frog want?") |
| 6 *Shells make it easier* | school (top) | The same kind of chain, then the Harbor stall opens: it buys anything for 2 shells and sells anything for 2. Reach the goal again | Barter or shells; the pet asks which way took fewer trades (both count) | The comparison is never a miss; it is spoken from the counted trades |

**What a rule test proves.**

- **Every round:** solvable by breadth-first search over basket states. Minimum trades of 1 per request at levels 1–3, exactly 2 at level 4 and exactly 3 at level 5. No round is trivially solvable by the first trade drawn on the left.
- **Level 5:** at least one legal off-path trade, and the goal still reachable from every state, because trading back is allowed.
- **Level 6:** the shell route is always exactly 2 trades (sell, then buy), so a level-6 barter chain must need at least 3 trades for the shell route to be strictly shorter.
- **Every bubble:** names a thing that exists in the round.
- **The hint:** always lies on a shortest path from the current state.
- **The ghost finger:** follows the solver's path.

**Accounting.** Misses are offers a friend cannot accept. Off-path trades and trading back are free. Hints are the glow after two misses or the lightbulb. Supported completion awards the sticker as usual.

**Cost.** A new game with `drag.ts`, `logic.ts`, voice lines (each friend's want spoken on touch-down), a how-to card, a ghost finger, a land and a sticker. Creatures come from `CRITTERS` until RW05's creatures exist.

### Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** at toddler, drag the pictured thing to the only friend asking.
- **Touch-down feedback:** the thing lifts and the friend's bubble pulses, and the voice names the thing.
- **On a trade:** both things fly at once (the two-way rule shown) and the friend does a happy hop.
- **Replay variety:** seeded friends, things and chain shapes.
- **Placement:** Cozy Village already holds 12 games (Market Stall and Lemonade Stand among them), so placement needs the land capacity test. The lean is Cozy Village if it fits, otherwise the future Maker Harbor.
- **Off-screen:** a swap day with toys at home, saying aloud what each person wants.

## Fit with the creature-world rework

These are proposals only, against the [rework brief](../../rework/README.md).

- **No new currency.** Shells appear only inside a round, as in Market Stall and Lemonade Stand, and reset each round. Swap Meet records nothing new and has no save field.
- **Permanent unlock through play.** An RW07-style adventure, *"Our new friend wants a kite"*, could ask for a Swap Meet chain and end with the kite flying over the creature home as a lasting decoration. The adventure owns that outcome, not the game.
- **Collecting.** The friends who trade could be the child's collected creatures, each with a favourite thing (a species-brief field). No creature is ever traded away. Things trade; creatures do not.
- **Never:** spoiling goods, restock timers, prices that drift unseen, a debt, or losing an owned item in a trade the child did not choose.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Harbor tickets versus "no new currency".** The roadmap's Maker Harbor tickets, fair board and Harbor Bank are a persistent balance. The rework brief says growth does not need a new currency. Lean: keep every money lesson inside a round (this study's proposals all do) and keep the tickets proposal parked until a project needs it. The Harbor Bank's real-time wait stays the only proposed exception; this study adds none.
2. **Real coins or shells.** 2.MD.C.8 names US coins, England's curriculum pounds and pence, and the family's languages point to several currencies. Lean: shells on the island (as in [Market Stall dive decision 2](../market-stall.md#decisions-for-the-developer)); real coins only on a grown-up card, after the currency choice.
3. **Swap Meet's name, land and band window.** Lean: toddler–school, levels 1–6; Cozy Village if the capacity test passes.
4. **The tracker's "household-budget cozy puzzle".** Lean: Harbor Orders instead. A household budget risks making a family's means the puzzle, and it has little deduction beyond arithmetic. Or keep a budget puzzle with fictional, non-household goods.
5. **Income and hardship.** Leave out NSPFE Spending 8-1d (budgets on minimum wage), debt, credit products and insurance from play. Lean: none in play; a grown-up card later, if anything.
6. **Lemonade Stand 5–6 and the captain's top-up.** At level 5, does the top-up ignore the jar (so saving never risks the week)? At level 6, does the top-up stop so the rainy-day jar matters? Lean: yes to the first; the second needs a child playtest.

**Decided 2026-10-23 (on the developer's delegation):** every lean: money lessons stay inside a round and Harbor tickets stay parked (the Harbor Bank the only real-time exception); shells on the island, real coins only on a grown-up card after a currency choice; Swap Meet keeps its name, toddler–school levels 1–6, Cozy Village if the capacity test passes; Harbor Orders replaces the household-budget puzzle; no minimum-wage budgets, debt, credit or insurance in play; Lemonade Stand 5's top-up ignores the jar and level 6's top-up stops so the rainy-day jar matters, with a child playtest as a later person check.
