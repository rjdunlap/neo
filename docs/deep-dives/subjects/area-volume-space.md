# Area, volume and space: covering, filling, seeing and moving shapes

**Subject:** `area-volume-space` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M and G, as defined in the [subject tracker](../subjects.md#the-audiences) · **Requested:** 2026-10-16 by the [standards audit](standards-audit.md) (the tracker's mathematics rows); widened by the [additional-subjects audit](additional-subjects.md) to reflections, rotations, translations, congruence and similarity · **Researched:** 2026-10-22 · **Session:** `claude/subject-area-volume-space`, [PR #793](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/793).

**Research and proposals only. Nothing built.** This study owns **area** (covering with unit squares, rows and columns, area as additive), **perimeter** and how it differs from area, **volume** by packing unit cubes, **views** of solids, **nets**, the **coordinate grid** (including the four quadrants the [integers study](integers.md) handed here) and **rigid moves and congruence**. It leaves alone its neighbours: capacity and pouring stay with [Fill and Dump](../fill-dump.md) and the [measurement study](measurement-units.md); length units with Inchworm; scale-factor similarity with the [ratio study](ratio-proportion.md)'s *Pixel Pictures: big picture*; quarter turns and the planned flip with [Tangram Town](../tangram-town.md); mirror symmetry with [Bug Builder](../bug-builder.md)'s planned *Fold a friend* and the notebook's Mirror Brush and Reflection Catch.

**Recommendation.** Define **Meadow Pens** levels 1–3 first: a new school game in which she paints squares of grass to make a friend's pen in the meadow, and **the fence builds itself around whatever she paints, piece by piece, from a visible stack**. Grass is area and fence is perimeter, and they are two different things she can count. The evidence below names two problems: children do not see a grid of squares as rows and columns until they are taught to, and older children treat area and perimeter as one thing. Meadow Pens answers the first with a field that fills by rows and the second with pens that have the same grass but different fence. It is not a Pixel Pictures or Bug Builder mode, because her decision is *what shape meets a friend's size request*, not *copy this picture* (decision 1).

## What people can do, and what remains uncertain

- **Rows and columns are not seen by themselves.** Outhred and Mitchelmore, *Young children's intuitive understanding of rectangular area measurement* (JRME 31(2), 2000; **abstract through search summary only**, the NCTM page returned 402): 115 children in Grades 1–4 solved covering tasks before area teaching; their strategies fell into five levels, and the authors propose four principles of rectangular covering learned in sequence, stressing the link between unit size and the rectangle's sides, and multiplication. A citing source reports their earlier finding that young children "do not automatically interpret arrays of squares in terms of their rows and columns".
- **A complete drawing of the rows helps.** Clements and colleagues, *Evaluation of three interventions teaching area measurement as spatial structuring to young children* (Journal of Mathematical Behavior, 2018; [abstract opened](https://digitalcommons.du.edu/curr_inst_fac/9)): seeing a complete drawing of the 2-D array, rows and columns organized, helped children's structuring and performance. This is the evidence for Meadow Pens 2's field that fills a row at a time.
- **Area and perimeter get confused, across grades and countries.** Small studies (search summaries only: a Grade 10 study in *Pythagoras*, 2016; a Greek study of sixth graders naming "area–perimeter confusion"; a 2021 counterexample study with Years 4–6 testing whether a larger perimeter always means a larger area) report the confusion and a belief that the two grow together, and attribute much of it to formula drills. Several recommend working on both at once with a visible conflict. Meadow Pens' design answer is that grass and fence are different objects, drawn and counted separately, and that two pens with the same grass can use different fence.
- **Cube arrays get double-counted.** Battista and Clements's work on 3-D cube arrays ([chapter record opened](https://researchconnect.buffalo.edu/en/publications/students-understanding-of-three-dimensional-cube-arrays-findings-/); error findings through search summaries of it and Battista 1999, NCTM 402): students count the faces they can see, missing interior cubes and counting a corner cube two or three times; a 2021 paper suggests spherical units as a start (a hypothesis, not a finding). The hay loft's answer: show a cutaway, one layer at a time, each layer the pen's floor, and never ask her to count an oblique drawing.
- **Nets: picturing the fold works.** A New Zealand assessment trial ([Matching cubes and nets](https://arbs.nzcer.org.nz/node/828), opened): of 41 Year 4 students, those who said they pictured the folding got the most matches; counting faces did worse; most found at least one cube net. Teacher guidance there is to cut out and fold nets. Not a controlled study.
- **Early spatial play.** Verdine and colleagues (*Child Development*, 2013; [SRCD summary opened](https://www.srcd.org/news/playing-blocks-may-help-childrens-spatial-and-math-thinking)): among more than 100 three-year-olds, those better at copying block structures did better on early mathematics; an association, not causation. The Tangram Town dive already records Levine and colleagues on early puzzle play. These justify the built toddler and preschool shape games, not a new one.
- **Not established:** that painting grass in a game builds area understanding that transfers to paper, floors or formulas. A correct pen proves a correct pen inside the toy. Whether the iPad's speech says "square units" and "perimeter" clearly is a device check.

## What fits

- **Fits as a new game: Meadow Pens.** Painting squares of grass for a friend who asks for a size, with a fence that builds itself from a visible stack, exists nowhere on the island. The learning is specific and visible: *area is how many squares cover it; the fence is how far around; the same grass can need different fence; rows times columns counts a rectangle; a loft packed in layers holds floor times height*.
- **Fits an existing game as a later mode:** four quadrants in Treasure Map (a river and a road cross the map, and the treasure can be on any side); Skyline View in Peekaround Island; translation as "slide the house three right" in Treasure Map (England Year 4).
- **Fits as later candidates:** Fold a Box (nets), Stencil Moves (congruence as a sequence of moves), Rooftops (a grown-up views puzzle), and two page cards.
- **Does not fit:** formula drills and written `A = l × w`; real units (cm², m³) before a decision; oblique 3-D drawings to count; timed or scored packing; dilations (the ratio study); real floor plans of a family's home (privacy); crowded late packing levels.

## A first game's ladder: Meadow Pens

Working ID `meadow-pens`, **not registered**. The decision is **which squares to paint** (or, at levels 2, 4 and 6, which number card), read from a friend's request for grass and, from level 3, for fence.

**Interaction.** A meadow grid of square cells, at least 100 logical units each, up to 6×4 in landscape and 4×6 in portrait (a layout audit in both orientations is part of the definition), clear of the pet at the bottom left. Tap or drag across cells to paint grass (touch-down puffs the turf at once, a pentatonic step per new square, rising); tap a painted cell to lift it. The **fence builds itself** around the painted shape, one piece per outside edge, from a **fence stack** beside the meadow that shrinks as pieces leave it, each piece clicking into place with its own tick. A friend (her creatures where the rework allows) waits by the gate with a request bubble: a picture of grass squares with dots, and from level 3 fence pieces with dots. Painted squares must touch side to side; a lone square sprouts a little flower and asks to join (no miss). The bell on the gate checks the pen. The gate never closes on a friend: friends walk in, play and wander out.

**Accounting and help.** Only a ringing that fails, or a wrong card, counts, once per answer; painting, lifting, repainting, the lone-square flower, tapping the friend to hear the request and watching a count replay never count. After two misses the right outline or card glows; a supported finish then paints the pen by itself and the round completes with its sticker. A round is three pens at levels 1 and 3, and five questions at 2 and 4–7. No level shows an equation or a written formula; levels 2 and 4–7 show only number cards.

**Rule tests a definition must name** (by enumeration over every authored or seeded case): every request fits the grid; level 1 accepts every connected shape of the requested size and only those; perimeter equals `4n − 2 × shared edges` for every painted set, checked against counted outside edges; every level-3 request has at least one connected solution, and the ten-fence request for six accepts only 2×3 and 3×2; no six-square shape needs more than 14 or fewer than 10 fence pieces; every level-2, 5, 6 and 7 card set holds the right answer, the documented mistake (sides added, or perimeter) and one other, with exactly one correct; every level-4 pair has a different answer for *more grass* and *more fence*, or a spoken *the same* when the grass is equal; each hint points at a correct outline or card.

**Speech.** Every line is in `voice-script.ts`: "squares of grass" and "fence pieces" throughout; whether "area" and "perimeter" join as names from level 3 is decision 3. Requests are always pictured as well as spoken.

**Cost.** One M game across three build slices; no new engine or persistence. A ghost-finger bot paints a solver's shape cell by cell and rings, or taps the right card.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is level 1's first painted square; the turf puffs on touch-down and the first fence piece leaves the stack before the friend speaks. Replay variety comes from seeded requests, field sizes and friends, from the flowers that grow in finished pens, and from day, dusk and evening skies over the same meadow. The sounds are a rising step per square, a wooden tick per fence piece, and a little gate bell. The off-screen activity: cover a placemat with sticky notes and count them, then run a finger round its edge and count the sides of the notes it passes.

## Fit with the creature-world rework

The friends who ask for pens can be her creatures, each request filing a journal entry for what the round actually showed (a pen with six squares and ten fence pieces). A finished pen is a place a friend visits, not a home they must stay in; finishing a level never takes a friend away or makes one wait, and nothing accrues. The meadow suits Rainbow Meadow today or a meadow ecosystem location in the [rework brief](../../rework/README.md), which decides whether a pen becomes a project.
