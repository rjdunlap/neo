# Cooking and recipes: make, measure and plan

**Subject:** `cooking-recipes` · **Audiences:** lap, toddler, preschool, pre-K, school, upper school, middle school and grown-ups · **Date:** 2026-10-14 · **Session:** `codex/cooking-recipes-subject`, [PR #320](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/320). Research and proposals only; no game or real-world recipe was built.

**Recommendation:** define **Recipe Workshop**, a tactile pretend kitchen, first; extend **Pet Kitchen** for equivalent measures and scaling; then define **Picnic Pantry**, an older-family menu puzzle. Their different pleasures are transforming ingredients, finding equivalent quantities, and choosing among feasible plans. Do not make three games about doubling. The [roadmap packets](../../ROADMAP.md#cooking-and-recipes-recommendations) hold the next actions.

## First game's proposed ladder

**Recipe Workshop** is a working name and identity, not approved registration. Define levels 1–3 first. Tentative windows: toddler 1; preschool 1–2; pre-K 2–3; school 3–5. Lap and grown-up omitted deliberately: reuse youngest toys and offer Picnic Pantry for older depth. Confirm the windows and land fit in the definition; do not alter existing games' ranges or pins.

| Level / first complete round | Decision, supported finish, mistakes and hints | Future rule proof / cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1 — My bowl** | Two already-prepared pretend ingredients, one bowl and a spoon. Tap an ingredient to add a portion; tap spoon to mix; Serve finishes after any ingredient. Mixing/decorating optional, every combination valid. Empty Serve repeats the invitation, no miss. After serving, pet presents exactly that bowl; no taste score | Ingredient counts bounded, every allowed creation can finish, repeated mix is harmless, undo restores the previous state. S within the M first game |
| **2 — Follow the picture** | Card shows one portion each of berry puree and a pale pretend base, then a mixing spoon. Either ingredient order works. A spoon tap mixes everything present; a later addition remains visibly separate until mixed again. Serve checks the displayed recipe; the goal stays visible. Optional topping choice unjudged | Both addition orders accepted; only explicit unsuccessful Serve checks miss. Two misses glow an action on a real solution path; one hint per unresolved bowl. Rules enumerate all states, including oversupply, and find an undo/add/mix recovery. M |
| **3 — Bowls for two** | Two large bowls share that base recipe; one request adds a star decoration, the other a leaf. Either bowl first; mix before adding its visible top decoration. Stirring after decorating visibly folds it into the mixture, so undo/rebuild can restore it | Either complete bowl order and interleaved work accepted. Decoration is constrained only where the request names it; free extra artistic choices outside that request do not miss. Check valid routes and recoverable folded-topping states. M |
| **4 — Mend the bowl** | Start with a visible incomplete bowl and its small action history. Card wants a mixed berry base with a star on top; current bowl lacks the berry. Inspect, undo/add/mix and restore the top. Several repairs accepted, no move limit | Action/undo trace exactly matches contents and layers; hint must progress from the actual current state, never a stale original plan. Failed goal checks follow level 2 accounting; experiments stay free. S–M |
| **5 — Same ingredients, different result** | Two picture requests use the same ingredients: one visibly layered, one stirred together. Decide when to mix and when to leave the layers; present both. A supported replay explains the contrasting result rather than announcing that one dish tastes better | Result is a function of operations and ingredient state, not a secret answer sequence. Test alternative legal procedures, common inputs, deterministic rendering data and undo. M; appeal/model clarity needs observation |

**Concrete first requested bowl (level 2):** one berry-puree portion + one base portion, then mix. Adding base then berry is just as valid as berry then base. Serving with only base says what is missing, keeps the bowl and shows the berry icon. Mixing before any addition is a harmless spoon animation. Adding a topping never changes the base's ingredient counts. A new addition after mixing stays distinct until another mix, so the rule has an observable consequence. A picture of food is not a real recipe, serving size, feeding recommendation or claim about which species can eat it.

**Input and presentation:** tap-to-add and tap-to-mix must complete the youngest round. A later spoon drag may follow the finger and animate a swirl, but cannot require circles, speed or duration; it uses the shared drag cleanup/cancellation contract. Touch-down depresses the chosen pot or spoon immediately; a pentatonic plip and visible ripple support hearing-off play. Every child target stays at least 100 logical units after scaling. Keep the corner pet clear; if a large chef pet replaces it, tapping it repeats the instruction.

**Completion/accounting:** the shell awards one sticker for a completed round, helped or independent. Level 1 has zero misses/hints and is not evidence of recipe mastery; use a one-level toddler window. Levels 2–5 count unsuccessful deliberate Serve checks, never adding, undoing, predictions, mixing or creative choices. After two misses on a bowl, show one persistent useful-action glow and count one hint; explicit Help offers a pictured next action and marks supported completion. Undo and restart remain available from any nonterminal state. No timed stirring, kitchen fires, angry customers or disappearing dishes.

**Replay variety:** seeded swaps among a small reviewed pool of prepared ingredient pictures and optional decorations; constraints and allowed changes stay the same within a level. Do not equate a paint color-mix with a food reaction. The definition must enumerate every allowed result so art, words, how-to and ghost finger agree. No kept dish is promised until it has an approved bounded creation format.

## Pet Kitchen extension packet

Keep the existing fraction game and level IDs. Define one appended **Equivalent measures** round before any new scaling ladder: the recipe needs **one common-unit cup**; available tools are a half-cup and a quarter-cup, with an undo for every pour. Two halves, four quarters or a half and two quarters all satisfy it. Each tool's label is replayable speech and a shape-marked fraction strip; the bowl and tools use the same whole. This is a discrete quantity model, not another motor-precision pour exercise or real recipe. Oversupply is recoverable; only Serve checks miss, with help showing a useful removal/addition.

Next define **Scale every ingredient** with a visible two-column batch table, such as `[2, 1]` becoming `[6, 3]` for three batches. Later a rational case may use `[1, 1/2]` becoming `[3/2, 3/4]` at scale `3/2`, always naming the cup unit. Show the same multiplier on both ingredients. Do not claim real baking, seasoning, cook times or equipment sizes scale this way. Test all equivalent representations using integer quarter-units or exact rationals, valid recoveries and consistent units. This adds decisions beyond the already-built double/halve levels; it does not reopen those as missing features.

## Picnic Pantry first case and older ladder

**First complete case to define:** pantry has **4 berry tokens, 2 oat tokens and 2 apple tokens**. Three original pretend dish cards each serve two pictured seats: Swirl uses `(2 berry, 1 oat, 0 apple)`; Crunch uses `(0, 1, 2)`; Apple Bowl uses `(1, 0, 2)`. Choose **two different dishes** for four seats. Swirl + Crunch and Swirl + Apple Bowl both fit. Crunch + Apple Bowl would need four apples, but there are two: the check highlights that shared demand and leaves the plan editable. These token costs and names are invented puzzle data, not edible recipes or nutrition claims.

Tap a dish card to select/unselect it; a requirement tray previews totals and remaining supplies. **Lay the picnic** checks the plan; two feasible menus must be accepted without a hidden favorite. The result draws the chosen dishes, not a generic success meal. Supplies are never consumed persistently. All edits and optional comparisons are free; only unsuccessful deliberate plan checks count misses, with a useful replacement after two and a supported full-plan reveal available. No guest becomes distressed while waiting.

**Deeper rungs:** (1) two distinct dishes with spare supply; (2) choose batch counts to reach a visible serving total; (3) combine two explicit pictured guest preferences while preserving alternatives; (4) revise one changed pantry item and compare with the original plan; (5) make a solvable case for a partner, or voluntarily minimize unused tokens after a valid finish. Dietary needs/allergens are not coded as ordinary preference puzzles; those need separate review. Do not add timed production, appliance scheduling or grocery packing here: those overlap Village Planner and Toy Drawer.

The solver enumerates legal card/batch combinations, checks aggregate demand against supply, ensures a first-case pair of distinct solutions, and finds a reachable repair from any allowed selection. A hint names the actual shortage and an improving edit. Puzzle generation uses seeded RNG; exhaustive finite-state properties prove feasibility and reject contradictory requests. For 11–12 use proposed top `school` levels; for 13+ and parents audit a `grownup` registration and Lantern Lake capacity. If adding a destination is needed, resolve that before implementation rather than shrinking controls.

## Experience, co-play and off-screen connection

The first unsupported action should be obvious: a prepared-ingredient pot above an empty bowl responds to a touch, without reading a recipe or picking an entire menu. Present only the tools used in the round. Keep the card visible, replayable and truthful for that level; finger demonstrations must perform the same inputs. A spoon swish and contents changing together communicate the action with sound off. Do not infer physical stirring ability from a tap bot.

Co-play can begin without multiplayer infrastructure: one person requests a bowl from a visible card; the other makes it, then swaps. An older child can explain two feasible pantry menus to a parent; neither needs private information. Explicit paired progression, controller face-offs and seat credit wait for the existing family-table decisions.

The **off-screen proposal** is to enact a picture procedure with age-reviewed pretend kitchen materials, then let someone else describe the result. No quantities to eat, small-parts list, real utensils or cooking instructions are supplied here. A future real Cook Together card belongs in the plain-HTML grown-ups' page and the `off-screen-companions` session, with a qualified food-safety review and caregiver-controlled optional jobs. Real participation, enjoyment and transfer require observation; a completed screen bowl proves none of these.

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Identity and first scope:** recommend a Recipe Workshop 1–3 definition, then a check that transformation/assembly is distinct enough from Pet Kitchen, Scoop Shop and Ready for Adventure. If the definition only counts portions or orders cards, fold it into an existing game. The decision to build/register and final band windows remain separate from this research.
2. **Named food-safety/content reviewer:** needed before practical recipes, food handling, knife/heat instructions, allergy/dietary guidance, infant feeding advice or printed cooking cards ship. The study drafts none. Screen-only pretend-play definitions can proceed; recognizable food representations and any implied safety need review before a cooking claim. Include culturally knowledgeable contributors before presenting a named culture's recipe as representative.
3. **Older placement:** retain today's `school`/`grownup` split pending the existing middle-band decision; audit Cozy Village/Lantern Lake fit and choose an existing destination before registration. Parent appeal is an open person check, not established by choosing a factory-game precedent.
4. **Later scope:** persistent recipes/dishes, real food-science experiments and credited multiplayer need separate definitions. None is necessary for a complete first bowl or pantry case.
