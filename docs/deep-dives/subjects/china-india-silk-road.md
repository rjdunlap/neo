# Early China, India and the Silk Road: a day's journey at a time

**Subject:** `china-india-silk-road` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M, G ([definitions](../subjects.md#the-audiences)) · **Date:** 2026-10-18 · **Session:** `claude/subject-china-india-silk-road`, [PR #593](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/593).

Research and proposals only. **Define Caravan Days first:** load a camel, reach the next water, plan a road one day's walk at a time, then swap at each stop so the right thing arrives at the far end. Its ladder follows what made the long roads across Asia work: nobody went the whole way, goods changed hands at stopping places a day apart, the desert and the mountains set where those places could be, and some things (paper, a puzzle, a game, a number written with an empty column) travelled further than any merchant. Each level's action *is* that structure, not a costume on a quiz. The inventions that travelled extend games that already own stamping, weighing, grouping and sequencing. Early China and the Indus cities appear through what people made and measured, never through rulers, armies or beliefs. The youngest bands get a camel that kneels and a bale that goes on, with no claim that an infant learns history. Older play adds prices that grow with distance, a planner's road with a well every day's walk, a dated journey to reason about, a source-limits case and a grown-up merchant's puzzle. No runtime band, zone, game or system is added here.

### Real-history boundaries

- **Living countries, living cultures.** China, India, Pakistan, Iran, Uzbekistan, Iraq and their neighbours are home to billions of people today. The island says *long ago, on the long road from the east* and *long ago, in the cities by the Indus*, never "China is silk" or "India is spices". Kolam, pachisi, kite festivals and tea are living practices: a reviewer from the culture concerned before any line is spoken or printed.
- **No religion.** Buddhism's spread (California 6.5.5, 6.6.8, 7.3.1), Hinduism, Confucianism, Daoism and Islam (7.2) are outside the island's scope. A reviewed grown-up line may say that stories and beliefs travelled the road too; the child's screen carries goods and crafts.
- **No conquest.** The Qin unification, the Mongol campaigns, the "Aryan invasions", fighter kites and flaming kites, gunpowder: out. The road's dangers in play are thirst, a closed pass and a lost bale, never people who mean harm (the `safety` row owns that boundary).
- **Labour and enslavement.** The Grand Canal was dug with conscripted labour; enslaved people were among what was traded along these routes (*recalled*; the Silk Road page's opened portion did not cover it); Akbar's living pachisi board used enslaved girls as pieces. Younger rungs omit all of it. The M/G case may state it in one reviewed sentence (decision 5); never "trade was only cloth and spice".
- **Disease.** Plague moved with people along the road (D2.Geo.12). A reviewed M note for the grown-up card at most; no game.
- **Names and scripts.** The child's oases have island names; real cities, dates and the name "Silk Road" (a German coinage of 1877) appear in the M rung's spoken clues and on the grown-up card. The Indus script is undeciphered, so there is no "write your name in Indus"; Brahmi digits and counting rods are shown as an empty column, not as a quiz about who invented zero.
- **Every historical line spoken or printed needs a named reviewer** (a historian of Central Asian or Indian Ocean trade, or a KS2 educator who teaches the Indus or Shang depth study). Rule tests check reachability, sums and solvability; they cannot check history.

## What fits, and what does not

- **Fits:** a camel that kneels and carries; the next water within reach; a road planned a day at a time; a swap at each stop so the right thing arrives; prices that grow with distance; mountain versus sand; wells placed by a planner; a dated journey reasoned about; a basin two scholars read differently; a merchant's ledger for grown-ups. Each is a fun action whose consequence is the learning.
- **Fits as a mode or note:** block print (Stamp Studio), market weights (Seesaw Balance), bead frame (Lasso Loops), the silkworm's thread (Story Steps), dress for the road (Weather Wardrobe), a provenance line (Tangram Town), a kite (whoever owns wind), a pound lock (Rain Garden).
- **Does not fit:** emperors, dynasties and dates as answers; the Great Wall as a building race; armies, conquests, bandits and fighter kites; gods, monks and temples as a game; "the four great inventions" as a quiz; a dress-up of living cultures; a grid of countries; a trade race with scarcity, debt or ruin; any claim that one civilization "gave" another its ideas.

**Mistakes and accounting:** level 1 counts nothing. "Can't tell" at level 8, when it is the right answer, is a correct answer, never a dodge.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is patting a camel, not answering a question. Touch-down kneels the camel or lifts the bale at once; the pet counts bales aloud and names each oasis as the camel arrives. Palette colours for sand, sky, a green oasis and the city's blue tiles; no sepia filter. Oases are landmarks a child can tell apart; the road is one drawn line; suns are large and countable; the waterskin is a bar of sun-sized drops. The camel's walk is the reward: a sway, bells, dust puffs, a long slurp at the water. Portrait keeps the road across the upper half, the camel and bales below it and the pet bottom-left; Go and Done are at least 100 units and off the pet. This is not a music game: pick an existing compatible island style in the definition.

Replay varies the oases' order, the legs' suns, the goods and the wants, never the rules. Nothing accrues; nothing is owed. Round completion uses `ctx.finish`; cleanup releases taps, drags and timers.

**Creature-world fit (proposals for the rework, not promises).** The camel is a natural creature to meet on the road and keep at the creature home (RW05's collection), with its two humps and sealable nostrils as the journal's discoveries. A block-printed cloth from Stamp Studio could become its saddle blanket (customization through making). Each oasis reached could stay lit on the home's map as a permanent, non-gating record of roads travelled. An RW07 adventure could ask *What will carry our bale across the sand?* and send a child to Caravan Days 2–3 for a bounded result. None of this is required for the first slice.

**Co-play:** crier and driver (above). **Off screen, proposed:** *I packed my camel* needs no review; *Chaupar at home* and *A paper kite* wait on review (a player from the culture for the rules; string, scissors and small parts for under-threes).

## Decisions for the developer

1. **Approve the island-names rule.** The child's oases have island names and the road is "the long road from the east, long ago"; real cities, dates and the name "Silk Road" (with its 1877 coinage) appear in level 8's spoken clues and on the grown-up card. 3. **The Swap Meet and Relay Woods boundary.** Levels 4–5 as Caravan Days, or as a Swap Meet *long road* mode once Swap Meet exists. Lean: define them here, cross-listed, and let the Swap Meet definition claim them if its chain engine fits; never two relay engines.
4. **The Ledger's home.** Caravan Days level 9 for 13+ via `grownup` in `bands`, or a separate `GROWNUP_GAMES` puzzle in Lantern Lake with a thirteenth-game capacity test. Lean: separate, as the river study leans for Two Tablets.
5. **Labour, enslavement and hardship depth.** Younger rungs omit them. Decide whether the M/G *What was the basin?* case and the grown-up card state in one reviewed sentence that enslaved people were among what moved on these roads and that the Grand Canal was dug by conscripted labour, or defer. 6. **Name a reviewer** (a historian of Central Asian or Indian Ocean trade, or a KS2 educator who teaches the Indus or Shang depth study) for every spoken or printed historical line: the relay, the caravanserai spacing, the camel, paper's dated journey, block printing, the Harappan weights, the basin debate, the edicts (with their caveat) and the tangram line. 7. **Religion stays out.** Confirm that Buddhism's spread (California 6.5.5, 6.6.8, 7.3.1), Hinduism, Confucianism and Islam are not covered, and whether a reviewed grown-up line may say "stories and beliefs travelled too". Kolam is *needs review*.

10. **The kite's owner.** A toddler kite is a toy with a provenance line; decide whether the weather/water or energy/waves study owns it, or whether it is dropped. Lean: a note to those studies, no slice here.
