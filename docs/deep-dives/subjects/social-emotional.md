## First game's proposed ladder

**Playdate Workshop** has six proposed levels; first definition/build packet is only 1–3. Suggested future ranges: preschool 1–1, pre-K 1–3, school 3–6; no lap/toddler/grownup registration. These ranges need a band/land audit, not a new runtime band. Every scene uses authored pictured requests and deterministic material rules, with seeded character/art permutations. Requirements stay the same when the cast changes.

| Level | First complete round / what changes | Rule evidence required when built / cost |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **1: Stamp and pass** | Two friends each ask for one star on their own page. One stamp pad, two pages. Pick either friend first, stamp, pass, stamp; see two finished pages. The pictured requests and three-step trace stay up | Both orders produce exactly the two requested marks; no page or tool lost; undo restores material ownership. Small scene once interaction exists |
| **2: Two ways to make** | Both friends ask for a joint banner. Choose alternate stamping with one pad **or** a shared roller; operate that plan and watch the same requested banner form | Both authored plans finish; action traces differ; irrelevant repeated taps cannot add extra progress. Medium alternative-tool art/action |
| **3: My page, our plan** | One friend wants a joint banner; the other asks to keep their own page. They explicitly accept either making separate pages beside each other or doing the two projects in turns. Choose and operate either plan | Accept both agreements and either legal starting actor. A joint-page-only draft leaves the separate-page request visibly unmet. Help addresses that request, not a preferred moral answer. Medium |
| **4: Mend the mark** | An accidental roller mark crosses the other page. Ask what repair is wanted; add a pictured patch or start a fresh page, both explicitly acceptable, then finish the creations | Both repairs preserve the unaffected page; replay shows the event and repair; no blame inference from a face. Medium authored mishap |
| **5: A changed request** | After preview, one friend says they want to watch rather than stamp. Reassign the operator or choose separate play, then run the revised plan | Change only the stated requirement; re-evaluate all plans, with at least two feasible endings and an exact unmet-request trace. Medium |
| **6: Make another agreement** | Two visible preferences conflict in the first proposal. Select a character-approved alternative (turn order, separate space or a different material), then compare the first and revised outcomes | All accepted plans have a lawful material trace and explicit agreement; no forced apology, touch or smiling endpoint. Comparison is optional; one feasible plan earns completion. Medium–large content packet |

**Rules to freeze in the definition:** separate physical state (tool/page/marks), each stated requirement, optional preferences, a draft plan and the executed trace. `Try plan` previews consequences; `Make it` runs the visible steps through the ordinary touch controls. Requirements concern the fictional request, not a guessed mental state. Ask/replay always reveals the same authored request, never a randomly reassigned answer. Undo/replay restore the exact round. Every authored case must have multiple allowed orders/plans where promised, and every offered tool/action must have a defined result. A small exhaustive enumerator is sufficient for this finite domain; do not build a reusable relationship engine.

**Mistakes, experiments and support:** assembling or previewing any plan, asking a character and undoing are free experiments (`misses = 0`), as with an unscored prediction. If a plan omits a stated requirement, the character calmly names it and the corresponding request gains an outline; the draft remains editable. No boing for a plausible social alternative. Two incomplete previews offer a persistent glow on the unmet request, not an automatic answer; a corrective hint that supplies the next action counts once per decision. A separate Help me make it path supplies both planning and enactment help, records at least two hints for the round, and finishes through the shell with the usual sticker. This keeps guided completion out of the smooth-round signal; it does not certify independent social skill. Exact support transitions and non-duplicating hint accounting need rule tests before building.

**Finish:** once the chosen agreement's requested creations/actions are enacted, a large pictured Finish is available. Finishing awards the ordinary one sticker through `ctx.finish`; replay/compare is optional. Home always permits immediate departure without a reward or obligation. The first slice stores no art, promises, preferences or personal feelings between visits.

## Common Ground: first older-family case

Freeze a **2×3 board** of chunky space tiles, a quiet drawing activity and a noisy pretend drum activity. Each occupies one tile; they cannot be orthogonally adjacent while used in the same scene. One character requires drawing at a window (top row); the other accepts any drum location. Preferences, explicitly optional: drawing near the shelf (top-left), drums near the doorway (bottom-left). Put the cards down, hear each account, preview the two activities, revise if desired, and keep any feasible plan. Two examples: drawing top-left/drums bottom-middle honors the shelf preference; drawing top-right/drums bottom-left honors the doorway preference. Diagonal separation is permitted **as a stated toy rule**, not an acoustic claim.

Both optional preferences cannot hold together under that rule. Show which preference each plan serves, and accept either; neither person is “more deserving.” Alternative with activities in separate pictured scenes is a later temporal mode, not a hidden answer in this first board. The six board cells alone are not a game: authored requests, enactment and the comparison must ship with the rule solver. Tests must enumerate all legal placements, validate the two example plans and the incompatibility claim, and explain each rejection from the public rules.

Deeper sessions: (1) change one request and revise, (2) distinguish a character's assumption from a confirmed requirement, (3) a genuinely infeasible case where showing the conflict and choosing an extra space or separate scene is a valid ending. Do not hide necessities or require consensus on optional preferences. No real clock, majority vote over a person's boundary, moral ranking, generalized scheduler or online player matching. Human trials must establish whether an older child/parent wants to play this rather than merely finding it correct.

## Experience and presentation

The first unsupported action is choosing a friend or pictured plan, not reading a response menu. Touch-down depresses the card/tool; stamping makes an immediate visible mark and a soft synthesized sound. The fun payoff is a pair of different creations, the roller's chunky trail and the puppets enacting the chosen arrangement. Faces and speech can express several feelings through a completed round; no happy-meter or needy pet waits after departure.

Keep both request bubbles visible beside their owners, distinguished by shape and portraits as well as color. Speech replays from the pet/character; no player speech is required. Free material taps explore; only explicit plan/run controls enact steps. The ghost finger must use those same controls and show a valid alternative after a deliberate incomplete preview. At least three authored art/request variations must obey each level's rules before the content pool expands.

Use existing Pixi art/palette, tap/drag, tween, voice and scene cleanup helpers. All child targets stay at least 100 logical units in both orientations, clear of the pet/Home/help; the six-cell older board must fit alongside requests and controls before registration. Grown-up-only controls can use their established 64-unit minimum. No imported images/sound, physics engine, new save fields or typed cross-game results are needed. Original authored speech belongs in `voice-script.ts`; the how-to must describe the selected level and demonstrate supported completion. These are future implementation requirements, not checks performed here.

Co-play uses two useful jobs: voice/point to a request and operate a plan, then swap. The pet fills either role in solo play. Respect the existing [family-role decisions](../../WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER.md#waiting-on-the-developer) before promising multi-profile credit or couch play. Off-screen continuation: optionally give two puppets different pictured requests and enact two endings with pretend materials; let either person decline. A released parent card must distinguish invented stories from practical conflict advice and credit its sources appropriately.

## Decisions and open questions

1. **Game identity and placement:** definition can proceed now. Confirm Playdate Workshop's operated-material mechanic warrants a new game; if it collapses to choose/watch/revise, implement within Story Theater. Audit proposed ranges/land capacity before registration. Common Ground's 11–12/13+ routing uses existing bands only after its own audit; no middle-band change is authorized.
2. **Content review:** recommend an early-childhood/SEL educator and accessibility reader review the authored requests, several acceptable endings, body/possession boundaries and existing Feelings Faces assumptions before implementation. This is a recommendation for fictional screen stories, not a new blanket approval requirement. Practical mental-health guidance, physical calming instructions and real conflict/parenting advice require a named qualified reviewer before release; none is drafted here.
3. **Family and older appeal:** compare the actual Common Ground round with Village Planner/Picnic Pantry before choosing which to build. Observe willing replay and explanation of an alternative; bot completion cannot establish either. Page cards belong to the unstarted off-screen-companions subject; no second card system or schedule.
4. **Claims:** no diagnosis, behavior score, mood history, forced personal disclosure or therapeutic marketing. Do not interpret completion/help use as empathy or emotional maturity. Existing game's event/helper mappings need their own reviewed change, preserving old levels and pins.
