# Engineering design: designing for everyone who will use it, under constraints, and repairing what exists

**Subject:** `engineering-design` · **Audiences:** L, T, P, K, S, U, M and G as [defined in the tracker](../subjects.md#the-audiences) · **Date:** 2026-10-17 · **Session:** `claude/subject-engineering-design`, [PR #527](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/527). Scoped by the [follow-up gap review](additional-subjects.md), which asked this study for "different users' stated needs, access and repair/reuse alongside structural constraints", and the [further gap review](further-gaps.md) (repair and practical making; structures and materials).

**Research and proposals only.** The [inventions study](inventions.md) (PR #392) already owns the ask–test–revise loop for one need: **Gadget Bench** meets a neighbor's need with a portable gadget, reads the first failure point on a test path and revises; **Workshop Cases** combines the best features of three prototypes. This study does not repeat that. It adds the part of engineering design the island has nothing for: **one shared design that has to work for every user at once**, inside a fixed space or budget, where a change that helps one user can block another. Recommend one new identity, **Welcome Ways**: build the entrance to a friend's house from big parts (steps, gentle and steep ramps, a landing, a rail, a wide door, a perch), then tap Try and watch each visiting creature use it. The first one who cannot get in stops at the exact part that stopped them, and says why. Its users are creatures with different bodies and ways of moving (a hopper, a long low swimmer, a glider), which suits the [creature-world rework](../../rework/README.md#creature-collection-and-expansion): species briefs already carry locomotion, and the payoff is a new friend who can now visit and uses what she built. Everything else extends what exists: repair as a **Gadget Bench** level, structures and parts budgets in **Wobble Works**, and the rework's "what will carry our garden across the pond?" project as a **Sink or Float** level. No game, band, zone, land registration or save format is added.

## What people can do, and what remains uncertain

- **Learning from design failure.** Johnson, Kelly and Cunningham, "Failure and Improvement in Elementary Engineering" ([*Journal of Research in STEM Education* 7(2), 2021](https://j-stem.net/index.php/jstem/article/view/101); abstract opened, full text not read) analyzed video of eight elementary classes and proposed a model classifying types and causes of failure and the conditions under which failure leads to improvement. Lottero-Perdue's ASEE papers (2015–2024, [peer.asee.org](https://peer.asee.org/elementary-student-teams-design-failure-experiences-and-factors-that-affect-their-opportunities-to-learn-from-failure-fundamental); **known from search summaries only**, pages returned 403 and a downloaded PDF could not be read) report that children bring everyday meanings of "fail" from sport and home, that productive responses include acknowledging the failure, trying again and analyzing it, and that unproductive ones include giving up and changing a design without a plan. A 2024 paper reportedly found that mid-build testing and adult interventions can help or hinder. **Implication:** Try is free and constant, the failure is shown at one part with its reason, and the voice never calls a design or a child a failure; it says "Roly can't roll up a step".
- **Preschool engineering in block play.** Bagiati and Evangelou (2016), "Practicing engineering while building with blocks", *European Early Childhood Education Research Journal* 24(1) (**known from reference lists only**), and Bairaktarova et al. on precursors of engineering thinking in free play (search summary). No finding is used beyond "preschool block building is studied as early engineering".
- **Making from a demonstration.** Beck et al. (2014) (reused from the inventions study) found under-sixes rarely invent a tool unaided but readily make one after seeing it made. **Implication:** the first rungs choose between shown parts; the pet demonstrates on request.
- **Gaps.** Nothing opened here establishes when children can hold two users' needs in mind at once, how children reason about access or bodies unlike theirs, or tween and parent appeal for this kind of puzzle. Those are hypotheses for observation.

## What fits

- **Fits as a new game:** Welcome Ways. Its decision (a shared structure that every user must pass, in a fixed space, where parts help some users and block others) is not in any built game or proposal, and the creature cast gives it varied, fictional users. Its fun is the parade: each friend hops, slides or glides in and the house fills.
- **Fits as levels of existing or proposed games:** Gadget Bench *Fix-It Day* (repair and reuse); Wobble Works fewer-pieces and sagging-bridge rungs (structures, materials, budget); Sink or Float level 8 *Garden Raft* (the rework's pond project); Block Tower stays as is.
- **Does not fit:** a scored imitation of disability (a child "playing" a wheelchair user against a clock); real accessibility rules or building advice; budgets as currency or a score; a collapse as the fun; a design that fails being called a failure of the child; "fewest parts" required to finish.
- **Needs review:** any human user with an access need (a wheelchair, a cane, a pram), any page card about noticing access in the real world, and any wording about disability. The [follow-up audit](additional-subjects.md) asked for lived-experience input before representation; until then users are fictional creatures and their needs are stated as body facts ("Roly rolls, so Roly can't climb steps"), never as deficits.

## A first game's ladder: Welcome Ways

Working ID `welcome-ways`, **not registered**.

### The model, stated once

An entrance is a short path of **cells** from the ground to a door at height 0–3, drawn side-on in a slight three-quarter view so that it has a front row and, from level 5, a back row; a landing at the end of the front row turns the path onto the back row going the other way (a switchback). Each cell holds at most one part: a **step** (rise 1, run 1), a **gentle ramp** (rise 1 over 2 cells), a **steep ramp** (rise 1 over 1 cell, introduced at level 5 as the part that fits a tight space), a **landing** (flat, turns the path), a **rail** (on the near or far side of a run), a **perch** (a ledge at door height). The door is **narrow** or **wide**. Each visitor has fixed rules, stated as body facts:

| Visitor (provisional) | Moves by | Can use | Cannot use |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Sprout** (rework species) or Bunny | Hopping | Steps, either ramp | — (the easy friend) |
| **Roly** (a round critter) | Rolling | Gentle ramps, landings | Steps; steep ramps (rolls back) |
| **River** (rework species, long and low) | Sliding on its belly | Ramps, landings that are two cells long | Steps; a landing one cell long (it can't turn) |
| **Wobble** (a young critter, slow and wobbly) | Walking | Steps, landings, a single ramp, and a run of two or more ramps with a rail beside it | A run of two or more ramps without a rail |
| **Cloud glider** (rework species) | Gliding | A perch at door height with no rail on its open side | Paths alone |
| **Bear** (big) | Walking | Anything | A narrow door |

**Early sockets.** In levels 1–4 every socket is a **two-cell slot** holding one rise-1 piece, so every choice shares a footprint: the gentle ramp, or a **step block** (a step at its front, a flat top). No steep ramp appears before level 5, so the first ramp a toddler places always lets Roly in.

A Try walks the visitors in a fixed order. The first visitor who cannot pass a cell stops there; that cell flashes and the pet says the reason. A design is **accepted** when every visitor in the round reaches the door, within the round's cells and plank count.

**Mistakes and hints (proposed; accounting is a developer decision).** Following Chain Reaction and Habitat Helpers, a **Try is an observation, never a miss**, at every level: a failed Try shows the stopping cell and its reason. After two Tries that stop the same visitor at the same cell, the cell glows with a part that would let them pass (from an accepted design) and one hint is recorded. "Show me" on the pause sheet does the same and counts one hint. Lean: no misses at all, so adaptation reads hints only, as Chain Reaction does; the alternative is to count a Welcome (invite everyone) on a design that has not passed a Try as a miss, as Gadget Bench's Give does. The round finishes when every visitor is inside; the new friend waves from the window and, in the rework, the house's visitor can come to her home.

**Exploration.** Placing, swapping and removing parts is free. Level 1 has no wrong part.

### A rule test

For every level plan and every seeded round: (1) an accepted design exists inside the cells and plank count; (2) every tray part is used by at least one accepted design or is a stated distractor with a named visitor it blocks; (3) a Try reports the **first** blocking cell along the visitors' order, and its reason line matches the rule that blocked; (4) from level 6, at least two distinct accepted designs exist, and the naive design (rails on both sides; steps everywhere) fails for a named visitor; (5) at level 7, the starting entrance welcomes exactly the two old friends and blocks the new one; (6) at levels 8–9, the plank budget is feasible and a design that ignores the shared landing is over budget; (7) the glow from a hint is a part of some accepted design reachable by replacing incorrect parts (see the levels 1–4 definition). Levels 1–4 also check that every slot choice shares one footprint and that no steep ramp appears. Enumeration is small (at most about 12 cells and 6 part kinds), so a brute-force solver in the test is enough.

### Cost

M: a new `logic.ts` with a path walker and a solver for tests; parts drawn with `Graphics`; visitor walk cycles. Reuse `drag.ts` sockets and Chain Reaction's run-and-replay structure. A ghost-finger `autotouch()` that places an accepted design and taps Try. Placement: Tinker Lab holds 13 games, so a `landLayout` capacity check is required (never smaller targets); the alternative is Cozy Village (12, the houses are there) or the proposed Maker Harbor.

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action:** level 1, dragging the only part into the only glowing gap; a 120-unit part, a 140-unit socket, touch-down lift and a click.
- **Touch-down feedback:** the part lifts and the visitors look at it; a part over a cell it cannot sit in turns grey before release.
- **Replay variety:** seeded house colours, door heights, which friends visit and tray order; the same level has several house sites.
- **The parade is the reward:** each friend's own way in (a hop, a slide, a glide onto the perch), a door chime per friend, the house light going on when all are in.
- **Speech:** reasons are short body facts ("River is long. River needs a long landing to turn."), never "wrong" or "failed". The instruction names the visitors with pictures; nothing needs reading.
- **Off-screen:** build an entrance from blocks or a cereal box for two toys that move differently, then let each try it (card wording needs review).
- **Co-play:** guest and builder; the grown-up voices a friend's need from a card.
