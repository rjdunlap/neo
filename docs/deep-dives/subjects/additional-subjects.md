## Writing and typing follow-up

**Developer follow-up, 2026-10-16:** writing has several distinct learning goals. `writing-composition` already covers messages, lists, stories, opinions and revision; Letter Trails is the starting point for handwriting. Add **keyboarding-editing** and **spelling-sentences** as unstarted research rows, with their boundaries recorded in the tracker. These are additional to the three subjects above, not completed studies.

- **Composition:** make something another person can understand; begin with pictured choices/word tiles, then authored sentences and revision. Keyboard fluency must not gate creative composition.
- **Handwriting:** letter formation, then independent production/spacing where appropriate; extend the Letter Trails research first. A trace completed on glass is not evidence of fluent paper handwriting.
- **Keyboarding/editing:** find a letter to label a picture, type a short invitation, then move the cursor and revise it. Research touch-screen and hardware keyboards separately: a full simulated keyboard cannot meet the child’s 100-unit target rule on the normal game view. Real keyboard input/DOM editing needs its own interaction and layout definition. No words-per-minute target, disappearing text or round-ending timer.
- **Spelling/sentences:** construct a word, choose a meaningful sentence, then revise capitals, punctuation or agreement. Inspect Word Monsters/Read & Do before choosing a new game. Spoken replay and accepted alternatives must support the task; creative choices and language varieties are not automatically mistakes.

Opened primary sources: [CCSS W.3.6](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/W/3/) explicitly includes keyboarding in supported digital writing; [W.4.6](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/W/4/) includes a longer keyboarding expectation. These motivate investigating keyboarding, not importing a page-length requirement into a round. [Grade 1 Language](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/L/1/) covers printing letters, sentence grammar, capitalization, punctuation and spelling. Full age progressions, instructional evidence and keyboard ergonomics remain unresearched here. No implementation or device checks.
