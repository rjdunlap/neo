# Rainbow Fingers deep dive

*ID `rainbow-fingers` · bands lap, toddler, preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–7 on 2026-10-10 · session `claude/roadmap-item-14-game-content-0zaz9u` ([PR 107](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/107))*

## The game today

A sheet of paper on a wooden easel, and a finger. At levels 1–2 she paints freely: a drag lays a rainbow whose hue turns as the finger travels, the note rises and falls with the finger's height, and a quick tap grows a flower. From level 3 the paper holds coloring-page pictures and the pet asks for one at a time. She picks a paint pot, drags over the picture, and when 70% of it is covered (`PAINTED`) it fills in neatly and the next picture is asked. A free painting can be kept on the treehouse picture board; a coloring page keeps nothing. The learning purpose is fine-motor control and creativity (`skills`: fine-motor, creativity) at levels 1–2, then color words, remembering what color a thing is, and mixing two primaries. It lives in Paint Pier, first in its list (`rainbow-fingers`, `fluffy-salon`, `stamp-studio`, `pixel-pictures`).

| Level | Plan (`PLANS` in `logic.ts`) | Bands whose window includes it | What she decides |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | free: rainbow painting | lap, toddler | nothing: paint anywhere |
| 2 | pots: seven pots (rainbow and the six colors), each says its name | lap, toddler, preschool | which color; a different one for each stroke |
| 3 | named, 2 pictures: "Paint the sun yellow!" | toddler, preschool, pre-K | which pot the voice named |
| 4 | named, 3 pictures | preschool, pre-K | the same, three times |
| 5 | recall, 3 pictures: "Paint the apple! What color is it?" | preschool, pre-K, school | what color the thing is |
| 6 | mix, 2 pictures: "Paint the leaf green! Mix yellow and blue." | pre-K, school | which two primaries (the voice names them) |
| 7 | recall-mix, 1 picture: the pumpkin, "What color is it?" | school | the color from memory, then its two primaries |

The ranges are lap 1–2, toddler 1–3, preschool 2–5, pre-K 3–6 and school 5–7 (`LEVELS` in `src/games/rainbow-fingers/index.ts`).

**What a round draws from.** Six coloring pictures, one per color (`THINGS`): sun (yellow), apple (red), leaf (green), pumpkin (orange), grapes (purple) and blueberry (blue). Each is a union of circles with a code-drawn outline and some decoration. Levels 3–5 shuffle all six and take 2 or 3, so a page never repeats a color. Level 6 draws two of the only three pictures whose color is a mix (pumpkin, leaf, grapes), so it has three possible pages. Level 7 is the pumpkin alone, every time. Free painting is not drawn from anything: the first hue is random, a tap's flower color is random (six colors), and the pitch follows the height. The saved sticker is a palette with four seeded blobs of color and no pool, so new content cannot change a saved one.

**How painting works.** Stamps of a 24-unit brush are laid every 5 units along the stroke. Every second stamp counts as a dab on the asked-for picture, and only a dab in the right color counts toward 70%. A wrong color gives a boing and a spoken hint (`paint.wrong`: "That's red. The sun is yellow!"), at most one miss every four seconds; the second wrong dab on a picture glows the right pot (a hint); twelve seconds without progress on a picture also glows it, and counts a hint. With no pot chosen yet, a dab says "Find the yellow pot!" and counts nothing. Mixing levels build a bowl: tap one primary and it pours in (the voice says its name), tap a second and the bowl makes the mixed color with a sparkle (the voice says that name too); a third pour starts over; tapping the bowl empties it. The free levels show the hang-it-up button after five strokes, or after one stroke and 25 seconds; the picture levels after the last picture. A press that stays put and a drag under 40 units count as nothing.

**Couplings.** How-to card: a step for each band of levels, scoped with `LevelLine`s, a finish line and a note. It has a ghost finger (`touchDemo`, `autotouch()` through `paintStep`, `fillPath` and `scribble`). Voice: 12 lines (`paint.start`, `.pots`, `.rainbow`, `.ask`, `.what`, `.mix`, `.remix`, `.mixhow`, `.pick`, `.wrong`, `.done`, `.finished`) plus the shared `color.*` names. Saving: a free painting is kept as at most 320 normalized brush and flower marks (`PAINTING_MAX_MARKS`, never a bitmap; past the cap, every second mark is dropped and later dabs are sampled less often). Music `STYLES.paint`, not silenced, since the loop does not compete with a painter's notes. No couch entry, journal entry or picnic step. Browser: `early` (`EARLY_ONLY=paint`) plays the levels with real touches, `fingerdemo` plays each level's ghost finger, `howto` has its card. [IDEAS](../IDEAS.md) already holds *Mirror Brush* (a live-mirror mode of this game, "promoted") and a round-two row that built level 7.

**Known gaps** (read from the code and docs; none has been seen on a device unless said). The first is serious.

1. **Level 7 cannot be finished in the live game.** `buildPots()` builds the primaries and the bowl for both `'mix'` and `'recall-mix'` (`index.ts:254`), but `pick()` pours into the bowl only when `plan.mode === 'mix'` (`index.ts:289`). At level 7 a pot tap only raises the pot, sets the brush to that one primary and says "Red!". The bowl never fills, orange can never be made, and every dab on the pumpkin is a wrong color (it says "Mix red and yellow to make orange!" for as long as she tries). The frame button needs the last picture painted, so the round never ends, no sticker is awarded and no result is recorded, so adaptation can't move her either. `logic.test.ts` passes because its harness models pouring for both modes in its own copy of the rule, and the ghost finger loops on the same tap because `paintStep` sees an empty bowl and asks for the pot again. This fits the last [VERIFICATION](../VERIFICATION.md) entry for the level ("Rainbow Fingers: pumpkin recall and mixing"): `early` and `fingerdemo` at level 7 returned without a completion result, and were recorded as not a pass. Nothing was run in this session; this is read from the code. A school-band child (about six and up) lands on levels 5–7. Fix first, in the first build slice, with one shared `pours(mode)` rule that both the game and the test use.
2. **A child who only pats or slaps never finishes levels 1–2.** A tap grows a flower instead of counting as a stroke: `lift()` calls `bloom()` for a touch under 14 units and half a second, and counts `finished++` only for a drag over 40 units. The frame button needs five counted strokes (or one and 25 seconds). A press held without moving paints one dab and counts nothing too. A one-year-old's natural gestures are pat, slap and hold, so she may never see the end of the round, and gets no sticker, however much she paints.
3. **The pots are 92 units across.** Hit circles are `radius: 46` and the spacing is 92, under the 100-unit rule. Seven pots in a row are 644 units wide at the bottom centre; in a 768-wide portrait view that leaves little beside the bottom-left pet. Not checked by a screenshot.
4. **Hint and miss accounting differs from the rule.** The glow comes after twelve seconds of no progress and counts a hint, not only after two misses; the second wrong dab on a picture also glows. A miss counts at most every four seconds. The recall level tells the answer at the first wrong dab ("The apple is red!"), and at levels 6–7 `paint.mixhow` gives both the color and the recipe at the first miss, so level 7 can never be a real recall. The same question is [Splish Splash's decision 5](splish-splash.md#decisions-for-the-developer).
5. **Narrow pools.** Level 6 has three possible pages, and level 7 is one fixed page, so a school child sees the same leaf and pumpkin every round. Levels 3–5 show only one picture per color, so the child learns "the sun" more than "yellow".
6. **Fairness of "what color is it?"** An apple can be green and so can grapes, and a child who says so is told she is wrong. A coloring page with a red apple can't say that.
7. **Speech.** `{thing}` is the picture's raw id in every `paint.*` line, so a new picture needs a spoken name (and "orange" the fruit would read "Paint the orange orange!"). `paint.remix` repeats `paint.what`. Free painting never names a color; `paint.start` and `paint.done` are the only lines in levels 1–2, each a single wording.
8. **A tap in a coloring level blooms a random flower**, baked into the paint layer under the outline, so a pink flower can appear inside the apple. Cosmetic; it needs a look.
9. **Colors are the only cue.** Pots have no mark besides their paint, so a color-blind child can't tell the red pot from the green one in a recall level; the spoken name is the only other cue. Common figures put red-green color blindness at about one boy in twelve (not checked here).
10. **Person checks open:** lap level 1 with a real child ([roadmap](../ROADMAP.md#needs-a-person-or-a-device)): pat, slap or drag, and a palm-sized brush; whether a four-year-old recalls a pumpkin's color and how many misses it costs; whether the spoken "orange", "purple" and "green" mix lines read right on a device.

**Developmental sources** (secondhand; none checked against the original).

- **Scribbling.** Lowenfeld's model has a scribbling stage in three steps, disordered, controlled and named, at roughly 2–4 years in one source and 18 months to 3 or 1 to 4 in others ([MDPI 2025, not opened](https://www.mdpi.com/2227-7102/15/6/681/htm); [course text](https://socialsci.libretexts.org/Courses/Irvine_Valley_College/Creative_Development_in_Young_Child_(HD131)/03%3A_Artistic_Development)). Levels 1–2 are for that stage. Named scribbling, a child naming her picture after she makes it, is an important milestone (the *Name your painting* sketch).
- **Color words.** In a study of Chinese 3- to 6-year-olds black, red and white were named most accurately, and purple and orange least ([Lin et al., 2001](https://journal.psych.ac.cn/xlxb/CN/Y2001/V33/I4/46)). In a study of Turkish 48- to 84-month-olds red, yellow and blue were named best ([Özcan et al.](https://digibug.ugr.es/bitstream/handle/10481/79891/442-451_JETT1305040%2b%2bID%2b1127.pdf?sequence=1)). So the mixed colors the ladder ends on are the ones children name least well, and the spoken name of every mix matters.
- **Mixing and light.** A common misconception is that mixing lights and pigments gives the same results, and one article blames the red-yellow-blue wheel taught in elementary school ([the Physics Teacher article, image scan, not readable](https://sci-ed-ga.org/about/docs/Confusing%20Color%20Concepts%20Clarified%20The%20Physics%20Teacher.pdf); [a conference paper](https://library.iated.org/view/ANDRADE2019LEA)). The game stays with paint and says so.
- **Teachers.** Kindergarten art guides teach primary colors, then secondary colors by mixing, and add warm and cool in first grade ([Donna Young](https://donnayoung.org/art/color.htm), a teacher's site). The search found no Common Core standard for it; art standards are the National Core Arts Standards, which I did not open.

## What makes sense here

**Two jobs in one game.** Levels 1–2 are a toy and should stay one (*Toca Boca*, and IDEAS' own note that free painting can stay a simple toy). Levels 3–7 are a small course in color: say the name, remember the name, make the color. The quantity direction of 2026-10-10 fits both halves differently. The toy half gets **new kinds of page** (a fold down the middle) and **gestures that all count** (the first fix). The course half gets **a pool of pictures** (6 to 21), **more colors to make** (pink, brown) and **new decisions about mixing** (read the neighbors on a wheel, recall every recipe, match a sample).

**Fits.**

- **Fix the live level first.** Level 7 can't be finished and the lap round can't be ended by patting. Neither needs a decision, and every later slice depends on them.
- **A pool of pictures** (*Color Lab*, the coloring-page apps): 15 more code-drawn pictures (banana, strawberry, frog, whale, puddle…), drawn so each color has at least three, with a spoken name table and accepted alternatives for apple and grapes. Cheap per picture, large in effect.
- **A crease down the middle** (butterfly blots, symmetry apps): a seeded page at levels 1–2 where what she paints on one side appears on the other. The mirror does the work, so there is no prediction and nothing to get wrong, which is IDEAS' *Mirror Brush* in its safest form. The fold button and the "predict the unfolded half" step stay with Bug Builder's *Fold a Friend*.
- **Predict, mix, fill in the wheel** (the first-grade lessons): level 8, where the empty petal's neighbors are the recipe. Easier than recalling the pumpkin's color, but a new kind of thinking.
- **More colors to make**: pink from red and white, brown from all three. White is the first ingredient that isn't a primary, and the first one that makes a color lighter.
- **A story for each mix** (*Mouse Paint*, *Little Blue and Little Yellow*): a line like "Yellow and blue hug and make green!" for the first mix, with the real names spoken after.
- **A journal** of five mixes she has seen, the way Sink or Float files what floated.

**Does not fit** is under "Left out on purpose".

**Arc by band.** Lap: any gesture paints and counts, a palm-sized splat, a crease page now and then. Toddler: pots with their names, then "paint the sun yellow". Preschool: named colors and what color things are. Pre-K: the first mix (told). School: the wheel, pink and brown, recalling every recipe, and a match-the-sample level. Levels below and between (two pots, copy the picture) wait on the level-order decision.

## Proposed ladder

### Fresh content inside existing levels

All ready except where it says decision. The rule tests are named for each.

1. **Guards first** (slice 1 is the level 7 fix; slice 2 is the rest).
   - *Level 7.* Pour for both mix modes through one shared `pours(mode)` function in `logic.ts`, used by `pick()`, `buildPots()`, `paintStep` and the tests (gap 1). Test: for every mix mode, two pours of the recipe make the picture's color and the bowl; the shared function is what the harness calls.
   - *Every touch counts* (gap 2): a bloom, a hold and a drag each count toward the frame (a pure function `strokesCounted`), the frame comes after five touches, and a palm-size splat (bloom radius at least 50 units, so it is 100 across) is the tap's reaction. Test: any five touches of any kind show the frame; no touch never does; the splat meets the 100-unit rule.
   - *Pots* (gaps 3 and 9): hit circles of 52 (104 across) at a spacing of 104, a small mark on each pot's lid (dot, stripe, star, heart, swirl, ring) so color is not the only cue, and a layout that wraps to two rows when seven do not fit beside the pet. Test: `potLayout(w, h)` for four views gives centres at least 104 apart, none inside the pet's clearance box, all inside the view.
   - *Free-play nudge:* after eight seconds without a touch at levels 1–2, a blank spot sparkles and the pet wiggles, with no hint counted.
   - *Hint accounting* (decision 5).
2. **More pictures: 6 to 21.** Yellow: sun, **banana, chick, lemon**. Red: apple, **strawberry, ladybug, cherries**. Blue: blueberry, **whale, puddle**. Green: leaf, **frog, turtle, broccoli**. Orange: pumpkin, **carrot, basketball**. Purple: grapes, **plum, eggplant**. Each is a handful of circles (`Thing.circles`) and a decoration, so it is drawn in code and covered by the existing coverage and fill tests, which loop over `THINGS`. The draw changes: today it shuffles one picture per color; with several per color, `pickThings` first picks distinct colors, then one picture of each, so a page never shows two yellows (the existing distinct-color test stays and gets stricter). The mix pool grows from 3 to 9 pictures for orange, green and purple. Tests: every color has at least three pictures; a page's colors are distinct for 200 seeds at every count; every picture fits the layout's 280 by 270 slot; fills cover past `PAINTED` at every scale. A new picture also needs its spoken name (item 3) and a recall-worthy color: a thing goes into the recall levels only if a four-year-old would give one color for it.
3. **Words and fairness.** A spoken-name table for every picture (gap 7), with a test that every id has a name and that no name equals a color word. Accepted alternatives for recall: apple red or green, grapes purple or green; painting a thing in an accepted color fills it in that color, counts no miss, and the voice says "Green apples are real too!". Test: every alternative paints without a miss; the told answer is always the first color. Three wordings for each of `paint.start`, `paint.done`, `paint.finished` and praise.
4. **Name the color she is painting.** At levels 1–2, a stroke that lifts says the rainbow name nearest its last hue ("Orange!"), at most once every six seconds, and a splat names its flower color. Cost S: the `color.*` lines exist. Test: the hue-to-name function covers all six names, in rainbow order, over 0–360°.
5. **Fold-down-the-middle pages** (levels 1–2, about one round in three by seed, no button): a dashed crease and faint wings on the paper; every dab on one side is mirrored to the other in the same color; a dab on the crease isn't doubled. The kept painting records the mirrored marks too (they are ordinary marks, so the saved shape does not change; the 320-mark cap halves them as it already does). Cost M. Tests: the mirror is an involution; a dab on the axis maps to itself; every marked position stays inside 0..1; the ghost finger's scribble leaves paint on both sides. Retire *Mirror Brush*'s first half from IDEAS and the roadmap (see the notes in the docs update).
6. **Level 7 stays one page, or grows to three** (decision 3). Today it is a single page, so a school child's hardest level ends after one picture. Lean: leave it a one-page *first recall of a recipe* and let level 11 carry the three-recipe recall, so the two are different decisions. If it grows instead, its pages are the pumpkin, then one green and one purple picture, and level 11 must include a pink or brown page (see its row). Test (if it grows): the pages are three distinct recipes and the first page's prompt names no color.
7. **A journal source** (decision 7): five entries filed when the bowl shows the mix: orange, green, purple (and pink and brown when those levels land). `RoundResult.discoveries` carries them, so a mix is filed only after she has poured it. Cost M: `journal.ts` gets a `'paint'` source, entries, lines and the test that checks each line against `RECIPES`.
8. **Co-play and off-screen.** Co-play: "Name the color and say what is that color in the room". Off-screen: yogurt or shaving-foam finger paint is there; add *a color hunt* (find three red things before the stroke), *mix two colors of play dough* and *a fold-and-press butterfly with a spoonful of paint* (the real thing the crease pages echo).

### Left out on purpose

- **Timers, scores, grades for a painting, "stay in the lines" marks and paint that dries or runs out.** The rules forbid them, and an outline here only tells her where the picture is.
- **Mixing light** (red and green make yellow, RGB). The game is paint, a grown-up's note says so, and light mixing is a different lesson.
- **Color-by-number grids and mirror-the-half puzzles:** Pixel Pictures' job (and Bug Builder's mirror wings).
- **Stamps, patterns and counting in paint:** Stamp Studio, Pattern Train and the counting games hold them.
- **Recorded narration** (*Draw and Tell*): parent recordings are a proposal.
- **Draw a Song** (*Kandinsky*): its own proposal in IDEAS.
- **AR, 3D, downloads of daily pages, paid packs, sharing:** nothing leaves the device and nothing is for sale.
- **A bigger toy** (layers, brush sizes, an eraser): levels 1–2 stay a simple toy.
- **A couch entry:** a calm, scoreless toy that doesn't suit a face-off; the co-play line covers it.
