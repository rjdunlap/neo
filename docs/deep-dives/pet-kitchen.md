# Pet Kitchen deep dive

*ID `pet-kitchen` · bands toddler, preschool, pre-K, school · levels 1–9 on 2026-10-19 (`24db4a79`) · session `claude/dive-pet-kitchen-finish` ([PR #622](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/622), resuming the claim in [PR #606](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/606))*

## The game today

A sandwich (or two) sits on the counter with a row of plates below, one per friend. She taps a cutting picture (halves, quarters, or thirds for the pizza), the whole splits into pieces on the tray, she drags pieces onto plates until every friend has the same, then taps the green arrow to serve. The recipe levels swap the counter for a recipe card and a bowl: tap a fruit to drop one in, and the bowl must hold double (or half) what the card shows. The learning purpose runs from equal shares of one whole (halves, quarters, thirds), through sharing two wholes and accepting either equal cut, to a named fraction of a whole (two quarters count as one half) and doubling or halving a pictured quantity. Home: Counting Cove, subject `counting-cove`; skills equal shares, fractions, doubling; music `STYLES.paint`; a ghost-finger bot (`touchDemo`, `autotouch` cuts fairly, deals the plates in turn and serves, or taps the missing fruit and serves).

Windows: toddler 1, preschool 1–4, pre-K 3–6, school 5–9. Two smooth rounds step up, two struggles step down, as everywhere.

**Content pools.** One sandwich drawing (brown crust, cream filling, a green line) at three sizes, one pizza with three toppings, two fruits (a red berry with seeds, a green apple). The share levels have no seeded content at all: level 1 is the same round every time. The recipe levels draw the card from two options at 6 and 8 (`recipe`), and level 7 draws one of three fractions (`FRACTIONS`). Friends are identical pink faces above the plates; the pet is only the corner guide.

**Accounting.** Only the serve tap can miss (`check`): an unfair share, a wrong fraction or a wrong count is a miss and the pieces stay where they are; cutting, moving, undo and the round arrow are free. The second miss in a round sets a persistent glow (one hint) on the most useful control: the next plate with less, the round arrow when there are too few pieces or the wrong cut for the fraction, or the fruit button or undo at the recipes. A round is one meal; `ctx.finish` runs 1.1 s after a fair serve.

**Journal.** None. **Couch.** None. **Story.** The Windy Picnic's *sandwiches* step plays level 1 for lap (as toddler) and toddler, level 1 for preschool, 3 for pre-K and 4 for school and grown-up (`src/content/picnic.ts`).

## What makes sense here

Pet Kitchen's identity is a whole that becomes pieces and friends who each get the same. Every proposal keeps that: she cuts (or finds the pieces already cut), gives each friend their share, and the friends show whether it was fair by eating it. The ladder below fills the audit's holes: a lap rung where every touch deals a piece (the dealing procedure, which needs no cut and no drag), a preschool rung where the cut itself can be unequal so "equal" stops being assumed, a school problem of two wholes for three friends that names the share after the act, two halves of different shapes that are the same amount, a comparison of two served fractions, and the cooking packet's equivalent-measures round as the top. Fresh content gives the friends faces, the counter a round whole, the fraction a picture, and the serve a scene.

What stays out: Cooking Mama's timers, medals and unlocks; Slice Fractions' no-help puzzles; a fraction written as a symbol (the [fractions study's](subjects.md) Ribbon Workshop and Frog Hop own fractions as numbers, and the study says sharing and equivalent measuring stay here); evening out uneven plates (IDEAS *Share Fairly* is Monster Munch's gated 13); scaling a recipe by three or three halves (the cooking packet's *Scale every ingredient*, which the ratio study assigns there); a changed reference whole ("half of which?", the fractions study's Whole Switch case).

## Experience and presentation

- **First unsupported action.** Today it is the cut button, found by a one- or two-year-old only by chance; after it come two drags and a serve. Level 10 (slice 3) makes the first touch the whole act: the pieces are already on the tray, a tap on any piece hops it to the plate with fewer, a tap anywhere else wiggles the nearest piece, and when the plates are equal the friends eat. Slice 1 makes the uncut sandwich answer a touch at every level (a wiggle and the cut instruction again, free) and invites after six seconds of nothing by bouncing the cut button.
- **Acknowledgment and consequence.** A piece scales to 1.08 on pick (`sfx.tick`) and lands with the bell; the cut splits the whole with `sfx.squish`; the plate under a dropped piece pulses once. After a fair serve (slice 2) the pieces slide to each friend's mouth and the friends chew (three small `scale.y` squashes with `sfx.munch`, a synthesized crunch), then the thank-you; the glow for a hint still breathes.
- **A concrete art and sound example.** The friends are `CRITTERS` dealt from the seed (cat, bunny, bear, pig…), drawn at 0.45 scale above each plate with `attach()` and the `calm` mood, turning to the happy mood when served; the pie for the round wholes is a `Graphics` circle in `swatch.yellow.light` with a crimped `swatch.brown.line` rim and a lattice of two `moveTo/lineTo` strokes; its cut icons are the same circle with one or two diameters. Level 11's lopsided cut icon is the sandwich with its line a third of the way across. The compare overlay (11, 13, 14) tweens both pieces to the counter's middle at alpha 0.7 so the overlap shows the difference or the sameness, and holds for 1.2 s.
- **Variety on replay.** Seeded wholes (sandwich or pie) and fillings at 1–4, seeded friends at every share level, a third fruit in the recipe pool, the fraction and the pair at 7 and 13 from the seed, the lopsided side at 11; never more friends per level or smaller pieces.
- **Reasoning versus precision.** The share levels ask for a drag of a 55-radius piece to a plate with 88 units of reach; the lap rung replaces the drag with a tap, and nothing else asks for precision or speed. The decisions are which cut (2, 4, 7, 11, 12), how many pieces (7, 13), and which plate (everywhere), never a timed or aimed action.
- **Supported ending and replay.** A truthful first-miss line, a glow after two misses on the next useful control, the round arrow always available; a round never ends in anything but a sticker; again replays the level with a fresh seed, next plays the one above.
- **Roles.** Solo stays the default. One cuts and one serves (IDEAS' two-seat row) needs no code: the cut buttons and the plates are separate controls, so two people can take turns on one iPad; at 13 one person can ask "who has more?" before the pet does.
- **Person or device checks.** Whether a one-year-old's tap lands on a piece or on a plate at level 10; whether a two-year-old reads the lopsided cut's pieces as "not the same" before serving them; whether critter faces at 0.45 scale read as friends in portrait; whether the chew reads as eating; whether "two thirds each" lands from the pet's voice alone; whether a six-year-old minds that the quarter cut leaves one piece in the tray at 13.
