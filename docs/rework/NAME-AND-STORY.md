# RW01: name and story packet

[Issue #485](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/issues/485) · [work item](WORK-ITEMS.md#rw01-name-and-story) · written 2026-10-17

**Exploration, not a decision.** This packet compares naming structures, recommends a *working* one, and writes the story premise, a pictured opening and one complete invitation and ending. Puddle Island stays the shipped title. Nothing is renamed in source, the manifest, `index.html` or the README. The final public name is the developer's call ([waiting on the developer](../WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER.md)). The name screen below is an informal search, **not legal clearance**.

## Summary

- **Working recommendation:** a world-led title, **Puddlewild**, with **Puddle Island as its first region** and a plain descriptive line under it (for grown-ups, e.g. *"explore, make and learn with creature friends"*) rather than a second brand.
- **"Discovery Club" stays a story premise, not a product subtitle.** The name screen found a pending US trademark application, **DISCOVERY CLUB PSP**, for children's educational apps, plus the Discovery family of kids' and education marks. On screen, the club shares the title's name: **the Puddlewild Club** (provisional). This packet still says "the club" for the idea.
- **The creatures' family name stays open.** "Puddlings" screened clean and works as a word a child can say. RW02 and RW05 can adopt it or drop it when the cast is set, and it is not proposed as the title.
- Functional rework (RW02–RW08) continues under the current title and placeholders; a final rename is one later, explicit change.

## The three naming structures

The criteria come from the work item. Each row is a reasoned judgement, not an observation. The child and parent checks are listed under [checks not yet run](#checks-not-yet-run).

| Criterion | A. World + learning line: **Puddlewild** (Puddle Island is the first region) | B. Creature-led: **Puddlings** | C. Current: **Puddle Island** |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Pronunciation (toddler to grown-up) | Two familiar words, "puddle" + "wild"; easy to say, but the compound is new | Three syllables built from a word she already knows; very easy | Easiest; two familiar words |
| Recall | Distinctive; the made-up compound sticks once heard | Distinctive, but easy to confuse with "pudding" (the search hits were all pudding games) | Plain and descriptive, so less distinctive |
| Parent understanding | Says "a world"; the descriptive line has to carry "learning" | Says "creatures"; a parent may expect a pet-collecting game first and learning second | Says "a place" only; no hint of creatures or learning |
| Broad age appeal (1 to about 11) | "Wild" reads as adventure for older children without leaving out a toddler | Cute; may feel young to an eight- to eleven-year-old | Gentle and young; the least appealing to the oldest band |
| Room for future regions | Strong: Puddle Island, then Discovery Marsh, Maker Harbor and others are places *in* Puddlewild | Weak: regions hang off the cast, not off the title | Weak: "Island" names one place, so other regions need a stretch |
| Fit with the creature rework | Good: creatures live in the wild | Strongest | Neutral |
| Cost of change | Title screen, manifest, voice lines naming the island; IDs and saves untouched | Same, and it ties the title to a cast that RW02 has not chosen yet | None |

**Why A:** it is the only structure that names a world big enough for regions, creatures and grown-up couch play. It also keeps "Puddle Island" exactly where it already belongs, as the island she knows. B is better as the cast's name than as the title. C is kept as the fallback, at no cost.

## The story

### Roles

- **The child** is a new member of the club: an explorer, maker and helper. She chooses where to go, makes things (a picture, a tune, a meal, a bridge) and decides what to try next. The story never tests her before she can play; it invites her.
- **The creatures** are friends with their own personalities, looks and favourite things. A companion travels with her, says the instruction again, cheers and is sometimes funny. Others wait at home, and nothing goes wrong while she is away. They **never answer the learning task for her**. A creature can carry, light, sing or splash something she has decided on (RW11), but the counting, matching, building and explaining stay hers.
- **The club** is the frame: a cosy home base with a door to the island. It holds her creatures, her creations, her tools and a journal of what she actually did. The club's question of the day is the invitation.
- **Grown-ups** sit beside a lap or toddler player, and a family recap shows them what a project involved (RW09). The couch route stays its own grown-up mode.

### Why the mini-games matter

Each project is a question that a few existing games answer. **The learning action is what makes the result happen:** shared food fills the right plates, a decoration she stamped hangs over the new friend's bed, a tune she composed becomes their song. Some games stay outside any project and are played freely through Explore. A project can include a quiet favourite or a physical game, so long as the step does something in the story.

### What a completed project changes

A finished project leaves a **visible, permanent, honest** change:

- a **new creature** joins the home (the adoption happens once, whether she finished with help or without);
- **what she made** appears or plays there: her actual stamped picture or composed tune, never a stand-in presented as hers;
- **fixed consequences** (a new room, a toy, the next invitation) are shown as part of the story, not presented as her result;
- the **journal** files what she did.

None of this is a mastery claim. Learning evidence is recorded separately (RW09).

### Fact and fiction

| Factual, taught as true | Fiction, framed as play |
| --- | --- |
| How many, which is longer, what floats, what sound starts the word, what a plant needs | Creatures with names, moods and wearables; a creature who glows or swims through puddles |
| Real animals, habitats and weather (Photo Safari, Habitat Helpers, weather games) | Creature "species", forms and talents (RW11), never described as real biology |
| Patterns, notes and rhythms she makes and hears | The club, its home and its doorways |

The rule for writing: a fact is said plainly and checked against the subject studies, and fantasy is never used to explain a fact. A glowing creature can light the cave, but the lesson about why things float comes from the floating game, not from the creature.

## One world at three ages

The same club, island and creatures, with less to manage for the youngest. These are design stages, not new bands (see the [brief](README.md#the-experience-that-grows)).

| | Lap / toddler (shared play) | Preschool adventure | Older-child investigation (about 6–10) |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| What she sees first | One creature, large, and one or two glowing toys | Her companion and a picture card for today's question | The question card, an open project and the journal |
| The invitation | "Tickle Pip! Make Pip splash!" The grown-up and the child poke, splash and dress the creature together | "A new friend is coming! Let's make them feel at home." Three pictured steps | "Why does the bridge sink? Let's test it." She plans, tries and revises, then explains what changed |
| Games used | One-action toys (Splish Splash, Peekaboo Barn, Bubble Pop) | Two or three short modes (e.g. Stamp Studio, Pet Kitchen, Song Maker; RW04 picks them) | Longer modes with prediction and revision (e.g. Sink or Float, Ramp Race trials, Robot Path) |
| What changes | The creature reacts and wears what she chose | A new creature arrives and uses what she made | A region's object works (the bridge holds), a creature joins, the journal records her explanation |
| Reading | None; everything is spoken and pictured | None needed; words appear beside pictures | Optional reading practice with spoken support; navigation never needs it |

## Pictured opening

This is the first time a profile enters the rework's home. It is written as on-screen beats: what she **sees**, what is **said** (voice-line style, with `{pet}` and `{name}` as in `voice-script.ts`) and what she **taps**. There are no new image assets: everything is drawn in code, as now. It replaces nothing yet: RW03 owns the routes, and RW05 and RW06 own the companion and home.

1. **Sees:** a round wooden door in a hillside by the puddles, with a lantern glowing over it. Her companion bounces beside it. **Said:** "Knock knock! Tap the door, {name}!" **Taps:** the door (a large target, with touch-down feedback: a wobble and a knock sound).
2. **Sees:** the door swings open on a cosy room: a pinboard, a shelf, a little bed and a round window onto the island. **Said:** "Welcome to the Puddlewild Club! This is our home. We explore, we make things, and we help our friends."
3. **Sees:** the companion hops to the pinboard, where the card for today's question glows: a picture of an empty basket and a "?". **Said:** "Every day there's a question on our board. Tap it to find out!" **Taps:** the card.
4. **Sees:** the card flips over to the invitation (below). A small "island" picture by the window also glows: that is Explore, for any game she likes. **Said:** "Or tap the window to play anything on the island."

**First time only:** steps 1–2 play once per profile, and step 3 onward is the home's normal greeting. A grown-up can skip the whole opening, and "Who's playing?" stays the front door.

## One complete invitation and ending: Welcome a New Friend

This is the story layer only. **RW04 owns the steps:** which games, modes, levels, request and result fields, prompts and accounting. The beats below say what the story needs from each step so that RW04 can satisfy or revise it. They define no game rules.

**Invitation (the card):**

- **Sees:** a sleepy little creature peeking out of a basket at the edge of the puddles.
- **Said:** "Someone new is at the puddles. They're shy, and they need a home. Let's make our club ready: something pretty, something yummy and a song to say hello!"
- **Sees:** three pictured steps on the card (a stamp, a bowl and a music note) and a green arrow.
- **Taps:** the arrow to begin, or any step picture to start there.

**Steps (the story's needs; RW04 chooses the mode):**

1. **Something pretty:** she makes a decoration. **Story need:** the actual picture she made is kept for the ending.
2. **Something yummy:** she prepares or shares food for the newcomer. **Story need:** a fixed, pictured result (the bowl is full) unless RW04 finds a portion the game truly reports.
3. **A song to say hello:** she makes a short tune. **Story need:** her actual tune is kept and replayed, never a fixed tune presented as hers.

Between steps, the card comes back with finished steps ticked, and the companion says what is still to do. She can leave at any point and come back to the same card. Only completed steps count, and nothing is awarded twice.

**Ending:**

1. **Sees:** back home, her picture is hanging on the wall and the bowl is on the floor. **Said:** "Everything's ready. Let's call our new friend!" **Taps:** the round window.
2. **Sees:** the shy creature waddles in, sniffs the bowl, looks up at her picture and wags. **Plays:** her tune. **Said:** "Listen, it's your song! They love it."
3. **Sees:** the newcomer curls up in the basket under her picture. **Said:** "Our new friend wants a name. Tap a name to hear it, then tap the green arrow." (This reuses the hatching name picker's pattern.)
4. **Said:** "Welcome home, {friend}! You made this home, {name}." **Sees:** the journal page for the project (her picture, a note for her tune, the new friend) and a glowing card for the next question.

**What changed (honestly):** a new creature now lives in the home (shown once, the same with or without help). Her real picture hangs there, and her real tune is the newcomer's song in the home. The food bowl and the room's tidiness are fixed story consequences. Round stickers stay the shell's, as now.

## Checks not yet run

Nothing below has been observed. Record results in [device checks](../DEVICE-CHECKS.md) when someone does them:

- **Pronunciation:** the child (when old enough), a 4–6-year-old and a grown-up say "Puddlewild" and "Puddlings" after hearing each once. Do they come out recognisably?
- **Recall:** a day later, can a parent and an older child name the app?
- **Parent understanding:** shown only the title screen and the descriptive line, can a parent say that it is a learning game with creatures?
- **Older-child appeal:** does an eight- to eleven-year-old think the title is "for babies"?
- **Device speech:** how iPad speech voices say "Puddlewild" (stress and breath between the two words), "Puddlings" and the opening lines.
- **A real name screen** before any public use: USPTO, App Store, Google Play and domain checks, and counsel if published.
