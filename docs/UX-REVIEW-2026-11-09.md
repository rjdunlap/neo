# Adversarial UX review, 2026-11-09

*Findings and recommendations only. Nothing here changes the app. Reviewed against `origin/main` at `33941901`.*

The question: what is unclear to a new player, and where would someone stop playing because they are overwhelmed, confused or unsure what to do next? This follows the [user research review](archive/USER-RESEARCH-2026-10-24.md) of 2026-10-24 and repeats its first-run walk now that the rework is the site root.

## How it was done, and what it cannot say

A first-run walk of the root app in desktop Chrome at 1024×768, as three new players: a one-year-old (lap), a four-year-old (pre-K) and a 38-year-old (grown-up). Speech was off throughout (`VITE_SPEECH=off`), which stands in for an iPad with its silent switch on. Rounds played: Word Glue (lap, level 1), Drift Cards (level 1, reached from a lap profile), Stamp Studio (from Welcome a New Friend), Crossword (grown-up, level 6) and Day by Day (pre-K, level 4), plus Home, Today's six, Explore, Places, Bubble Beach, the Arcade, Favorites, Family play, the couch menu, the grown-ups' page and the classic map.

Limits: no child, no iPad, no controller and no real voice were observed; that is 5 of 175 games; the timings below were measured with speech muted and may differ with it on. Where a control's meaning is given for Stamp Studio it was read from its picture, not from the code. Nothing here belongs in [OBSERVATIONS](OBSERVATIONS.md).

## Headline

The games are mostly easy once a round has started. The risk of stopping is in getting to a round and in the unlabelled controls around it.

## Findings, worst first

### 1. With sound off, the app says nothing about what to do

- The egg on the hatch screen sits still: `HatchScene` has no idle cue, so the first screen is a picture (the earlier review's R7, still open).
- Home shows five round picture buttons at lap, seven at pre-K and six for a grown-up, with no labels and no one obvious "play".
- Welcome a New Friend's page is a newcomer in a basket, three cards and a green arrow. Without its spoken ask there is no hint what the cards are or which one the arrow starts.

### 2. One picture means different things on different screens

| Picture | Meanings seen |
| --- | --- |
| Four colored squares | All games (Explore, and beside Back on Today's six); "back to the list" inside a round; the Arcade's topics button |
| Six squares and a dot | Today's six, one row of squares away from the picture above |
| Basket | Continue the project (Home); back to the project (in its round); the wardrobe (her friend's room) |
| Flowers | Explore (Home); Places (inside Explore); back to Places (in a land) |
| Green arrow | Start a round; finish a creation; start a project step |
| Black left arrow | Back everywhere; a tool at the bottom right of Stamp Studio |
| Circular arrow | Play again (result); a tool in Stamp Studio |

### 3. Back and the question mark inside a round need a hold, and nothing says so

Both are `HoldButton`s in `GameScene` (Back 0.5 s, the question mark 0.9 s). A tap only squashes the button; every other Back in the app is a tap. Crossword also has a second, yellow question mark that reveals a letter. Esc opens the Paused sheet, which offers Keep playing and Show me but no way to leave the game.

### 4. Today's six does not suit a brand-new player

- The one-year-old's six included Word Glue (compound words), whose grown-up tip says "Let her name one first".
- The four-year-old's six included Day by Day at level 4: written day names and a three-step how-to card, for a game never played before.
- The dots under each card are never explained.
- A finished round returns to the grid; nothing offers the next of the six.
- Movie Night's card is a video play button.

### 5. Five ways to browse one catalog, and their names disagree

- Explore offers Places, Favorites, All games, Arcade and Family play. The Arcade's header reads "All games".
- The All games card shows four games a page with no page count, youngest first even for the grown-up: 175 games is about 44 pages.
- The Arcade filters by topic but not by age, and its topics reuse the Places pictures under other names (Senses for Bubble Beach, Shapes for Rainbow Meadow).
- An empty Favorites shows one All games card and nothing about the heart that fills it.
- Family play opens a page holding one Family play card.

### 6. In a land, games and scenery look alike

In Bubble Beach every game carries a sparkle, so the sparkle marks nothing. Tide pools, rock mounds and small crabs sit among the games at the same size; taps on the pools and the resident gave no visible answer. The signpost swaps in the bigger-kids games under the same title, which put the one-year-old's profile two taps from Drift Cards, a school-age logic puzzle, with nothing on screen saying who it is for.

### 7. The how-to card is a reading job before every round

It runs 80 to 110 words in rule-book language ("A check with any wrong word is one miss and circles the wrong squares"). It shows "This level · 6" to a first-time player. Its demonstration caption says "a pointer clicks and drags" for tap-only games. The earlier review's R3 (a no-reading lap start) and R4 (a one-line card and tip) are still open.

### 8. Taps are dropped and screens are slow to answer

- Day by Day ignores the arrow for about a second after a card is picked, while the card's name is spoken; nothing shows it is busy.
- A tap on a Home or menu picture takes two to four seconds to change the screen, with only her friend's cheer in between.
- The player chooser takes four to five seconds to fade in on every launch.

### 9. Rounds do not show their length, and the result screen changes

Word Glue ran five pairs with no count of what was left. The result buttons differed between rounds (heart, again, back and home in one; treehouse, heart, again and basket in another), all unlabelled. Her friend sits over the finished picture in Stamp Studio's result.

### 10. Grown-ups get child surfaces and stale words

- An adult must color and name a pet, then lands on the same wordless Home.
- The grown-ups' page is one long scroll with a level picker for every game.
- It speaks of "the home spot on the map", "Puddle Lagoon", "the picnic blanket" and a "Swiping path (original)", none of which exist at the root, and shows "Meet everyone (development)".
- The small door on Home opens the whole classic app with no label; the classic map showed no way back.
- Couch play goes by four names (Family play, Grown-up play, An evening on the island, a couch trip), defaults to two players, and its button sits on a one-year-old's player card.

### 11. Stamp Studio from the project

It opened with no how-to card. Five unlabelled tool buttons appeared after the first stamp, and of six taps on the paper only two left a stamp.

## What held up

Word Glue at lap finishes with single taps. The glow after two misses works. The add-player form names what is missing. The grown-up's six shows each level's name. Crossword is clear.

## Recommendations, in priority order

| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| U2 | **One picture, one meaning.** Give Today's six, the wardrobe and Stamp Studio's tools their own pictures; keep the four squares for All games and the basket for the project | 2 | Small to medium; shell and one game. Built: Today's six is a day's page, her friends' way in a nest, Undo a hook and Turn a curved arrow; the flowers, the green start/finish arrow and the Arcade's all-topics tiles are left as they were |
| U3 | **An honest Back in a round.** Either a tap, or a ring and a "hold" cue on touch-down; and a Leave choice on the Paused sheet | 3 | Small; shell. Built in PR #1462: Back and the question mark respond to a tap, and Paused offers Leave |
| U4 | **Show what to touch when it is quiet.** A wobble on the egg, a bounce on the project's next card (the earlier R7) | 1 | Small |
| U5 | **A first six she can start.** For a player with no rounds yet, pick the six from each game's first level for her band and hold tips to her band; offer the next of the six on the result | 4 | Medium; content and shell. Defined 2026-11-10 ([U5](definitions/todays-six.md#u5-a-first-six-she-can-start-status-ready-defined-2026-11-10)): the next of the six on the result was already built; a game new to her plays its first level; tips get a lap version; the dots and Movie Night's card are left out |
| U6 | **One way to browse.** Fold the All games card into the Arcade, add a "for my age" topic that opens first, show the page count, and say on an empty Favorites how the heart works | 5 | Medium; shell |
| U7 | **Never swallow a tap.** Let a second tap interrupt a spoken name, and show the pressed picture as chosen while a screen changes | 8 | Medium; shell, then per game |
| U8 | **The grown-ups' page speaks of the root.** Remove the map words and the development row, and fold This week's rows behind a search or "played this week" | 10 | Small to medium |

U1 to U4 are built and U5 is defined. U6 to U8 need a short definition first ([READY](READY.md#next-ten)). Findings 6, 7 and 11 are left to the earlier review's R3 and R4 and to an observation of real play: which of them matter depends on what she and her grown-up actually do.
