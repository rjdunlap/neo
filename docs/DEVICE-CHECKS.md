# Needs a person or a device

Part of the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md), split into its own file on 2026-10-13 so the roadmap can be read in one piece; the same rules apply (statuses, what to add where: see [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md#documentation-and-git)).

- [ ] **Family quality pilots, when built:** find a remembered game, choose an unfamiliar one with the available input, inspect a different mode, leave and return, then switch profiles. Compare a landscape/portrait view with sound off and observe whether both people contribute in a shared-role round. Record understandable actions and voluntary replay/revision, not time spent or stickers earned; see [the review protocol](FAMILY-EXPERIENCE.md#what-a-quality-review-records). These observations do not establish other games' readiness.

- [ ] **The next-level button and Bubble Pop's scenes with her:** does a small hand tap the orange next-level button by mistake (it sits above home, away from the green again); does a night or sea round still read as bubbles to pop, and do the fish and stars read as scenery and not as something to tap?

- [ ] **Bubble Pop 12–14 and the target sign on an iPad:** device speech for "Start at 7", "Count back from 9" and the number words for tens and fives (ten, twenty, thirty … and five, ten, fifteen …); whether a pre-K child reads the sign's numeral and arrow, and whether ten drifting bubbles on level 13 are too many to follow.
- [ ] **Bubble Pop 15, Pop anywhere, with her (2026-10-13):** does she pop by swatting or patting at all, does the nearest-bubble rule feel fair when a touch lands far from every bubble, and is eight pops about right before the rainbow bubble? Chrome automation cannot tell. Two smooth rounds step her up to level 1; a grown-up can pin 15 in the grown-ups' page.
- [ ] **Pet Says 13, Pat the pet, with her (2026-10-13):** does she pat the pet on her own, is a poke and "Pat the pet!" after ten quiet seconds the right nudge, and does a squished pat during a move feel fine? Level 13 is placed first in lap's order; a grown-up can pin 13 or 1.
- [ ] **Sing-Along Barn with her:** does a one-year-old tap along (any tap plays the next note, so does mashing feel musical rather than noisy); do the 25-tap and 38-tap songs hold her; does the melody match a printed source of "Old MacDonald" (written from the traditional tune by scale degree, not checked against a printed copy); and does device speech read "{animal}!" and the instruction well. Chrome checks do not establish these.
- [ ] **Lantern Lake and Lantern Lights by touch, with a grown-up:** is a lantern easy to hit on an iPad at the 5 by 5 size (cells are about 100 units), do Undo, Hint and Start over read as buttons, does the pond feel like a puzzle rather than random tapping, and does the grown-up's pet birthday into Lantern Lodge make sense. Chrome checks do not establish these.
- [ ] **Picture Logic by touch, with a grown-up (2026-10-13):** can a finger paint a run on 64-unit squares without catching the next row, is the edge-slide (a held finger within 48 units of the window's edge, 420 units a second) easy to steer rather than a runaway, do Fill, Cross and Slide read as tools, and is a 15 by 15 picture that slides under pinned clue numbers a pleasure or a chore on an iPad? Device speech for the two new instructions and the two fix lines is unchecked. Chrome automation proves the rules, the gestures and the layout, not the feel.
- [ ] **Shape Buddies:** iPad drag comfort, device speech, and whether a child understands changing a shape to bridge or catch. The solo level is checked in Chrome only.

- [ ] **Frog Hop support:** device speech for the full sum and split, whether off-by-one explanations help a child count hops rather than the starting pad, and whether the free card reminder is clear. Browser checks do not establish these.

- [ ] **Robot Path preschool opening.** Does a three-year-old understand adding one to three arrows, then pressing Play, and use Undo or replayed instructions to revise? Levels 1–2 are small rock-free boards; actual iPad speech, finger comfort and child understanding remain unchecked.

- [ ] **Fill and Dump pouring on an iPad:** does pulling right to tip the jug feel natural, are the purple fill lines and tray-return arrow understood, and is the six-percent cup tolerance forgiving? Preschool's levels 1–5, device speech and child play remain unjudged.

- [ ] **Goodnight Room barn, animals and colors.** On an iPad, check target spacing, device speech for names and colors, whether preschool/pre-K players understand living animals among objects, and whether orange fish inside a blue bowl and brown puppy/teddy are clear color matches. No child or device judgment is established by browser play.

Keep these open until someone actually does them. None of them blocks [Next up](ROADMAP.md#next-up).

**Grown-ups at the computer or TV**

- [ ] **Gem Swap:** try the four trays with a person: do selection outlines and falling clears make the next choice readable, and does the player want another board? Real iPad targets/speech and real controller play remain unverified.
- [ ] **Tile Pairs:** try the six layouts with a person, and check whether the visible stack, free-tile tint and clearing hint make pair choices understandable; judge whether the person wants another board. Real iPad touch, device speech and controller use remain unverified.

- [ ] **The chooser on real hardware (2026-10-09).** On an iPad (and a phone): does a tap on a card open the island, not couch play (the chooser reads a real `pointerdown`, with a click's `detail` only as a fallback, and headless Chrome is not WebKit), does the first tap still wake sound and speech, and can a small hand pick its own card without reaching the gear or the couch button? On a TV with a controller: do the stick and the bottom button move the ring and open couch play for the highlighted person, and is the ring easy to see from the couch? With a keyboard: does Tab show the ring, and does Enter on the gear still only hold it?
- [ ] **The quiet music-making games on the iPad (2026-10-09).** Jelly Drums, Song Maker, Sound Garden, Rhythm Neighbors, Clap the Syllables and the treehouse tune plaque no longer have the island's loop under them (a probe in headless Chrome shows the loop at about zero and the beat clock still running; the plaque's dip came back after the duck). Does the bare room feel empty to a small child or does her own sound carry it, is Song Maker's playhead still easy to follow, and does Pattern Train's bell level want the same treatment?
- [ ] **Speech voices for other languages on the iPad (2026-10-11).** Which voices her iPad has for the languages under consideration (Settings, Accessibility, Spoken Content, Voices), whether one needs a download, how they sound at the game's rate (0.95) and pitch (1.15), which were tuned on English voices, and whether a native speaker would accept them for a toddler. Blocks the stage 1 build of [Additional languages](ROADMAP.md#additional-languages); the definition does not wait.
- [ ] Play couch trips with real Pro Controllers on a TV and write down what is wrong: text size from the couch (pages scale to about 1.4 times at 1080p), how-to pacing (Secret Code's and Bumper Garden's demos run 30–40 seconds), whether the bots are a good example, how fast trips open new games (one tier per trip is a guess), how face-off feels to whoever loses, and whether the finale's four-second ceremony is a treat or a wait.
- [ ] Judge the challenge courses: whether Pond Practice is easy enough and the Five Ponds hard enough, whether Cloud Hopper's small clouds suit a stick, whether any of the three gives a reason to run again, whether a hint after three extra slides or two missed launches is too soon for a score chase, and whether the squashing spring reads well or the launcher wants to become a catapult. Five to ten minutes per course is a hypothesis, not a timer.
- [ ] Compare three versus four choices per stop, and the pacing of a whole trip, before adding a dice board or more party structure.
- [ ] **Try the couch settings from the couch.** Step 1 is built: can she find Settings with a controller alone, do the TV defaults (controller pictures, the window's own scaling, Large text on request) feel right from the sofa, is Large or Extra large needed at all, does "Choose for me" ever pick wrong (a laptop with a controller attached; a keyboard on a TV), is the couch volume separate from the iPad's, and does Laptop mode show sensible key prompts on the Mac's own screen? Typing and word games wait on this.
- [ ] **Keys and a pad on the map and lands (Lands stage C2, 2026-10-13).** With a real keyboard, a pad on a laptop and a pad on the TV: does the first key show the ring without pressing anything, do arrows land on the spot you mean on every land (the ring sits round the picture, not the name), is Enter held through the map's zoom safe in the land, and is the ring easy to see from a couch. Chrome automation only synthesises keys.
- [ ] **Sudoku Garden on the TV.** Is the number tray (bottom button on a square, then choose, then bottom again) quick enough with a stick, or does it want a faster way to enter a digit; are the colored blossoms a help or clutter; is hold-to-repeat the right speed for a 9 by 9 bed; are the hints clear and not too chatty; is a bed's par (entries equal to empty squares) a good score, or should a replaced number cost less; are the 6 by 6 beds too easy and the 9 by 9 beds too long for one evening, and is a single sitting of Six Beds (about two and a half minutes for the bot; a person is unmeasured) the right size?
- [ ] **An island round on keys or a pad (Lands stage C1, 2026-10-13).** With a real controller (or a keyboard on an iPad or desktop), open a Memory Match or Frog Hop round from the island: does Enter on the how-to card play without a stray move, does the first arrow or stick turn the ring on only then, does a touch round stay touch until a key, does Start (pad) or Esc pause and resume, does the celebration's ring show on the first press and move with the stick, and does Enter on "again" replay the level? Also whether spoken lines that say "tap" are confusing while on keys. Only a synthetic gamepad in headless Chromium has been used.
- [ ] **Pond Conga on an iPad.** Does a swipe feel prompt (turns are made at the next step), do two turns in a stroke feel natural, and is a touch-to-set-off clear without the line of help? Does a held-down finger or the edge of the pond get in the way of the swipe? Is a bump read as a bump, and is the pond's one-target size (cells 42 to 55 units) comfortable? Not judged on a device yet.
- [ ] **Pond Conga on the TV.** Is 2.4 to 3.4 steps a second relaxing or still hurried with a stick, and does a turn made at the next step feel immediate or late? Does the bonk (the whole line turning about, a one-second breather, one more step) read as a gentle bump or as a punishment, and is it clear which way the line faces afterwards? Is the faint second crumb a help for planning, and is Crumb Trail's par of 259 a fair target, or are 98 steps on the last pond too long for the mood? Are the dots of a hint kept up well enough, or does the line outrun them? Is a bot run a good demo?
- [ ] Now that the pause-menu restart and "Just me" have landed, replay Pond Practice, the Five Ponds and Cloud Hopper alone: is a restart easy to find, does a shelf that shows par and her best ("3 above par") make her want another go, is Watch the best routes a treat or a spoiler, is it clear where *Just me* is (the start page, or Settings), and which of the puzzles below does she ask for first?

**On the iPad**

- [ ] **Clock Tower controls and speech.** Are the inner/outer hand zones clear, the helper numbers and target sign legible, and “3:25”, quarter to and 12 o'clock spoken usefully? Does a displayed fictional schedule make level 5 fair, and can a preschooler copy a one-hand model if that opening is built? Level 7's portrait look and device speech are unrecorded; proposed interval pickers and appointment layouts need review when built ([deep dive](deep-dives/clock-tower.md#known-gaps-and-checks-to-plan)).

- [ ] **The how-to cards on the iPad.** Does a grown-up read them or tap straight through Play, and is a card before every game (the choice made 2026-10-08) too much with a small child on the lap, or right? Does the demonstration help, and is the 40% effects level while it plays right for its sound? Does a hand tapping and dragging read as "do this" or as something that might be touched, and does a hand waiting over a flipper's button until the ladybug arrives (Bumper Garden, whose first level also runs about 49 seconds) read as patience or as a fault? Is the text the right size from arm's length, and is the hold-to-open `?` still needed, or should a visible tap button replace it for grown-ups? Should "again" also show the card?
- [ ] **Puzzle Pals levels 5–7 without a lid, with a person.** Do the near-blank pieces (the duck's top-middle pair, ground pieces told apart by a flower's colour) cost a child misses, and does that step her down? Decides whether the box lid is needed ([deep dive](deep-dives/puzzle-pals.md), Known gaps).
- [ ] **Habitat Helpers with a person.** Do the code-drawn clover, pool, brush, seed grass, reeds and berry hedge read without text; does hearing each piece's role make the small model clear; does opening the gate feel like testing rather than submitting an answer; on level 4 does the `?` on the sign, with a touch on each prepared piece to hear what it gives, make the prediction something she can reason about and not only guess; and is sharing the pond as Bunny's water and Duck's water-and-shelter intuitive? Review the two spoken journal facts with a knowledgeable grown-up, and listen to device speech for the piece names. Known gap: when two tests leave a full garden of the wrong pieces, the glow lights what to add but not which piece to take out; the "garden is full" line gets her there.
- [ ] **The grown-ups' gear and Esc on the iPad and on a laptop (built 2026-10-09).** There is no play timer any more, and the gear replaced the two-corner hold. Does a two-second hold on the gear stop a toddler's resting hand, and a tap say "ask a grown-up" without being a chore; does Screen Time list the Home Screen app for an App Limit (Settings → Screen Time → See All Activity after some play), and does a Guided Access time limit end a session cleanly; and on a laptop does Esc → hold the gear feel natural? If a toddler opens the page, lengthen `GEAR_HOLD_SECONDS`.
- [ ] Device basics: offline speech voices, first-touch audio, small-hand dragging, herding and pulling, orientation changes, Add to Home Screen, Guided Access, and that a new version arrives after the home-screen app is closed and reopened (how long iPadOS keeps a suspended app on the old one is unknown).
- [ ] **The lands map on the iPad (built 2026-10-09).** Can a small child find a land again and get back from a game to it; do the hosts and the signpost read without words (does "for bigger kids" mean anything to her, and do older games behind it frustrate or delight); is twelve spots on one map screen too busy; is the home spot found, and is its long path still wanted beside the lands (and the subject cards beside the path); do the twinkles on every land of a new profile mean anything, and do the hearts on the home shelf get used? Check that every game is reachable in every band, in portrait too, as the catalog grows.
- [ ] **Light Lab 7 on an iPad.** Check that the three endpoint targets are easy to tap on the 7 by 5 grid and that the spoken prediction and replay make sense.
- [ ] **Duck Pond 11 on an iPad.** Check that the bridge clearly hides its ducks and the lily pads remain comfortable to tap in landscape and portrait. Also (from the [deep dive](deep-dives/duck-pond.md#the-game-today)): whether a toddler can use level 5 (numeral only, three to six ducks) and stops at the number without a check, and whether a child ignores or uses dots on the pads once they exist.
- [ ] **Market Stall with a four- to seven-year-old.** Does she count each coin as one (the counted-coins slip from the [deep dive](deep-dives/market-stall.md#the-game-today)), do pips on the coins help a three-year-old match a coin to the tag's dots, is counting up from the price understood next to the coin values said on touch-down, does the 12-coin counter feel like a nudge or a wall, and does a toddler give "two" and stop? Also the named payment-slip and full-counter lines, and ten words with device speech, and whether bigger-is-worth-more shell coins confuse a child who has seen real coins.
- [ ] **Frog Hop with a six- or seven-year-old.** Does she count the pad the frog stands on (the one-off line from the [deep dive](deep-dives/frog-hop.md#the-game-today)), do the landing numerals said aloud help or crowd the counting, does she choose the stop at ten when it is offered, and does a three-year-old use the dots on levels 1–2? Also tens words and "100" on a pad with device speech, and whether the brook and flower-path scenes are calm.
- [ ] **Peekaboo Barn with her (lap and toddler).** Does a one-year-old open a hiding place at level 1 by swatting or by looking first, and is the bear's growl gentle? Whether a four- or five-year-old can follow a 0.9-second swap decides the Switcheroo levels ([deep dive](deep-dives/peekaboo-barn.md#decisions-for-the-developer)).
- [ ] **Animal Snack on a device.** Does the spoken count at level 5 read right ("3 carrots", "3 bales of hay" are the plan; today it says "3 carrot"), does the half-second of ignored taps while an animal eats feel slow to a three-year-old, and does a one-year-old drag a snack to a mouth or only tap ([deep dive](deep-dives/animal-snack.md#decisions-for-the-developer))?
- [ ] **Splish Splash with her (lap and toddler).** Does a one-year-old scrub, pat or slap the mud, and does a palm-sized brush feel right; do the spoken part names ("tummy", "cheeks"), the clue lines and the room lines read right on a device; does a four-year-old get "what do we hear with?" ([deep dive](deep-dives/splish-splash.md#decisions-for-the-developer))?
- [ ] **Jelly Drums with her (lap and toddler).** Is 24 notes a long round for a one-year-old; does she wait for an echo; do the spoken color words and song titles read right on a device; can a four-year-old say "same or different" about two three-note tunes; is playing a tune backwards fun or frustrating at five; is the listen-again target found; and does the bare room still sound like music with the new instruments?
- [ ] **Rainbow Fingers with her (lap and toddler).** Does a one-year-old pat, slap or drag, does a palm-sized splat read as paint, and is a 104-unit pot right for a two-year-old; do the spoken picture and color names ("orange", "purple", new pictures) read right on a device; can a six-year-old find the wheel's neighbors ([deep dive](deep-dives/rainbow-fingers.md#decisions-for-the-developer))?
- [ ] **Stamp Studio with her (lap through school).** Does a one-year-old pat or press, and does the pop teach anything; do a three- and a four-year-old understand "up in the sky", "next to" and "between" when asked to place a stamp; does a 0.7 and a 1.5 stamp read on the treehouse board; do the new stamp names, animal sounds and composed "I see…" sentences read right on device speech; and does a six-year-old find that a tile repeats ([deep dive](deep-dives/stamp-studio.md#decisions-for-the-developer), decision 11).

**On a phone (the stand-in until the iPad arrives; built 2026-10-09, [README](../README.md#try-it-on-a-phone))**

- [ ] On a real iPhone and a real Android phone, sideways: does the island read at about half the iPad's size (the grown-up tip, the how-to cards and the pet's speech bubbles are the smallest text), does a notch or rounded corner leave only a cream strip (the safe-area insets are checked with faked values in Chrome, never on a real device), and does the island button, the book and the gate's top corners sit where a thumb lands?
- [ ] Held upright, does the turn prompt appear, go when the phone turns (iOS settles its safe area a moment after a turn) and leave the game where it was? Does the phone's rotation lock leave a grown-up stuck on it with no way past (there is deliberately no "play upright" button)?
- [ ] First-touch sound, the silent switch (`navigator.audioSession.type = 'playback'`, iOS 16.4 and later) and the phone's own speech voices; Add to Home Screen on both platforms and an update arriving after the app is closed and reopened.

**With her now (lap and toddler)**

- [ ] **Owl Walk Home with a person (toddler and preschool).** Does a two-year-old tap the stone or still the owl; do the twinkling matching stones read as "these match" or as decoration; do three-year-olds find the *next* one or tap any stone of the color; is the pet's narrated turn too long; do heart, circle, star and triangle and the color names sound right on the iPad; does "fly home" read once it is said ([deep dive](deep-dives/owl-walk.md#experience-and-presentation)).
- [ ] Whether tapping the soil (Garden Grow), the singing flowers, saying goodnight (Goodnight Room), Animal Snack, Stop and Go and Opposites hold a one-year-old's attention; Block Tower's tumbling; Pet Says as co-play (does she copy, pat the pet or watch, do the mittens read on the pip's face, and is "Quiet dance" clear to a three-year-old); Owl Walk Home's turn-taking; whether she notices the heart, the treehouse or the journal at all.

**Later, at three to five**

- [ ] Word Monsters, Photo Safari and Clap the Syllables: vocabulary, spoken prompts, answer clarity, and whether device speech produces usable letter sounds and clear words. Do not claim phonics coverage from letter-name playback alone.
- [ ] Tangram Town: actual device pronunciation of square, triangle, rectangle and trapezoid, and whether once-per-piece pickup cues are clear while she moves and turns the shapes.
- [ ] Physical play: Peg Garden and Egg Catch (reachable goals feel reachable, motion slow enough, forgiving catches); Bounce Back with two real hands and the pet taking over; Dot Link's line through dots for small fingers; Bumper Garden's half-screen flipping; Lasso Loops' loop gesture with small fingers.
- [ ] Clarity: Fluffy Salon and Sound Garden (satisfying free play, clear exits, guided modes that keep creative exploration); Little Helpers and Mail Carrier (visible quantities and unambiguous requests at viewing distance; whether a Little Helpers bunch reads as equal teams); Seesaw Balance's tipping as weight; Teddy Doctor's spoken-only clues; Quick Tricks' arrow between tricks; Stamp Studio, Pet Kitchen, Rhythm Neighbors and Tangram Town (stamp moving, equal pieces, reply scoring, silhouette help and understanding the square/two-triangle wall alternatives and turn/check repair); Peekaround Island (turning reads as looking from another side, "behind" the crown, drop spots easy to hit).
- [ ] The Windy Picnic: does she understand who needs help, what to try and what changed; are the blanket and request targets easy to hit; does the journal make sense.

**Later, at six to eight**

- [ ] The Wonder Woods games: Light Lab's mirrors as turning the beam, Penguin Slide's tap-the-ice direction and planning with combined block/snow stops and paired-block routes, Secret Code's marks, Frog Hop's number line, Market Stall's coin sizes, Clock Tower's hand dragging, Inchworm Measure's worms and ruler, Block Tower's eighth-of-a-block dragging and whether its mirrored level-6 table edges make sense, Mail Carrier's map key, Chain Reaction (moving one piece at a time, predicting before running, discovering several designs), and whether Seesaw Balance 8–9 explains "do the same to both sides".
- [ ] The newer ladder steps with an older child: Robot Path loops, word families, make ten, leftovers, and Pixel Pictures' logic puzzles.
- [ ] **Day by Day with a child and on an iPad.** Are spoken day-card naming and the placing arrow understood, does the Sunday/Monday wrap make sense, and can she use the weather records to count sunny days? Check device speech; completion with hints is supported practice.

- [ ] **Orchard Rows with a child and on an iPad.** Are whole-row taps comfortable, equal-group speech clear, and the turned orchard understood as the same trees counted by columns? Observe missing-row reasoning separately from supported completion.
- [ ] **Read & Do with a child and on an iPad.** Are the hats, duck sizes and jump/sleep icons understood, are individual word taps and the speaker easy to replay, and does the device speak each word naturally (especially "the" and "can")? Observe independent reading separately from completion with listening support.
- [ ] **Lemonade Stand with an older child or a grown-up.** Is the weather sign readable without reading, is a weak first batch an experiment or a failure to her, does the lightbulb get found and does what it says change the next choice, are the friends and the SOLD OUT sign fun to watch or slow after the third day, is a lemon making two cups clear on level 4, is a five-day week too long for one sitting, and does anyone want a saved week or a "close the stand" button? Does the pet's closing comparison say something the table did not already show?
