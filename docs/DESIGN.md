# Puddle Island: design and architecture

How the app works today: its principles, architecture, shared systems and contracts. What each game does is in [GAMES.md](GAMES.md); plans and proposals are in the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) and the [idea notebook](IDEAS.md); checks performed are in [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md). Contributor rules are in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

## Vision

Puddle Island is a home-made iPad learning game for the developer's daughter, who turns one in October 2026. Its activities grow from lap play through early school (ages 6–8), and the longer-term direction reaches elementary school through roughly grade 5 / age eleven. The inspirations are the discovery and playful learning of JumpStart, the companion and world of Neopets, and the variety of small arcade and Kongregate games. The island is an age trail of places full of small games, with a pet, its treehouse, a short story, and a separate couch route for grown-ups.

The developer is not an artist. All game artwork is generated in code with PixiJS Graphics, and sounds and music are synthesized with Web Audio. The visual direction should be achievable with the shared shape, animal, scenery, and particle builders, without sprites or an external asset pipeline.

## Play principles

- Large touch targets (at least 100 logical units), immediate touch-down feedback, and forgiving dragging.
- Spoken instructions; reading is never required to play. Tapping the pet repeats the instruction.
- Gentle feedback for mistakes, hints, and a glow after two misses. No failure screens.
- Predictable rewards: one sticker for each completed round.
- No ads, purchases, streaks, or timers that end a round. Session length is a grown-up setting, with a gentle goodnight flow.
- Early play supports a grown-up and child playing together. Games can include co-play prompts and related off-screen activities.
- Difficulty changes quietly within a chosen age band. Grown-ups can pin a level.

The current age bands in `src/progress/bands.ts` are lap (18–24 months), toddler (2–3 years), preschool (3–4 years), pre-K (5–6 years) and early school (`school`, 6–8 years). These are application groupings, not developmental assessments.

Each game supplies level ranges for the bands it supports. Two consecutive smooth rounds at the current level (at most one miss and no hints) move up; two struggling rounds (at least four misses or at least two hints per round) move down. Both changes stay within the selected band's range. Modes can change the task itself, rather than merely adding more objects.

## Architecture

### Application and scenes

`src/app/App.ts` owns the Pixi application, scene transitions, logical view, and frame loop. `Scene.ts` provides content, particle, and UI layers plus tracked updates. `routes.ts` creates scenes, while `session.ts` handles the session timer. The 1024×768 design area fits the display; layouts respond to the available logical width and height.

The game shell in `GameScene.ts` owns navigation, the guide pet, instructions, co-play prompts, saving, difficulty progression, stickers, and celebrations. This lets a new minigame concentrate on its own activity.

The shell has a grown-up **how-to card** for every touch game, from `src/content/howto.ts`: the game's goal, the usual gestures, how the round ends and any note about mistakes or experiments, plus the current level's `describeLevel` line. It comes two ways. **Every time a game is opened**, `GameScene` shows it as an intro before the round: a full-screen card with the game's picture and name (the name is spoken), a big green Play and a Back (the house). No `Game` has been built yet, so nothing runs, counts or speaks behind it; Play builds the game and starts it, and Back leaves. Stray taps on the card do nothing. The intro is skipped when a story request opens the game, when she taps "again" straight after a round (`go.game`'s fourth argument), when the game has no card, and when a grown-up has turned **Show how to play before each game** off (`settings.howToCards`, default on, in the grown-up zone). **A game with a bot also shows a demonstration** in a window, by what the person plays with: `islandDemo` (`src/content/demos.ts`) gives a game with `GameModule.touchDemo` a **ghost finger** (`input: 'finger'` on a touch screen, `'mouse'` on a desktop; `pointerKind()` in `src/engine/input.ts` follows the last real pointer, so a touch laptop follows whichever was used last, and starts from `(pointer: coarse)`) at *her* current level, so the window matches the card's "this level" line; a couch game with no touch bot yet keeps the couch's controller demonstration at the couch's demo level (`demoFor`, `src/couch/catalog.ts`; a stopgap, because a controller highlight is wrong for touch); every other game is text alone. `GameScene` makes a `Demo` (`src/couch/demo.ts`; the couch's how-to screen uses the same class) that `HowToPanel` places in a window (beside the text when the screen is wider than tall, above it otherwise, a little smaller as the text steps down to fit), updates each frame while the card is open (the round is held still, the demonstration is not), sets to `eventMode: 'none'` so no touch reaches the copy of the game inside it, and destroys with the card. While a demonstration plays, `audio.hush` lowers effects to 40% behind the spoken title (`closeHelp` gives them back); the card's note under the window says what is playing (a finger, a mouse pointer, or a controller moving the highlight). During a round, a quiet `?` under the home button opens the same card by holding it (a long hold, so a small hand does not open it) without Play, Back or a demonstration; it holds the round still while open. Neither way is a hint or changes progress. `HowToPanel` draws both and steps its text down to fit a short screen; the `howto` browser suite checks every game's card in landscape and portrait. The couch has its own how-to screens in `src/couch/catalog.ts`.

**Ghost finger.** A game opts in with an optional `Game.autotouch(dt): TouchIntent | null` and `touchDemo: true` on its module (`src/games/types.ts`). The intent names what a capable child would do next, as spots on display objects (`Spot { on, x?, y? }`, in that object's own coordinates, followed if it moves): `{ tap }`, `{ drag, to, lift? }` (a held thing rides 40 units above the finger, as `draggable` does; `to` is where the *object* should land) or `{ trace, via[] }` (a finger kept down, for steering and tracing). Return null while there is nothing to do yet. `GhostFinger` (`src/engine/ghost.ts`) owns everything else: it travels a drawn hand (or mouse cursor, `kind`) to the spot, presses, carries and lifts at a gentle pace, rests between gestures, and sends a real touch's events straight to the first spot's object with `emit` (`pointerdown`, `globalpointermove`, `pointerup`, `pointertap`, from pointer id `GHOST_POINTER`). Nothing is hit-tested and no window listener hears it, so it cannot reach the card's own Play and Back, and a game's own handlers (`onTap`, `draggable`, a steering layer's listeners) receive it as they would a finger; a game that listens on `window` (Splish Splash, Bouncy Launch, Rainbow Fingers) needs more thought before it can have one. The target may vanish under the finger (a bubble pops at once): the finger lifts where it is. `draggable` frees its one-at-a-time lock when a drag ends, because a ghost's drag never reaches the window `pointerup` that used to free it (without that, a real drag after Play was ignored; tested in `ghost.test.ts` and the `howto` suite). Keep the choice of target in the game's `logic.ts`, where a test can check that it always picks a right target and finishes a round; `BROWSER_SUITE=fingerdemo` proves the bot finishes at the game's first and last level inside the 40 seconds a demonstration gets before it replays.

### Minigame contract

Each `src/games/<id>/index.ts` exports a `GameModule`, registered in `src/games/registry.ts`. Metadata includes its subject (the `region` field, which groups it inside a place), supported bands, level ranges and descriptions, spoken title, music, hub icon, and sticker drawing. `create(ctx)` returns a game with `start`, `update`, `resize`, and `destroy` methods.

A game lists only the bands it supports. Its level table is a `BandLevels` (`src/progress/difficulty.ts`), read through `rangeFor(table, band)`; the shell only ever asks for a band the game lists, so adding a band never requires touching games that skip it.

A game draws into `ctx.stage`, uses the shared input, drag, tween, particle, and voice helpers, and reports completion through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`. Spoken lines belong in `src/content/voice-script.ts`. Shared art and audio should be reused so new activities feel part of the same world.

Optional parts of the contract, used by a few games and ignored by the rest: `RoundResult.creation` hands the shell something she made to keep in the treehouse; `RoundResult.discoveries` files journal entries; `RoundResult.score` / `scores` carry a couch face-off result; `GameContext.couch` asks a game to alternate two players or play a challenge course; `Game.control()` and `Game.autoplay()` take controller input and drive a bot; `Game.restart()` lets the couch pause menu start the round's current part over. Touch play never depends on any of them.

### Art, input, and sound

- `src/art/`: palette, parametric critters, props, shapes, scenery, particles, and sticker framing.
- `src/engine/`: view scaling, random generation, tweens, touch filtering, dragging, and `ball.ts`, a small deterministic ball, peg and wall simulation (with an optional ceiling and springy "kick" pegs) whose `simulate()` previews a shot. A tween whose target is destroyed mid-flight ends instead of freezing the scene.
- `src/audio/`: audio unlock and buses, instruments, sound effects, generative music, and speech.

Use the shared palette and pentatonic note steps. Route taps through `onTap` and drags through `drag.ts` for consistent cooldown, palm rejection, and finger offset behavior.

### Persistence and grown-up UI

`src/progress/save.ts` defines the save record and `migrate()`, which repairs incomplete or invalid input. `store.ts` persists it through `idb-keyval`: most changes are batched for 300 ms and flushed when the page is hidden, but the shell writes a finished round and its sticker immediately, because a write that only starts as the page closes can be lost. New fields need defaults, migration behavior, and tests so existing progress survives updates.

The current save contains profile, settings, game statistics and recent round history, and earned sticker records. It also stores pet customization, the highest band celebrated on the map, optional normalized sticker placements, and `stories.picnic`: the Windy Picnic's done steps (a bounded list of three known IDs, kept in story order), whether this telling's ending was shown, and whether its keepsake was earned. (An earlier list of opened regions is dropped on load.) Invalid or unknown placement pages become unplaced stickers; unknown story steps are dropped, an ending needs every step, and an ending always keeps the keepsake. Legacy saves keep their progress, start with an unhatched pet, and start the picnic untold. It also stores `favorites`, the games she has hearted (oldest first, at most twelve, short unique strings; one that no longer exists simply never shows). It also stores `settings.howToCards`, the grown-up's switch for the how-to cards (on unless a save says otherwise). An earlier version also kept a list of games already explained; it is gone, and a restored save that has it is read without it. Resetting progress in the grown-up zone also resets the story and the hearts.

The save also holds the treehouse: `room` (six furnishings and an optional framed sticker), `creations` (a picture-board slot and a tune slot, each with what is on show and one earlier piece) and `journal` (found entries and how many she has seen). Each is bounded and repaired on load; see [Pet treehouse](#pet-treehouse). Couch play keeps its own separate save ([Couch play](#couch-play)).

Grown-up controls are plain HTML in `src/parent/panel.ts`. The parent gate is on the island map and requires holding both top corners for three seconds.

### Delivery and verification

Vite builds a static site; `vite-plugin-pwa` generates the manifest and offline cache. Pushes to `main` deploy through GitHub Actions. Commit and push only when asked.

Unit tests cover saves, difficulty, game registry constraints, view geometry, critters, and the musical scale. The dev-only `neo` and `kit` browser helpers support scripted play-throughs. New game verification should include a completed round, wrong answers, hints, and resizing. Production offline behavior must be checked with the built app, separately from the dev server.

## The child's island

### The age trail

The island map is the age selection. `src/content/places.ts` defines five places on a switchback trail, from the shore over the summit: Puddle Lagoon (lap), Daisy Meadow (toddler), Bumpy Hills (preschool), Starry Peak (pre-K) and Wonder Woods (`school`, early school, ages 6–8). Each place holds every game that supports its band, at that band's levels, so a game that grows with the child stands in several places. Within a place, games are grouped by subject using the ten stable IDs in `src/content/world.ts` (senses, music, art, animals, numbers, everyday life, colors and shapes, puzzles, stories, science); those IDs used to be map regions.

Every place is open to everyone. The grown-up's age band is the child's home: the pet waits beside that place on the map, and the play button goes straight there. Raising the band throws the pet a birthday and walks it up the trail once; the highest celebrated band is saved, so lowering and restoring a band does not replay it.

A place lays out its games in a zigzag of two rows along a sandy path. When they do not fit on one screen, the land swipes sideways, with momentum, and arrow buttons page it. Because a swipe may start on a game, a game opens when the finger lifts without having swiped; it still squishes on touch-down so the response is immediate. Each place remembers its scroll for the session, so coming home from a game lands where the child left.

The grown-up **Finding games** setting can instead select `SubjectPlaceScene`: four illustrated subject cards per page, then up to four game cards in the chosen subject. Arrows and horizontal swipes page both views; moving more than 24 logical units cancels selection. Card presses respond immediately, and release selects. Native cancellation/lost-release listeners are released when leaving. A picture-grid button returns to the subject choices, with separate subject-list and game-page memory per band for the session. Spoken names and the guide support navigation without reading. The original `PlaceScene` remains the default and retains its separate scroll memory. `settings.placeLayout` is `path` or `subjects`; missing or invalid values migrate to `path`, and backups preserve the choice. The iPad comparison remains open.

### New games and favorites

Two small, ungated aids to finding a game. A game is **new** while `stats.plays` is zero (`isNew` in `src/content/shelf.ts`: a finished round in any place or as a picnic step counts, reading a level does not); a `Sparkle` (a four-point gold star that twinkles and never takes a touch, tracked by the scene) sits on its landmark in `PlaceScene` (also in `PlaceScene.sparkles`, by game ID) and on its card in `SubjectPlaceScene`, where a subject card twinkles while any of its games is new. The Favorites card never twinkles. Nothing says "new" in words.

A **heart** is how a game is kept. `GameScene.celebrate` puts a pink heart button (outlined when off, solid when on, a star burst and a spoken line when it turns on) beside the new sticker, away from the celebrating pet and the again/home buttons; `store.toggleFavorite` flips it and `after.heart` exposes it. A heart changes nothing about stickers, levels or adaptation. `favorites` in the save is oldest-first and bounded to twelve (`FAVORITES_MAX`): a new heart on a full list lets the oldest go. `shelfFor` picks a place's shelf: its hearted games in the order they were hearted, the newest five (`SHELF_SLOTS`), skipping games that do not play in that band. `PlaceScene` shows them on a `Shelf` (`src/ui/shelf.ts`, a translucent panel on a wooden plank with a heart set in it, each game's own landmark drawing and a 116 by 128 touch area, launching like a landmark) standing in the sky above the back row, fixed while the land swipes; with no hearts there is no shelf. `SubjectPlaceScene` leads with a **Favorites** card (a big heart) only when this place has a heart, leading to up to twelve hearted games four to a page; a remembered Favorites page whose hearts are gone falls back to the subjects. Undecided: whether the shelf should also feed the map, whether a grown-up should be able to curate it, and how it feels on a device.

## Pet treehouse

`RoomScene` is one room reached from the map's treehouse button. `src/content/room.ts` holds the rules (six items from `ROOM_ITEMS`, a floor region, `clampSpot`, move, flip, tidy); `src/art/room.ts` draws each piece from shapes; the saved `room` field in `SaveData` is `{ items: [{ id, x, y, flip }], frame: { game, seed } | null }` with positions as fractions of the room, so a layout survives any screen shape. `migrate()` always returns exactly one of each item, clamped to the floor, and a frame only when it is well formed (a sticker the save no longer has just shows an empty frame). The room is part of the one save, so a backup carries it. The frame shows a sticker without using it up: the sticker stays in the book and can still be placed on a page. There is no hunger, no dirt, no timer and no currency; leaving the room, or the app, changes nothing. Touching a furnishing sends the pet to play with it (`RoomScene.use`); the lamp's dimming is not saved. A moved item that was only tapped (less than 14 units of travel) is a tap, not a move.

**Creations.** What she makes can hang on the wall. A game hands the shell something she made or completed through the optional `RoundResult.creation` (`src/content/creations.ts`): Stamp Studio sends its stamps as a `picture` (up to 24: kind, color, position as fractions of the paper, size, turns); Rainbow Fingers' two open-ended levels send the brush dabs and tap-grown flowers as another `picture` (normalized positions and sizes, paper aspect, and at most 320 code-drawn marks; guided coloring pages send nothing); Pixel Pictures sends the last design completed in the round as a 4×4 to 6×6 palette grid (at most 36 colored cells); and Song Maker's free levels send the lit jellies as a `tune` (a grid of 2 to 8 beats and 3 to 5 pitches with at least two different jellies; a copied song is not hers and sends nothing). `GameScene.celebrate` shows a tree house button beside the new sticker only when a creation came back; tapping it calls `store.keepCreation` and turns into a check, and nothing is kept otherwise (so a round that ends because the session's time is up goes straight to goodnight and offers nothing: open). The save has a picture-board slot shared by all three visual media, and a tune slot; each has `current` (on show) and `previous`. Keeping puts the new one on show and moves the one it replaces to `previous`; keeping what is already on show changes nothing, so a repeat tap cannot push the earlier one out; `undoCreation` swaps the two, so asking again puts things as they were. So at most two visual works and two tunes are kept, and nothing is ever removed outright. `cleanCreations` repairs a damaged or restored save (bad stamps, paint marks or pixel cells dropped, visual work bounded, coordinates and aspect clamped, notes off the grid dropped, and too-short tunes or empty visual works become empty places). In `RoomScene`, the current visual work hangs on a board and the tune on a plaque beside the window and frame (`src/ui/creation-art.ts`, redrawn from `stampArt`, paint marks, pixel cells or a grid of colored jellies); touching the board sends the pet to look and touching the plaque sends it to sing the song beat by beat (`tuneBeats` turns it into the scale steps the game itself uses, played on `sfx.marimba`, each column lit as it sounds); an empty board or plaque says what could hang there. A small back arrow above each appears only when there is an earlier one. Tidy and a reload leave both alone. Not built: taking a creation down, a gallery of more than one earlier piece.

**Discovery journal.** `JournalScene` (`go.journal()`, a magnifying-glass button in the treehouse) is a paged grid of cards, eight to a page, one for every entry in `JOURNAL` (`src/content/journal.ts`): the nine things in Sink or Float (floaters first) and the seven animals in Animal Snack. Entry ids are `game:key` and never change. A game reports what a round actually showed through `RoundResult.discoveries`: Sink or Float notes each thing that went into the water (a thing that was only sorted correctly was never tested, so it is not found), Animal Snack notes each animal that ate its favorite food. `GameScene.finish` files the new ones with `store.discover`, and the celebration shows them in a small card (a magnifying glass and up to three pictures) before the buttons. The save's `journal` is `{ found, seen }`: `found` in the order found, only known ids, once each, and `seen` how many she had looked at when she last opened it (never more than found), which only drives the twinkle on the treehouse button and on new cards. A found card shows the picture and name; an unfound one a dim silhouette and a question mark. Touching a card speaks its observation (or "not found yet") and fills the strip below with the observation text and where it was found; the green arrow opens the game at her band or the highest band that game plays (`go.game` does nothing for an unsupported band). Unit tests keep it honest: every thing and animal has exactly one entry, every entry's game exists, and each spoken line is true to the game's own data (a floater's line says it floats, a sinker's that it sinks, an animal's names its food). Not built: other sources, notes she writes herself, a trail to the spot in a game, and a journal entry for a creation.

## The Windy Picnic

The first Island Errands story, built as the roadmap's small pilot. Its rules live in `src/content/picnic.ts`, its art in `src/art/picnic.ts`, and its scene in `src/app/scenes/PicnicScene.ts`; the island map shows it as a picnic blanket beside Daisy Meadow, open to every age like the places (it is a story, not a place on the age trail).

- **The muddle.** The wind has blown Juniper the gardener bunny's picnic away. Three request pictures (a striped blanket, a plate of sandwiches, an invitation with a note) can be tapped in any order; the first not yet done glows. Juniper says each request and tapping her repeats it, as does the pet guide.
- **The blanket**, played in the scene: two blankets (lap and toddler) or three (striped, spotted and checked, each with its own color) hang in the tree, on the clothesline or on the bush. Juniper's is striped. At early school two blankets are striped, so the request also says where it landed. A wrong blanket is explained (its pattern, or where it is) and two misses make the right one glow; the right one floats down and spreads out. Touches go to the nearest blanket. This step has no round result, so it gives no sticker.
- **The sandwiches and the invitation** are Pet Kitchen and Jelly Drums rounds at a level chosen per band (`GAME_STEPS`; a band a game lacks plays its nearest supported band, so lap plays Pet Kitchen's toddler level). Finishing the round completes the step, however much help it took. Coming back, the plates arrive on the blanket or the friends (a cat, a duck and a bear) hop in. Leaving a round early returns to the picnic with nothing changed.
- **The ending.** With all three done the friends eat to Juniper's invitation song, and her photo of the picnic is saved as the one keepsake as soon as it appears, then flies into the journal. A reload before then replays the ending rather than skipping it.
- **The journal** (top right) shows the three requests with a check and a picture of what each changed; tapping a picture replays its request or recap, and a green arrow goes straight to the next request. After the ending it shows the photo and offers to tell the story again, which clears the steps and keeps the photo. The grown-up zone summarizes the story's progress.
- **What it does not do.** The picnic shows a fixed picture of each step (four plates, three friends, Juniper's tune), not what the child made: the round result carries only misses and hints. Nothing expires, nothing is lost, and the story never awards stickers of its own.

A round can also be played as a step of the Windy Picnic: `go.game(id, band, { step, level })` plays the story's level for the child's band, and `go.picnic(step)` comes back to show what changed. In a story round the shell records the round and awards its sticker as usual, but leaves the game's adaptive level where it was, marks the step done (idempotently), and sends home and the celebration's basket button back to the picnic. When a scene is destroyed, its tweens and the promises from `ctx.say`/`ctx.instruct` stop resolving, so a game waiting on a line or an animation can't carry on after the child has left.

### Proposed: story results

Not implemented. To carry what the child made into a story, a round could finish with an optional, typed `story` field beside `misses` and `hints`, defined per step, for example `{ kind: 'shares', plates: number[] }` from Pet Kitchen or `{ kind: 'tune', notes: number[] }` from Jelly Drums. The story would also need to pass the step's request into the round (an exact quantity to share, a tune to finish) through the game context, and each participating game would opt in by reading it. The shell would save the result with the step, bounded in size and validated in `migrate()` like the rest of the save, so a replayed round replaces the step's result rather than adding another. Games that don't opt in keep finishing with `{ misses, hints }` alone.

## Couch play

A route for one grown-up, or two taking turns (and, later, a child watching or joining), played with a controller or keyboard on a computer connected to a TV. It is separate from the child's island in code, save and rules: `CouchScene` (`src/app/scenes/CouchScene.ts`) is a DOM overlay over the Pixi canvas, and `src/couch/` holds everything it needs. Unlocks, scores and face-off live only here; the child's island keeps every game open.

- **Contract additions (all optional, so touch play is unchanged).** `Game.control(input, dt)` takes semantic controller input; `Game.autoplay(dt)` returns the input a capable player would press; `Game.restart()` (optional) puts the current part of the round back to its first position, with tries already made still counted, and is offered in the pause menu when the game implements it and its `COUCH_INFO` entry has a `restart` label (Penguin Slide: "Start this pond again"); a restart asked for mid-animation waits for it to finish; `Game.askForHint()` (optional) is help she asks for, offered in the pause menu when the game implements it and its entry has a `hint` label (Sudoku Garden: "Show a hint"), for a puzzle with no misses to count; a hint it shows marks a course run as helped through the same `progress` callback; `RoundResult.score` / `scores` carry a face-off result; `GameContext.couch` says a shared-board game should alternate two players (`versus`) or play a fixed-board challenge (`course`: a resume point and a `progress` callback).
- **Catalog.** `src/couch/catalog.ts` holds, per couch game, the spoken goal line, control rows (named by controller position), who plays (turns or together), the face-off kind (`twin`, `shared` or `team`) with which score wins, the band and the demo level, the level each stop plays, and couch wording for lines that tell a touch player to tap or pull. `src/couch/party.ts` holds the ID list, the unlock tiers and the pure rules; both are tested.
- **How to play.** The first time a game is chosen, `CouchScene.intro` shows its name, spoken goal, a code-drawn controller (`controller-art.ts`) with the pressed parts lit, and a window running `Demo` (`demo.ts`): a real, separate instance of the game on a fixed seed, in a scaled and masked container with its own tweens, particles and pet, played by the game's `autoplay` through the same `control()` a controller uses. Nothing in it is saved, rewarded or spoken. A game that has been explained shows a name card with **Play** and **How to play**: the card starts the round by itself after a moment, but moving the highlight (right or down) cancels that and waits, so a player can ask for the how-to without racing the countdown. The face-off turn card offers it too. Pause offers it mid-round, and the couch menu has a **How to play** guide, one card per open game, whose how-to screen has a Back button instead of Play, so it never selects a game or touches the trip; seeing a game's how-to there counts as explaining it (the NEW mark clears). `CouchScene.intro` takes where it was opened from (`first`, `pause`, `guide` or `turn`), and Back returns there. A browser check that the bot completes a round is also a check that the controls can.
- **Saves.** `neo.couch.v1` (localStorage), version 3: the trip in progress (six stops, mode, reshuffle count, a pending first turn), completed trips, one sticker count and latest seed per game, the games already explained, the keepsake, two optional player names (cleaned and bounded to fourteen letters), each challenge course's run and records, and the grown-up's `settings` (below). Fields added after version 3 (names, courses, settings) did not change the version: repair gives an older save the defaults, and a build from before a field reads the same save and drops what it does not know, where a version bump would make that older build discard the whole save. Versions 1 and 2 are upgraded (a save that had finished a trip gets the keepsake with an unknown date); repair never keeps a selection the save has not unlocked, and a restored backup is written back byte for byte as play wrote it.
- **The finale.** `finaleOf(save)` (`src/couch/finale.ts`, pure and tested) summarizes a finished trip: the verdict (a face-off winner is named, a loser never is; a level score is "you both win the evening"), who took each stop, the games the trip opened and how many the next will. `FinaleStage` (`src/couch/finale-stage.ts`) is the Pixi sky under the DOM page: six lanterns rise and light one after another in the color of whoever took the stop (blue Player 1, pink Player 2, gold shared), then confetti and a cheering pet; it owns its tweens, particles and pet, so leaving the screen stops all of it. Coming back to the page (from Controller setup, say) shows the lit sky without replaying the fanfare. The page itself is glass panels, a scoreboard in a face-off, a six-stop recap, the opened games and the keepsake.
- **The keepsake.** One, "Lantern Night", kept from the first finished trip of either mode and never added to: `completeRound` sets it when a trip's sixth stop settles (`keepsake ??= { at }`), so a replayed or reloaded finish cannot award it again. It is a postcard drawn in DOM and CSS plus the pet, dated `at`, shown on the finale the first time and on the couch start page afterwards.
- **Challenge courses.** A course is a fixed run of parts in one game, played in a row as one round and scored by the tries it takes. The parts are frozen data in each game (`games/penguin-slide/course.ts`: two courses of five ponds as pictures in text, **Pond Practice** (minimum 18, no dead ends, so nobody is ever stuck) and **The Five Ponds** (minimum 30, each pond with one shortest route and traps), with the fewest slides for each pond worked out by the solver, never typed in; `games/bouncy-launch/course.ts`: twelve target clouds and how small they are; `games/sudoku-garden/course.ts`: **Six Beds** (six 6 by 6 beds, par 124 entries) and **Three Big Beds** (three 9 by 9 beds, par 149), counted in entries, each placed number being one try and a bed's empty squares its par; `games/lantern-lights/course.ts`: **Dusk on the Pond**, a 4 by 4 and four 5 by 5 ponds counted in presses with par 36 from the solver; `games/picture-logic/course.ts`: **Pond Pictures** (three 10 by 10 pictures, par 182) and **The Big Pictures** (two 15 by 15, par 230), counted in fills, a picture's filled squares being its par; `games/ferry-jam/course.ts`: **Busy Harbors**, five frozen harbors counted in slides, par 64 from the solver; `games/word-search/course.ts`: **Pond Words** (three themed grids, par 25) and **The Big Hunt** (two 14 by 14 grids, par 24), counted in guesses, one per word being par; `games/island-bridges/course.ts`: **Island Hopping**, five frozen seas counted in planks, par 120 from the solver; `games/pond-conga/course.ts`: **Crumb Trail**, four frozen ponds counted in steps, par 259 from the solver), each with a `version` that must change if a part does. `src/couch/courses.ts` is the registry (`practice`, `ponds`, `clouds`, `beds`, `bigbeds`, `lanterns`, `pictures`, `bigpictures`, `harbors`, `words`, `bigwords`, `bridges`, `conga`): which game plays it, at what level, what its parts and its score are called, the fewest tries for each part, and the text the page shows. `GameContext.couch.course` carries a resume point and a `progress` callback. Penguin Slide counts every successful slide (undone ones included; blocked bumps and the undo button add nothing) and shows each pond's score for a moment before moving straight on; Bouncy Launch counts every launch, a miss included, on clouds that are smaller than the learning levels' (so a launch is judged by feel), keeps quiet between launches, and brings its hint (the landing preview and a ring for the right pull) after two misses at one cloud. Each finishes once. `src/couch/course.ts` holds the pure rules, tested and generic over every course: a `Run` (player, token, finished parts, tries at the part in progress, whether a hint was shown), a `PlayerRecord` per player (best with no help and best with help kept apart, last ten runs, two badges), `startRun`/`noteProgress`/`finishRun` (a stale token or an unfinished run settles nothing) and `completeCourse`, which adds the run's single sticker. The shell saves after every try, so a reload keeps them; a run resumes at the part it was on, restarting that part with its tries so far still counted. A hint, once shown, marks the run as helped for good. Records compare only within a course version; when a version changes the old records are retired (at most three kept), never mixed in. Badges per player: *Finished* (any finish) and a perfect run (the minimum, no hint), each earned once. `CouchScene` shows a Challenges menu (both courses with each player's mark), a course page (both players' records, the rules of the score, and a way in or back into a run left halfway) and a result (the total against the minimum, the best, new badges, and tries on each part).
- **Playing alone.** `settings.players === 'one'` (`CouchScene.alone()`) changes what is drawn, never what is saved: the start page is *Just for you* (a **Puzzle shelf**, and a Together-only **Start a trip**), a face-off is not offered, turn cards and the face-off tally are not drawn, the course page shows only Player 1's card of marks, the Resume button carries no name, Settings shows one name field, and the Challenges menu becomes the shelf. Records stay Player 1's, in the same `CourseSave.players` pair, so switching back to two loses nothing, and a trip or run already under way finishes the way it began. `src/couch/shelf.ts` (pure, tested) holds the shelf's rules: `shelfEntry` (her best, preferring a run with no hint; the minimum, which is par; tries above par; whether a run is half played), `standing` (the line under a puzzle: “21 slides · 3 above par (18)”) and `canWatchRoute`. The shelf today is the courses; a new puzzle joins it by being a course. Past six puzzles its cards drop their description (the course page has it) so the shelf fits a 1024 by 768 window without scrolling up to nine puzzles (it scrolls with the controller beyond that).
- **Watch the best routes.** `CourseInfo.routes` says a course's game can replay the best way through each part; Penguin Slide's ponds can, because its demo bot already walks the solver's next slide from wherever the penguin is, and every course pond has exactly one shortest route (tested). `Demo` takes an optional `part: { course, board }`: it gives the game a `couch.course` resume at that part and ends the replay when the part's `progress` reports it finished, then rests and plays it again. `CouchScene.watchRoute` shows the pond in the same window as How to play, with a button per pond. Nothing is scored or saved. `canWatchRoute` refuses it in the middle of a run (it would be a hint she did not ask for) and offers it after a run finishes or once the course has been finished at least once.
- **Settings.** `src/couch/settings.ts` (pure, tested) holds what the Settings page chooses, kept in the couch save as `settings: { place, volume, music, text, players }` (`players` last, so a restored backup keeps its key order), bounded and repaired on load. `place` is `auto`, `tv` or `laptop`; `resolvePlace` turns `auto` into `tv` when a standard controller is connected (`CouchControls.controllers`, counted by the sampler every frame) or the window's scale is at least `TV_SCALE` (1.35: a 1080p screen in full screen is about 1.4, a laptop window with its browser chrome is below it), and `laptop` otherwise, so prompts follow the controller as it connects. The place only decides what is *named*: `prompts(parts, place)` and `padArt(place)` in `controller-art.ts` draw either controller glyphs and the controller diagram or key caps (`keyCaps`, the stick and d-pad being one set of arrow keys) and a keyboard diagram that lights the same way, and `menuHint` and `cardHint` word the lines under menus; the controls themselves accept a controller and the keyboard at once in every place, and a row's `keys` line (which arrows, whose keys) stays unless the key cap already says it. `volume` is a step from 0 to 5 (`gainFor`: 4 is 0.8, the island's own default) and `music` a switch; `CouchScene.applyPrefs` pushes them into the audio engine when a couch scene starts and again on any change or restore, and `destroy()` calls the island's `applySettings()` so the child's volume returns (a scene change between couch screens passes through that, unheard). `text` is `normal`, `large` (1.2) or `xlarge` (1.4): `unitFor` multiplies the window scale (still kept between 1 and 2.2) and `applyPrefs` writes the result to `--u` on the couch overlay itself, overriding the root value `App.applyView` sets, and again from `resize`. The page (`CouchScene.settingsPage`) is a grid of panels of choice buttons with `aria-pressed`, rebuilt after each change with focus kept on the same button; the pause menu's four buttons (`quickSettings`) change the same fields. Names and the backup moved here from Controller setup. `players` is `two` (the default, so an older save keeps playing as it did) or `one`; it is also a choice on the couch start page. Not built: a Tablet place and pointer play on the couch stage, and an offer filter for keyboard-only games.
- **Readable from a couch.** `App.applyView` sets a CSS length `--u` (the window's scale against the 1024-unit design area, kept between 1 and 2.2); every length in `src/couch/style.css` is `calc(N px * var(--u))`, so couch pages grow with a TV (about 1.4 at 1080p) and stay as they were at 1024 by 768. The text size setting multiplies it on the couch overlay. Pages are laid out to fit a 1920 by 1080 screen without scrolling (checked for every couch game's how-to screen and in-round bar at five window sizes). The controller reminder under a round fades after ten seconds so it never keeps covering the round (Secret Code's stones, for one), and shows again when a pause ends. The finale ceremony takes about four seconds before its panels fade in (not at all with reduced motion).
- **Unlocks.** `UNLOCK_TIERS` is an ordered table; a tier opens per completed trip (either mode). It is derived from the trip count and never stored, so reloads and restored backups cannot award twice, and nothing locks again. The trip-complete screen names newly opened games and says only how many remain; the NEW mark clears when a game's how-to screen has been seen.
- **Stick-driven launchers.** A finger can pull Bouncy Launch's pet back along any line, which stretches the spring sideways like a slingshot; a stick cannot, and a spring that leans left when a button says "more power" looks wrong. In couch play (`GameContext.couch` set, or any `control()` call) the spring squashes straight down by the power instead: stick down or right is more, up or left is less, the red plate and the pet ride down with it, the power persists between launches (a retry is a nudge), and a hint's outline marks where to push the plate to. The power is also the landing spot, so a launch needs no pull to be measured. Touch play, and the child's version, are unchanged. A catapult arm (the pet in a cup that swings back) would suit the finger's sideways pull better and is a possible redraw of the launcher for both.
- **Choosing.** `offers()` draws three from the opened games, seeded and stable across reloads, preferring games not yet played this trip and not the last two; a free shuffle changes the seed.
- **Face-off.** `twin`: each player plays their own board in turn (a different seed per turn, so the second player never faces a board whose answer they have watched), scored relative to that board's own best so boards compare fairly; the first turn is saved so leaving between turns changes nothing. `shared`: one board, the game alternates players and reports `scores`. `team`: both score. Ties score for both; equal-information is the rule that decides which games qualify (a game whose boards are fixed per level, like Robot Path, can only be a team game). One sticker per stop. The trip ends with a tally and never a loser: everyone lights every lantern.
- **Grown-up-only games.** `GROWNUP_GAMES` in `src/games/registry.ts` holds games made for the couch route that the child's island must never list (Sudoku Garden is the first). They are not in `GAMES`, so `PlaceScene`, the subject layout, the favorites shelf, the parent panel's level pickers, the sticker book, the journal and `go.game` cannot see them (the registry tests also keep their ids apart from `GAMES`); `couchGameById` knows both lists and is what `CouchScene`, the couch tests and the couch browser suites use. Such a game is still a `GameModule` (it needs a hub icon, sticker and voice lines, which the couch screens draw and speak, and declares one band so the couch catalog's level checks have a range) and a `COUCH_IDS` entry with a tier slot and a catalog entry, but has no touch how-to card and no child history; its stickers are couch stickers. Nothing about it has a touch path: the stage takes no pointer input on the couch.
- **Sudoku Garden.** Its rules are `src/games/sudoku-garden/logic.ts`: flat grids (0 is empty) over a `geometry(size)` of houses and peers (6 by 6 with 2 by 3 boxes, 9 by 9), `search` for counting solutions and growing full beds (fewest-choices-first, digits in random order when given an `Rng`), `techniqueSolve` (lone, place, pair and locked steps, easiest first, each recorded with its house, pattern and what it rules out; `stopAtPlacement` is what a hint needs), `gradeOf`, `makePuzzle` and `hintFor`. `course.ts` freezes the two courses' beds (`beds` and `bigbeds`), reads them with `parsePuzzle` (solution found by the solver) and takes the par from the empty squares, never from a typed number. The scene (`index.ts`) draws the bed and a number tray into the couch stage, takes input only from `control()` (edge and held directions, with a 0.4 second delay and then repeat), counts every placement as a course try, and reports a bed's numbers through `progress` like Penguin Slide does; its `autoplay` walks to the nearest number the easy steps can place and chooses it from the tray, which is both the how-to demo and the browser check that a controller can finish a bed. Trip stops play levels 1 to 3 (6 by 6 beds drawn from the seed, scored in entries over the empty squares); the 9 by 9 levels exist for the generator, and the one course that uses them is frozen data.
- **Lantern Lights.** Its rules are `src/games/lantern-lights/logic.ts`: a board is a flat array (`true` is lit); `solve` finds every set of presses that lights a board by Gaussian elimination over two numbers (rows as bigint bit masks) and returns the fewest, how many ways win (2 to the free choices) and whether none do; `makePond` presses a plan's number of different lanterns on an all-lit pond; `hintFor` picks a lantern in a fewest-press way, nearest the cursor. `course.ts` freezes Dusk on the Pond's five ponds, with the par from the solver. The scene (`index.ts`) draws code-drawn paper lanterns on a night pond (each lantern is a lit and a dark picture, turned over with a tween that a later flip takes over from), takes input only from `control()` (with `HeldDirection`), counts every press as a course try and reports through `progress`; its `autoplay` presses the hint's lantern, so a bot always plays a fewest-press way. The grown-up-only games share `Game.askForHint` and the pause-menu button.
- **Picture Logic.** Its rules are `src/games/picture-logic/logic.ts` and its drawings `pictures.ts` (rows of characters in palette letters; the 6 by 6 level reuses Pixel Pictures' logic-solvable pictures, and `placements` is exported from `pixel-pictures/logic.ts` for the line solver). A grid is a flat array of marks (empty, filled, crossed); `lineStatus` judges a line done, over or open from its clue and marks alone; `paint` is the pure rule for what a brush does to a square and whether it counts (only a fill does); `deductions` finds every square one line decides, and `hintFor` picks one with its line and clue. `course.ts` freezes Pond Pictures and The Big Pictures by reference to the drawings, with the par from the drawing's filled squares and a test pinning each picture's clues. Painting by holding a button needs to know the button is *down*, which the sampler's one-frame `action` and `undo` do not say, so `PlayerControls` gained `hold` and `holdUndo` (true for as long as the bottom or left button is down); the scene starts a stroke on the press (the first square decides the brush) and carries it along as the cursor moves while the button is held, and its `autoplay` keeps `hold` true in every frame of a run, because a stroke ends the moment the button is not held. The scene (`index.ts`) draws clue gutters, the grid and a reveal in the picture's colors, counts every fill as a course try and reports through `progress`.
- **Ferry Jam on the couch (Harbor Rush).** An island game can take a couch mode the way Penguin Slide did, and Ferry Jam did: `control()`, `autoplay()`, `restart()`, `askForHint()` and a course, with the touch drag path untouched. `boatToward` and `focusPath` in `ferry-jam/logic.ts` are the highlight's pure rules (the nearest boat in a quarter-turn cone, else the nearest on that side; a test walks every harbor of the game and every boat along the best way out to prove each boat can always be reached); the scene keeps `focus` (the boat highlighted) and `grab` (the boat held, its lane position and how far it can go), moves a held boat one cell per push with `HeldDirection`, and commits a slide when it is set down somewhere new, which `moves` counts exactly as a touch drop does. On the couch the round arrow is hidden, and the automatic hint after many slides is off (a hint is asked for in the pause menu and marks a run helped). `course.ts` freezes Busy Harbors by picking five of the game's solver-checked harbors, with the par from `solve`; because its bot follows the solver and the `progress` callback reports a finished harbor, the course has `routes: true` and **Watch the best routes** replays each harbor.
- **Word Search.** Its rules are `src/games/word-search/logic.ts`, its content `words.ts` (nine themed lists of plain capital words, kept as data) and `blocked.ts` (profanity and slurs that must never be readable in a grid). `makeGrid` places a plan's words longest first, then redraws only the filler letters until every listed word occurs exactly once in the eight directions and no blocked word can be read; `lineBetween` turns two squares into the squares of a straight line (or nothing), `wordAt` reads a marked line either way against the words not found yet, and `hintFor` picks the first letter of the unfound word nearest the cursor. `course.ts` freezes the two courses' grids with their words, found by looking (`parseGrid` insists on exactly one occurrence), and a test checks the block list against them. The scene (`index.ts`) draws the letters and a pale capsule for the line being marked, a colored band for each found word, and the word list as a panel; it counts every checked line as a course try and reports through `progress`.
- **Pond Conga.** Its rules are `src/games/pond-conga/logic.ts`. `step` is the whole rule of play: a requested turn (straight back is ignored), the leader moves one cell, and the result is a `move`, a `crumb` (the line grows by one duckling) or a `bonk` (a lily pad, the bank or the line itself: nothing moves, the whole line is reversed so the old tail leads, and the heading continues outward from it, so a line can never box itself in; the tail's cell counts as free unless the line is growing). Every step and every bonk is one try; the pace (`speed`, steps a second) is constant within a pond and barely rises across levels, and the scene starts a pond only after the first turn of the stick. The fewest steps for a pond is exact: `lowerBound` adds the lily-pad-aware breadth-first distances between the crumbs (no route can be shorter), and `makePond` keeps a pond only if `solveRoute` finds a route with that many steps and no bonk (`legRoutes` walks the shortest paths to the next crumb with the line's own body simulated, memoizing dead places, and `solveFrom` backtracks over legs). So par is a fact about the pond, not a typed number; `parsePond` refuses a frozen picture that has no such route. `hintPath` is the shortest bonk-free way from wherever the line has wandered, a few steps longer if its own tail is in the way for a while (a property test wanders at random and then follows hints, and every crumb is eaten with at most four bonks). The scene (`index.ts`) ticks in `update` (turns are queued by `control`, two at most), glides the line between ticks, shows the next crumb and a faint one after it, and reports each step through the course `progress`; its `autoplay` follows the pond's own route while the line is on it (so a bot run is exactly par) and the hint's direction otherwise. `course.ts` freezes Crumb Trail's four ponds as pictures (`S` the leader, `s` the line, `#` a lily pad, 1 to 9 then a to c the crumbs in order) with the pace in `speed`.
- **Island Bridges.** Its rules are `src/games/island-bridges/logic.ts`: `layoutOf` (the pairs of islands that face each other and which bridges would cross), `canAdd`, `isSolved`, `trouble` (an island that has too many planks or can no longer reach its number, from the numbers alone), `solve` (propagation of each island's need against its room, with search over what is left and a connectivity check on a finished try; a `noGuessing` option stops after propagation, which `bycounting` uses to grade the easy levels), `makePuzzle` (grow a layout and its planks, number the islands, keep it only if there is exactly one answer, and sort the islands into reading order so a puzzle read back from its picture is numbered the same), `islandToward` and `focusPath` (the highlight, tested to reach every island), and `hintFor`. `course.ts` freezes Island Hopping's five seas as pictures (digits for islands), with the par from `parsePuzzle`, which refuses any picture without exactly one answer. The scene (`index.ts`) draws the sea, plank bridges (a double bridge as two lines) and green or red islands, has three modes (`move`, `add`, `remove`), counts every plank laid as a course try and reports through `progress`.
- **Why these fourteen.** Penguin Slide, Bouncy Launch and Bounce Back came first. Then games that are seeded, tap-only or simple to map to a stick and two buttons, short, and varied in what they ask: Memory Match (memory, shared board), Rhythm Neighbors (listening, team), Light Lab and Secret Code (planning and deduction), Peg Garden (aim), Bumper Garden (a two-player flipper game). Five more followed, each a few lines of `control()` on an existing game, with the last three tiers opening them two, two and one at a time: **Pattern Train** (a ring over three choices; pre-K shape, animal and two-gap levels, never the bell levels, which need listening and a confirm), **Egg Catch** (the stick slides the basket on the two catching levels; with two controllers both steer the same basket, and the chute levels stay touch puzzles), **Frog Hop** (a ring over the lily pads, or the number cards on the counting level; add, take away, how many hops, and the line to 20), **Sink or Float** (its one guessing level with the surprises, float on the left and sink on the right; guesses are never wrong) and **Robot Path** (steps from the stick, the bottom button plays, the left button takes the last step back out; levels 4–6, the rock puzzles). All five are team games. Robot Path's boards are authored per level rather than drawn from the seed, so a second player would face a route they had watched, and Egg Catch's catching levels would mostly end level; the other three are gentle by design. That leaves face-off trips with more team stops than before, so the next couch games should be ones that can score on seeded boards. Considered and set aside: Owl Walk Home (a cooperative child's game).
- **Not built yet.** Further courses, family leaderboards, the daughter joining as a co-pilot, simultaneous face-off boards, Joy-Con support, and real controller and TV validation.

### Extending couch play

**Adding a couch game.** A game becomes a couch game by implementing the optional `control(input, dt)` and `autoplay(dt)` on its `Game`, and adding one entry to `src/couch/catalog.ts` (goal line, control rows, face-off kind, demo level, the level of each stop) and one tier slot in `src/couch/party.ts`. The how-to screen, name card, offers, NEW mark and unlock then follow. `autoplay` is a bot that returns the input a capable player would press; it drives the how-to demo and is the browser check that the controls can complete a round (`BROWSER_SUITE=couchgames`, which waits on the game's public `finished` flag). A face-off game reports `score` (or `scores` for a shared board) through `ctx.finish`; fairness needs boards drawn from `ctx.rng`, never fixed per level. **The couch screen takes no pointer input for the game** (`CouchScene` sets the stage's `eventMode` to `none`), so any on-screen button a touch game draws is dead there and looks like a promise: hide it when `ctx.couch` is set and give its job to `control()` or, for a start-over, to `Game.restart()` and the pause menu. (Penguin Slide's round arrow was the first case.)

**Adding a challenge course.** A challenge is a fixed run of parts in one couch game, scored by how few tries it takes. Put the parts in a frozen data file beside the game (`src/games/<game>/course.ts`, with a `version` that must change if a part does, and the fewest tries worked out by the game's own solver where there is one). Make the game play it when `ctx.couch.course.id` names it: start at `resume.board` with `resume.attempts` already spent, count every try (a slide you undo and a launch that misses still count), call `progress({ board, boards, done, attempts, par, minimum, assisted })` after every try, finished part and shown hint, and call `ctx.finish` once at the end. Then add an entry to `COURSE_IDS` and `build` in `src/couch/courses.ts` (the game, level, names, the fewest tries per part, the text for the page and the badges). The runs, per-player records, badges, versioning and save repair in `src/couch/course.ts` are generic, so they need no change; the Challenges menu, the course page and the result pick the course up. Add the data's own tests (every part valid, the minimum right, the way a player could get stuck) and extend `BROWSER_SUITE=couchcourse`. A course appears on the puzzle shelf with no further change. Set `routes: true` in its `courses.ts` entry only if its game can play the best way through one part by itself under `Demo`'s `part` option (the game's `autoplay` must follow the solver from the part's start, and the part must have a worked-out best way); the Watch the best routes button then appears.
