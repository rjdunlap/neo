# Puddle Island: design and architecture

How the app works today: its principles, architecture, shared systems and contracts. What each game does is in [GAMES.md](GAMES.md); plans and proposals are in the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md) and the [idea notebook](IDEAS.md); checks performed are in [VERIFICATION.md](VERIFICATION.md). Contributor rules are in [AGENTS.md](../AGENTS.md).

## Vision

Puddle Island is a home-made iPad learning game for the developer's daughter, who turns one in October 2026. Its activities grow from lap play through early school (ages 6–8), and the longer-term direction reaches elementary school through roughly grade 5 / age eleven. The inspirations are the discovery and playful learning of JumpStart, the companion and world of Neopets, and the variety of small arcade and Kongregate games. The island is a world of themed lands full of small games, each played at the person's own levels, with a pet, its treehouse, a short story, and a separate couch route for grown-ups.

The developer is not an artist. All game artwork is generated in code with PixiJS Graphics, and sounds and music are synthesized with Web Audio. The visual direction should be achievable with the shared shape, animal, scenery, and particle builders, without sprites or an external asset pipeline.

## Play principles

- Large touch targets (at least 100 logical units), immediate touch-down feedback, and forgiving dragging.
- Spoken instructions; reading is never required to play. Tapping the pet repeats the instruction.
- Gentle feedback for mistakes, hints, and a glow after two misses. No failure screens.
- Predictable rewards: one sticker for each completed round.
- No ads, purchases, streaks, or timers that end a round. There is no screen-time timer: play limits belong to the device (Screen Time app limits, Guided Access).
- Early play supports a grown-up and child playing together. Games can include co-play prompts and related off-screen activities.
- Difficulty changes quietly within a chosen age band. Grown-ups can pin a level.

| Navigation | The "Who's playing?" chooser (the front door), hatching, an island map of eleven themed lands, the person's home spot and the Windy Picnic, land scenes, the home spot's path of every game for their age, game host, the picnic scene and its journal, sticker book, the pet's treehouse room, and an Esc pause sheet |

| Progression | Per-game level ladders, automatic adjustment within age bands, grown-up level pins |
| Pet | Four-tap hatching, eight colors, spoken name choices and grown-up name entry; customized guide shared across scenes; a treehouse room with saved creations and a discovery journal |
| Rewards | Five illustrated pages with free sticker placement and a paged tray; positions saved as page fractions |
| Saves | Version 2 local IndexedDB record with migration from version 1: profile, settings, levels and history, stickers and their placement, pet and trail state, the Windy Picnic, favorites, and the treehouse (room, creations, journal); backup and restore |
| Couch play | A separate controller route (`CouchScene`) with its own localStorage save: six-stop trips in Together or Face-off mode, a how-to-play screen with a bot demo (reachable again at any time), games opened by finished trips, a controller-ready catalog, a finale and keepsake, and versioned challenge courses (current counts in the README and roadmap); see [Couch play](#couch-play) |
| Grown-up controls | Child name, pet name and color, age band (her home spot on the map and where her levels start), the home spot's layout, sound settings, play history, and level settings |
| Audio | Synthesized sound effects and music, device speech driven by line IDs |

The current age bands in `src/progress/bands.ts` are lap (under 2), toddler (2), preschool (3), pre-K (4 and 5), early school (`school`, 6–12; much of its current content is designed for 6–8) and grown-up (`grownup`, 13 and up). A person's birth month and year set their band by these boundaries (`bandForYears` in `src/progress/profiles.ts`); with no birth the saved band is kept. These are application groupings, not developmental assessments.

Each game supplies level ranges for the bands it supports. Two consecutive smooth rounds at the current level (at most one miss and no hints) move up; two struggling rounds (at least four misses or at least two hints per round) move down. Both changes stay within the selected band's range. Modes can change the task itself, rather than merely adding more objects.

## Architecture

### Application and scenes

**Two pages (2026-10-19).** The site root (`index.html`, `src/rework.ts`) boots the creature-world rework: the "Who's playing?" chooser (`go.start()`), then Home, with a plain HTML link to the island ("Island games", top-right, shown only on the root's entry scene); `classic/index.html` boots the island game (`src/main.ts`) described below. Both share one origin, so saves, profiles and the one root service worker (`sw.js`, scope the site root, registered by `classic/` with an explicit `../sw.js`) are shared. `classic/` links its own manifest (`public/classic/manifest.webmanifest`) so a home-screen copy made there opens the island. The harnesses add `classic/` to a bare `GAME_URL`. The root's `src/rework.ts` loads the illustrated creature art and overrides two routes: `land` shows `LocationScene` for an ID in `LOCATIONS` (today `rainbow-meadow`, `counting-cove`, `puzzle-peaks`, `bubble-beach`, `music-mountain`, `treehouse` (Painted Canyon), `cozy-village`, `story-grove`, `tinker-lab`, `barnyard` and `lantern-lake`, all listed on Home's Explore picker (`ExploreScene`, `exploreIds()`); `src/app/scenes/LocationScene.ts`, a `LandScene` given an illustrated resident, a scenery drawer and a Home action through its optional `LandOptions`, including a `backdrop` and `headingColor` override: Story Grove's night sky and Tinker Lab's cave are used only by the root location), else the classic land, `hub` is `HomeScene` (the companion on a day backdrop; Explore opens the picker of built locations; lap and toddler get the ball on Home, preschool and up "Play with {pet}") and `room` is `PlayScene` (creature play: the ball, the kept picture and tune, the basket to Friends; Back and Home both return Home). `App.creatureWorld` is true at the root only and switches the root-only parts of Friends and Dress (a Home button beside Back, an arrow for Back). The RW02b creature preview is reached at `?preview` (`?cast` stays dev-only). In DEV the root also loads the testkit. The Home backdrop is a `.webp` (`src/art/home/`), so the precache globs in `vite.config.ts` include `webp`; a game launched from a location uses the land `origin`, so Home returns to it. `classic/` keeps the `LandScene` defaults. RW08 retires `classic/` when the revamp ([RW13](rework/WORK-ITEMS.md#rw13-island-revamp-ecosystems-as-locations)) replaces it.

`src/app/App.ts` owns the Pixi application, scene transitions, logical view, and frame loop. `Scene.ts` provides content, particle, and UI layers plus tracked updates. `routes.ts` creates scenes, while `pause.ts` decides what Esc does (a scene that can pause holds still behind the pause sheet; the grown-ups' page, the chooser, hatching and couch play are left alone). The 1024×768 design area fits the display; layouts respond to the available logical width and height.

On a transition, `App` removes the old scene's root from view at once. `Scene.destroy()` clears its tweens and tracked updates immediately, but releases the detached Pixi display tree one task later (`setTimeout(…, 0)`). A promise that resolved in the last update may already have queued its `await` continuation; JavaScript still runs that continuation once after teardown, and synchronous Pixi destruction used to leave it writing through a null transform (seen in Frog Hop and Peekaboo Barn). The one-task grace lets it finish without showing or updating the old scene. Later scene-owned waits never resolve, and game-owned timers and global callbacks still need their own cleanup or guards.

**Phones.** The page is `viewport-fit=cover`, so the canvas fills the whole screen, under a notch, rounded corners and the home indicator. `App.applyView` reads those cutouts from the padding of a hidden `#safe-area` element (`--safe-top/right/bottom/left`, which default to `env(safe-area-inset-*)` in `src/style.css`), clamps them (`clampInsets`) and computes the view inside them (`computeView(w, h, insets)`); the root is placed at `(insets.left, insets.top)` and a cream frame (`App.frame`, absent when there are no cutouts) covers the strips the cutouts leave, so scenery drawn past the island's edge never shows in them and taps there go nowhere. A scene therefore sees only the usable rectangle: nothing needs its own notch logic, and the canvas stays at `(0, 0)` of the window, so client coordinates, `gaze` and the test kit agree with canvas coordinates (the parent panel and the hatch name field use the same `--safe-*` properties in CSS). A `ResizeObserver` on the probe re-runs the layout when iOS settles the values after a turn. A browser check or a person fakes a notch by setting the properties on `<html>`, which headless Chrome cannot report. A phone held upright (`needsTurn`: a touch screen taller than 1.6 times its width, so every iPad and tablet shape is left alone) gets `TurnPrompt` (`src/ui/TurnPrompt.ts`, drawn in CSS pixels over the root and the curtain, a phone turning clockwise to show a wide picture), and `App` stops updating the scene and the session clock, puts the audio to sleep, stops speech and hides the plain-HTML buttons above the canvas (`html.turning` in `src/style.css`: couch play's entry and the hatch name field) until the phone is turned; the view is still laid out for the scene underneath, so turning back needs no rebuild. A scene that sets `upright` (the DOM-based couch) is never covered. Phone targets are about half the iPad's size (a 100-unit target is about 50 points at 844 × 390): the layouts are not enlarged for phones, and a phone check cannot stand in for small hands on the iPad.

The game shell in `GameScene.ts` owns navigation, the guide pet, instructions, co-play prompts, saving, difficulty progression, stickers, and celebrations. This lets a new minigame concentrate on its own activity.

**Direct next game (D1b).** Ordinary completed lap/toddler touch rounds of Bubble Pop, Sing-Along Barn and Fill and Dump offer a pictured Next game and Another after the celebration buttons arrive. `src/content/discovery.ts` filters that fixed pool, excludes the finished game and last suggestion when alternatives exist, then prefers unplayed games and oldest completed history with stable ties. `src/app/discovery.ts` remembers only the last displayed suggestion for the active visit; save identity and a profile-change epoch invalidate old callbacks on switching, reset, restore or undo. Nothing is persisted for discovery. The tile stays fixed through resize, Heart, Keep and overlays. Keys/pads hide it; returning touch releases before it reappears. `DiscoveryTile` gives touch-down feedback and acts on a matching release inside, handles native cancellation/lost capture, and navigation latches once. Launch uses ordinary `go.game(id, currentBand, undefined, false, originalOrigin)` with no level override: the target's current pin/ordered range and full intro apply, and every hop retains the original land/place/journal return context. Invalid pictures recover without launching another target. Again, Next level and ordinary rewards remain their own paths; Another retains pending Keep and deliberate navigation leaves it unsaved. Every celebration element reflows with the usable view. Offer/replacement speech waits for ordinary result or Keep speech and drops obsolete announcements. **Little surprises mix (D2b).** On the lap/toddler home spot (place band = profile band, in both the `PlaceScene` path layout and the `SubjectPlaceScene` first page) a mix entry opens a preview sheet of three stops, the frozen pool Bubble Pop, Sing-Along Barn and Fill and Dump, offered only when all three are touch-playable at that band. `src/content/mix.ts` holds the pool, eligibility and a pure reducer `step()` (launch/finish/skip/unavailable; an item leaves `upcoming` once, a stale round or second finish is ignored). `MixMemory` in `src/app/discovery.ts` is in memory only and bound to the `store.data` object, so a switch, reset, restore or undo ends it; the next `GameScene` claims a round token. `Scene.holdsMix()` (default false) lets `App.show` end the mix when any scene other than its round or origin home spot enters. D1b's offer is suppressed in a mix; the result shows Next stop and Skip (or All done) in D1b's rectangles, touch-only, and the pause sheet adds "Skip this game". Again and Next level relaunch the same stop; Home returns to the home spot, which reopens the preview. All done goes to the home spot, where the ending sheet opens. Skipped or unavailable stops add no stats. Other mixes, saved preferences and controller discovery remain proposals in [DISCOVERY.md](DISCOVERY.md).

The shell has a grown-up **how-to card** for every touch game, from `src/content/howto.ts`: the game's goal and, for the level on the card, the gestures, how the round ends and any note about mistakes or experiments, plus that level's `describeLevel` line. Steps, finish and note are plain strings (true at every level) or `LevelLine`s (`{ from, to, text }`, inclusive) that `howToFor(mod, level)` resolves, so a card never describes another level; a unit test bans level-relative wording ("later levels") and checks every game at every level has one to four steps. It comes two ways. **Every time a game is opened**, `GameScene` shows it as an intro before the round: a full-screen card with the game's picture and name (the name is spoken), a big green Play and a Back (the house). No `Game` has been built yet, so nothing runs, counts or speaks behind it; Play builds the game and starts it, and Back leaves. Stray taps on the card do nothing. On the intro, a pair of held arrows (`HoldButton`, 0.35 s, so a small hand's tap does nothing) beside "this level" steps `GameScene.level` within `mod.levels(band)`, the same levels the grown-up zone's picker offers: `HowToPanel.show` swaps in that level's card and `GameScene.demoAt` its demonstration (the controller stopgap demonstration keeps its own level), and still no round is built. Nothing is saved by looking or leaving; Play builds the round at the chosen level, and finishing it moves the saved level there through `recordRound`, as any round does (a grown-up's pin is not moved, so with a pin the choice holds for that round only). The arrows are left off when the band has one level and on the "?" card in a round. A game may set the optional `GameModule.levelCard(level)` (a `LevelCard`: `name`, a spoken `line` and a `picture()`; only Bubble Pop does, `MODE_CARDS`/`modeCardFor` in its `logic.ts`, drawn by `mode-art.ts`); `howToFor` then adds `HowToCard.mode` (and omits the key otherwise), and `HowToPanel` labels the level `THIS LEVEL · <NAME> · level <n>`, shows a 112-unit picture tile beside the arrows (a tap speaks the line, nothing else), and, on the intro only, a decorative ladder (`eventMode` none) of `levelsInOrder` for the played band, easiest first: the current level outlined, a green dot on the level her progress suggested when the card opened, a red pin on a pinned level (neither follows the arrows). Beside a demonstration the ladder sits under the window; if the card cannot fit at the smallest text scale the ladder is dropped first. The "?" card shows name and tile only. The intro is skipped when a story request opens the game, when she taps "again" straight after a round (`go.game`'s fourth argument), when the game has no card, and when a grown-up has turned **Show how to play before each game** off (`settings.howToCards`, default on, in the grown-up zone). **A game with a bot also shows a demonstration** in a window, by what the person plays with: `islandDemo` (`src/content/demos.ts`) gives a game with `GameModule.touchDemo` a **ghost finger** (`input: 'finger'` on a touch screen, `'mouse'` on a desktop; `pointerKind()` in `src/engine/input.ts` follows the last real pointer, so a touch laptop follows whichever was used last, and starts from `(pointer: coarse)`) at *her* current level, so the window matches the card's "this level" line; a couch game with no touch bot would keep the couch's controller demonstration at the couch's demo level (`demoFor`, `src/couch/catalog.ts`; a stopgap, because a controller highlight is wrong for touch; none now, all fifteen couch games have a touch bot); every other game is text alone (every touch-playable island game now has a ghost finger; controller-only modules retain their controller demonstration). `GameScene` makes a `Demo` (`src/couch/demo.ts`; the couch's how-to screen uses the same class) that `HowToPanel` places in a window (beside the text when the screen is wider than tall, above it otherwise, a little smaller as the text steps down to fit), updates each frame while the card is open (the round is held still, the demonstration is not), sets to `eventMode: 'none'` so no touch reaches the copy of the game inside it, and destroys with the card. When a demonstration ends, `Demo` follows the scene rule above: it takes its objects out of sight at once but destroys them a task later; do not "simplify" that back to a synchronous destroy. While a demonstration plays, `audio.hush` lowers effects to 40% behind the spoken title (`closeHelp` gives them back); the card's note under the window says what is playing (a finger, a mouse pointer, or a controller moving the highlight). During a round, a quiet `?` under the home button opens the same card by holding it (a long hold, so a small hand does not open it) without Play, Back or a demonstration; it holds the round still while open. Neither way is a hint or changes progress. `HowToPanel` draws both and steps its text down to fit a short screen; the `howto` browser suite checks every game's card in landscape and portrait. The couch has its own how-to screens in `src/couch/catalog.ts`.

**Keys and a pad on the island.** `GameScene` owns a `CouchInput` in island mode (`island` option: no Esc, no W/S; it ignores keys while `App.paused` or the grown-ups' page is open) sampled every frame, and `InputInUse` (`src/app/input-in-use.ts`) says which input is in use: touch until a real key or button edge, touch again on a pointerdown. The intro card takes Enter (Play), Backspace or B (Back) and left/right (the level). A round calls `game.control()` only after a real press inside the round (the Enter that pressed Play does not count, since several games turn their ring on at the first `control()` call) and only where `controllerPlays(mod, level)` allows: the levels couch play has played (`controllerLevels(id)`: the couch trip stops plus `demoLevel` from `COUCH_INFO`); other levels stay touch until audited. `GameModule.liveController` marks the twenty-three games that switch to a controller at run time; a game that reads `ctx.couch` at build time to choose touch or controller is not covered (none now; couch play keeps that path). A game that re-lays itself out for the controller at its first `control()` (tile-pairs, gem-swap, lantern-lights, picture-logic, island-bridges, pond-conga, sudoku-garden, word-search, and from C1b group 3 bouncy-launch, whose spring squashes by power and springs back up, ferry-jam, which hides its undo button, shows its focus ring and puts a key-held boat back, and penguin-slide, which hides its undo button) implements the optional `Game.touchReturned()`: `GameScene` calls it at the first pointerdown after keys (not once the round is finished) and the game puts its touch layout back, dropping any drag; the touch that triggered it may be lost. The celebration shows a ring over again, next, home, heart and keep, moved with `nextSpot`; the first press only shows it, input is ignored for 0.35 s after the buttons appear, and Enter presses a button through `RoundButton.press()`. Esc has one owner, `App.escape()`: the Esc key and Start on a pad (`PadStart`/`padStart` in `src/app/pause.ts`, polled in `App.tick` even while paused, so a pad can pause and resume) both go through it. **The map and the lands** (`src/app/screen-keys.ts`) use `ScreenKeys`, an island-mode `CouchInput` plus a white/ink ring over the spots, moved by `nextSpot` through the pure `SpotFocus` (focus is kept by spot id, so it survives the signpost turning). `App.input` is an `InputInUse` shared by the plain island screens (touch again on a window pointerdown; `GameScene` keeps its own). Input is ignored for `ENTRY_GUARD` (0.35 s) after a scene appears, and a fresh sampler takes its first frame as its baseline, so a held Enter does not carry into the next scene; the first key or button after a finger only shows the ring, then Enter or the bottom button presses the focused spot and arrows move it. The map rings the lands, home, picnic and the players, book and treehouse buttons (not the gear), starts on the pet's spot and takes no Back (an accident would throw her to the chooser); a land rings the games showing, host, signpost, home and book, and Backspace or B returns to the map. The land's `destroy()` frees the keys. The pause sheet's buttons show a visible `:focus-visible` outline only (`src/style.css`). The chooser's Enter still opens couch play, and the home spots, picnic, stickers, treehouse, journal and hatching have no ring. **The pause sheet** lists a round's own entries under "Keep playing" through `Scene.pauseActions()` (`GameScene` returns the game's `Game.restart` and `Game.askForHint`, labelled by `pauseLabels()` in `src/app/pause.ts`: the couch's `COUCH_INFO` labels where the couch plays the game, plain "Start again"/"Show a hint" for a grown-ups-only game, none for a child's game the couch has not labelled unless the level explicitly supplies `Game.hintLabel` (Planet Hop 12, whose requested help counts for adaptation); none before the round is built, over the how-to card or once finished); each resumes first, then runs. A pad uses the sheet through `PadSheet` (made fresh per sheet, ignores buttons held as it opens): d-pad or left stick moves focus over the buttons, wrapping, and A presses the focused one on release so the round never sees it; the held gear is left out. A demonstration for the input in use is not built yet ([Lands stage C](ROADMAP.md#lands-navigation-and-discovery)).

**Ghost finger.** A game opts in with an optional `Game.autotouch(dt): TouchIntent | null` and `touchDemo: true` on its module (`src/games/types.ts`). The intent names what a capable child would do next, as spots on display objects (`Spot { on, x?, y? }`, in that object's own coordinates, followed if it moves): `{ tap }`, `{ drag, to, lift? }` (a held thing rides 40 units above the finger, as `draggable` does; `to` is where the *object* should land) or `{ trace, via[] }` (a finger kept down, for steering and tracing). Return null while there is nothing to do yet. Three optional fields on any intent: `pause` (seconds to rest after the gesture, 0.55 by default, shorter when the next touch belongs with this one and longer to make a rhythm's long gap) and `receiver` (send the events to this object instead of the first spot's, for a layer that takes touches anywhere, such as Duckling Parade's steering ground, while the finger points at a duckling that moves) and `when` (the hand travels to the first spot and hovers there, pointing, until `when()` returns true and then presses; it returns `'cancel'`, or six seconds pass, to give the touch up; for a touch that has to land at a moment, such as Bumper Garden's flipper meeting the ladybug). `GhostFinger` (`src/engine/ghost.ts`) owns everything else: it travels a drawn hand (or mouse cursor, `kind`) to the spot, presses, carries and lifts at a gentle pace, rests between gestures, and sends a real touch's events straight to the first spot's object with `emit` (`pointerdown`, `globalpointermove`, `pointerup`, `pointertap`, from pointer id `GHOST_POINTER`). Nothing is hit-tested and no window listener hears it, so it cannot reach the card's own Play and Back, and a game's own handlers (`onTap`, `draggable`, a steering layer's listeners) receive it as they would a finger; a game that listens on `window` (Splish Splash, Rainbow Fingers) needs more thought before it can have one; Bouncy Launch was given a `pointerup` listener on its pet beside the window ones (a real release reaches both, and the second finds the pull already gone), which is the cheapest way when the object is the one touched. The target may vanish under the finger (a bubble pops at once): the finger lifts where it is. `draggable` frees its one-at-a-time lock when a drag ends, because a ghost's drag never reaches the window `pointerup` that used to free it (without that, a real drag after Play was ignored; tested in `ghost.test.ts` and the `howto` suite). `touchDemo` belongs to the whole module, so the bot has to play every level the game has (a game whose top level is a different class needs a bot for both). Keep the choice of target in the game's `logic.ts`, where a test can check that it always picks a right target and finishes a round; `BROWSER_SUITE=fingerdemo` proves the bot finishes at the game's first and last level (`FINGER_LEVELS=3,4` plays those instead, to time the ones between) inside the 75 seconds a finger demonstration gets before it replays (a controller demonstration gets 40), and plays without a wrong move or a hint, because a demonstration shows right play (a bot that sweeps its finger past a wrong choice, as Duckling Parade's first one did, fails it). The seed is fixed, so the same round plays each time. Pacing: the hand's taps are never closer than about a second (`MIN_TAP_GAP`), so a rhythm game plays long and short gaps by resting longer or shorter (the game only needs a long gap to be clearly longer than a short one); a game that needs a touch at a moment uses `when`. A bot may look at the draw its game is about to make with `ctx.rng.clone()` (`Rng.clone()` is a copy that will draw the same numbers without using the game's up): Peg Garden's pearl drift and Bumper Garden's kick are random, and a demonstration shows right play, which a child cannot see ahead; the game's own stream is untouched. Bumper Garden moves its ladybug and flippers in fixed 1/120-second steps (`STEP`), so its look ahead (`planFlip`, run on the same `swingFlippers` and `kickBall` the game uses) agrees with it on any display; the plan names the step, and while the hand hovers over the button the game holds the ladybug at that step for the frame the press takes (`due` in its `index.ts`; real play never sets one).

### Minigame contract

Each `src/games/<id>/index.ts` exports a `GameModule`, registered in `src/games/registry.ts`. Metadata includes its subject (the `region` field, which groups it inside a place), supported bands, level ranges and descriptions, spoken title, music, hub icon, and sticker drawing. `create(ctx)` returns a game with `start`, `update`, `resize`, and `destroy` methods. A game where she makes the music (Jelly Drums, Song Maker, Sound Garden, Rhythm Neighbors, Beat Builder, Clap the Syllables, Pet Says) gives `music` a near-silent style (`silenced(STYLES.x)` in `src/audio/music.ts`, or `STYLES.quiet`) so the island's loop never competes; it keeps a style playing, never `music.stop()`, because a playhead or bobbing jelly reads `music.beats()`, `beat()` and `bpm()` from it, and `src/games/registry.test.ts` names the games. The how-to card opens before the round is built, so `GameScene.openIntro` plays a silenced game's style at once rather than leaving the place's loop under the card's demonstration. The treehouse tune plaque calls `music.duck(seconds)`, which dips the room's loop on the audio clock while her kept song plays and needs no cleanup if she leaves first.

A game lists only the bands it supports. Its level table is a `BandLevels` (`src/progress/difficulty.ts`), read through `rangeFor(table, band)`; the shell only ever asks for a band the game lists, so adding a band never requires touching games that skip it. A range may add `order`, the band's level IDs easiest first (distinct, with `min` and `max` its lowest and highest ID, checked by the registry test), when an easier level was appended above hard ones or the band skips IDs that belong to another band (pre-K 3–9 then 13): `stepLevel`, `nextLevel`, `nextLevelOffer`, the how-to arrows and the grown-up picker follow it, a saved level or pin left out lands on the nearest listed ID, while saved levels, pins and history keep their IDs and the picker's numbers are the IDs. `store.levelFor` starts a game she has never finished (no plays, no history, saved level still the default 1) at the first level of the band's order, so an entry placed before level 1 is where a new player begins; a saved level or pin is never moved. An entry level has a high ID but is easy, so `GameModule.entryLevels` names it (Bubble Pop `[15]`, Pet Says `[13]`) and `landing` starts a band that does not list it (a birthday into toddler, a pin) at that band's own easiest level instead of clamping it to the hardest; a registry test fails when a band places an ID first without declaring it. Day by Day declares bridge `[10]` within school order `[2, 4, 10, 3]`; an entry may sit before a harder level without being the band’s first. The registry checks every declared entry belongs to a supported band. Bubble Pop 15 *Pop anywhere* (lap `[15, 1, 2, 3]`) and Pet Says 13 *Pat the pet* (lap `[13, 1, 2]`) are the first users; players who already played these games keep their saved level and meet the entries only if a grown-up pins them.

A game draws into `ctx.stage`, uses the shared input, drag, tween, particle, and voice helpers, and reports completion through `ctx.finish({ misses, hints })`. Spoken lines belong in `src/content/voice-script.ts`. Shared art and audio should be reused so new activities feel part of the same world.

Optional parts of the contract, used by a few games and ignored by the rest: `RoundResult.creation` hands the shell something she made to keep in the treehouse; `RoundResult.discoveries` files journal entries; `RoundResult.score` / `scores` carry a couch face-off result; `GameContext.couch` asks a game to alternate two players or play a challenge course; `Game.control()` and `Game.autoplay()` take controller input and drive a bot; `Game.restart()` lets the couch pause menu start the round's current part over. Touch play never depends on any of them.

### Art, input, and sound

- `src/art/`: palette, parametric critters, props, shapes, scenery, particles, and sticker framing.
- `src/engine/`: view scaling, random generation, tweens, touch filtering, dragging, and `ball.ts`, a small deterministic ball, peg and wall simulation (with an optional ceiling and springy "kick" pegs) whose `simulate()` previews a shot. A tween whose target is destroyed mid-flight ends instead of freezing the scene.
- `src/audio/`: audio unlock and buses, instruments, sound effects, generative music, and speech.

Use the shared palette and pentatonic note steps. Route taps through `onTap` and drags through `drag.ts` for consistent cooldown, palm rejection, and finger offset behavior.

### Persistence and grown-up UI

`src/progress/save.ts` defines the save record and `migrate()`, which repairs incomplete or invalid input. `store.ts` persists it through `idb-keyval`: most changes are batched for 300 ms and flushed when the page is hidden, but the shell writes a finished round and its sticker immediately, because a write that only starts as the page closes can be lost. New fields need defaults, migration behavior, and tests so existing progress survives updates.

The current save contains profile, settings, game statistics and recent round history, and earned sticker records. It also stores `creatures` (the friends she owns and which one is with her; see below), the highest band celebrated on the map, optional normalized sticker placements, and `stories.picnic`: the Windy Picnic's done steps (a bounded list of three known IDs, kept in story order), whether this telling's ending was shown, and whether its keepsake was earned. The Woodland Picnic keeps the same shape under `stories.woodland`. (An earlier list of opened regions is dropped on load.) Invalid or unknown placement pages become unplaced stickers; unknown story steps are dropped, an ending needs every step, and an ending always keeps the keepsake. Older saves keep their progress and start the picnic untold (see below for the pet). It also stores `favorites`, the games she has hearted (oldest first, at most twelve, short unique strings; one that no longer exists simply never shows). It also stores `settings.howToCards`, the grown-up's switch for the how-to cards (on unless a save says otherwise). An earlier version also kept a list of games already explained; it is gone, and a restored save that has it is read without it. Resetting progress in the grown-up zone also resets the story and the hearts.

**Creatures (save version 3, RW05 slice 1).** `SaveData` is version 3: the single `pet` field is gone, replaced by `creatures: { owned: Individual[], companion: string | null }`. An `Individual` is `{ id, species ('sprout' | 'river' | 'glider'), name (1-40), look: { color, worn }, metAt (at most 60 chars) }`; at most `MAX_CREATURES` (24). `cleanCreatures()` repairs on load: unknown species, malformed ids (`[a-z0-9]{4,32}`) and duplicate ids are dropped (the first kept), a missing name or colour falls back to the species' first name or palette colour, the earliest 24 stay, and the companion points at an owned id, else the first, else null. An older (version 2) save keeps its progress and stickers but owns no creature, so that profile meets the egg again; the old `pet` is not read (the development-save exception). `store.meet(species, name, color, metAt)` is idempotent on `metAt`, refuses beyond 24, writes at once and makes the first friend the companion; `store.choose(id)` works only for owned ids and writes at once; `store.companion` is a getter. `store.reset()` keeps profile, settings and creatures; `undo` swaps the whole save; `BACKUP_VERSION` is unchanged. `cardName` falls back to the companion's name, else Pip, and `applySettings()` sets `voice.pet` to the companion's name. The reseed path is the dev-only "Meet everyone (development)" button on the grown-ups' page (`import.meta.env.DEV`), which seeds the river and the glider (`metAt` `dev:<species>`); that page's name and colour fields edit the companion and are disabled until there is one.

**The companion seam.** `src/creatures/species.ts` (Pixi-free) is the species table: art, palette colour, names and a pentatonic chirp (sprout: teal; river: orange; glider: purple). `src/creatures/companion.ts` wraps a species rig as `Companion` (feet at 0,0, fitted to about the old pip's size, with poke and chirp, hop, cheer, moods and a body-centred touch area); `GameContext.pet` is a `Companion`. `loadCollectionArt()` runs at boot in `src/main.ts` and caches the three illustrated sets, the portraits, the card paper, the egg and the wearable pictures; a failed load falls back to the Graphics rigs or `drawEgg`. A chosen colour that is not the species' own is a hue rotation (`recolor()`, a `ColorMatrixFilter` at the screen's resolution), since a tint cannot turn teal art pink. `makeCompanion()` returns a teal sprout stand-in before the first friend. **`CompanionRig` (RW05 slice 2, in part):** `ctx.companion()` (in `GameScene`, `CouchScene` and the couch demo) gives a game the active friend as a rig with named parts (`part(name)`, `partPoints(name)`), `bodyBounds()` (not `bounds()`, which would shadow Pixi's), `spin()`, `wiggle()`, `poke(quiet)` and `color`. The per-species parts table is `src/creatures/parts.ts` and records misfits (the glider has no neck, hands or feet, and its tummy is its cream underside); `parts.test.ts` checks it. Eight large-pet games use the rig; Splish Splash and Weather Wardrobe still draw a `Critter` from `companionSpec()` through `ctx.petSpec`, until the misfit decision lets them move. The hatch meets a sprout and calls `store.meet('sprout', name, color, 'start')`.

**Friends.** `FriendsScene` (`go.friends()`) is reached from a basket in the treehouse (bottom-left; her friend walks to it). It shows a live stage friend (a tap pokes it and repeats `friends.pick`), 150-unit cards (portrait, name, a heart "with me" mark, a ring on the previewed card), a Come with me button and Back to the treehouse; past six friends, held arrows page the cards (`src/creatures/friends-layout.ts`, tested in the lands' four views). Choosing records no sticker, stat, miss or hint. `go.friends(previewId?)` opens it with that friend on the stage.

**Wearing and dressing (RW06 slice 1).** `look.worn` is a list of wearable ids (`src/art/creatures/wear-slots.ts`, Pixi-free: `WEARABLES`, `SLOT`, `MAX_WORN` 7). `cleanWorn()` repairs on load (unknown ids dropped, duplicates keep the first, two items in one slot keep the later, cut at seven; an item a species does not fit is kept); `meetIn` creates `worn: []`; there is no version bump. `store.wear(id, item, on)` writes at once and returns false for an unowned id; reset keeps looks and a restored backup keeps `worn`. `Companion` takes `look.worn`, exposes `worn`, `dress(worn)` and `wear(item, on)`; the Graphics fallback rig draws nothing. `wearableBy(species)` gives the fitting items (sprout 7, river 6, glider 4). **The corner guide leaves off the `held` slot** (`GUIDE_LEAVES_OFF` in `makeGuide()`), because the sprout's umbrella would pass the bottom-left clearance; the item stays saved and shows on the result star, in the treehouse, on Friends and in Dress. `DressScene` (`go.dress(id)`, from Friends' Dress button; layout in `src/creatures/dress-layout.ts`) puts an item on or off with a tap and has a take-everything-off button; Back returns to Friends with that friend previewed. Friends' cards and the chooser's portraits are still undressed pictures, and the two large-pet games still on the stand-in (Splish Splash, Weather Wardrobe) do not show the look yet.

The save also holds the treehouse: `room` (six furnishings and an optional framed sticker), `creations` (a picture-board slot and a tune slot, each with what is on show and one earlier piece) and `journal` (found entries and how many she has seen). Each is bounded and repaired on load; see [Pet treehouse](#pet-treehouse). Couch play keeps its own separate save ([Couch play](#couch-play)).

**Profiles (storage only).** `store` is still the singleton every scene reads, but it is now the *active profile's* save. Who has a save is a profile index at `neo.profiles` (`src/progress/profiles.ts`, pure and tested): `{ version: 1, active, profiles: [{ id, legacy, birth, startBand, addedAt }] }`, at most eight people, never empty (a missing index becomes one `legacy` profile, so a device from before profiles plays as it did). The legacy profile's save is still `neo.save`, so an older cached build reads it unchanged; every other save is `neo.save.<id>`, and each profile has its own `.previous` undo snapshot. A birth month and year, and a grown-up's chosen *start here instead* band, belong to the index entry and never to `SaveData`, because `migrate()` rebuilds from a whitelist and an older build re-saving `neo.save` would strip them. Once a profile has either, `Store.syncBand()` writes the effective band (`effectiveBand`: the chosen start, else the age through `bandForYears`, else the band the save has) into `profile.band`, so the places, games and picnic that read it need no change; there is no `grownup` band yet, so everyone from 6 up starts at `school`. A birth is checked by shape when loaded and by the clock only when typed (`checkBirthInput`), so a device with a wrong clock cannot lose one. `switchTo(id)` reads the other save first, then in one step flushes the pending write under the old key and swaps; an index that could not be read is never written over. A backup of every profile is `{ kind: 'puddle-island-backup', version: 3, … }` (`exportAll`): `restoreAll` replaces a profile with the same id (keeping its own undo snapshot), adds an unknown one if there is room, and never deletes anyone, and `parseBackup` refuses anything that is not a save or one of these (a couch file, `{}`) instead of restoring a blank. The older `exportJson` / `importJson` still act on the active profile alone. **The chooser (slice 1b).** `go.start()` is `ChooserScene`, the front door: plain HTML over the backdrop (`upright`, so an upright phone is not asked to turn there), a card for each person from `store.peek` (their friend drawn once into a canvas from `petSpecFor(pet)`, an egg until it hatches; the name is the person's, else their friend's) and Add a player. A profile with nothing in it (`isBlankProfile`: no name, no friend, no birth, no round) is hidden, and Add fills it in rather than making a second, so the first person stays at `neo.save`; with eight people and no blank one the Add card is replaced by a note. **The input that picks a card picks the mode** (`src/app/chooser.ts`, pure and tested): a tap or click (`sourceOfClick`: a real `pointerdown` in the 1.5 s before the click, with the click's `detail` only as a fallback, since nothing shows that every touch browser sets it) opens the island, and Enter, a controller button, a click with no pointer behind it and the small controller button on each card open couch play with that person as Player 1 (`couchNameFor`, at most 14 letters; with no name the couch keeps its own). Keys and controllers go through a `CouchInput` that exists only while nothing else wants Enter and the arrows (it is stopped while the add form or the grown-ups' page is open), only a focused card or Add acts on them, and a 0.35 s guard ignores a new screen's first input; `nextSpot` moves the ring, which shows only once a key or a controller has been used (`chooser--keys`), since the first card is focused from the start so Enter has somewhere to act and a finger must not see a ring. `selectPlayer` (`src/app/players.ts`) is the one place a player is switched: it awaits `store.switchTo`, calls `playerChanged` (sound and names through `applySettings`, and `forgetPlaces`, `forgetSubjects` and `forgetJournalPage`, which clear the scroll positions and journal page the scenes keep per band) and the caller builds a fresh scene. Sound is unlocked synchronously in the tap, before anything is awaited. Add a player asks a name and a birth (`readNewPlayer`, held to the clock), then hatches; from 13 (`skipsEgg`) the egg is skipped. The map's top-left button is `go.start()`. The grown-ups' page (`openParentPanel(onClose, onGone)`) edits whoever is chosen at the top by switching the active profile, writes a birth through `store.setBirth` and a chosen band through `setStartBand` (choosing the age's own band clears it), removes a player, backs up and restores everyone, and on close puts back whoever was playing when it opened, or calls `onGone` if they were removed. Sound is still saved per player, so it is labelled so; a device-level setting and the comfort questions wait for settings that exist to bind them to.

Grown-up controls are plain HTML in `src/parent/panel.ts`. They are reached by the **gear** (`src/ui/grownups.ts`): plain HTML so a mouse, a keyboard (hold Enter or Space) and a finger all work, held for two seconds (`GEAR_HOLD_SECONDS`) while a ring fills. A shorter press shows a hint and, at most every six seconds, speaks `parent.ask`. It sits top-right on the chooser and the map, and inline on the pause sheet (`src/ui/pause-sheet.ts`) that `App` opens on Esc, which is the only way in once a game is running on a keyboard. The page closes to the island map, so a changed pet or band shows at once. It is a speed bump for a small hand, not a lock: nothing behind it can do lasting harm, because a reset or restore keeps one undo snapshot (`neo.save.previous`) and there is no timer to turn off. A reset or restore asks `store.undo()`; undoing twice swaps back.

### Delivery and verification

Vite builds a static site; `vite-plugin-pwa` generates the manifest and offline cache, with an explicit 3 MiB per-file precache bound so the code-drawn catalog bundle is included (the precache globs include `webp`, for the Home backdrop). Pushes to `main` deploy through GitHub Actions. Commit and push only when asked.

Unit tests cover saves, difficulty, game registry constraints, view geometry, critters, and the musical scale. The dev-only `neo` and `kit` browser helpers support scripted play-throughs. New game verification should include a completed round, wrong answers, hints, and resizing. Production offline behavior must be checked with the built app, separately from the dev server.

## The child's island

### The island: lands and a home spot

A person's profile sets their band, so the map does not choose an age; it is a world to explore (the roadmap's [Lands](ROADMAP.md#lands-navigation-and-discovery) section has the decision and the later stages). `MapScene` shows the **themed lands** (including Lantern Lake), the person's **home spot** and the Windy Picnic, laid out by `mapLayout` in `src/content/lands.ts` (a table for a wide view and one for an upright one, as fractions of the usable rectangle). The "Who's playing?" button is top-left, the gear top-right, the treehouse bottom-left and the sticker book bottom-right; every spot's touch circle (`MAP_RADIUS`, 80) stays apart from the others and from those buttons, and the gear's keep-out grows on a phone because it is a fixed size in CSS pixels (`mapButtons`). A land twinkles (`Sparkle`) while it holds a game for her that she has not played. The pet only decorates the map (`eventMode` none): it waits beside the land she was in last this session (`lastLand` in `LandScene.ts`), or beside the home spot, and hops to a spot she taps before the map zooms in.

**Lands.** `LANDS` (`src/content/lands.ts`, pure and tested) gives each land a stable `LandId`, a name and spoken line (the ten subjects' `subject.*` lines; the art subject is now called Painted Canyon (was Paint Pier), since "Treehouse" is the pet's room), a host, a backdrop style, its own tune (a variation of the hub style), and an explicit list of its games. A land's id is its own, not a game's `region`: the original subject lands hold their subject’s games, while Lantern Lake holds grown-up games from several subjects; grown-up Marble Jump and Log Roll also live in Puzzle Peaks and Tinker Lab, keeping each land within its full-size target capacity; proposed lands such as Maker Harbor may do the same. A test keeps every registered game in exactly one land. Grown-up games are in the same `GAMES` registry; `couchGameById` is an alias, and `inputs` identifies controller-only modules. `LandScene` draws the land's backdrop, its host (a critter from `CRITTERS`, Juniper the gardener in Rainbow Meadow, Hazel the postkeeper in Cozy Village; `makeHost` in `src/art/lands.ts`) and its games on one screen through `landLayout`: rows that start on the ground and below the corner buttons, the host first and the signpost last, clear of the bottom-left pet, with each game's drawing fitted into the largest box that lets everything fit (170×155 down to 140×130; the touch area, `spotRect`, is always over 100 units). A test lays out every land at every band in four views (an iPad either way up, a phone, a phone with a notch); a land that could not fit returns null and fails that test, so a new game that would crowd a land is a decision, never smaller targets. Tapping a game (touch-down, through `onTap`) plays it; tapping the host speaks `land.try` and the suggested game's title and makes that game glow (`suggest`: a game she has not finished a round of, else the one played longest ago, never the same twice in a row); tapping the pet repeats `hub.pick`. The first visit to a land in a session speaks its line.

**What a land shows.** The games whose youngest band is at or below hers (`visibleIn`, `forAgeOrYounger`), so younger favorites never disappear as she grows and a person at the top band sees every game. When a land also has games for bigger kids (`olderIn`), a **signpost** (`Signpost`, a wooden arrow with a star) turns the land to show those, and turned again (an arrow home with a heart) shows hers. Both sets are built when the scene starts and the turn only swaps which is visible; the turn is remembered per land for the session. Nothing is hidden behind a gate: a grown-up's *start here instead* still moves the whole start.

**Levels.** Every launch goes through `playBand(bands, band)` (`src/progress/bands.ts`): her own band if the game has it, else the highest band it has below hers (a game for younger children plays its most demanding levels), else the lowest above (a bigger kids' game plays its easiest). A game's level table has nothing for a band it does not list (`rangeFor` returns level 1), so the route never builds a game at such a band; `go.game` resolves the band itself rather than refusing. The picnic's `bandForGame` is the same function.

**The home spot.** One spot on the map is drawn as her age place (`placeFor(profile.band)`, from `src/content/places.ts`: Puddle Lagoon for lap through Wonder Woods for early school to Lantern Lodge for the grown-up band) with "all your games" under it. It opens `PlaceScene` (or `SubjectPlaceScene` under the grown-up's **Home spot layout** setting, `settings.placeLayout`), which lists every game for her age and younger (`gamesFor`, the same set as the lands together), grouped by subject. The path zigzags two rows along a sandy path and swipes sideways with momentum when the games do not fit, with arrow buttons to page it; a game opens when the finger lifts without having swiped, and it squishes on touch-down. Each place remembers its scroll for the session. The subject-card layout shows four illustrated subject cards per page, then up to four game cards in the chosen subject, with its own page memory per band. **Birthdays.** `world.band` is the highest band celebrated on the map; when her band rises above it the map throws a party (confetti and `map.birthday`) and the home spot turns into the new age place, once. The old switchback trail between the age places is gone.

**Coming back.** `go.game(id, band?, story?, again?, origin?, level?)` carries where a game was opened from (`Origin` in `src/app/App.ts`: a land, a place or the journal). Home, the intro's Back and the celebration's home button go back there (`GameScene.goHome`), and "again" passes it on and replays the level just finished (a story round plays its own level); the celebration's orange **next level** button (a triangle running into a bar, above home, clear of the celebrating pet) calls `go.game` with `level` set to the level just played plus one, held inside her band's range, and `again` true so no card shows (`GameScene.startLevel`; `nextLevelOffer` in `src/progress/difficulty.ts`). It is absent in a story round and at the top of her band's range. The green "again" button replays the level just finished (`go.game` with that `level`), so the two buttons mean what they look like; her saved level still steps up or down as always (`recordRound`), which the map applies the next time the game opens. Finishing that round moves her saved level there through `recordRound`, as the how-to arrows do, and a grown-up's pin stays put; a story round goes back to the picnic, and with no origin home is her home spot. The journal's arrow opens a game at `playBand` with the journal as its origin. `selectPlayer` also calls `forgetLands()`, which clears what the lands remembered (greetings, turned signposts, the last suggestion and the last land).

### New games and favorites

Two small, ungated aids to finding a game. A game is **new** while `stats.plays` is zero (`isNew` in `src/content/shelf.ts`: a finished round in any place or as a picnic step counts, reading a level does not); a `Sparkle` (a four-point gold star that twinkles and never takes a touch, tracked by the scene) sits on its landmark in a land and in `PlaceScene` (also in `PlaceScene.sparkles`, by game ID), on its card in `SubjectPlaceScene`, where a subject card twinkles while any of its games is new, and on a land on the map while any of its games for her is new. The Favorites card never twinkles. Nothing says "new" in words.

A **heart** is how a game is kept. `GameScene.celebrate` puts a pink heart button (outlined when off, solid when on, a star burst and a spoken line when it turns on) beside the new sticker, away from the celebrating pet and the again/home buttons; `store.toggleFavorite` flips it and `after.heart` exposes it. A heart changes nothing about stickers, levels or adaptation. `favorites` in the save is oldest-first and bounded to twelve (`FAVORITES_MAX`): a new heart on a full list lets the oldest go. `shelfFor` picks the home spot's shelf: the hearted games in the order they were hearted, the newest five (`SHELF_SLOTS`), any island game included, so a bigger kids' game hearted after a signpost round is there too (it plays at `playBand`). `PlaceScene` shows them on a `Shelf` (`src/ui/shelf.ts`, a translucent panel on a wooden plank with a heart set in it, each game's own landmark drawing and a 116 by 128 touch area, launching like a landmark) standing in the sky above the back row, fixed while the land swipes; with no hearts there is no shelf. `SubjectPlaceScene` leads with a **Favorites** card (a big heart) only when there is a heart, leading to up to twelve hearted games four to a page; a remembered Favorites page whose hearts are gone falls back to the subjects. Undecided: whether the shelf should also feed the map, whether a grown-up should be able to curate it, and how it feels on a device.

## Pet treehouse

`RoomScene` is one room reached from the map's treehouse button. `src/content/room.ts` holds the rules (six items from `ROOM_ITEMS`, a floor region, `clampSpot`, move, flip, tidy); `src/art/room.ts` draws each piece from shapes; the saved `room` field in `SaveData` is `{ items: [{ id, x, y, flip }], frame: { game, seed } | null }` with positions as fractions of the room, so a layout survives any screen shape. `migrate()` always returns exactly one of each item, clamped to the floor, and a frame only when it is well formed (a sticker the save no longer has just shows an empty frame). The room is part of the one save, so a backup carries it. The frame shows a sticker without using it up: the sticker stays in the book and can still be placed on a page. There is no hunger, no dirt, no timer and no currency; leaving the room, or the app, changes nothing. Touching a furnishing sends the pet to play with it (`RoomScene.use`); the lamp's dimming is not saved. A moved item that was only tapped (less than 14 units of travel) is a tap, not a move.

**Creations.** What she makes can hang on the wall. A game hands the shell something she made or completed through the optional `RoundResult.creation` (`src/content/creations.ts`): Stamp Studio sends its stamps as a `picture` (up to 24: kind, color, position as fractions of the paper, size, turns); Rainbow Fingers' two open-ended levels send the brush dabs and tap-grown flowers as another `picture` (normalized positions and sizes, paper aspect, and at most 320 code-drawn marks; guided coloring pages send nothing); Pixel Pictures sends the last design completed in the round as a 4×4 to 6×6 palette grid (at most 36 colored cells); and Song Maker's free levels send the lit jellies as a `tune` (a grid of 2 to 8 beats and 3 to 5 pitches with at least two different jellies; a copied song is not hers and sends nothing). `GameScene.celebrate` shows a tree house button beside the new sticker only when a creation came back; tapping it calls `store.keepCreation` and turns into a check, and nothing is kept otherwise (so a round that ends because the session's time is up goes straight to goodnight and offers nothing: open). The save has a picture-board slot shared by all three visual media, and a tune slot; each has `current` (on show) and `previous`. Keeping puts the new one on show and moves the one it replaces to `previous`; keeping what is already on show changes nothing, so a repeat tap cannot push the earlier one out; `undoCreation` swaps the two, so asking again puts things as they were. The **X** over an occupied place calls `takeDownCreation`: it leaves the board or plaque empty and makes the work just removed the one behind the back arrow, so the action is reversible; if there was an older backup, that backup makes room just as it does when a newly kept work replaces it. So at most two visual works and two tunes are kept. `cleanCreations` repairs a damaged or restored save (bad stamps, paint marks or pixel cells dropped, visual work bounded, coordinates and aspect clamped, notes off the grid dropped, and too-short tunes or empty visual works become empty places). In `RoomScene`, the current visual work hangs on a board and the tune on a plaque beside the window and frame (`src/ui/creation-art.ts`, redrawn from `stampArt`, paint marks, pixel cells or a grid of colored jellies); touching the board sends the pet to look and touching the plaque sends it to sing the song beat by beat (`tuneBeats` turns it into the scale steps the game itself uses, played on `sfx.marimba`, each column lit as it sounds); an empty board or plaque says what could hang there. A small back arrow above each appears only when there is an earlier one. Tidy and a reload leave both alone. Not built: a gallery of more than one earlier piece.

**Discovery journal.** `JournalScene` (`go.journal()`, a magnifying-glass button in the treehouse) is a paged grid of cards, eight to a page, one for every entry in `JOURNAL` (`src/content/journal.ts`): twenty-seven observations from six games, plus twenty-six "A is for apple" entries from Letter Trails (`letter-trails:A` to `Z`, filed only for a letter whose picture was shown). Sink or Float supplies nine things (floaters first), Animal Snack seven animals, Photo Safari six action words, Seesaw Balance the heavier-side and equal-weight rules, Bouncy Launch the bigger-pull rule, and Habitat Helpers the food/water/shelter need observed for Bunny and Duck. Entry ids are `game:key` and never change. A game reports what a completed round actually showed through `RoundResult.discoveries`: Sink or Float notes each thing that went into the water (a thing that was only sorted correctly was never tested, so it is not found); Animal Snack notes each animal that ate its favorite food; Photo Safari notes actions in successful photographs; Seesaw records a heavier side going down or equal sides becoming level only after the child changes a tray; Bouncy Launch records its rule only after two plainly different pulls; Habitat Helpers records only visitors that actually arrived in a completed round. `GameScene.finish` files the new ones with `store.discover`, and the celebration shows them in a small card (a magnifying glass and up to three pictures) before the buttons. The save's `journal` is `{ found, seen }`: `found` in the order found, only known ids, once each, and `seen` how many she had looked at when she last opened it (never more than found), which only drives the twinkle on the treehouse button and on new cards. A found card shows the picture and name; an unfound one a dim silhouette and a question mark. Touching a card speaks its observation (or "not found yet") and fills the strip below with the observation text plus the exact revealing action: for example, put the duck into the water before it is found, and after that, that it was found when the duck went into the water. The action is fixed entry content rather than extra save history. The green arrow opens the game at the band nearest hers that it has (`playBand`), and home from that game comes back to the journal. Unit tests keep it honest: every promised observation has exactly one entry, every entry's game exists, each spoken line is true to that game's rules, and each source action says exactly what reveals it. Not built: notes she writes herself, launching at the exact level or request, and a journal entry for a creation.

## The Windy Picnic

The first Island Errands story, built as the roadmap's small pilot. Its rules live in `src/content/picnic.ts`, its art in `src/art/picnic.ts`, and its scene in `src/app/scenes/PicnicScene.ts`; the island map shows it as a picnic blanket in the bottom corner beside Rainbow Meadow, open to every age like the lands (it is a story, not a land).

- **The muddle.** The wind has blown Juniper the gardener bunny's picnic away. Three request pictures (a striped blanket, a plate of sandwiches, an invitation with a note) can be tapped in any order; the first not yet done glows. Juniper says each request and tapping her repeats it, as does the pet guide.
- **The blanket**, played in the scene: two blankets (lap and toddler) or three (striped, spotted and checked, each with its own color) hang in the tree, on the clothesline or on the bush. Juniper's is striped. At early school two blankets are striped, so the request also says where it landed. A wrong blanket is explained (its pattern, or where it is) and two misses make the right one glow; the right one floats down and spreads out. Touches go to the nearest blanket. This step has no round result, so it gives no sticker.
- **The sandwiches and the invitation** are Pet Kitchen and Jelly Drums rounds at a level chosen per band (`GAME_STEPS`; a band a game lacks plays its nearest supported band, so lap plays Pet Kitchen's toddler level). Finishing the round completes the step, however much help it took. Coming back, the plates arrive on the blanket or the friends (a cat, a duck and a bear) hop in. Leaving a round early returns to the picnic with nothing changed.
- **The ending.** With all three done the friends eat to Juniper's invitation song, and her photo of the picnic is saved as the one keepsake as soon as it appears, then flies into the journal. A reload before then replays the ending rather than skipping it.
- **The journal** (top right) shows the three requests with a check and a picture of what each changed; tapping a picture replays its request or recap, and a green arrow goes straight to the next request. After the ending it shows the photo and offers to tell the story again, which clears the steps and keeps the photo. The grown-up zone summarizes the story's progress.
- **What it does not do.** The picnic shows a fixed picture of each step (four plates, three friends, Juniper's tune), not what the child made: the round result carries only misses and hints. Nothing expires, nothing is lost, and the story never awards stickers of its own.

A round can also be played as a step of a story: `go.game(id, band, { story?, step, level })` plays the story's level for the child's band (no `story` means the Windy Picnic; `StoryRound` in `src/app/App.ts` is a union of the two stories' steps), and `go.picnic(step)` or `go.woodland(from?)` comes back to show what changed. In a story round the shell records the round and awards its sticker as usual, but leaves the game's adaptive level where it was, marks the step done (idempotently), and sends home and the celebration's basket button back to the picnic. When a scene is destroyed, its tweens and the promises from `ctx.say`/`ctx.instruct` stop resolving, so a game waiting on a line or an animation can't carry on after the child has left.

### The Woodland Picnic

The second Island Errand, for the `school` band only, from Wonder Woods. Rules live in `src/content/woodland.ts`, art in `src/art/woodland.ts`, the scene in `src/app/scenes/WoodlandScene.ts`. It reuses the Windy Picnic's contract with a story ID: `src/content/world.ts` has `WOODLAND_STEPS`, `STORY_STEPS`, `StoryId` and `StoryStep`; the save holds `stories.woodland` (a `StoryProgress<'woodland'>`, repaired in `migrate()`: an old save gets an empty telling, only that story's own steps are kept, and `ended` needs every step); the store's `completeStep(step, story = 'picnic')`, `endStory(story)` and `retellStory(story)` act on the named story, and `store.woodland` reads it. `GameScene` sends a story round home to its own story's scene and completes the step in its own story; the round keeps its usual sticker and the game's adaptive level stays put.

- **Entry.** The first stop on Wonder Woods' path in `PlaceScene` (a `StorySpot`, `scene.story`, the same step as a game so nothing shrinks) and a "Woodland Picnic" card on `SubjectPlaceScene`'s top-level page after Favorites; `go.woodland(from?)`. Home returns to Wonder Woods (`go.place('school')`). Other bands see neither.
- **Three jobs, any order,** from Hazel the squirrel postkeeper: invitations (Mail Carrier 6), baskets (Seesaw Balance 7) and the beat (Beat Builder 3, on its `PicnicStage`). Each finished round changes the clearing (flags and lit stepping stones, two baskets on a pole, a drum and bell on a stump).
- **The ending.** With all three done, a bear, a bunny and a duck walk in along the stones and a stated eight-step beat (`PICNIC_BEAT`, drum and pentatonic bells) plays twice; Hazel's photo is kept once (`endStory('woodland')`). A photo button then opens it large with a green "tell it again", which clears the steps and keeps the photo.
- **What it does not do.** No typed story result, no Robot Path, no younger version, no stickers of its own; the Windy Picnic is unchanged.

### Proposed: story results

Not implemented. To carry what the child made into a story, a round could finish with an optional, typed `story` field beside `misses` and `hints`, defined per step, for example `{ kind: 'shares', plates: number[] }` from Pet Kitchen or `{ kind: 'tune', notes: number[] }` from Jelly Drums. The story would also need to pass the step's request into the round (an exact quantity to share, a tune to finish) through the game context, and each participating game would opt in by reading it. The shell would save the result with the step, bounded in size and validated in `migrate()` like the rest of the save, so a replayed round replaces the step's result rather than adding another. Games that don't opt in keep finishing with `{ misses, hints }` alone.

## Couch play

A route for one grown-up, or two taking turns (and, later, a child watching or joining), played with a controller or keyboard on a computer connected to a TV. It is separate from the child's island in code, save and rules: `CouchScene` (`src/app/scenes/CouchScene.ts`) is a DOM overlay over the Pixi canvas, and `src/couch/` holds everything it needs. Unlocks, scores and face-off live only here; the child's island keeps every game open.

- **Contract additions (all optional, so touch play is unchanged).** `Game.control(input, dt)` takes semantic controller input; `Game.autoplay(dt)` returns the input a capable player would press; `Game.restart()` (optional) puts the current part of the round back to its first position, with tries already made still counted, and is offered in the pause menu when the game implements it and its `COUCH_INFO` entry has a `restart` label (Penguin Slide: "Start this pond again"); a restart asked for mid-animation waits for it to finish; `Game.askForHint()` (optional) is help she asks for, offered in the pause menu when the game implements it and its entry has a `hint` label (Sudoku Garden: "Show a hint"), for a puzzle with no misses to count; a hint it shows marks a course run as helped through the same `progress` callback; `RoundResult.score` / `scores` carry a face-off result; `GameContext.couch` says a shared-board game should alternate two players (`versus`) or play a fixed-board challenge (`course`: a resume point and a `progress` callback).
- **Catalog.** `src/couch/catalog.ts` holds, per couch game, the spoken goal line, control rows (named by controller position), who plays (turns or together), the face-off kind (`twin`, `shared` or `team`) with which score wins, the band and the demo level, the level each stop plays, and couch wording for lines that tell a touch player to tap or pull. `src/couch/party.ts` holds the ID list, the unlock tiers and the pure rules; both are tested.
- **How to play.** The first time a game is chosen, `CouchScene.intro` shows its name, spoken goal, a code-drawn controller (`controller-art.ts`) with the pressed parts lit, and a window running `Demo` (`demo.ts`): a real, separate instance of the game on a fixed seed, in a scaled and masked container with its own tweens, particles and pet, played by the game's `autoplay` through the same `control()` a controller uses. Nothing in it is saved, rewarded or spoken. A game that has been explained shows a name card with **Play** and **How to play**: the card starts the round by itself after a moment, but moving the highlight (right or down) cancels that and waits, so a player can ask for the how-to without racing the countdown. The face-off turn card offers it too. Pause offers it mid-round, and the couch menu has a **How to play** guide, one card per open game, whose how-to screen has a Back button instead of Play, so it never selects a game or touches the trip; seeing a game's how-to there counts as explaining it (the NEW mark clears). `CouchScene.intro` takes where it was opened from (`first`, `pause`, `guide` or `turn`), and Back returns there. A browser check that the bot completes a round is also a check that the controls can.
- **Saves.** `neo.couch.v1` (localStorage), version 3: the trip in progress (six stops, mode, reshuffle count, a pending first turn), completed trips, one sticker count and latest seed per game, the games already explained, the keepsake, two optional player names (cleaned and bounded to fourteen letters), each challenge course's run and records, and the grown-up's `settings` (below). Fields added after version 3 (names, courses, settings) did not change the version: repair gives an older save the defaults, and a build from before a field reads the same save and drops what it does not know, where a version bump would make that older build discard the whole save. Versions 1 and 2 are upgraded (a save that had finished a trip gets the keepsake with an unknown date); repair never keeps a selection the save has not unlocked, and a restored backup is written back byte for byte as play wrote it.
- **The finale.** `finaleOf(save)` (`src/couch/finale.ts`, pure and tested) summarizes a finished trip: the verdict (a face-off winner is named, a loser never is; a level score is "you both win the evening"), who took each stop, the games the trip opened and how many the next will. `FinaleStage` (`src/couch/finale-stage.ts`) is the Pixi sky under the DOM page: six lanterns rise and light one after another in the color of whoever took the stop (blue Player 1, pink Player 2, gold shared), then confetti and a cheering pet; it owns its tweens, particles and pet, so leaving the screen stops all of it. Coming back to the page (from Controller setup, say) shows the lit sky without replaying the fanfare. The page itself is glass panels, a scoreboard in a face-off, a six-stop recap, the opened games and the keepsake.
- **The keepsake.** One, "Lantern Night", kept from the first finished trip of either mode and never added to: `completeRound` sets it when a trip's sixth stop settles (`keepsake ??= { at }`), so a replayed or reloaded finish cannot award it again. It is a postcard drawn in DOM and CSS plus the pet, dated `at`, shown on the finale the first time and on the couch start page afterwards.
- **Challenge courses.** A course is a fixed run of parts in one game, played in a row as one round and scored by the tries it takes. The parts are frozen data in each game (`games/penguin-slide/course.ts`: three courses of five ponds as pictures in text (a `B` in the legend is an ice block), **Pond Practice** (minimum 18, no dead ends, so nobody is ever stuck), **The Five Ponds** (minimum 30, each pond with one shortest route and traps) and **The Frozen Ponds** (minimum 31, one block pushed in each of four ponds and two in the last, each block needed, a careless push can trap the penguin), with the fewest slides for each pond worked out by the solver, never typed in; `games/bouncy-launch/course.ts`: twelve target clouds and how small they are; `games/sudoku-garden/course.ts`: **Six Beds** (six 6 by 6 beds, par 124 entries) and **Three Big Beds** (three 9 by 9 beds, par 149), counted in entries, each placed number being one try and a bed's empty squares its par; `games/lantern-lights/course.ts`: **Dusk on the Pond**, a 4 by 4 and four 5 by 5 ponds counted in presses with par 36 from the solver; `games/picture-logic/course.ts`: **Pond Pictures** (three 10 by 10 pictures, par 182) and **The Big Pictures** (two 15 by 15, par 230), counted in fills, a picture's filled squares being its par; `games/ferry-jam/course.ts`: **Busy Harbors**, five frozen harbors counted in slides, par 64 from the solver; `games/word-search/course.ts`: **Pond Words** (three themed grids, par 25) and **The Big Hunt** (two 14 by 14 grids, par 24), counted in guesses, one per word being par; `games/island-bridges/course.ts`: **Island Hopping**, five frozen seas counted in planks, par 120 from the solver; `games/pond-conga/course.ts`: **Crumb Trail**, four frozen ponds counted in steps, par 259 from the solver), each with a `version` that must change if a part does. `src/couch/courses.ts` is the registry (`practice`, `ponds`, `frozen`, `clouds`, `beds`, `bigbeds`, `lanterns`, `pictures`, `bigpictures`, `harbors`, `words`, `bigwords`, `bridges`, `conga`): which game plays it, at what level, what its parts and its score are called, the fewest tries for each part, and the text the page shows. `GameContext.couch.course` carries a resume point and a `progress` callback. Penguin Slide counts every successful slide (undone ones included; blocked bumps and the undo button add nothing) and shows each pond's score for a moment before moving straight on; Bouncy Launch counts every launch, a miss included, on clouds that are smaller than the learning levels' (so a launch is judged by feel), keeps quiet between launches, and brings its hint (the landing preview and a ring for the right pull) after two misses at one cloud. Each finishes once. `src/couch/course.ts` holds the pure rules, tested and generic over every course: a `Run` (player, token, finished parts, tries at the part in progress, whether a hint was shown), a `PlayerRecord` per player (best with no help and best with help kept apart, last ten runs, two badges), `startRun`/`noteProgress`/`finishRun` (a stale token or an unfinished run settles nothing) and `completeCourse`, which adds the run's single sticker. The shell saves after every try, so a reload keeps them; a run resumes at the part it was on, restarting that part with its tries so far still counted. A hint, once shown, marks the run as helped for good. Records compare only within a course version; when a version changes the old records are retired (at most three kept), never mixed in. Badges per player: *Finished* (any finish) and a perfect run (the minimum, no hint), each earned once. `CouchScene` shows a Challenges menu (both courses with each player's mark), a course page (both players' records, the rules of the score, and a way in or back into a run left halfway) and a result (the total against the minimum, the best, new badges, and tries on each part).
- **Playing alone.** `settings.players === 'one'` (`CouchScene.alone()`) changes what is drawn, never what is saved: the start page is *Just for you* (a **Puzzle shelf**, and a Together-only **Start a trip**), a face-off is not offered, turn cards and the face-off tally are not drawn, the course page shows only Player 1's card of marks, the Resume button carries no name, Settings shows one name field, and the Challenges menu becomes the shelf. Records stay Player 1's, in the same `CourseSave.players` pair, so switching back to two loses nothing, and a trip or run already under way finishes the way it began. `src/couch/shelf.ts` (pure, tested) holds the shelf's rules: `shelfEntry` (her best, preferring a run with no hint; the minimum, which is par; tries above par; whether a run is half played), `standing` (the line under a puzzle: “21 slides · 3 above par (18)”) and `canWatchRoute`. The shelf today is the courses; a new puzzle joins it by being a course. Past six puzzles its cards drop their description (the course page has it) so the shelf fits a 1024 by 768 window without scrolling up to nine puzzles (it scrolls with the controller beyond that).
- **Watch the best routes.** `CourseInfo.routes` says a course's game can replay the best way through each part; Penguin Slide's ponds can, because its demo bot already walks the solver's next slide from wherever the penguin is, and every course pond has exactly one shortest route (tested). `Demo` takes an optional `part: { course, board }`: it gives the game a `couch.course` resume at that part and ends the replay when the part's `progress` reports it finished, then rests and plays it again. `CouchScene.watchRoute` shows the pond in the same window as How to play, with a button per pond. Nothing is scored or saved. `canWatchRoute` refuses it in the middle of a run (it would be a hint she did not ask for) and offers it after a run finishes or once the course has been finished at least once.
- **Settings.** `src/couch/settings.ts` (pure, tested) holds what the Settings page chooses, kept in the couch save as `settings: { place, volume, music, text, players }` (`players` last, so a restored backup keeps its key order), bounded and repaired on load. `place` is `auto`, `tv` or `laptop`; `resolvePlace` turns `auto` into `tv` when a standard controller is connected (`CouchControls.controllers`, counted by the sampler every frame) or the window's scale is at least `TV_SCALE` (1.35: a 1080p screen in full screen is about 1.4, a laptop window with its browser chrome is below it), and `laptop` otherwise, so prompts follow the controller as it connects. The place only decides what is *named*: `prompts(parts, place)` and `padArt(place)` in `controller-art.ts` draw either controller glyphs and the controller diagram or key caps (`keyCaps`, the stick and d-pad being one set of arrow keys) and a keyboard diagram that lights the same way, and `menuHint` and `cardHint` word the lines under menus; the controls themselves accept a controller and the keyboard at once in every place, and a row's `keys` line (which arrows, whose keys) stays unless the key cap already says it. `volume` is a step from 0 to 5 (`gainFor`: 4 is 0.8, the island's own default) and `music` a switch; `CouchScene.applyPrefs` pushes them into the audio engine when a couch scene starts and again on any change or restore, and `destroy()` calls the island's `applySettings()` so the child's volume returns (a scene change between couch screens passes through that, unheard). `text` is `normal`, `large` (1.2) or `xlarge` (1.4): `unitFor` multiplies the window scale (still kept between 1 and 2.2) and `applyPrefs` writes the result to `--u` on the couch overlay itself, overriding the root value `App.applyView` sets, and again from `resize`. The page (`CouchScene.settingsPage`) is a grid of panels of choice buttons with `aria-pressed`, rebuilt after each change with focus kept on the same button; the pause menu's four buttons (`quickSettings`) change the same fields. Names and the backup moved here from Controller setup. `players` is `two` (the default, so an older save keeps playing as it did) or `one`; it is also a choice on the couch start page. Not built: a Tablet place and pointer play on the couch stage, and an offer filter for keyboard-only games.
- **Readable from a couch.** `App.applyView` sets a CSS length `--u` (the window's scale against the 1024-unit design area, kept between 1 and 2.2); every length in `src/couch/style.css` is `calc(N px * var(--u))`, so couch pages grow with a TV (about 1.4 at 1080p) and stay as they were at 1024 by 768. The text size setting multiplies it on the couch overlay. Pages are laid out to fit a 1920 by 1080 screen without scrolling (checked for every couch game's how-to screen and in-round bar at five window sizes). The controller reminder under a round fades after ten seconds so it never keeps covering the round (Secret Code's stones, for one), and shows again when a pause ends. The finale ceremony takes about four seconds before its panels fade in (not at all with reduced motion).
- **Unlocks.** `UNLOCK_TIERS` is an ordered table; a tier opens per completed trip (either mode). It is derived from the trip count and never stored, so reloads and restored backups cannot award twice, and nothing locks again. The trip-complete screen names newly opened games and says only how many remain; the NEW mark clears when a game's how-to screen has been seen.
- **Stick-driven launchers.** A finger can pull Bouncy Launch's pet back along any line, which stretches the spring sideways like a slingshot; a stick cannot, and a spring that leans left when a button says "more power" looks wrong. In couch play (`GameContext.couch` set, or any `control()` call) the spring squashes straight down by the power instead: stick down or right is more, up or left is less, the red plate and the pet ride down with it, the power persists between launches (a retry is a nudge), and a hint's outline marks where to push the plate to. The power is also the landing spot, so a launch needs no pull to be measured. Touch play, and the child's version, are unchanged. A catapult arm (the pet in a cup that swings back) would suit the finger's sideways pull better and is a possible redraw of the launcher for both.
- **Choosing.** `offers()` draws three from the opened games, seeded and stable across reloads, preferring games not yet played this trip and not the last two; a free shuffle changes the seed.
- **Face-off.** `twin`: each player plays their own board in turn (a different seed per turn, so the second player never faces a board whose answer they have watched), scored relative to that board's own best so boards compare fairly; the first turn is saved so leaving between turns changes nothing. `shared`: one board, the game alternates players and reports `scores`. `team`: both score. Ties score for both; equal-information is the rule that decides which games qualify (a game whose boards are fixed per level, like Robot Path, can only be a team game). One sticker per stop. The trip ends with a tally and never a loser: everyone lights every lantern.
- **Grown-up games on the island.** The six puzzles that used to be couch-only (`GROWNUP_GAMES`, now retired) are ordinary `GAMES` with `bands: ['grownup']`, listed in the Lantern Lake land (`src/content/lands.ts`); `couchGameById` is kept as an alias of `gameById`. A `GameModule` declares what plays it with `inputs` (`'touch'` the default, `'controller'`): `playsByTouch(mod)` is false for a controller-only game, and `go.game` then opens its couch puzzle-shelf course (`FIRST_COURSE`) rather than `GameScene`, which has no controller input until Lands stage C. All grown-up games play by touch on the island; eight also list controller input (Pond Conga was the last controller-only one; its route stays for the next: Pond Conga: one layer over the whole pond (`tapLayer`) follows one finger, a swipe of 36 units (`swipeDir`, clearly along one axis) turns the line and starts the finger over so one stroke can turn it twice, a touch lifted without a turn sets the line off, and Hint and Start over are buttons of 64 units or more beneath it, the pond clear of the home and help buttons; the pond is one target, so its cells may be under 64; a `control()` call flips it back to the couch layout; Picture Logic: a tool (Fill, Cross, Slide) and a pointer layer over the paper; a stroke is projected on to the row or column of its first movement (`axisOf`, `strokeCells`), so a swift finger still paints every square it crossed; the squares sit in a masked window with the clue numbers in two pinned layers that follow the scroll along one axis, and `touchFrame` in `logic.ts` is the pure layout the tests run over every picture and view; Word Search: one pointer layer over the grid tells a tap from a slide, a grid bigger than its window slides under a drag with a mask, edge strips and the hint scrolling the ringed letter into view, and a drag along a word marks it when the grid is all in view; Lantern Lights: a tap presses a lantern; Undo, Hint and Start over are buttons, hidden when `ctx.couch` is set; misses are always 0 and each hint counts. Sudoku Garden: see below). The band's level ranges are the games' own whole ladders re-keyed from `school` to `grownup`, so no saved level was renumbered. A controller-only puzzle opened from the island passes its `origin` (a land, a home spot or the journal; Lantern Lake when it has none) into `CouchScene` through `go.couch(play, origin)`, and every rebuild of that scene carries it on, so its "Back to the island" buttons and Esc on the course page return to that origin (`goToOrigin`, `src/app/origin.ts`, shared with `GameScene`'s home). Opened from "Who's playing?" (no origin) it still returns to the chooser. A key cap (`src/ui/keycap.ts`) sits on each controller-only icon in a land, the home path and subject cards, and the island says once, on opening, that the puzzle needs a keyboard or a controller (`couch.island`); the grown-ups' page shows no level picker for such a game, since the course sets its own level and a pin would never apply.
- **Sudoku Garden.** Its rules are `src/games/sudoku-garden/logic.ts`: flat grids (0 is empty) over a `geometry(size)` of houses and peers (6 by 6 with 2 by 3 boxes, 9 by 9), `search` for counting solutions and growing full beds (fewest-choices-first, digits in random order when given an `Rng`), `techniqueSolve` (lone, place, pair and locked steps, easiest first, each recorded with its house, pattern and what it rules out; `stopAtPlacement` is what a hint needs), `gradeOf`, `makePuzzle` and `hintFor`. `course.ts` freezes the two courses' beds (`beds` and `bigbeds`), reads them with `parsePuzzle` (solution found by the solver) and takes the par from the empty squares, never from a typed number. The scene (`index.ts`) draws the bed and a number tray into the couch stage, takes input only from `control()` (edge and held directions, with a 0.4 second delay and then repeat), counts every placement as a course try, and reports a bed's numbers through `progress` like Penguin Slide does; its `autoplay` walks to the nearest number the easy steps can place and chooses it from the tray, which is both the how-to demo and the browser check that a controller can finish a bed. Trip stops play levels 1 to 3 (6 by 6 beds drawn from the seed, scored in entries over the empty squares); the 9 by 9 levels exist for the generator, and the one course that uses them is frozen data. **By touch** (`!ctx.couch`; a call to `control()` switches the same game to the couch layout, which the couch's own how-to demonstration relies on) a layer over the bed resolves a tap to a square and a row of targets (a tile per number, Marks, Erase, Hint, Start over) each at least 64 logical units: beside the bed on a wide view, under it, with a larger bed, on a tall one; `autotouch()` taps a square, then its number. `GameContext.demo` is set while the how-to card's demonstration plays, and the game then shows `shortBed` (ten empty squares) so the ghost finger finishes inside the card's 75 seconds.
- **Lantern Lights.** Its rules are `src/games/lantern-lights/logic.ts`: a board is a flat array (`true` is lit); `solve` finds every set of presses that lights a board by Gaussian elimination over two numbers (rows as bigint bit masks) and returns the fewest, how many ways win (2 to the free choices) and whether none do; `makePond` presses a plan's number of different lanterns on an all-lit pond; `hintFor` picks a lantern in a fewest-press way, nearest the cursor. `course.ts` freezes Dusk on the Pond's five ponds, with the par from the solver. The scene (`index.ts`) draws code-drawn paper lanterns on a night pond (each lantern is a lit and a dark picture, turned over with a tween that a later flip takes over from), takes input only from `control()` (with `HeldDirection`), counts every press as a course try and reports through `progress`; its `autoplay` presses the hint's lantern, so a bot always plays a fewest-press way. The grown-up-only games share `Game.askForHint` and the pause-menu button.
- **Picture Logic.** Its rules are `src/games/picture-logic/logic.ts` and its drawings `pictures.ts` (rows of characters in palette letters; the 6 by 6 level reuses Pixel Pictures' logic-solvable pictures, and `placements` is exported from `pixel-pictures/logic.ts` for the line solver). A grid is a flat array of marks (empty, filled, crossed); `lineStatus` judges a line done, over or open from its clue and marks alone; `paint` is the pure rule for what a brush does to a square and whether it counts (only a fill does); `deductions` finds every square one line decides, and `hintFor` picks one with its line and clue. `course.ts` freezes Pond Pictures and The Big Pictures by reference to the drawings, with the par from the drawing's filled squares and a test pinning each picture's clues. Painting by holding a button needs to know the button is *down*, which the sampler's one-frame `action` and `undo` do not say, so `PlayerControls` gained `hold` and `holdUndo` (true for as long as the bottom or left button is down); the scene starts a stroke on the press (the first square decides the brush) and carries it along as the cursor moves while the button is held, and its `autoplay` keeps `hold` true in every frame of a run, because a stroke ends the moment the button is not held. The scene (`index.ts`) draws clue gutters, the grid and a reveal in the picture's colors, counts every fill as a course try and reports through `progress`. By touch (any play outside the couch) the same scene lays itself out with `touchFrame`; a first call to `control()` switches it to the couch layout (`useController`).
- **Ferry Jam on the couch (Harbor Rush).** An island game can take a couch mode the way Penguin Slide did, and Ferry Jam did: `control()`, `autoplay()`, `restart()`, `askForHint()` and a course, with the touch drag path untouched. `boatToward` and `focusPath` in `ferry-jam/logic.ts` are the highlight's pure rules (the nearest boat in a quarter-turn cone, else the nearest on that side; a test walks every harbor of the game and every boat along the best way out to prove each boat can always be reached); the scene keeps `focus` (the boat highlighted) and `grab` (the boat held, its lane position and how far it can go), moves a held boat one cell per push with `HeldDirection`, and commits a slide when it is set down somewhere new, which `moves` counts exactly as a touch drop does. On the couch the round arrow is hidden, and the automatic hint after many slides is off (a hint is asked for in the pause menu and marks a run helped). `course.ts` freezes Busy Harbors by picking five of the game's solver-checked harbors, with the par from `solve`; because its bot follows the solver and the `progress` callback reports a finished harbor, the course has `routes: true` and **Watch the best routes** replays each harbor.
- **Word Search.** Its rules are `src/games/word-search/logic.ts`, its content `words.ts` (nine themed lists of plain capital words, kept as data) and `blocked.ts` (profanity and slurs that must never be readable in a grid). `makeGrid` places a plan's words longest first, then redraws only the filler letters until every listed word occurs exactly once in the eight directions and no blocked word can be read; `lineBetween` turns two squares into the squares of a straight line (or nothing), `wordAt` reads a marked line either way against the words not found yet, and `hintFor` picks the first letter of the unfound word nearest the cursor. `course.ts` freezes the two courses' grids with their words, found by looking (`parseGrid` insists on exactly one occurrence), and a test checks the block list against them. The scene (`index.ts`) draws the letters and a pale capsule for the line being marked, a colored band for each found word, and the word list as a panel; it counts every checked line as a course try and reports through `progress`.
- **Pond Conga.** Its rules are `src/games/pond-conga/logic.ts`. `step` is the whole rule of play: a requested turn (straight back is ignored), the leader moves one cell, and the result is a `move`, a `crumb` (the line grows by one duckling) or a `bonk` (a lily pad, the bank or the line itself: nothing moves, the whole line is reversed so the old tail leads, and the heading continues outward from it, so a line can never box itself in; the tail's cell counts as free unless the line is growing). Every step and every bonk is one try; the pace (`speed`, steps a second) is constant within a pond and barely rises across levels, and the scene starts a pond only after the first turn of the stick. The fewest steps for a pond is exact: `lowerBound` adds the lily-pad-aware breadth-first distances between the crumbs (no route can be shorter), and `makePond` keeps a pond only if `solveRoute` finds a route with that many steps and no bonk (`legRoutes` walks the shortest paths to the next crumb with the line's own body simulated, memoizing dead places, and `solveFrom` backtracks over legs). So par is a fact about the pond, not a typed number; `parsePond` refuses a frozen picture that has no such route. `hintPath` is the shortest bonk-free way from wherever the line has wandered, a few steps longer if its own tail is in the way for a while (a property test wanders at random and then follows hints, and every crumb is eaten with at most four bonks). The scene (`index.ts`) ticks in `update` (turns are queued by `control`, two at most), glides the line between ticks, shows the next crumb and a faint one after it, and reports each step through the course `progress`; its `autoplay` follows the pond's own route while the line is on it (so a bot run is exactly par) and the hint's direction otherwise. `course.ts` freezes Crumb Trail's four ponds as pictures (`S` the leader, `s` the line, `#` a lily pad, 1 to 9 then a to c the crumbs in order) with the pace in `speed`. **By touch** the pond is the swipe's single target (`resizeTouch`); `autotouch` plans the ghost finger's stroke from the route (`headAfter`, `swipeDir`), landing so the swipe counts between the step before a turn and the turn's own step (a swipe takes about half a second), grouping up to three turns on neighbouring steps into one stroke, and the how-to demonstration (`ctx.demo`) paddles at one step a second over `firstCrumbs(pond, 4)` so the hand keeps up and it finishes inside the card's 75 seconds.
- **Island Bridges.** Its rules are `src/games/island-bridges/logic.ts`: `layoutOf` (the pairs of islands that face each other and which bridges would cross), `canAdd`, `isSolved`, `trouble` (an island that has too many planks or can no longer reach its number, from the numbers alone), `solve` (propagation of each island's need against its room, with search over what is left and a connectivity check on a finished try; a `noGuessing` option stops after propagation, which `bycounting` uses to grade the easy levels), `makePuzzle` (grow a layout and its planks, number the islands, keep it only if there is exactly one answer, and sort the islands into reading order so a puzzle read back from its picture is numbered the same), `islandToward` and `focusPath` (the highlight, tested to reach every island), and `hintFor`. `course.ts` freezes Island Hopping's five seas as pictures (digits for islands), with the par from `parsePuzzle`, which refuses any picture without exactly one answer. The scene (`index.ts`) draws the sea, plank bridges (a double bridge as two lines) and green or red islands, has three modes (`move`, `add`, `remove`), counts every plank laid as a course try and reports through `progress`. **By touch** (`!ctx.couch`; a call to `control()` switches the same game to the couch layout, which the couch's own demonstration relies on) a layer over the sea resolves a tap with `edgeAt` (the edge whose stretch of water, the cells between its islands, holds the point; in a cell shared by crossing bridges the nearer line, then the one with planks) and three buttons (Take off as a switch, Hint, Start over) each at least 64 logical units: beside the sea on a wide view, under it, with a larger sea, on a tall one; `autotouch()` taps a point `waterOf` gives an edge, switching Take off first for a plank that does not belong, and `headStart` leaves only the answer's last six planks for the demonstration (`GameContext.demo`).
- **Why these fourteen.** Penguin Slide, Bouncy Launch and Bounce Back came first. Then games that are seeded, tap-only or simple to map to a stick and two buttons, short, and varied in what they ask: Memory Match (memory, shared board), Rhythm Neighbors (listening, team), Light Lab and Secret Code (planning and deduction), Peg Garden (aim), Bumper Garden (a two-player flipper game). Five more followed, each a few lines of `control()` on an existing game, with the last three tiers opening them two, two and one at a time: **Pattern Train** (a ring over three choices; pre-K shape, animal and two-gap levels, never the bell levels, which need listening and a confirm), **Egg Catch** (the stick slides the basket on the two catching levels; with two controllers both steer the same basket, and the chute levels stay touch puzzles), **Frog Hop** (a ring over the lily pads, or the number cards on the counting level; add, take away, how many hops, and the line to 20), **Sink or Float** (its one guessing level with the surprises, float on the left and sink on the right; guesses are never wrong) and **Robot Path** (steps from the stick, the bottom button plays, the left button takes the last step back out; levels 4–6, the rock puzzles). All five are team games. Robot Path's boards are authored per level rather than drawn from the seed, so a second player would face a route they had watched, and Egg Catch's catching levels would mostly end level; the other three are gentle by design. That leaves face-off trips with more team stops than before, so the next couch games should be ones that can score on seeded boards. Considered and set aside: Owl Walk Home (a cooperative child's game).
- **Not built yet.** Further courses, family leaderboards, the daughter joining as a co-pilot, simultaneous face-off boards, Joy-Con support, and real controller and TV validation.

### Extending couch play

**Adding a couch game.** A game becomes a couch game by implementing the optional `control(input, dt)` and `autoplay(dt)` on its `Game`, and adding one entry to `src/couch/catalog.ts` (goal line, control rows, face-off kind, demo level, the level of each stop) and one tier slot in `src/couch/party.ts`. The how-to screen, name card, offers, NEW mark and unlock then follow. `autoplay` is a bot that returns the input a capable player would press; it drives the how-to demo and is the browser check that the controls can complete a round (`BROWSER_SUITE=couchgames`, which waits on the game's public `finished` flag). A face-off game reports `score` (or `scores` for a shared board) through `ctx.finish`; fairness needs boards drawn from `ctx.rng`, never fixed per level. **The couch screen takes no pointer input for the game** (`CouchScene` sets the stage's `eventMode` to `none`), so any on-screen button a touch game draws is dead there and looks like a promise: hide it when `ctx.couch` is set and give its job to `control()` or, for a start-over, to `Game.restart()` and the pause menu. (Penguin Slide's round arrow was the first case.)

**Adding a challenge course.** A challenge is a fixed run of parts in one couch game, scored by how few tries it takes. Put the parts in a frozen data file beside the game (`src/games/<game>/course.ts`, with a `version` that must change if a part does, and the fewest tries worked out by the game's own solver where there is one). Make the game play it when `ctx.couch.course.id` names it: start at `resume.board` with `resume.attempts` already spent, count every try (a slide you undo and a launch that misses still count), call `progress({ board, boards, done, attempts, par, minimum, assisted })` after every try, finished part and shown hint, and call `ctx.finish` once at the end. Then add an entry to `COURSE_IDS` and `build` in `src/couch/courses.ts` (the game, level, names, the fewest tries per part, the text for the page and the badges). The runs, per-player records, badges, versioning and save repair in `src/couch/course.ts` are generic, so they need no change; the Challenges menu, the course page and the result pick the course up. Add the data's own tests (every part valid, the minimum right, the way a player could get stuck) and extend `BROWSER_SUITE=couchcourse`. A course appears on the puzzle shelf with no further change. Set `routes: true` in its `courses.ts` entry only if its game can play the best way through one part by itself under `Demo`'s `part` option (the game's `autoplay` must follow the solver from the part's start, and the part must have a worked-out best way); the Watch the best routes button then appears.
