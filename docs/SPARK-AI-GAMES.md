# Spark AI Games, reviewed for Puddle Island

*Reference review · 2026-11-10 · `claude/spark-ai-games-review`. Documents only: nothing was built. Proposals here are proposals until the developer or a dispatch takes one.*

**What it is.** [Spark AI Games](https://sparkaigames.com/) is a free browser portal of 202 "original mini games" for phone, tablet and desktop, with no download. Its own catalogue sorts them as 40 puzzle, 35 board and card, 34 arcade, 20 action, 16 sports, 14 idle, 13 runner, 11 driving, 9 skill, 9 ".io" arena games and one casual game. Almost every game is a known genre under a new name and theme (Logjam is Rush Hour, Twofold is 2048, Catnap Quilt is Queens), built to one house template: a Journey of levels where each world adds a twist, a Daily board, a timed Rush with a leaderboard, a shop, trophies and a how-to page. The portal adds a Daily page, friend rooms by code, robot opponents, weekly ranks, an optional account and an adverts script.

**How this was checked.** On 2026-11-10 the home page and all 202 game pages were downloaded and read as text: every game's name, category, tagline, description, keywords and its "How to play" and "Good to know" paragraphs. The whole catalogue was then compared with the island's [175 games](GAMES.md), the [idea notebook](IDEAS.md) and the [puzzle-pages](deep-dives/subjects/puzzle-pages.md) and [strategy-games](deep-dives/subjects/strategy-games.md) studies. Thirteen games were opened in headless Chrome at a phone size (390 by 844): the title screens of Logjam, Reversi, Dotline, Wordwheel and Gomoku, and **the first board only** of eight: Sumgrid (solved), Linkloop (six turns, not solved), Catnap Quilt (the first lesson screen), Shadow Stage, Stamp and Pass (the day's memo and first traveller), Parking Lines, Paper Glider Lab and Pullpin. Nothing past a first board was played, no sound was heard, no account was made, and friend rooms, pass-and-play and the 28 rules guides were not opened. So what is said below about later levels is Spark's own description, and a real phone was not involved.

**Recommendation in one paragraph.** Most of Spark's puzzle shelf is already on the island (22 of its games match a built one) or already a row in the notebook (23 more match a proposal or a recorded choice, several of them decided). That is the first finding: the island's grown-up shelf is not missing much. What is worth bringing in is short. **Two new games:** a crossword of sums (P1), which is the best fit on the whole site because it is a phone puzzle for the two grown-ups and a missing-number game for the school band at once; and Reversi for two on one phone (P2), the first board game the developer and their wife could play against each other. **Three more with a definition each:** turn-the-tiles pipes (P3), pair paths that fill the board (P4) and a turning shadow puzzle (P5), which gives an existing sketch its interaction. **Two smaller ones** (P6, P7) and **one level** for Shell Rows (P8) follow. Spark's wrapper (dailies with streaks, Rush clocks, leaderboards, coins, shops, hearts, hints that cost stars, rooms that earn "even while you are away") stays out by the island's existing rules, and so do its code, art and levels, which its pages forbid copying. Only the public genres are taken, rebuilt in the island's own code and art.

**The house template, as seen on a phone.** Every title screen opened has one large Play button and five or six small ones (Daily, Rush, Shop, Trophies, How to play, a level map). Boards sit in the top two thirds and controls in the bottom third, in thumb reach. A first board teaches one thing with one sentence and a ghost hand: Sumgrid's is "2 + □ = 5" across and "5 + □ = 9" down sharing the 5, with only the two missing tiles (3 and 4) in the tray. Results are three stars with named reasons (Sumgrid: "Solved", "No hints", "No red lines"). Its how-to texts keep repeating four promises: every board is checked by a solver, a logic board has one answer, hints explain the next step, and undo is free. Of 202 games, 144 have a daily board, 61 a robot opponent, 48 online rooms and 9 say "play with a friend on the same device".

## Proposals

Ordered by how well each fits the current focus, the two grown-ups on their phones, and then by learning purpose for the bands below. Sizes are the notebook's S, M and L. Each new game needs a definition before a READY line, and each grown-up one meets Lantern Lake's capacity: thirteen games today, so a fourteenth pages the land.

### P1. A crossword of sums (new game, M)

*After Sumgrid.* Number tiles go into the gaps of small crossing sums so that every line across and down is true; a tile on a crossing serves two lines. **Fun action:** drag a tile in and the line lights. **Learning:** the missing number in a sum, the equals sign as "the same as", and using one line to settle another. **Why a new game:** [Fruit Sums](games/fruit-sums.md) picks fruit to make one number and any way counts; here the tiles are given, each has one place, and the lines constrain each other. It is not Kakuro either (Sum Crates, PP5, has no written sums and forbids repeats). **Ladder sketch:** one gap in one line (school entry); two crossing lines with exactly the missing tiles, as Spark's first board; a spare tile that fits nowhere; take-away lines; answer-first lines ("9 = □ + 4"); three-number sums; times lines and a 5 by 5 lattice at the grown-up band. **Rules to test:** one filling makes every line true; the tray holds exactly the answer plus the level's spares; a hint names a gap with only one possible tile and says why. A wrong tile turns its line a soft color and stays movable: one miss when the board is checked, never a locked state. **Phone fit:** nine to thirteen cells, a tray under the board, tap-then-tap as well as drag. Bands: school, grown-up.

### P2. Lily Flip: Reversi for two (new game, M)

*After Reversi; the first of the notebook's* Board games against the pet. Two colors of lily pad on a pond grid; a pad set so that it traps a straight line of the other color flips the whole line. **Fun action:** one tap flips a row of pads. **Learning:** looking ahead, counting what a move wins and what it gives away, why corners hold. **Why this one first:** no docs here mention Reversi, yet it suits a phone better than the row's other games: no hidden information, so passing one phone works; every move shows its result at once; the rules fit one spoken sentence; a game is about five minutes. **Modes:** two people on one phone, and against the pet with the search depth as a handicap (the notebook's design). **Ladder sketch:** positions before games, as Shell Rows does: "one move flips four, find it" on 4 by 4; a whole 6 by 6 game at the school band; 8 by 8 for grown-ups; a replay that shows the turning points afterwards, which Spark's Reversi also advertises and Shell Rows' replay already does. **Island rules:** the count at the end is a result, not a grade; the pet never gloats, and a sticker comes for finishing the game either way. Shared with SG2: Shell Rows' minimax and replay.

### P3. Puddle Pipes with a real ladder (definition for an existing seed, M)

*After Linkloop and Clockwork Atlas.* The notebook's [Puddle Pipes](IDEAS.md#smaller-seeds) is three elbows turned so water reaches a flower, for ages 4 to 8. Spark's version shows what sits above that: boards where **every line must meet another and nothing is left loose**, so every tile matters and the picture lights when it closes; a par in turns; long-press to turn back; "twin" tiles that turn together; packs where water must reach every outlet. **Fun action:** tap, the pipe turns with a click, water runs as far as it can. **Learning:** connection and planning, then parity (a twin pair). **Generator:** draw a random connected network, record each tile's shape, scramble the turns; one answer up to tiles that look the same turned. One tap per move makes this the most phone-friendly board in this review. Bands: preschool to grown-up in one game.

### P4. Pair paths that fill the pond (new game, M)

*After Dotline (Numberlink, Flow).* Join each pair of twins with a path so that no paths cross and every tile is used; each board has one answer. **Learning:** planning routes that leave room for each other. The island's [Dot Link](games/dot-link.md) is the other dots game (lines through same-color neighbours that pop), and its dive only cites this genre as a reference, so this would be a new Lantern Lake board. Spark's later twists, from its how-to text: bridges that carry one path over another, hedges to go round, tunnels that wrap an edge. **Check first:** drawn paths on a 7 by 7 board at phone width against the 64-unit grown-up target; 5 by 5 and 6 by 6 fit without question.

### P5. Turn the shadow (the interaction for an existing sketch, M)

*After Shadow Stage.* The notebook's Shadow Stage seed and the theatre study's [TP6 Shadow Play](deep-dives/subjects/theatre-puppetry.md#candidates-and-recommended-order) place cut-outs between a lamp and a screen. Spark's game does it with one gesture: flat colored plates hang on a rod, **drag left or right to turn the whole mobile**, and at one angle the separate shadows join into a picture that answers a riddle ("Naps all day, prowls all night, and always lands on its feet"). **Fun action:** the shadow snaps into a cat and comes alive. **Learning:** how a flat shape's shadow narrows as it turns, and reading a whole from parts. **Why it is cheap here:** with one turning axis each plate's shadow is its outline squeezed sideways by the cosine of its angle, which is plain 2D drawing in Pixi `Graphics`; no 3D engine is needed. Later rungs in Spark's text: a second axis, two mobiles, a lamp that slides. Recommend TP6's definition start from this gesture; a preschool form ("turn it until you see the bunny") would sit beside [Peekaround Island](games/peekaround-island.md).

### P6. Everybody goes at once (new game or a Robot Path mode, M–L)

*After Parking Lines.* Draw a route from each duck to its nest, press Go, and all walk together; routes may cross as long as no two ducks are in the same place at the same moment. **Learning:** planning in time as well as space, the step after [Robot Path](games/robot-path.md)'s single program. Free-drawn routes need a fair collision rule that a child can predict, so the definition should try a grid version first (routes as cell paths, one step per beat). Lower priority than P1 to P5.

### P7. Ticket Booth (seed, S–M)

*After Stamp and Pass, without the border or the clock.* Friends show a ferry ticket; today's sign gives one rule (the ticket's day matches today's calendar), and each level adds one (the right dock, a stamp present, the name matches the badge). A ticket that does not fit is not refused: she taps the part that is wrong and the friend is sent to the right dock. **Learning:** reading a date and checking evidence against a stated rule. **Check overlap first** with [Notice Board](games/notice-board.md), [Read & Do](games/read-do.md) and [Day by Day](games/day-by-day.md); it may be a level of one of them.

### P8. Shell Rows: the vanishing match (level, S)

*After Spark's Tic Tac Toe variant.* Each player keeps only their last three marks on the board; setting a fourth lifts the oldest. A match can no longer end in a tie, which is what makes plain three-in-a-row dull for two adults. It needs no new board or art and suits two people on one phone. Place it after [SG2](deep-dives/subjects/strategy-games.md#candidates-and-recommended-order) (Shell Rows 4–5, forks and a whole gentle match), as a school and grown-up level.

## What not to take

- The wrapper on every game: Daily boards and streaks, Rush clocks, leaderboards, coins, shops, trophies, star ratings with a "no hints" star, hearts, and anything that earns while the player is away.
- Accounts, friend rooms, the adverts script and "Surprise me" as a way to keep someone playing.
- Staking card games, shooters, arena and ".io" games, and the action, runner and driving shelves generally: they are reflex games with a fail state, and the island's [arcade review](IDEAS.md#fun-and-arcade-review-2026-10-09) already holds the few motions worth keeping.
- Pullpin's lava, monsters and poison, and Stamp and Pass's border desk and citations. Pull-the-pin itself is a fair prediction puzzle, but it needs falling grains and liquids that `engine/ball.ts` does not model; it is not proposed.
- Any Spark name, level, picture, sound or sentence.

## Not established

- Who makes Spark AI Games, how the games are produced, and how many people play them. The name suggests AI-assisted production; no page read says so.
- Whether its claims hold (one answer per board, every deal winnable, hints that explain): they were read, not tested beyond one Sumgrid board.
- How any game feels after its first board, with sound, or on a real phone. Thirteen of 202 were opened, in headless Chrome.
- Whether adverts appear in play. The script is loaded on game pages; none showed on the screens captured.
- Whether a 7 by 7 path board (P4) or an 8 by 8 pond (P2) is comfortable at phone width on the island; that is a layout check for each definition.
