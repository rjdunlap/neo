# Pok Pok, reviewed for Puddle Island

*Reference review · 2026-11-10 · `claude/pokpok-research-inspiration-b711e0`. Documents only: nothing was built. Proposals here are proposals until the developer or a dispatch takes one.*

**What it is.** [Pok Pok](https://playpokpok.com/) (first *Pok Pok Playroom*; the [App Store](https://apps.apple.com/us/app/pok-pok-montessori-preschool/id1550204730) now titles it "Pok Pok | Montessori Preschool") is a subscription app of about twenty hand-drawn "digital toys" for children of 2 to 7: a town, a busy board, musical blobs, a marble machine, a forest, a kitchen. It has no levels, no winning or losing, no text and no spoken language. Esther Huybreghts and Mathijs Demaeght began it for their own toddler; the studio Snowman (*Alto's Adventure*) incubated it and spun it out under Melissa Cash. It launched in May 2021 with six toys at $3.99 a month, had seventeen by June 2024 and over a million downloads, came to Android in October 2024, and won an Apple Design Award (2021) and an App Store Award for cultural impact (2023). On 2026-11-10 the US store showed 4.7 from 26 thousand ratings, monthly plans from $6.99 and a lifetime purchase at $89.99.

**How this was checked.** The app was **not installed or played**: it is a native app behind a paid trial, and a session neither signs up for trials nor enters payment details. What was read on 2026-11-10, about thirty pages: Pok Pok's own FAQ and nine of its blog posts (launch, Forest, Busy Board, Marble Machine, Number Journey, Phonics and its update, Cooking, Code Factory), the store listing and its version notes, Apple's *Behind the Design* feature, the Sketch interview, TechCrunch, GamesBeat, MobileSyrup, MacStories, 9to5Mac's update archive, and five reviews (Common Sense Media, iMore, parent.tech, New Literacies, store reviews). No sound was heard and no animation was seen in motion. Everything in the "what it does" tables below is therefore **read**, not seen, and most of it is the company describing itself.

**Recommendation in one paragraph.** Pok Pok is the best worked example of the island's youngest end: play that starts on the first touch, cannot go wrong, needs no words and can be put down. It is also weakest exactly where the island is strong: reviewers call its toys predictable and thin on persistence, and since 2024 it has been adding goal-shaped "learning toys" (Number Journey, Phonics, Code Factory) because its own families asked, which is the island's position reached from the other side. So nothing here overturns a rule. What it adds is a set of **tests** the island can apply to itself and one small bug they expose: a sound test (would you leave it on in a restaurant?), a toy test for every lap opening (one big thing that answers the first touch, then grows by her touches), a put-down test for the rework's unlock goals, residents that carry on when nobody touches them, and the reminder, from Pok Pok's own reversal on Phonics, that a tap should always be able to do what a drag does. The bug: the island's two melodic games use the same rainbow for opposite pitches ([P1](#p1-one-rainbow-one-direction-code-s)). Its subscription, date-bound events, recorded audio, wordless goal rounds and calm-and-regulation marketing stay out.

### The newer "learning toys"

In an Instagram poll, 14% of Pok Pok's families asked for more traditional learning ([Number Journey post](https://playpokpok.com/blog/new-toy-number-journey/)), and the three most recent big toys answer them. They are the closest to what the island builds.

| Toy | What the child does | What is worth noticing |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Number Journey** (Nov 2024) | Fills the slots of a vehicle with passengers, each one a single "1"; a full vehicle drives away | A goal with no fail state: the only feedback named is a tone per passenger and the drive-off |
| **Phonics** (Jul 2025, changed Oct 2025) | Drags letter units into Elkonin boxes under a pictured object; a tap says a sound, a swipe blends, the word is spoken | It first spelled by sound ("chrain" for train) and showed the standard spelling only at the end. Families wrote that this confused children who already knew the spelling, and that small hands could not manage the drags. Eleven weeks later: standard spelling on screen, bigger pieces, **more tap-based play** and a more forgiving touch area ([update post](https://playpokpok.com/blog/new-phonics-update-powered-by-your-feedback/)) |
| **Cooking** (Nov 2025) | Any ingredient into any tool (boil, fry, bake, blend, chop, noodle), served to guests who show a craving but may eat anything or push it away | It grew from watching children in the House toy make turkey smoothies and cook steak in the clothes dryer: the absurd use is the play ([post](https://playpokpok.com/blog/new-toy-cooking/)) |
| **Code Factory** (early 2026) | Places red and yellow "director" buttons on the paths of workers who are already walking; machines turn deliveries into goods for a market; oil and rubbish pile up unless vacuumed | A program edited **while it runs**, with no Run button and no puzzle to pass; mess is an optional problem ([post](https://playpokpok.com/blog/new-toy-code-factory/)) |

### What reviewers and parents say

The praise is consistent: beautiful, calm, and above all **easy to put down**. One parent of four says it is easy to stop "unlike other apps"; another's three-year-old lost interest, wandered off, and stopped when asked without a tantrum ([parent.tech](https://www.parent.tech/p/review-pok-pok-the-calm-creative-kids-game-collection-every-parent-needs-to-know-about)). The criticism is as useful:

- **Predictable.** Common Sense Media, the most independent source, finds the interactions "pretty predictable" and wants more silliness and surprise.
- **Thin on persistence.** [New Literacies](https://newliteracies.ai/guides/pok-pok-playroom/) (one person's independent rating site) scores agency, curiosity and creativity strong and persistence limited: no friction, little productive struggle.
- **Small details, no zoom.** Common Sense Media notes that the dense scenes cannot be zoomed, so details are missed. That is a tablet-first design showing.
- **Hazards as props.** Pill bottles, needles, irons and cords appear in the House scenes; the same review flags them.
- **Gibberish wears on adults**, who are advised to use headphones: the restaurant rule holds for effects better than for voices.
- **Too many icons.** A parent asks for favourites, to limit which toys show on the home screen.
- **Price and billing**, the commonest store complaint; and one parent "would not describe it as non-addictive".

No independent study of the app was found. Its claims about calm, regulation and learning are its own.

## Proposals

Ordered by the current focus: the first three bear on the phone playtest the developer and their wife are doing now; the rest are for the youngest band and the rework and can wait.

**Queued 2026-11-10** (the developer asked for them to go on the backlog): P1 (built 2026-11-11, PR #1509), P4 (with P7 as one of its columns), P3's definition and P5's pilot are lines in [READY](READY.md#pok-pok-review-2026-11-10); P2's and P3's questions are in the [phone playtest list](DEVICE-CHECKS.md#what-the-phone-playtest-can-answer-now); P6 is an acceptance line in [RW10](rework/WORK-ITEMS.md#rw10-collection-book-and-chosen-unlock-goals); P8 is in [RW14's later list](rework/POLISH.md#later-not-sliced-yet).

### P1. One rainbow, one direction (code, S)

*Built 2026-11-11, PR #1509.*

Pok Pok's Musical Blobs keeps a rule a child can learn without being told: blue is always C. The island's two melodic games share a rainbow but not a direction. In [Jelly Drums](games/jelly-drums.md) red is the big low jelly and blue the little high one (`COLORS` and `STEPS` in `src/games/jelly-drums/index.ts`: "Big jelly, low note: C D E G A"). In [Song Maker](games/song-maker.md) `ROW_COLORS` runs red to blue from the top row, which is the high row, under a comment that says it matches the jelly drums. So red is the lowest note in one and the highest in the other.

- **Change:** reverse Song Maker's row colours so red is its lowest row, and keep the colour-for-step table in one place (beside the steps in `src/audio/notes.ts`) with a test that both games read it.
- **Tier:** existing-game fix: typecheck, Song Maker's rule tests, one filtered play; `fingerdemo` only if the demonstrated row changes colour on the card.
- **Built:** `PITCH_COLORS` and `pitchColor(rank, count)` in `src/audio/notes.ts` (red lowest, blue highest; fewer than five pitches spread over the whole rainbow); Song Maker's bottom row is red, Jelly Drums and the treehouse tune plaque read the same table, and a test holds the direction and that no game keeps its own table.
- **Checked:** a kept tune stores rows and columns, not colours, so saved tunes needed no change. Sound Garden colours by creature art, not pitch, and Beat Builder's three rows are unpitched drum rows, so neither changed.

### P2. The restaurant test (a phone playtest question; docs, S)

Pok Pok's founding sound rule was that a parent should never need to mute it in a restaurant, and its sounds were chosen to bear repeating. The island's sound is synthesized and spoken, [Today's six](definitions/todays-six.md) plays six rounds and six celebrations in a sitting, and the two players are on phones, often in the same room. The existing [Sound question](DEVICE-CHECKS.md#what-the-phone-playtest-can-answer-now) asks whether sound wakes and how the voices sound. Add to it:

> Would you leave the sound on with someone beside you? By the sixth game, which sound or line had you tired of?

A named sound becomes one READY line under the phone lines, as any [playtest note](OBSERVATIONS.md) does. The usual repairs are already in the engine: a repeated effect that steps through two or three pentatonic variants rather than one, a shorter celebration on the second and later games of a sitting, and `settings.speech` for the voice. Nothing is changed until a note names something.

### P3. A tap can do what a drag does (a question now; a definition later)

Pok Pok shipped Phonics as a drag toy and within eleven weeks had enlarged the pieces, widened the touch areas and added "more tap-based play", because families said small hands could not do it. A grown-up thumb on an upright phone is the island's small hand today, and one playtest note has already been about steering (Pond Conga, fixed by swiping anywhere). The [phone-portrait](definitions/phone-portrait.md) batches ask whether each game plays comfortably with a thumb; make the drag part explicit:

> Which drags were awkward with a thumb, and would tapping the piece and then the place have been easier?

**Defined 2026-11-11:** [tap the piece, then the place](definitions/tap-then-place.md) gives `src/engine/drag.ts` one opt-in `tapToPlace` path (Hat Band's hand-rolled tap is the precedent; [Tangram Town's dive](deep-dives/tangram-town.md) had asked for its own game; the [TotoLogic review](TOTOLOGIC.md#p6-tap-to-fill-withdrawn) withdrew its tap-to-fill reading once the app was played on 2026-11-11, and gives a plainer example instead: that app's first activity is a drag-only sort where a tap on a piece does nothing and no drag is shown). Three slices are in READY: P3a the engine path with Hat Band moved onto it (built 2026-11-11), P3b Block Garden (built 2026-11-11), P3c Tangram Town's levels 1–3 (built 2026-11-11).

### P4. The toy test for lap openings (docs, S; then one game at a time)

About fifty games list the `lap` band. Pok Pok's rules reduce to five questions that can be asked of each one's first lap level from its code, how-to card and `describeLevel`, with no device or child involved:

1. **Does the first touch anywhere do something?** (The island's own [never swallow a tap](definitions/never-swallow-a-tap.md), at its strictest.)
2. **Can it go wrong?** At lap it should not be able to.
3. **Does it play with the voice off?** If the spoken line is the only thing that says what to do, it does not.
4. **Does it start with one big thing and grow by her touches?** Busy Board's single red button is Pok Pok's version. The island's is already built: Jelly Drums level 10, *One big jelly*, where one enormous jelly answers a touch anywhere and its friends wake after six touches. That level is the pattern to name and copy.
5. **Does a silly use get an answer?** Pok Pok's Cooking exists because children put steak in the dryer. Its reviewer's main complaint is predictability; an unexpected reply to an unexpected touch is the cure.

**First slice:** a table in the [Quick Tricks dive](deep-dives/quick-tricks.md#similar-games) or a page of its own, one row per lap-band game with a yes or no to each question. **Then:** a game that fails 1 or 4 gets a lap level in the *One big jelly* shape when it next comes up for work, at the new-level tier. This is a rule audit, not a claim about a child.

**Progress:** all ten lands are read (2026-11-11), 57 of 57 lap games, in [the toy-test page](definitions/toy-test-lap-openings.md), with their words-off lists (P7); the audit is done and five READY lines were queued from Tinker Lab (after the ones from the other lands). `bubble-pop`'s opening bubble landed 2026-11-12 (PR #1564). What remains is the other queued lap-opening lines, one game per session.

### P5. Residents that carry on (a note for RW13 and RW14; M)

Forest's creatures "don't just freeze and wait": they wander, eat, hide, and react to what is dragged near them. The rework's residents ([slice 2](rework/POLISH.md#slice-2-residents-in-six-locations-and-her-own-friend-at-home)) stand with an idle motion, a poke reaction and a hello, and her friend at home fetches the one ball ([creature play](rework/HOME.md#creature-play-and-home)). A pilot on **one** resident in one location: two or three marked spots it walks or flies between on its own, one thing in the scene it uses when it gets there (Tote at a rock pool, Trill at a chime), and a turn of the head toward her last touch. Rules it must keep: nothing is hungry, nothing is owed, nothing changes while she is away, and its touch circle still clears every spot and the signpost. Judged by eye from a short capture; if it reads as alive, the other residents follow as content.

**Pilot built (2026-11-11, [PR #1535](https://github.com/rjdunlap/neo/pull/1535)).** Trill at Music Mountain rests 5–9 s, walks to a wind chime at a free spot within 420 view units, uses it for 2.4 s, comes home, and faces its way when walking and her last touch when still (`src/creatures/wander.ts`; `carriesOn` is set for Trill only). Every stop clears the land's other spots, guide corner, buttons and heading, and nothing is saved, hungry or owed; a resize restarts it at home. Whether it reads as alive is still to be judged from a short capture on a phone; the other residents wait on that.

### P6. The put-down test for unlock goals (a line for RW10; docs)

Parents praise Pok Pok most for what it does not do: a child stops without a fight, because nothing is nearly finished. The rework wants collecting and unlocks, and [RW10](rework/WORK-ITEMS.md#rw10-collection-book-and-chosen-unlock-goals) already forbids quotas, farming and schedules. One acceptance line would hold it to Pok Pok's standard without giving up goals:

> A goal's progress is shown in the collection book, which she opens by choice. The result screen never shows a goal as nearly full, never counts rounds still to go, and offers stopping as plainly as playing again.

For the grown-ups playing now, the matching playtest question is whether the sixth game of the six feels like an ending.

### P7. A words-off list for the family's languages (docs, S)

Pok Pok needs no translation because it has no words. The island speaks English; [additional languages](definitions/additional-languages.md) start with European Portuguese and wait on a family reviewer. Question 3 of P4 produces, for free, the list of lap and toddler levels that play completely with the voice off. Published as a short list (later, a mark on the grown-ups' page), it tells a grandparent who speaks Portuguese or Arabic which games they can sit down to today, in their own words, without waiting for any translation. It claims nothing about what she learns.

### P8. A stepped wobble (art exploration, S, optional)

"Everything could jitter a little bit" is how a tiny team made still drawings feel hand-made, and hand animation runs at a few drawings a second, not sixty. One cheap trial on one prop or one location's foreground: an idle wobble that updates eight times a second rather than every frame. If it sits well beside the mesh-rig creatures in a screenshot review it is a line for the [art bible](rework/ART-BIBLE.md); if it looks like a dropped frame rate, it is dropped. No new art is generated for it.

### What not to take

The subscription, trial and lifetime price; events that arrive and leave by date; recorded audio; gibberish as the only voice; goal rounds with no instruction or hint at all; the time-keypad gate; pill bottles, needles, irons and cords as playthings; scenes dense with small details that only a tablet shows; an ever-longer home screen of icons; and "Montessori", "calming", "sensory-friendly" or any regulation wording about the island's own games.

## Not established

- **Anything about the app in use.** It was not installed. Feel, pace, sound, how the home screen scrolls, and whether it turns upright on a phone are unknown; every mechanic above is from a description.
- **Whether any of it works as claimed.** No independent study was found. "Easy to put down" rests on a handful of parent reviews, one of which disagrees, and a "90% of parents find their kid calmer" figure on a listing site has no source.
- **The current toy list and dates.** Six at launch and seventeen in June 2024 are reported; the store text names about twenty. The blog dates Code Factory to February 2026 while the store's version notes show it in the spring; a Geometry toy has a blog post that was not read.
- **How the newer toys score mistakes.** Number Journey's and Phonics' posts describe right answers and say nothing of wrong ones.
- **P1's wider claim.** Answered when it was built: Sound Garden and Beat Builder do not colour by pitch (see P1); no other music game was read.
