## Design notes

*lap–school (lap plays levels 1–2) · after Pikmin.* Tap a fruit (or pumpkin, watermelon, acorn, pinecone, blueberries, a toy boat) and a helper runs to carry it home; bigger things need more helpers, shown by a badge of hollow rings that fill as each helper arrives (at 4 and 5 a numeral sits over the rings; extras show as orange rings outside the frame). Each round is seeded into an orchard (basket), a beach (rowing boat) or the woods (bucket), and the same thing does not come twice running where the place has another. Needs never fall into a plain 1, 2, 1, 2 swap. Later levels wait for a whistle so the child sends exactly enough: too few cannot lift, extras walk back. The level before equal groups asks how many more are needed when some are already helping: the empty rings are the gap, and a short whistle says it ("3 are here. It needs 5. 2 more!"). **Level 9 *Whistle when it's full*** (toddler after 2, preschool after 2) is the gentlest whistle: four things needing 1–3, the whistle wiggles when every ring is full; an early whistle strains and blinks the empty rings, and a helper sent past full bounces off and walks back, all with no miss or hint. Level 6 (equal groups she builds): a bunch of two or three same-size fruits that each need the same team of two or three (2×2, 3×2, 2×3, each once before any repeats). She taps a fruit to send the next helper to that fruit's team (the nearest fruit to the tap); a full team hops and the pet says it is full (not a miss). The whistle lifts when every team is exactly the asked size; a short team strains. Badge rings fill per team. A wrong whistle counts by the team size ("2, 4, 6"), and each lift ends with "3 groups of 2 make 6". **Level 7 *Everyone helps*** (pre-K 3–7 and school): a crowd of 4–7 is split between two fruits with different needs (each total can be split in more than one way; four rounds alternate two totals), and the crowd is exactly the total, with extra helpers hidden. A wrong whistle makes a short team strain and an over-full team's extras walk back, and counts a miss; two misses ring both teams' places (one hint). Praise says the parts ("2 and 3 make 5"). **Level 8 *Strong helpers*** (school 5–8): a fruit needs 4–8; the crew is four small helpers and three big ones, and a big helper counts two (a "2" on its tummy). She taps the helper she wants, or the fruit for the nearest; any exact mix lifts, and the spoken count is the running power. A hint rings a valid mix and the helpers to send. Extras are trimmed. Pumpkin and watermelon now need 4–8. Windows: pre-K 3–7, school 5–8. Device speech and whether a child reads the team rings are unjudged. **Every touch answers.** At level 1 a touch anywhere (not only the fruit) sends a helper. While a fruit is dropping, lifting or being praised, any touch makes the nearest helper hop and squeak; at levels 2–6 a tap on the sky or grass makes the nearest waiting helper wave and chirp, and a tap on a waiting helper that cannot be sent home hops it. With no helpers left, a fruit tap makes the crowd shrug and the pet says "Everyone is busy helping!" (no boing, no miss). A whistle with nobody sent is a nudge ("Send some helpers first! Tap the fruit."), not a miss; from level 3 only a whistle with the wrong number counts. Every fruit of a bunch goes into the basket. Whether a small child notices the hop and wave is unjudged.
