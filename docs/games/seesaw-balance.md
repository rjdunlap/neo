## Design notes

*After Hasee Bounce (Neopets) and balance toys; levels 8–10 after DragonBox and PhET's Equality Explorer.* Friends, blocks, number weights and presents go on level trays at the ends of a springy seesaw, so only weight matters. Each weight has two friend faces to draw from (1 duck or cat, 2 bunny or dog, 3 pig or cow, 4 bear), no two friends in a round look the same, presents are wrapped plain, dotted or striped, and the mystery box at levels 6–7 is a parcel or a crate. At levels 1–2 a tap on a loose friend hops it to the empty tray (a drag still works); after two overs at levels 3, 6 and 7 the glow rings the excess, and when the tray is under it rings the next block on the grass. Levels: make a little friend go up, choose a friend heavy enough, level it with blocks (one at a time, so it never tips too far), find the heaviest of three look-alike presents by testing pairs (testing is never a mistake) and put it in a wagon, make the same weight with different number weights (any combination counts), and weigh a mystery box with blocks then say how heavy it is. The journal records the heavier side going down and equal sides becoming level only after a tray change actually shows each rule. Level 7: two identical hidden weights balance together; the child divides the visible block total into two equal groups to name one box's weight, and wrong answers demonstrate the two groups aloud. Levels 8–9 (school): the seesaw starts level with a mystery box and blocks on one tray and only blocks on the other (level 9 has a box on each side too). Anything can come off. Taking something off one side tips it, which is said aloud and never counted as a miss; two tips in a row light the thing whose twin should come off (or the thing to put back). When the box stands alone and the seesaw is level, the blocks across from it are its weight; a wrong number is a miss and the blocks are counted aloud. Level 10 leaves two identical boxes balancing blocks after equal things are taken away, so she shares the visible blocks between the two boxes to name one weight. Level 11 (school, *Two boxes*): a square box stands alone against blocks and is read first (the pads show at once, nothing drags; the right pad tags the square box with that number, a wrong pad is a miss). Then the square box, a different round orange box and some blocks face a pile of blocks, and she takes the same off both sides, with the tagged square box worth its number. The round box never moves: touching it wiggles it and says it stays (free). A move that gets closer to level is free and resets the tips; the square box leaving says it tipped; after two tips in a row one glow per pair shows a narrowing move (1 hint). Only the number pads miss, and the round answer writes the number on the round box only. Two pairs, each a reading and a weighing.

Level 12 *Wheee!* opens lap play and starts the toddler ladder: touching anywhere on the empty half of the plank hops a heavier friend onto that tray, lifting the first duck (then other little riders). Touching the rider pokes it and repeats the instruction, free. Four lifts finish with no misses or hints. Level 13 *Who is heavier?* sits between preschool 2 and 3: two different-weight friends already tip the trays; tap the heavier, lighter, then heavier friend over three comparisons. A wrong tap boings and explains down/heavier and up/lighter; the second miss in a comparison counts one hint and glows the asked friend, which remains tappable. Correct taps play a rising pair of notes for heavier and a falling pair for lighter. Lap order is [12], toddler [12, 1, 2], preschool [2, 13, 3, 4]; entry levels 12/13 land at the easiest step of a new band that does not list them. IDs 1–11 stay unchanged. Both modes use the ordinary shell sticker and discovery path; no reading is needed.

Level 14 *Line them up* (pre-K): test three distinct-weight, look-alike presents in pairs on the swap-on-tray seesaw, freely. Drag them to three shelf spots marked with small, middle and big block pictures, light to heavy; either end can be filled first. A wrong spot boings and sends the present back; two misses count one hint and glow the right spot for the present picked up. The demo records three visible pair results and orders from those results alone.

Level 15 *Guess the pumpkin* (pre-K/school): tap one of three numbers for a free guess, spoken back and kept on a sign. Measure the pumpkin (2–6) with six unit blocks, then choose its actual weight. The final pads concern the measurement; a wrong answer counts the blocks, and the second glows the measured answer. The pet compares guess and measurement aloud: just right, close (within one), or a bit more/less. The guess never changes misses or hints.

Level 16 *How much heavier?* (school): two fixed tagged weights (2–6) start tipped. Add unit blocks to the raised, lighter tray until level, then choose how many it took (difference 1–4). A heavy-side drop comes back with a spoken reminder, free; excess blocks and wrong final numbers miss, two bring help, and final support counts only the added blocks. Each new level has three puzzles and finishes through the ordinary sticker shell, including with help. Pre-K order is [3,4,14,5,6,7,15]; school [6,7,15,16,17,8,9,10,11].

Level 17 *Which way will it tip?* (school): two tagged weights on each tray stay flat on a visible prop. Tap either tray/down arrow or the middle equal sign to predict. The prop releases, the springy seesaw shows the result, and the pet adds both pairs aloud and explains the comparison. Totals are at most eight; each round has left-down, right-down and equal cases in seeded order. All predictions are unscored, including a different outcome. The green Continue arrow waits for the explanation and advances once; the third Continue completes through the ordinary sticker shell. No discovery entry is filed.
