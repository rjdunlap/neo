## Design notes

*grown-up, on the island by touch and on the couch · after the classic word-search puzzle.* A grid of letters hides the words of a themed list ("By the pond", "In the garden", "The weather"). **By touch (the island):** tap a word's first letter (it lights at once), then its last letter to check the line; tapping the same letter again lets go, and tapping a letter the mark does not run straight to moves the first letter there, free. On a grid that is all in view (the 6 by 6 level 5 and the 8 by 8) a finger can also drag along the word. Every letter's square is at least 64 logical units, so a grid that does not fit beside the list (10 by 10 and up on the smallest view; the 14 by 14 even on a tall one) sits in a window and **slides** under a drag (a short touch is a tap, a longer one slides; soft strips with an arrow show where more grid is), and a hint slides the ringed letter into view. **Hint** and **Start over** are buttons (Start over clears the words found; guesses already made still count). A wrong line is a miss and a hint counts as help. **On the couch:** the controls below. the stick or D-pad moves (hold to repeat); the bottom button marks a word's first letter, and with the cursor on its last letter checks the line, a pale capsule showing the line being marked; the left button lets go of a mark. A word reads either way, so marking from the last letter to the first works. A found word keeps a colored band across the grid, its list entry is struck through and its dot takes the band's color, and the word is spoken. A mark that is not a straight line (across, down or on a slant) cannot be checked: it is neither right nor wrong and costs nothing.

**Score and help.** Every checked line is a guess, right or wrong; letting go of a mark and a crooked mark are free. The fewest is the number of words, so a perfect grid has no wrong guess and the score is guesses over par (a wrong guess counts as a miss for adaptation and is spoken about once a grid). The pause menu has **Show a hint** (marks a challenge run as helped; it rings the first letter of the word nearest the cursor and names it) and **Search this grid again** (forgets what was found; guesses already made keep counting).

**The rules** are in `logic.ts`, the words in `words.ts` and the block list in `blocked.ts`. `makeGrid` places each of a plan's words (longest first) somewhere it fits, sometimes crossing one already there, in the directions the plan allows, then fills the rest with letters and redraws only the filler until every listed word occurs exactly once in the grid, read in all eight directions, and none of the blocked words can be read anywhere (a 14 by 14 grid read in eight directions would otherwise show a short word by accident about one time in five). A test fails if a theme word contains a blocked word, forwards or backwards. Fifteen everyday themes plus two root-family themes (*Words that carry*, *Words that look*; levels 1–2 only, and the pet says the root's meaning once, on the first word found); no theme word contains another and RAIN is not in weather. Four levels: 8 by 8 with five words across and down, 10 by 10 with seven including slants, 12 by 12 with nine in every direction (so some read backwards), 14 by 14 with twelve; a seed picks the theme. Rule tests assert that every level hides exactly its words exactly once in the directions it allows, that backwards words only appear where allowed, the exact meaning of a line between two squares, a word reading both ways and a found word no longer matching, the hint's nearest-first-letter rule, and that the same seed makes the same grid.

**Level 5, A small grid to start** (added 2026-11-04; grown-up entry, `order` `[5, 1, 2, 3, 4]`, `entryLevels: [5]`): a 6 by 6 grid of four words of three to six letters, across and down only, always all in view so a drag works. Squares are 98 units at 1024 by 768 (the word list narrows from 262 to 222 for it) and 132 at 1024 by 1365, never under 96 or over 132. Rules, misses and hints are level 1's; a root-family theme can be drawn (*Words that carry*). A level past the top plays level 4, not this one. The couch trip and courses are unchanged.

**Level 6, A message in the leftovers** (added 2026-11-05; `order` `[5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6]`): an 8 by 8 grid of nine words across, down and down-right, with up to eight pebble **stones** (`#`) that no word uses. The letters left over, read in order, spell a short themed sentence (3-4 authored per theme, 13-24 letters, none holding a theme or blocked word); a row of blanks under the grid shows its length. A line through a stone is never a word. When the last word is found the leftover squares light in order, fill the blanks, and the pet says "The letters left over say: ..." (device speech). Rules, misses and hints are level 2's; the how-to demo grid has no message. The couch trip and courses are unchanged (levels 1-5 grids are identical).

**Level 7, Just the theme** (added 2026-11-05; `order` `[5, 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7]`, `planFor` past the top plays 7): a 10 by 10 grid of six hidden words in every direction. The list shows only the theme title and a blank per word with its length ("____ (4)"; long blanks tighten to fit the column); found words fill their blanks. A checked line that spells no hidden word is **free**, not a miss (`wordsearch.free`, spoken once a grid), and on this level only marking a word already found again costs nothing (levels 1-6 still count that a miss). Free lines and repeats are not counted as couch guesses. **Hint** is two steps on the shortest unfound word: the first fills its first letter into the blank (`wordsearch.hidden-letter`), the second rings its start in the grid (`wordsearch.hidden-ring`); each counts as a hint, neither names the word, and a fully hinted word that is still unfound is ringed again. Generation (`strayThemeWord`) rejects any theme word that is not hidden but can be read in the grid, and root-family themes are not drawn. Instruction lines `wordsearch.go-hidden` and `go-hidden-keys`; the new lines use device speech. The how-to card's slide step covers 2 to 7, a level-7-only step explains the blanks, and the hint step is split 1-6 and 7. The couch trip and courses are unchanged.

**Level 8, School opening** (added 2026-11-05; `school` band added, school plays only this level; `entryLevels` `[5, 8]`; `PICTURE_GRID`, appended as the eighth plan): a 6 by 6 grid with four picture words across, at most one a row, three or four letters, from three picture themes in `words.ts` (`PICTURE_THEMES`: Animal friends, By the pond, Look outside; 18 words, kept out of `THEMES`), no other picture word readable in the grid. Squares, picture tiles and buttons are 100 units; the list is a column of picture tiles (210 wide) beside the grid in landscape and a row beneath it in portrait; pictures come from Pond Typing's `typingPicture`, and unfound tiles are pale green because the hen and rain pictures are white. A tap on a tile is free and says the word (`wordsearch.word`); a found word's band, hop and spoken word are as before. Every school miss says `wordsearch.miss-school`; from the second miss on a grid the nearest unfound word's start is ringed and its tile turns yellow, which does not count as a hint (the ring repeats on each later miss, its spoken hint only when the ringed word changes); **Hint** still counts. Instruction `wordsearch.go-school`. Home stays Lantern Lake and Wonder Woods lists it by subject; grown-up levels 1-7, the couch and the frozen course grids are unchanged. The how-to card has two steps and a note scoped to level 8. Not built: a second school level (across and down, five words), lowercase, more picture themes.

**Couch.** A trip stop (face-off `twin`: each plays their own seeded grid, scored in guesses over the words) plays levels 1 to 3. Two puzzle-shelf courses of frozen grids: **Pond Words** (pond, garden and weather; 7 + 9 + 9 words, par 25) and **The Big Hunt** (a sea grid and a cozy grid on 14 by 14, 12 words each, par 24); `course.ts` holds them with a `version`, and tests assert each word is in its grid exactly once and from its theme, no blocked word can be read, and the big grids use backwards words. It opens in the last tier of trips and is on the shelf from the start. Not built: Letter Pond, typed answers, her own word lists, a timer (none is wanted).
