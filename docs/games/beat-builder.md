## Design notes

*After Chrome Music Lab's Rhythm and step sequencers.* Toddlers play level 1 with a gentler Done: the green check appears once one square has sounded all the way round, a one-sound beat is a whole beat, and turning every square off takes the check away again (no misses, no hints). Level 9 shows three cards (circle, square, circle returns): turn the cards to bring the first part back, edit the parts if you like, hear the song, then check. Level 8 makes a piece of two parts (circle and square): make one, copy it, change the copy, hear both, then check. Level 7 locks the drum and asks her to move the claps to match a heard target (a clap-only replay button helps). Level 6 (mend the beat) starts a copy that differs from a small model in one square; add or remove it, then check. A step-sequencer grid with a looping playhead: rows are drum, clap and bell, columns are 4 or 8 steps. Free play reveals a green check with at least three active squares and two playhead returns to the start since the last edit; one composition finishes with that check. Visible copying completes three exact matches automatically; ear copying uses replay/check for three matches (two on the longer three-row grid); repeating completes two exact matches automatically. The how-to states these controls and counts separately for each of the five modes. Copying a visible beat leaves wrong squares off as gentle misses; copying by ear has free two-loop replay and a check. Editing/checking waits until the replay finishes; tapping the note again restarts it freely. Two wrong checks show glowing plus marks for missing sounds and minus marks for extra sounds; these update after each edit. Repeat levels give the first half and ask for the second to match, with missing-sound marks after two misses. Help counts once per target; the ghost finger can add or remove sounds. Bell taps and loop playback use the same pitch for each column. Targets at levels 2–5 come from twelve named four-step drum/clap phrases and eight named eight-step drum/clap/bell phrases, selected with the round seed without replacement. Level 4 repeats one short phrase twice. Each nickname is spoken before play; no reading is needed. Every row has sound and rests, the drum opens, and the long phrases never sound all three instruments together. Level 1 stays free composition, and sticker art uses its own unchanged seed. The woodland clearing frames the unchanged grid. A bear responds to the drum, a bunny to the clap and (on the three-row grid) a cat to the bell, during square previews and target/player playback. Trees, the wooden stage and friends take no touches. The game uses a near-silent `quiet` music style so the child's beat is the music.
