## Design notes

*After ScratchJr-style sequencing and the Kidbots tile idea, with no coding claims; [definition](../definitions/cue-studio.md).* The saved companion stands on a stage above a wooden shelf of one slot (level 1) or two (levels 2–3). Two action cards sit in a palette: Wave (a hand) and Turn (a circle arrow). She taps a card to fill the first empty slot; she can tap an occupied slot (it shows an arrow and a dark outline) and then a card to replace it. A full shelf with nothing selected changes nothing and the pet says "Choose a place to change". Undo takes back the last change, one step. Play runs the show left to right on the pet (Wave is a mitten raised and lowered, Turn a full spin), each slot glowing with a lit trace dot as it acts; Step runs one move per press. At creative levels 1–2, Finish (a blue tick) appears only after a complete run of the current show, and any edit needs a new run.

At pre-K levels 1–2, misses are always 0, since a show is free creation; empty Play or Finish and edits that change nothing are inert, not wrong. Help (the bulb) first offers a Wave in the first empty slot (one hint per slot, never twice even after Undo or restart), then plays a full unwatched show (one hint, once), then points at Finish for free. Bands: pre-K 1–2, school 3–3 (decided 2026-10-23; widened to 3–4 with level 4 and 3–5 with level 5 and 3–6 with level 6). At level 3, the pictured request is Wave then Turn, starting from Turn then Wave. Play and Step are free experiments, with separate requested and actual traces. Only a full wrong Check counts a miss; two wrong Checks supply the earliest mismatch clue (one hint). Help gives a clue, then repairs that slot and demonstrates it, then repairs the next mismatch; assistance identities survive Undo and Start again, so the same clue/repair is never charged twice. Only a successful Check enables Finish; an edit or Undo clears it. Start again cancels a running Check. Programs are not saved; no couch play. Layout deviations from the definition: the landscape palette sits 32 units further left (x 96 and 224), and the portrait palette is at y 600 with the controls at y 748, so they clear the shelf. The ghost-finger bot plays level 1 (Wave, Play, Finish) and level 2 (Wave, Turn, Play, select slot 1, Turn, Play, Finish); at level 3 it watches the swapped show, repairs each slot, then presses Check and Finish. Music: the quiet style. Co-play: ask her what the pet will do before pressing Play, watch together, and take turns choosing a move. Off-screen: take turns giving each other a two-move show to act out ("wave, then turn around"), then swap the order.

Level 4, Which signal? (school; school range is now 3–4, entry 3, ordinary step rule): two signal rows, a bell and a flower, each a request picture (signal over move), a signal pad, one action slot and a non-interactive result chip. She fills each row from the Wave/Turn palette and fires either signal freely in any order; an empty row shrugs ("Nothing is set for the bell yet") with no miss or hint. The seed picks the request: bell Wave and flower Turn, or the reverse. Both rows start empty, and Play and Step are hidden. Check needs both rows filled, fires bell then flower, accepts each row by its own signal and marks each chip with a tick or cross; only a wrong Check is a miss. Two wrong Checks or Help give the clue (one hint); each later Help sets one wrong row (one hint each, counted once across Undo and Start again). Help identities are `clue:`/`repair:` plus `slot:<n>` (level 3) or `row:bell`/`row:flower` (level 4). One board is drawn behind both rows; a chip keeps what its row last did after an edit but drops its tick or cross. The Undo icon is the shared `undoIcon` in `src/ui/icons.ts`. Voice lines read "When the bell rings, wave." (an event starts an action); the wording was checked 2026-11-16 against CS Unplugged Kidbots, which starts a program on a spoken signal (see the level 5 note).

Level 5, A repeated phrase (school; range now 3–5, entry 3, ordinary step rule): a two-slot shelf with a Repeat cap (a loop arrow around two dots) at its right end; the shelf starts empty with Repeat off. With Repeat on, a dark bracket wraps both slots, two faint ghost copies of the slots stand after the cap (scenery; the acting copy brightens on the second pass) and the body plays exactly twice; off, it plays once. The request is one wide tappable strip showing the four-move trace as two bracketed pairs (the seed picks Wave, Turn or Turn, Wave twice); tapping it speaks "Wave, turn. Then wave, turn again." Each slot has two trace dots, one per pass, and Step advances one executed move (four presses for a repeated show). A body with an empty slot is incomplete: Play performs its filled move once and Check only points at the empty slot, with no miss. Toggling the cap is an edit: Undo restores Repeat with the program, and it clears a run and a successful Check. Check compares the played trace with the four-move request: the right pair with Repeat off fails at the cap ("Your show played once. The picture asks for it twice."), and a wrong body fails at the first wrong slot with first/second wording. One miss per failed Check; two failed Checks bring the clue (one hint). Help gives the clue for the earliest mismatch, then each press fills or repairs one slot with the cap last (it turns Repeat on and plays the show twice through); identities `clue:<target>` and `repair:<target>` (targets `slot:0`, `slot:1`, `repeat`) are charged once across Undo and Start again; then Help points at Check, then Finish. The pet moves right to centre x 880, keeping its built height, so the ghosts fit beneath it at x 772–976. The how-to card and ghost finger run the phrase's two moves, Repeat, Play, Check, Finish. Voice wording ("Repeat two times", "plays them again", "When the bell rings, wave.") was read 2026-11-16 against CS Unplugged Kidbots "Fitness unplugged" (a loop for ages 5–7 as "whatever is in the hula hoop will be repeated"; a program starts on a spoken signal) and the Scratch Wiki Repeat block page; this is a session source check, not an educator review.

Level 6, Pass the cue (school; range now 3–6, entry 3, ordinary step rule): two rows. The pet's row has two slots and a three-card palette (Wave, Turn, and Send, a leaf with an arrow); the bird's row has a receiver tile that toggles leaf or shell and a fixed picture of the fan. A code-drawn teal bird stands under the pet in the right-hand column. Send flies one leaf to the bird, and delivery is synchronous: the fan opens fully before the pet's next command. A leaf that meets a shell receiver lands beside the bird and fades, with no miss. The request is the trace Wave, Send, then the fan opens; the tagged trace (pet wave, pet turn, pet send, bird fan) must equal it. Numbered order badges sit on the pet slots and the fan picture, and the receiver tile pulses; "nobody listening" shows as an empty ring with a dash on the receiver tile. Two seeded starts: A = Wave, Send with a shell (one repair: the receiver); B = Send, Wave with a leaf (two repairs, one per slot). Play and Step are free experiments (Step runs one command, with the delivery inside the Send's step); only a wrong Check is a miss, and two wrong Checks or Help give a clue (one hint). Help targets are `slot:0`, `slot:1` and `receiver`, each charged once across Undo and Start again; the receiver repair sets the leaf and plays the show. Everything is a rule in `logic.ts`; the [definition](../definitions/cue-studio-p4.md) records where the build differs from the spec.
