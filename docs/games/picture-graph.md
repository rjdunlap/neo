## Design notes

*After classroom picture graphs and tally charts.* Critters play in a meadow; the child builds a bar graph below by tapping above a bar to add a block (tapping the bar takes one away), then checks it. A wrong bar is a miss ("count the ducks again"); two make that kind's critters glow. Then questions about the graph she built: which has the most and the fewest (tap a bar), how many more of one than another (number pads), and a finished graph to read (how many in all; which two bars are the same). Counts never tie where "most" or "fewest" is asked (rule-tested). Two more levels add a **key**: one block stands for two critters, shown on a card ("block = two dots"), on every row of the graph's side scale (2, 4, 6...) and in the meadow, where the critters stand in pairs. On level 5 she builds the bars from the pairs and then answers how many more of one kind than another; on level 6 the keyed graph is already built and she answers how many critters in all and how many more. Answers count critters, so the number of blocks (the answer read straight off the bars) is always among the choices, and picking it says "that is how many blocks, each block is two critters" (rule-tested; counts are whole numbers of blocks and no two kinds tie, so the difference is never nothing). A bar with one block per critter is a miss ("each block is two ducks; count the pairs again"). **School level 7** is one complete graph cycle: counts from four to seven make the group of five tally marks visible. Tap above a column to add a mark, or on/below it to take one back; a wrong tally check gently says the count and glows that column after two misses. Once the tallies match, she uses the meadow to build the ordinary one-block-per-critter bar graph and answers how many more. This order gives the tally notation a job before she reads a graph built from the same data. **Fair meadows and a visible first pick:** single critters (levels 1–3, 7) are placed apart by `groupSpots` above the graph, so none touch or stand on it; `bear` joins the kinds and the meadow backdrop is seeded from three. On "which two are the same?" the first tap lifts and outlines the bar and tapping it again puts it back, free (a quick double tap costs nothing); its hint glows the answer bar not yet lifted. Keyless "how many more" (levels 3, 7) always offers the taller bar's own count, with its line "That is how many ducks. How many more ducks than pigs? Look above the pigs' bar" (still a miss). After two misses on "how many more" a dashed line runs from the shorter bar's top across the taller bar and the blocks above it glow (counted aloud by twos with a key); on "how many in all" every block glows. The pad still glows, one hint. **Level 8, *Line them up*** (an easy entry: first in pre-K's order `[8, 1, 2]`, declared in `entryLevels`; school plays it after level 2): shells, stones, sticks (driftwood) and feathers lie on the sand, never litter, each in a 100-unit tap square. A tap hops one into its kind's row of the tray, where the slots line up one to one; lining up is never wrong. Then "which row has the most?" (tap the row) and, at school, how many more of one kind than another (number pads, always offering the longer row's count with its own line). Pre-K lines up two kinds, school three; counts 2–6, all different; two shores a round. A wrong row or number is a miss ("look at the rows again"); after two, dashed lines join the rows thing to thing and the things nothing matches glow (and the right pad). **School level 9, *A key of five*** (school order ends `[…, 7, 9]`): per graph, three tally cards (10–35 each, whole groups of five, distinct counts, at most 7 blocks) stand where the meadow was, and a key shows one block = one group of five tally marks. She builds the bars one block per group, checks, then answers how many more in critters. A wrong bar (for example one block per mark) is a miss ("count the groups of five again"); after two, that card's groups of five glow. The choices are the answer, the number of blocks and whole fives away; picking the blocks is a miss ("That is how many blocks. Each block is five critters!"), and the how-many-more hint counts the fives aloud (5, 10, 15). The bot is unchanged. **School level 10, *Two pictures, one meadow*** (school order ends `[…, 9, 10]`, max 10; scene `compare.ts`): a tally card (3 kinds, 2–8 each, whole pairs, all different) sits at the top with three finished graphs below it, each a whole tap target and each showing its key (one dot or two): a plain graph of the card, a graph keyed "block = two" and a plain trap whose bars are as tall as the keyed graph's. Tap a graph and it lifts with an outline; tap it again to lower it (free); tap another to make the pair. A wrong pair is a miss: the keyed graph with the trap says "just as tall, but look at the keys… count 2, 4, 6" (`graph.pair.key`), any other wrong pair "look again" (`graph.pair.look`). After two misses both right graphs glow and the keyed graph's first bar is counted aloud by twos with that bar and the card's first row glowing (one hint). Two cards a round, one sticker. How-to steps hold from level 10 (level-9 steps now end at 9). **School level 11, *What the graph can say*** (school order ends `[…, 10, 11]`, max 11; `claim` mode in the main graph class): a finished graph of three kinds (1–6 each, all different) with a picture card above it showing who the claim is about, and three 112-unit stamps on the right (green check yes, red cross no, purple little graph with a question mark "the graph can't say"). Each graph gets three claims, one yes, one no ("there are more {a} than {b}" and "there are the most {a}", one of each) and one the graph can't say, about the kind with the tallest bar: who came first, who is hungriest or who is sleepiest. (The dive's "biggest" and "fastest" were replaced because world knowledge decides size and speed, so a child answering from what she knows would take a miss for knowing something.) Two graphs a round, one sticker. A wrong stamp is a miss, the boing sounds and the named bars light with a line (`graph.claim.look` for yes or no, `graph.claim.can` when a claim could be answered, `graph.claim.cant.<about>`, for example "The graph shows how many critters, not who came first"); after two misses the right stamp glows (one hint). Co-play: the grown-up makes up claims and she stamps them. How-to steps hold from level 11 (level-10 steps now end at 10).
