## Design notes

*grown-up, on the island by touch and on the couch · after Picross and Cross Pix, built on Pixel Pictures' logic levels.* A grid hides a picture. The numbers beside each row and above each column say how long the runs of filled squares in that line are, in order. Fill what the numbers allow, cross out what they rule out, and when the filled squares are exactly the picture's, its squares take their colors and it says what it is ("It is a sailboat!"). **By touch (the island):** touch a square to fill it (a filled one empties); a drag along a row or column paints a run, taking the squares between however fast the finger moves, the first square deciding whether the stroke fills or empties. Three tools sit beside the picture (under it on a tall view): **Fill**, **Cross** (marks a square, lifts a cross) and, when the picture is bigger than its window, **Slide**; **Hint** and **Start over** are buttons. Every square and button is at least 64 logical units, so the 10 by 10 pictures slide a little and the 15 by 15 ones a lot on a landscape tablet (an upright tablet shows a 10 by 10 whole): the clue numbers stay pinned along the top and left while the squares move under them; Slide, or a drag on the clue strips, moves the picture, soft strips with an arrow show where there is more, a painting finger held near the window's edge carries the picture along under it, and a hint switches to the tool it needs and slides its square into view. When the picture is done it shrinks to show all of itself before it takes its colors. **On the couch:** the stick or D-pad moves (hold to repeat) and the cursor's row and column are tinted so a far-off number is easy to follow; the bottom button fills the square (on a filled one it empties it); the left button crosses a square out (on a crossed one it lifts the cross); and **holding** either button while the stick moves paints a whole run, the first square deciding what the stroke does. A line's clue turns grey when its filled runs match it and red when no way of filling it fits the marks, judged from the clue alone and never from the answer.

**Score and help.** Every fill counts, and a filled square that is emptied and filled again counts twice; crosses, lifting them and emptying are free. The fewest is the picture's filled squares, so the score is fills over par (a filled square the picture does not have is never refused, shown or counted as a miss, decided 2026-10-23; only hints adapt the level). The pause menu has **Show a hint** (marks a challenge run as helped) and **Empty this picture** (fills already made keep counting). A hint names a mark that cannot be right first (a filled square outside the picture, then a cross inside it), otherwise a square that one line decides from what she has marked rightly, preferring a fill to a cross and the square nearest the cursor: *"In row 4, the clue 6, 2 leaves only one way to fit. The ringed square must be filled."* with the line outlined and the sentence written beside the grid. Fill and cross hints also say why, from the line's clue and what is known (`whyFor`): the line already has its runs (or its clue is nothing), the clue and its gaps fill the whole line, a long run overlaps the middle of an empty line, or the old "only one way to fit" sentence; each has its own spoken line.

**The rules** are in `logic.ts`, the drawings in `pictures.ts`. `deductions` finds every square one line decides on its own: the squares every way of fitting the clue to what is known agrees on (it reuses Pixel Pictures' `placements`), so a hint is exactly what a person doing line logic would find next. A picture is accepted only if line logic alone solves it (`solvableByLines`), which also makes its solution unique. `passesNeeded` counts the rounds of "every line, once" a picture takes, which is how the gentler and harder 10 by 10 pictures are told apart (three passes or fewer is the easier bank). Seven levels, played in the order 5, 1, 2, 3, 6, 4, 7 (the grown-up range is 1–7): level 5 is a small 5 by 5 to start (Pixel Pictures' seven 5 by 5 pictures, each solved by line logic in three passes or fewer from empty; 96-unit squares, the whole picture in view, no Slide) and is where she starts; level 7 is a 15 by 15 from a harder bank (`HARD_15`: a kite, a crab, an umbrella, a rabbit and a penguin, needing 9, 7, 8, 7 and 10 passes, each more than any easier 15 by 15, whose most is 5). **Level 6, Two colours** (added 2026-11-04) is five 10 by 10 pictures in blue plus one warm colour (`TWO_COLOUR_10`: the sun over the sea, a duck on the pond, a hot-air balloon, a boat on the waves, a bluebird), each solved by Pixel Pictures' `colorLineSolvable`. By touch, Fill becomes two colour pots named for their colours (Blue, and Yellow or Orange): a pot paints over the other colour and empties its own square; clue numbers are drawn in their run's colour; a done line fades and an impossible one is outlined red; runs of different colours may touch. A wrong-colour fill is not a miss; the hint names it first ("is the other colour… make it blue") and switches to the right pot, and colour hints name the colour and the clue ("3 blue, 2 orange") with the reasons done, full and fits (no overlap reason on colour lines). A controller's fill button steps a square empty, first colour, second colour, empty (live controller only; there is no pot switch by keys and the colour hint lines do not say how to reach the second colour by keys, open and minor). The intro suggests trying one colour first. Accounting is unchanged (only hints adapt). The older four: a 6 by 6 warm-up drawn from the pictures Pixel Pictures can set as logic puzzles, an easier and a harder 10 by 10 level, and a 15 by 15 level; a seed picks the picture, from eight easier and eight harder 10 by 10 pictures (cup, crown and bell join the easier; snail, flower and whale the harder; heart and tree sit in the harder bank and cat and fish in the easier by measured passes) and five 15 by 15 (a snowman and an owl are new). Rule tests assert every picture is square, in palette letters and named, solvable by line logic and neither nearly empty nor nearly full, that the harder bank needs more passes than the easier, that every deduction matches the picture from many partly-solved states, that a player who follows every hint from an empty grid finishes every picture with exactly its par in fills (never a wrong one), that a wrong mark is named first, and the exact rules of each brush.

**Couch.** A trip stop (face-off `twin`: each plays their own seeded picture, scored in fills over the picture's squares) plays the 6 by 6 and 10 by 10 levels (levels 5, 6 and 7 are touch only; the couch trip and courses are unchanged); the 15 by 15 pictures are the course. Two puzzle-shelf courses of frozen pictures: **Pond Pictures** (a sailboat, a cat and a duck; par 46 + 76 + 60 = 182) and **The Big Pictures** (a lighthouse and a butterfly; par 102 + 128 = 230); `course.ts` holds them with a `version`, and a test pins each picture's clues so an edit without a version bump fails. It opens in the last tier of trips and is on the shelf from the start. Not built: a replay of the best way, pictures she draws, a 20 by 20 size.
