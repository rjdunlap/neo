## Design notes

*After museum layer-matching activities and the NPS/AMNH fossil-record work; [definition](../definitions/lost-layers.md).* Two sea cliffs stand side by side. Gull Cliff is a whole record of six layers, a fossil picture in some of them. Shell Cove is a broken one: three rock pieces with a wavy line where the sea took rock away. She drags a Shell Cove piece (any of the three can move) beside Gull Cliff; each fossil tie goes green when the twin matches and red when it does not, live, and a wavy line must not be squeezed. It fits stamps a story card ("in the mud" or "washed away"). Level 1 *Two stories*: two arrangements can fit; the stamped cards are themselves the claim buttons, and Can't tell appears after the first card and finishes once both are stamped. Level 2 *One more look*: both cards start stamped with the piece on the lower fit; three look cards sit left of Gull Cliff (a tap previews, free), one is dragged into the slot at the top of the right column and Look replaces It fits; only the mud look (a dark stripe or not) separates the stories. She then claims the story that survives.

Misses: level 1, It fits with anything red, and claiming one story while the other also fits (the other fit is then shown and stamped with help); Can't tell with one card is free. Level 2, a useless look, and after the look Can't tell or the ruled-out story; Can't tell or a story claim before the look is free ("not yet"). Two misses on a step (arrange, look, claim) glow its answer, one hint per step; "Show a hint" (the pause sheet's label) counts at most once per step, and Start again keeps misses and hints. Four invented creatures, two layouts, nothing saved or filed in the journal; no couch play. Layout deviations from the definition: the story cards are the claim buttons (no separate buttons), cards are 200x110, the level-2 right column stacks slot, Look, cards and Can't tell, and the look cards sit left of Gull Cliff. The ghost-finger bot plays both levels. Music: bubbles style. Co-play: one slides the pieces, the other says which fossils match; at the end say what the rock can and cannot tell. Off-screen: lay two strips of striped paper side by side with a torn gap and ask "what could be missing?"
