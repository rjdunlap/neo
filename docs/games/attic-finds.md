## Design notes

*After museum "mystery object" handling sessions and Tinybop Simple Machines; [definition](../definitions/attic-finds.md).* An invented attic thing (a bird box or a fish churn) sits on a rug. Tapping a part opens a magnifier with the pet's spoken line (the nearest of the object's three spots wins, and a looked part gets a tick); Turn flips the thing to its back, where a rod links the crank to the bird or fish; a tap on the crank (or its handle, which also opens the handle clue) turns it one full turn of four clicks and the bird or fish bobs. Level 1 *Try it*: two pictured cards (bob, roll); the first card tap or a crank is a free prediction or trial, then "Which one happened?" asks for a pick. A wrong pick boings and replays the effect; two wrong picks glow the right card (one hint); level 1 never records a miss. Level 2 *Show the clue*: a claim card ("the crank makes it bob"), a slot and Check; three clue chips show "?" until looked at (tapping a "?" chip opens its part, turning the thing if needed), and a looked chip is tapped or dragged into the slot. A Check on a part that does not show it is a miss (the part flashes, the chip returns, the pet explains); two misses glow the rod chip and Turn (one hint). Check with an empty slot is free. Two objects per round, the second is not the first; both shelved ends the round with one sticker. Preschool 1, pre-K 1–2, school 2. **Levels 3 *Up and down* and 4 *Peek inside*** ([definition](../definitions/lid-and-lever.md)) open the game to lap (level 3) and toddler (3–4, `entryLevels` [3, 4]): one thing with a giant handle (level 4 adds a 220-unit-wide peek door on the front). Each touch on the handle turns it half a turn and the bird or fish rises, the next touch sinks it back; touches during a turn queue one more turn. Level 4's door opens and closes freely to show the rod the handle pushes. A Finish picture appears after the third turn and only her tap ends the round, with one sticker; free play continues until then. Both levels count 0 misses and 0 hints and nothing is kept. **Levels 5 *Same job* (pre-K 1–5, school 2–6; [definition](../definitions/same-job.md)) and 6 *Right for the job* (school)** add a rug of three old things and a load for a friend: level 5 has two jobs (leaves: basket, tray, hoop; acorns: basket, pail, tray), level 6 three (leaves, acorns, water with basket, tray, pail). A tap on a thing tries it on the load for free: it holds, or the load falls through, rolls off, sloshes or drips, and a magnifier shows the deciding part. She drags every thing that can do the job into a three-slot cart and taps Check. A Check with a failing thing is one miss (the thing hops back to the rug); a Check that leaves out a working thing is not a miss (the pet asks for one more). After two misses on a job the working things glow (one hint, counted at most once per job together with Show a hint). Nothing is saved; the loads stay on the shelf for the round. Deviations from the definition: a try animates where the thing sits (the corner pet does not walk it to the friend); tapping a thing in the cart puts it back on the rug (it is not tried there); the context line is spoken at the start and the pet's repeatable instruction is the current job line. Skills add comparing. Pre-K and school now start at 1 and 2 with orders [1,2,5] and [2,5,6]. **Levels 7 *Two sources* and 8 *What can we tell?* (school only; [definition](../definitions/household-casebook.md))** open one invented bird-box case: the museum wants to borrow the bird box, and three papers lie on the attic table. At level 7 she drags a copy of the paper that shows each claim into its slot and taps Check; a wrong Check is one miss (both copies hop back, the pet says why), while a free read of a paper, moving a copy around and an empty Check are not misses. At level 8 each claim gets a stamp, *The papers show it* or *Can't tell*; changing a stamp is free, an empty Check is free, and a wrong Check is one miss. *Can't tell* is right when no paper shows the claim ("no record is not proof": no paper from a house is no proof it had no bird box), and the poster is advertising, not evidence. After two misses on a board the right answers glow (one hint, counted once per board). A right row at level 8 speaks "The papers show it!" or "None of the papers shows it. Can't tell!". School now runs 2–8 with order [2,5,6,7,8]. The how-to card has steps for 5–8. Open: a resize during a Check leaves the papers at their old positions until the animation ends. Level 9, *Mend the exhibit* (school only; scene in `exhibit.ts`, model and layout in `cases.ts`): the museum card has three captions that claim more than the papers show (the first round is Seaside: love, cranking, homes). She taps a row to open its caption strip of three cards and picks one the papers show, then taps one of three new papers (the useful house paper, the other house's paper, one copy) into the question slot, and taps Check. An empty question slot is never a miss; any wrong Check is one miss (wrong captions stay and the first one's strip reopens; a paper that could not answer hops back); after two misses the answer glows (one hint). A strip stays open across a resize or turn. School now runs 2–9 with order [2,5,6,7,8,9]. The how-to card has steps for 5–9. The pause sheet's "Show a hint" cranks and names the effect at level 1 and glows at level 2 (once per object). Start again rebuilds the same round and keeps its misses and hints. After 6 s idle the crank wiggles once (an invitation, not a hint). Not built: the definition's "rub along it" crank gesture (a tap suffices) and the tap-then-slot-tap for a looked chip (one tap puts it in the slot). Nothing is saved, no journal discovery, nothing real; no couch play. The ghost-finger bot plays both levels, and `fingerdemo` is clean at level 9 too (its first level 1 trial is deliberately the wrong picture). Music is `STYLES.paint`. Co-play: one cranks while the other finds the clue; at level 2 both say which part they would pick before Check. Off-screen: look at one ordinary thing at home with a handle or a lid and say what it is for ("I don't know" is fine).
