**Starts as:** read a color word (five colours) and choose a hat; tap words or the speaker for free spoken support

**Grows into:** big/small phrases on a seeded animal, then four sentences for one of two animals whose animal and action both matter; wrong choices act out their meaning; school and pre-K also get signs to make ready for a reader: toggle the first word's capital or stamp the full stop, then Send (levels 4–6); school also drags word tiles into a sign so animals act a friend's wish (level 7); mend a broken sign that acts itself out by one insert or take-out (level 8) or one swap (level 9)

## Design notes

*From the gap-review Read & Do sketch.* Read a printed sign, then tap a picture to bring its meaning into the scene. Level 1 draws three different answer colours from red, blue, yellow, green and pink, each round with the answer and two other seeded hats. Level 2 has three rounds: big, small and a seeded third size, each on a different seeded animal from duck, cat, pig and dog; both pictures show that animal, so only the size word decides. School level 3 plays all four sentences (jump or sleep for each of a seeded pair of the four animals): four picture cards use an upward arrow for jump and a Z for sleep, so both the animal and verb matter. Each pool and its choice positions are shuffled with the round's seeded RNG; no sign is automatically spoken or decorated with its answer. Every printed word is an unscaled 108-by-100 target, and a separate speaker replays the sign word by word. These listening supports are free and do not count as hints. A wrong choice acts out its meaning and names it, then re-reads the sign; each wrong choice is one miss and the second on that sign adds one glow hint. The hint remains through retries without further hint charges. Complete the pool for one shell-awarded sticker, including with help. The vocabulary is 15 words; the voice lines for green, pink, pig and dog use device speech (no clips rendered). Story Grove is its home land. Co-play: read the sign together and let the child choose; off-screen: write a short sign to match a toy or drawing. Listening-supported completion is not independent reading evidence; actual device speech and child comprehension remain unjudged. Levels 4–6 (*Capital and stop*) ask whether a sign is ready for a reader. Each round shows three seeded signs, drawn from the eight *the + duck/cat/pig/dog + jumps/sleeps* clauses (three different animals, both verbs). Level 4 *Start the sign* (pre-K, school) prints and locks the full stop; a tap on the first word toggles the/The (size and case change), then Send. Level 5 *Finish the sign* (pre-K, school) prints and locks the capital; a 100-unit full-stop stamp is dragged onto the dotted end socket, or tapped and then the socket tapped, and tapping a placed stop returns it. Level 6 *Ready for a reader* (school) needs both, in either order. Send checks only that level's convention: a failed Send is one miss and the pet names where a sentence starts or stops (the capital first at level 6), and two failed Sends on a sign glow the needed control as one hint (the pause sheet's "Show a hint" does the same, one hint per sign). Show it (the animal acts the sign as it stands), the speaker (reads the words and says "Full stop." if one is placed), toggling and moving the stop are free; there is no do-it-for-me. One sticker per round, including with help. Pre-K plays levels 1, 2, 4 and 5 (`order` [1, 2, 4, 5]) and school plays 1–7. Voice lines `read.jumps`, `read.sleeps`, `read.cap/stop/both.instruction`, `read.cap/stop.missing`, `read.ready` and `read.stamp` use device speech (no clips rendered); the six levels' proof is listening-supported conventions practice, and whether a pre-K child understands "first word" and "end of the sentence" is unjudged. Level 7 (school), *Make the sign*: a pictured wish card shows what a friend wants the animals to do (never spoken), and the sign reads a locked *The*, an empty row and a locked stop. She drags word tiles (big, small, the stage animal, a decoy animal, jumps, sleeps) into the row; inserting, moving and taking a tile out are free, and a tile tap speaks its word (a tap is judged by finger travel under 30 units; tiles wait until the instruction ends). Show it acts the sign as it stands; Send judges `act()` (act, *which*, *none* or *unreadable*) against the wish. Three seeded signs per round: one for a lone animal, then two with a big and a small animal on stage where a size word is needed. A failed non-empty Send is one miss with a reaction and a spoken reason; two on a sign glow the next tile of the shortest accepted sign (one hint); an empty Send is free. Show it and Send sit in a right-hand 150-by-100 column at this level only. One sticker per round, including with help. Levels 8–9 (school), *Mend the sign*: three authored broken signs per round (level 8: an insert I1 first, then a delete D1–D3, then I2/I3 or another delete; level 9: S1–S3, a wrong action, animal or size) are acted out first by the animals (free: `read.mend.watch`, then a read-back and the act-out), and she fixes the sign with the level 7 tray. Level 8 needs one insert or one take-out; level 9 a swap, dropping a tray tile on a slot word (a held preview dims and wobbles the target; `resolveDrop` uses 27-in/40-out hysteresis on rest centres; take-out then insert also works). A failed Send is one miss with a spoken reason, two on a sign glow the next step (level 9: the tray tile and the target word) as one hint, and the sign is never already right. One sticker per round, including with help. Connected stories remain a proposal. Voice lines `read.mend.watch/instruction/swap.instruction/hint.swap`; device speech and portrait comfort of the swap are unjudged. Device speech for the `read.make.*` lines and portrait comfort of the drag are unjudged.
