## Design notes

*After Cooking Mama and JumpStart's cafeteria.* The friends at the plates are creatures dealt from the round's seed (calm, then happy when served). At levels 1–4 the whole is a seeded sandwich or round pie (the pizza stays at level 9); a fair serve slides the pieces to the friends' mouths and they chew (a munch sound) before the round finishes. Cut one or two wholes into halves or quarters, move equal pieces between plates, then serve. Both halves and quarters are accepted when they share the wholes equally. A round-arrow control restores the wholes to try another cut. The school ladder then asks for a named fraction of one sandwich (a half, a quarter or three quarters; two quarters count as a half), halves an even picture recipe for one friend, then cuts one pizza into three equal slices for three friends. The earlier two recipe levels double two kinds of fruit; every recipe has undo. Only unsuccessful serving checks count as misses; cutting, arranging and undo are exploration. A missed serve says why: serving before the cut repeats the instruction, too few pieces for the friends (halves for four) says to cut more, an empty serve says nothing is on the plates yet, and unequal plates say to move a piece to a plate with less; two misses highlight a useful next action (the working cut before anything is cut, the recut button for too few pieces). A touch on the uncut food wiggles it and repeats the instruction (at most every 3 s, never a miss), and six seconds of quiet before the cut bounce the cut buttons. At the named-fraction level (7) a tappable fraction bubble over the plate shows and says the fraction asked for (`kitchen.fraction-ask`), and a fair serve counts the pieces aloud ("one quarter, two quarters, three quarters") before the round ends. The recipe levels now pick two of three fruits (banana joins the pool). **Level 10, *One for you, one for me*** (the lap band's only level, and toddler's first): the food is already cut into halves or quarters (seeded); she taps a piece on the tray and it hops to the plate with the fewest. There is no serve button, no miss and no hint: a touch off a piece only wiggles one, and when the pieces are dealt the friends eat and the round finishes. The Windy Picnic plays a Pet Kitchen round as its sandwich step (level 10 for lap, level 1 above). **Level 11, *Same or not the same?*** (preschool after 1, pre-K first): one sandwich, two friends, and a choice of cut, down the middle or a lopsided third and two-thirds (the lopsided side is seeded). Pieces now carry an area, so a serve is fair only when the plates match by area. Choosing the lopsided cut and moving its pieces is free exploration; serving one piece each of unequal area is a miss: the two pieces lift and overlay on the counter ("This one is bigger! That's not the same.") and return. The second miss glows the round arrow and says to cut down the middle (`kitchen.recut-other`). **Level 12, *Two pizzas, three friends*** (school, last in the order): two pizzas, three friends, cuts of halves, thirds or quarters. Only thirds share out (two slices each); halves or quarters leave a piece in the tray that nobody can have, which is a miss (a new `leftover` trouble, `kitchen.leftover`), and the second miss glows the round arrow. **Level 14, *Different shape, same share*** (pre-K after 4, school first): two sandwiches, two friends, and two cut pictures (down and across). Each sandwich takes its own cut, one tap cutting one sandwich: down makes two tall halves, across two wide halves, each half the same area. A plate with one tall and one wide half, or two alike, is fair. Serving while a sandwich is still whole is a miss ("One sandwich is still whole! Cut it too, then share the pieces.") and the second miss glows the cutting picture not used yet (one hint); unequal plates are the usual miss. After a fair serve one tall and one wide half lift and lie one over the other in the middle ("A tall half and a wide half. They cover the same sandwich. The same amount!", or "Two tall/wide halves. The same shape, and the same amount!" when both were cut the same way, which is accepted with no miss), then the friends eat. The round arrow puts both sandwiches back. The plates are visible from the start. **Level 13, *Who has more?*** (school, last in the order): one sandwich, two friends (never the pet; each is named aloud) with a shaded-picture bubble for what they asked: one half and one quarter, or three quarters and one quarter (seeded; the half-and-quarter pair is not guaranteed first because the game has no round counter). Cuts are halves or quarters. A serve that does not match both asks is a miss that names the plate ("The {friend} asked for {amount}. Count the pieces."); halves get the recut line, and the second miss glows the next useful control (recut, the unmet plate or the serve). One quarter may stay in the tray. After a right serve the pet asks "Who has more? Tap their plate.": a wrong plate is a miss and lays both servings one over the other at 0.7 opacity for 1.2 s ("Look! {more} is more than {less}") before they slide back; a second wrong plate glows the right one. Each stage charges at most one hint. **Journal:** the discovery journal files `pet-kitchen:half` (every half of a fair, non-lopsided halves cut served at the share and deal levels, including 14), `pet-kitchen:quarter` (any plate holding two quarters, as at 4, 7, 10 and 13) and `pet-kitchen:third` (every third served at 9 and 12) from any finished round that showed them; the lines are spoken from the journal card, not mid-round. **Level 15, *One cup, many ways*** (school, last in the order): a recipe card shows one cup (a cup and a strip of four shaded quarters; a tap replays "One cup. Four quarters make one whole cup."). Two scoop buttons, each a shape-marked strip (the half scoop two cells with one shaded, the quarter four cells with one shaded), pour into a jug with quarter marks and a bold blue one-cup line, saying "A half cup" or "A quarter cup"; the jug holds six quarters, and a scoop past the brim is not poured (a spoken line pointing to the back arrow, never a miss). The back arrow undoes the last scoop. The green arrow serves: exactly one cup in any order (two halves, four quarters, a half and two quarters) finishes with "Yes! {scoops} make one cup"; too little, too much or an empty jug is a miss with a spoken reason, and the second miss charges one hint and glows the useful step (the back arrow if over, the half scoop if two or more quarters short, the quarter scoop if one short). Pouring and undoing are free. The ghost finger fills the cup one of three seeded ways and undoes off-route pours. No journal entry for 15; the game gains the `measuring` skill. Band orders: pre-K `[11, 3, 4, 14, 5, 6]`, school `[14, 5, 6, 7, 8, 9, 12, 13, 15]`; 14 is an entry level.
