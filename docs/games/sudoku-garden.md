## Design notes

*grown-up, on the island by touch and on the couch · after sudoku, and Garden Rows grown up.* Fill a bed so every row, column and box holds each number once: a 4 by 4 bed to start (level 7, boxes two by two, ten squares to find; a new grown-up starts there and it needs only the number that fits), 6 by 6 beds (boxes two rows by three columns) and 9 by 9 beds. On a 4 by 4 bed the four tray tiles sit in the three-column tray, so the 4 is alone on a second row. A square holds a numbered blossom whose color is its number's, so a bed reads at a glance as well as by its digits; numbers given at the start are inked, numbers placed are blue, a number that repeats in its row, column or box turns red with its twin and a soft boing (spoken once a bed). A wrong number is never refused. **By touch (the island):** tap a square to choose it (a given number can be chosen too, to look along its row, column and box), then tap a number in the tray; **Marks** pencils the number instead, **Erase** takes a number or its marks out, **Hint** and **Start over** are buttons. Every square and tile is at least 64 logical units: the bed sits beside the tray, or under it on a tall view. A number that is wrong against the solution counts as a miss and a hint counts as help, so the island adapts on both; a wrong number also gets a gentle boing, a red ring on the square and the spoken `sudoku.wrong` line ("The 4 does not belong in that square…"), at most once every four seconds (a clashing wrong number still says the clash line first), on touch and on the couch alike; the how-to card's demonstration plays a bed with ten squares left. **Controls on the couch:** the stick or D-pad moves the cursor (hold to repeat); the bottom button on a square opens the number tray at the right of the bed (a tile per number with how many are left to place, and a pencil tile); in the tray the bottom button places the highlighted number or, on the pencil, switches marks on; the left button closes the tray, or takes a number (or its marks) back out of a square.

**Score and help.** Every number placed counts one entry (a replaced number costs another); taking one out and pencil marks are free; placing the number that is already there changes nothing. The fewest is the bed's empty squares, so a perfect bed is a bed with no mistaken or replaced number, and the score is the entries over par. The pause menu has **Show a hint** (marks a challenge run as helped for good) and **Start this bed again** (entries already made keep counting). A hint points at a number she placed that cannot be right first (a clashing one if there is one); otherwise at the next number the techniques below can place from where the bed stands, with its reason spoken and written above the bed: *only the 4 fits there* (a lone number), *the 4 has only one square left in this box* (an only place), or, on the hardest beds, the pair or pointing line that rules numbers out first, with its squares marked, or (level 8) the X-Wing, with the two lines lit, the four corners ringed and the target ringed. A square left with no number that fits turns orange and the game says one of her numbers has to come out.

**The rules** are in `logic.ts` and carry the tests. `search` counts solutions (a bed must have exactly one) and grows random full beds. `techniqueSolve` works a bed the way a person does, easiest step first: **lone** (only one number fits a square), **place** (a number has one square left in a row, column or box), **pair** (two squares in a house can only hold the same two numbers), **locked** (a number's squares in a house all lie along one line, or one box) and **xwing** (tier 4: a number with only two places in each of two rows, in the same two columns, or the same with rows and columns swapped, is ruled out of the rest of those columns; `Step.lines` holds the two lines). A bed's *grade* is the hardest technique it needs. `makePuzzle` takes numbers out of a random full bed in random order, keeping each removal only if the bed stays unique and workable, and throws a bed away unless it has exactly the plan's blanks and grade. Six levels: 6 by 6 with 14, then 21 empty squares (lone numbers only), 6 by 6 with 24 that needs an only place; 9 by 9 with 42 (lone), 52 (only places) and 55 (a pair or pointing line). A 6 by 6 bed almost never needs a pair, so it has no third rung. **Level 8, A 9 by 9 bed with a crossing pair of rows** (grown-up top level, added 2026-11-05; `LEVELS` grownup 1–8, `order` `[7, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 8]`, `entryLevels` `[7]`; `gradeOf` now 1–4): random grade-4 beds are only about 0.4% of generator attempts, so it draws from `WING_BEDS`, 12 beds grown once by `makePuzzle`'s own removal (55 blanks, grade 4) and checked by the solver in tests, and `shuffleBed` shows each a new way per round (digits relabelled, bands/stacks and rows/columns within them reordered, maybe transposed). The hint is `rules-out` via the X-Wing when its removals ruled another number out of the hinted square or the placed number out of its peers, otherwise the last removal step; the line is `sudoku.hint-xwing`. On about 70% of beds (29 of 40 seeds) a follow-the-hints player is told the X-Wing; on the rest a later pair or pointing line freed every square. Fill marks is offered here (9 by 9). Couch untouched.

**Level 9, A 6 by 6 bed of patches** (grown-up, added 2026-11-05; tier 2, 20 blanks; `LEVELS` grownup 1–9, `order` `[7, 1, 2, 3, 9, 4, 5, 6, 8]`, `entryLevels` `[7]`). The boxes are irregular six-square patches from eight authored `PATCH_LAYOUTS` (six connected patches of six squares, none a straight line, at most one plain 2 by 3, each checked to admit a full bed). The logic functions take a `Shape` (a `Size` or a `Geometry` from `patchGeometry(layout)`) and `Puzzle.patchwork` carries the geometry; patches are houses of kind `box` whose spoken word is `patch`, so the clash and hint lines say "patch". On patchwork beds the only-place step scans patches first, so a hint names a patch when one has a number with one place left (*in this patch, the 5 has only one square left*); a bed is never put through `shuffleBed`. The bed draws a 6-unit wood line along each edge between different patches and tints each patch's soil (green, yellow or brown at alpha 0.45; neighbours differ, cached per geometry). The instruction is `sudoku.go-patch` (`-touch`), the how-to card has a level 9 step and its goal says "row, column and box (or patch)", and the co-play hint mentions a patch. Courses and the couch trip are unchanged. Rule tests (49) assert layouts are valid, 300 seeds make unique beds with 20 blanks and grade 2, peers come from the patch, hints finish a bed and name a patch, the bot places right numbers and the short demonstration bed keeps its patches. Rule tests assert every plan makes exactly its blanks and grade over many seeds, one solution, every technique step sound against the solution (a placement is the solution's number; nothing ruled out is in it), and a follow-the-hints player always finishes, with and without a wrong number to start.

**Level 10, A 6 by 6 bed with sums in the cages** (grown-up, added 2026-11-05; tier 2, 6 givens and 30 blanks; `LEVELS` grownup 1–10, `order` `[7, 1, 2, 3, 9, 10, 4, 5, 6, 8]`, `entryLevels` `[7]`). Every square is in a dashed cage of 2–4 squares joined edge to edge with its sum in a small label; the numbers in a cage add up to the sum and never repeat in it, on top of row, column and box. Cages ride on the `Geometry` (`cageGeometry`, `cages`, `cageOf`; cage-mates are `peers`, not `houses`), `makeCageBed` grows beds (about 1 ms each; 300 of 300 test seeds hit 30 blanks, so there is no authored fallback) and keeps one only if its sums are needed and the start has a move at a look, and `isSolved` checks sums. Two techniques: `cage-last` (tier 1; the last square of a cage is the sum minus the rest) and `cage-sets` (tier 2; the sets a sum allows rule numbers out). Hints: `cage-last` (`sudoku.hint-cage-last`) and a rules-out via cage-sets (`sudoku.hint-cage-sets`, or `-or` when several sets are kept). Drawing: 6-unit dashed outlines (alpha 0.75, red while a cage is off its sum), the sum on a paper patch, the number at 0.46 of a square and 0.06 lower, marks in two rows, and square edges faint (alpha 0.3) so cages read. A cage whose squares are all filled with the wrong sum rings red, is spoken once a bed (`sudoku.cage-sum`, inside the four-second quiet window) and is never a second miss (a wrong number against the solution is still the miss); a repeat in a cage is a clash that says "cage". The instruction is `sudoku.go-cages` (`-touch`), the how-to card has a level 10 step and the co-play hint mentions cage sums. The couch trip, courses and face-off never reach level 10. Rule tests (13 new) cover cages, uniqueness, the two techniques, hints and sums. Still open: whether sum labels and dashes read on an iPad ([DEVICE-CHECKS](../DEVICE-CHECKS.md)).

**By touch (the island, Lantern Lake).** Touch the pond and lift to set the line off (nothing moves before then); swipe the way the line should turn, and one stroke can turn it twice (turns wait in line and are made one a step; a swipe straight back is ignored). **Hint** (dots along the way to the next crumb, kept up until it is eaten) and **Start over** (the line goes back, steps keep counting) are buttons of at least 64 units under the pond; the pond itself is the target, so its cells may be smaller, and it keeps clear of the home and help buttons. The accounting is the couch's: a bump is a miss, a hint is help. The how-to demonstration paddles at one step a second over the first four crumbs.

**Level 9, A long conga** (added 2026-11-04; 11 by 7, 6 lily pads, 8 bites, 2.6 steps a second, `long: true`). The line starts seven ducklings long in an L: the leader at (3,6) heads right along the bottom bank and the tail turns up the left bank (`longStart`), so the tail is in the way when doubling back. Pads never land on the start cells or the bottom-bank runway. Par stays the open-water lower bound (a bonk-free fewest-steps route exists, so "exact fewest steps" still holds), and generation also requires the line to stand on a fewest-steps way to the next bite (`tailInWay`/`tailLegs`: a duckling other than the one right behind the leader that is still there when the leader would arrive) in at least `TAIL_LEGS` = 4 of the 8 legs. `LEVELS.grownup` is 1 to 9 with `order` `[8, 1, 2, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9]`, entry still 8. Not on the couch trip (Crumb Trail unchanged). Uses the existing `conga.go`/`conga.go-touch` line; the how-to card scopes level 8 to `from 8 to 8` and adds two level-9 steps.

**Fill marks (touch, 9 by 9 beds, levels 4–6; added 2026-11-05).** A **Fill marks ✎** button pencils every number that still fits (`candidateMask`, `fillMarksRow`/`fillMarks` in `logic.ts`) into each empty square that has no marks of her own, one row at a time (a soft pop a row, 0.07 s apart); her own marks are kept, and a placed number already clears itself from its peers' marks, so the marks stay equal to what fits. It uses only the rules on the board, never the solution, so it is free and not help: no miss, hint or entry is counted, and a bloom stops a sweep in progress. `offersFillMarks(size)` shows it on 9 by 9 beds only (not on 4 by 4 or 6 by 6, where marks would read lone numbers out for her). It is a third full-width button row under Hint and Start over beside the bed (landscape), or a row under them at the right on a tall view, clear of the pet; every target stays at least 64 units. The voice says `sudoku.fill-marks` once a game, and the how-to card has a step scoped to levels 4–6. The couch tray and the bot are unchanged. Still open: whether it feels like help or like the game playing for her ([DEVICE-CHECKS](../DEVICE-CHECKS.md)).

**Couch.** A trip stop (face-off `twin`: each plays their own seeded bed, scored in entries over the empty squares) plays the three 6 by 6 levels. Two challenge courses of frozen beds are on the puzzle shelf with par: **Six Beds** (six 6 by 6 beds, par 124 entries) and **Three Big Beds** (three 9 by 9 beds, one of each grade, par 149); `course.ts` holds them with a `version`, and tests assert each is unique, solvable by the techniques and graded as stated. It opens in the last tier of trips and is on the shelf from the start. Not built: a keyboard fallback for typing digits (the tray works with arrows and Enter), a daily or random bed on the shelf, 4 by 4 or 12 by 12 beds, killer or other variants.
