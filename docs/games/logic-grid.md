## Design notes

*Levels 1–8 built · Puzzle Peaks · [definition](../definitions/logic-grid.md) · id `logic-grid`.* A small mystery that comes out right: the pie has gone missing, a few picture clues are spoken, and she marks a grid of yes and no squares until one answer is left, then says who has it. Nobody is in trouble. Preschool plays levels 1–2, pre-K 2–3, school 3–6, grown-up 7 (level 8 waits on a later build). Skills: deduction, reasoning. Two boards a round.

**1 Who is where?** Two friends of cat, duck, bear by the pond, tree and bush; two direct clues ("Cat is at the pond"). **2 Not there:** dog, bunny, pig; one direct clue and one *not* clue ("Bunny is not at the tree"). **3 Three friends** (pre-K, school): a 3 × 3 grid of three of cat, duck, bear, dog by the pond, tree, bush and blanket, the missing thing a ball; three *not* clues (the no-redundant-clue gate leaves no direct clue, since with no spare place a direct clue plus any one not-clue already finishes). **4 The friend in the red hat** (school): each friend wears a hat (red stripes, blue spots, yellow stars, shuffled per board and shown on its header card) and every clue is a *not* clue naming the friend by hat. **5 Who, where and what** (school): two 3 × 3 blocks that share the friend rows, friend × place and friend × thing (apple, balloon, flower), with four to five clues (five in practice): at most one direct, at least one *different-person* clue ("The friend at the pond is not the one with the balloon", a place-cross-thing card) and at least one hat clue (the level 4 hats return on the friend headers); the missing thing is a kite and the event clue names a place ("The kite came down by the bush") or a thing ("The kite string was tied to the balloon"). A different-person clue alone decides no square, so it is never a miss. The five clue cards are small (128 × 100) in one row under the grid with Help beside them, and the written clue is printed in a caption strip for the card last lit, tapped or spoken. **6 This one or that one** (school): one 4 × 4 block, four friends of six critters, all four places, the kite; clues are *not*, *either-or* ("Pig is at the pond or the bush"; alone it crosses the friend's other two places) and *neither-nor* ("Neither Cat nor Dog is at the tree"; alone it crosses both squares), 4–6 clues (4 in practice, sometimes 5), no direct clue, at least one either and one neither and at least one hat clue (hats on *not* and *either* clues only); a fourth hat, a green zigzag (levels 4–5 still draw only the first three). The clue cards are the narrow 128 × 100 cards (two wide beside the grid in landscape, rows of three under it upright) with the written clue in a caption under them, Help below the caption: an either card is a friend or hat over two places stacked with "or", a neither card two friends, a red cross and a place. Picture clue cards (friend or hat, check or cross, place) speak when tapped. **7 Case file** (grown-up): three linked 4 × 4 blocks in an L, friend × place, friend × snack (apple, orange, lemon, pear) and snack × place under the places, with 64-unit squares, 100-wide row headers and 80-deep column headers that show written names beside the pictures; the missing picnic basket is drawn in-game. 5–8 clues are written on 212 × 64 cards in a list (one column beside the grid in landscape, two under it upright), each tapped to speak; kinds are direct, not, either and neither on friend × place and friend × snack, plus snack-and-place clues ("The pear was at the pond", "The friend at the pond did not bring the pear"), and some name friends by hat. The rule solver gained a *link* step (two squares of one friend, place and snack decide the third). Help sits in the L's empty quarter. The event names a place or a snack ("There was a bit of lemon on the basket") and the reveal names who, where and with what. A counted miss at this level says "That square goes against this clue" and rereads the clue. The ghost-finger demo taps only the solver's yes steps (each yes crosses its row and column), since 48 squares would outrun its clock.

**The grid.** A square cycles empty, yes, no, empty. A yes draws lighter automatic crosses on its row and column (derived; they lift when the yes goes). When the grid is solved the pie appears by its place and she taps the friend there; the sticker follows the second board.

**Misses and help.** A mark that one shown clue rules out by itself (a direct clue decides its square and the rest of its row and column; a *not* clue its square; at level 6 an either-or clue the two squares it rules out, a neither-nor clue either of its two) is one miss once it has rested 2.5 s, or when she taps another square or Help; counted once per square and clue a board, so the yes passed through on the way to a no is never a miss. A full wrong grid is one miss unless its wrong yeses already counted, and those yeses lift; a wrong friend is one miss. After two misses the solver's next square glows. Help points at the deciding clue and pulses the square (one hint); pressing it again while that step is still next marks it (no second hint); in the answer step Help says where the pie is (one hint) and again answers. A ghost finger replays the rule solver (two taps for a no). The rule solver also takes a column step at levels 3–6: a place's one friend left is a yes (Help: "Only one friend can be at the {place}") and, at level 5, an 'apart' step across the blocks for the different-person clues; at level 6 each clue's told marks go one square at a time (`told()`). Rules, the generator (every true clue, removed at random while an exact solver stays unique, then gated by an elimination-only rule solver) and the clue counts are in `src/games/logic-grid/logic.ts` (rule tests per level); voice lines are `lg.*`, with a rendered title clip; the how-to card has a ghost-finger demonstration. **8 Who came first?** (grown-up; range 7–8): three 5 × 5 blocks in the L (friend × place, friend × time, time × place) at 64-unit squares; the time row is dawn, morning, noon, afternoon, evening (never shuffled; a rising and setting sun is drawn in-game); five places (the four plus a barn) and a fifth hat (purple checks). 7–9 written clues (cards 264 wide in smaller type) add *order* clues ("Bear came before Duck", "Pig came right after Cat", "Pig came two steps after Cat") and an unaligned *pair* clue ("Of Cat and the friend at the pond, one came at dawn and one at noon") to every earlier kind, at least one of each. The rule solver gained 'order' and 'pair' steps; a fast exact counter (`countSolutions`) and a ZebraLogic-style additive generator build a board in about 13 ms. A mark one clue rules out alone also covers order (the last time for the earlier friend and the first for the later; with "two steps", the last two and first two) and pair (the friend at its place, the friend's other times and the place's other times). The L (748 × 728) is masked under a window with pinned header strips; a drag on any header slides it, a still tap names the header, and Help, the bot, the two-miss glow and a tap on a half-hidden square slide that square into view (a hidden square takes no mark); the basket picture follows the slide. Portrait on a tall screen shows the whole L with the list beneath and slides only when the window is smaller. Help sits above the pet's corner. Not built: the upper-school 4 × 4 ordering rung, the couch.
