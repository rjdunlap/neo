## Design notes

*After teaching clocks and JumpStart's clock activities.* Drag the clock's hands; the outer ring takes the long hand and the middle the short one, and each snaps to the marks the level uses. The short hand slides between numbers as the minutes pass, like real gears. Levels: the hour hand alone, o'clock, half past, quarter past and to, matching the clock to one of three visible fictional appointments, one hour later, and (school, level 7) five-minute times such as 3:25, counting by fives with small helper numbers around the rim. A wrong time is read back aloud ("The clock says half past 4. We want 3 o'clock"); two misses show faint yellow target hands. On the hour, the tower chimes the hour. Four correct tasks complete a round. Setting levels keep a digital request sign on screen; tapping it repeats the instruction without counting a miss or hint. The one-hour-later sign shows the starting time, not the answer. A circle marks the short hour hand and a diamond the long minute hand; a length/shape key and spoken guidance explain middle versus edge selection. Five-minute speech uses explicit words such as “three twenty five” rather than a colon. Level 6 compares a faint old clock with an animated one-hour advance after each check, including 11→12 and 12→1, with unchanged minutes explained aloud. Level 7 marks zero at 12, explains a full turn as 60 minutes into the next hour, and adds spoken fives with an animated arc from zero to the target minute when help appears after two misses. Both explanations use the current task’s times; the comparison and arc take no touches. Level 3 always includes half past and level 6 always includes both wraparounds (11→12 and 12→1). Level 8 (one hour earlier, school, placed before level 7 by `order` because its half-hour minutes are simpler than five-minute precision) reverses level 6: the sign shows the starting time, the comparison reads "−1 hour", the hour hand sweeps backwards, and every round includes 12→11 and 1→12; it reuses the same hands, bell and help. Level 9 (half an hour from now, school) keeps the independent setter at quarter snaps with a `+30 min` duration badge on the sign: each round covers all four quarter-hour starts (crossing and noncrossing, including crossing 12), shows a spoken +30-minute arc clockwise around the rim on wrong and right checks (naming the crossing into the next hour when minutes wrap), and adds faint yellow target hands after two misses. Level 10 (how long, school) drops the hands: two fixed clocks (with digital times) show a stated same-day start and end, and she picks the length with a plus/minus hours stepper (0–3) and a half-hour toggle, with the chosen words ("That is 2 and a half hours") always shown; choices are free until the bell. The first round is 3:00 to 5:30, every round mixes whole and half hours with four distinct lengths and end faces, and one interval runs from before noon to after it. A wrong check says the chosen length and where it would end (an empty choice says "no time at all"); two misses write the hour hops on a strip under the clocks (3:00 → 4:00 → 5:00 → 5:30), count them aloud and glow the stepper side or toggle still to set. Rules store absolute start/end minutes so a full 12-hour turn can never pass for the answer. Level 11 (fit a visit, school, after level 10) is staged: a sign says when a friend is free and how long a visit takes (first round: free 2:00 to 3:30, 30 minutes, starts 1:45, 2:30 and 3:15), a time strip under it shows the free time as a green band, and three start cards (a little clock and the digital time) offer exactly one start where the whole visit fits, boundaries included. Tapping a card draws that visit on the strip (yellow inside, orange when it hangs out) and either says it fits or explains why not: before our friend is free, or finishes after the free time ends. Until a start is chosen the hands are dimmed and the bell repeats the question; then she sets the chosen start with the level-4 quarter snaps and rings the bell. Choosing and setting share one task's misses and its single hint: after two misses the fitting card glows or, once chosen, yellow hands show the start. Rounds use 30-, 60- and 90-minute visits, free times opening on the half hour from 9:00 to 3:00, wrong starts that are both early, both late or one of each (so the fitting card is first, middle and last once each), at least one visit that fits exactly at an edge, and four different start faces. Tapping the sign replays it for free. Held upright the sign, strip and a row of three cards sit at the top with the tower under them. Level 5 supplies a fresh pretend plan on each task: three existing event pictures with analog and digital times. Tap a picture to answer; tap its separate time area to hear the appointment for free, without submitting or changing misses/hints. The first board shows lunch 12:00, park 3:00 and bath 6:30; subsequent boards seed three distinct o'clock/half-past times and pictures from breakfast, school, lunch, park, bath and bed. Four distinct target times complete the round, including with help. Wrong picture choices receive a comparison hint; two on a task glow the matching card and count one hint. The visible board supplies the evidence, never a required family routine. A native cancellation listener releases a canceled hand and is removed on destroy. Actual device pronunciation remains unchecked.

**A chime line never holds the next task (U7 fix batch 14, 2026-11-12).** `clock.yay` (levels 1–4, 7) or `clock.itis` (level 5) starts after the chime; level 6's `clock.later.example` starts with its sweep and the chime rings over it. The pulse and the next task follow at once, and the next instruction waits for the line or is said at her first hand turn, pick, bell press, replay or request-card tap, or a guide tap. At level 6 an early pick or bell says the instruction first and is then judged, so the guide's stored instruction stays the current task's; the comparison panel stays up over the next task until its example line ends. The last task's line is heard out before `clock.done`.

**Upright on a phone (2026-11-19).** `towerLayout` in `layout.ts` places the sign, tower, face, hand key, bell and (level 6) the comparison, tested over every level and five upright views (plus 360 × 800) in `upright.test.ts`. Held upright the request sign rides above the tower roof (level 6's "later" comparison to its right) and the hand key and bell sit in a row under the face; on the reading level the face shrinks to 60% with the three pretend-plan cards stacked beneath it (side by side they needed 900 of the 768 units). Sideways keeps the old layout.
