**Starts as:** one big animal; a touch anywhere gives it a snack, and two friends walk in

**Grows into:** eleven animals from level 3 (sheep, horse, hen and mouse join the first seven); snacks float to their eaters, "who eats grass?" or "who says moo?", drag each snack to its animal, give 2 to 4 with a numeral and countable dots, give two animals their own different snacks and amounts; read a chalkboard menu (a numeral 4 to 9 and a food word, then two lines) and give exactly that many; sort each animal onto a plants, both or meat mat by what it truly eats; four seeded scenes (meadow, orchard, kitchen garden, dusk) with a far barn on the horizon

## Design notes

**Level 11, Plants, meat or both? (2026-11-28, dive slice 11; [definition](../definitions/animal-snack-diet-sort.md)).** The `diet` mode tops the school window (`LEVELS.school` 8–11). One sort per round: two plant eaters and two that eat both, plus the cat on a fair coin (never first), walk up one at a time; `snack.dietintro` opens and `snack.diet` asks each ("Plants, meat, or both? Tap its mat."); tapping the waiting animal asks again, free. Three mats replace the feeding spot (`dietMats`: at least 160 by 120, 32 apart, a 12-unit tap margin), with a green outline round *plants* and *both* and a red one round *both* and *meat*, so the middle mat reads as the overlap; a sorted animal hops on, shrinks to `MAT_SCALE` (two fit a mat), nibbles once with its bite sound and is no longer a target. `DIET` in `logic.ts` gives each of the eleven a kind and two spoken foods with a source per row; each new source (MSD Veterinary Manual for the dog, feral-hog extension for the pig's earthworms, All About Birds for the duck's snails, poultry extension for the hen, Animal Diversity Web for the mouse) was opened once on 2026-11-28 and confirmed, so no animal was left out. The dog's bone and the bear's honey are never said. A wrong mat boings and shakes its head and says one true food with a question (`dietalso`, `dietnomeat`, `dietnoplants`) without naming the right mat; the second miss on one animal glows its mat (one hint), later misses only repeat. The finish says `dietdone` ("All {n} on their mats: plant eaters, meat eaters, and both!"), or `dietdonenomeat` when no cat came, then the pet's turn. The intro and both closing lines carry a slot (`{meat}`, `{n}`) so they stay on device speech, as the voice-clip coverage test allows, instead of needing new rendered clips (the definition's wording had none). Nothing files a journal discovery. Upright on a 390×844 phone the mats sit apart at the bottom under the waiting animal ([screenshot run](../verification/2026-11-28-animal-snack-level-11-plants-meat-or-both.md)); the empty space between the waiting animal and the mats on a tall upright view is the usual `friendFeet` height, kept as defined.

**One word in European Portuguese (WL2, 2026-11-22).** With the setting on, the pet adds the animal's word in the munch (level 1) and *who* (level 3) modes and the food's word in the float (level 2) and match (level 4) modes; the later modes and the four later animals with their foods add none. The word table is `src/content/words/pt-PT.ts`; the setting is *A word in another language* on the grown-ups' page, per profile, off by default, and only a local pt-PT voice speaks it. See [the definition](../definitions/additional-languages.md#as-built-2026-11-22).

**A right answer never holds the next ask (U7 fix batch 1, 2026-11-10).** In *who* rounds `snack.yum` starts after the eating and the next ask follows at once (its instruction cuts the yum); on a right count the bell rings, a 0.4 s beat passes and the next ask follows, with `snack.full` no longer awaited. On the round's last ask the line is still heard out, since the closing `snack.done` would cut it. Hints (`snack.toomany`, `snack.more`) and `snack.done` stay held.

*After farm feeding toys and animal sound books.* Cow, bunny, dog, cat, duck, pig and bear, each with one favorite food (hay, grass, bone, fish, seeds, apple, honey; the bunny's grass replaced a carrot on 2026-10-22, since carrots are a sugary treat and rabbits mainly eat grass). Lap levels are cause and effect: tap an animal and it munches its snack with its own voice, or tap a snack and it floats to the animal who loves it. Then "who eats grass?" (tap the animal; at level 3 every second question asks "who says moo?" instead, with a speaker bubble that replays the sound, and the bunny's hop is never the clue), dragging each snack to its eater, and giving an animal 2–4 snacks before ringing the bell. The food sign shows the requested numeral and dots; speech counts tufts of grass, bones, fish, apples, bales of hay, handfuls of seeds and pots of honey. Idle lap play gently nudges a friend without counting a hint; overlapping animal taps choose the closest body. A wrong animal shakes its head and says what it eats instead (on a sound question it answers in its own voice, "not me", and counts as a miss); too many snacks come back. Two misses glow the right animal or the next useful thing. Each snack is eaten in three shrinking bites, with a synthesized sound per food (a crunch for apple, a swish for hay and grass, a gnaw for bone, a gulp for fish, pecks for seeds, a lick for honey), a puff of crumbs and a poke from the animal per bite; no rule changes. Each animal that eats its favorite food files a discovery-journal entry. (Level 1 once left the animals stacked off screen at the top-left; they are now laid out whenever a round creates them, and the play-through asserts it.) A tap while an animal is eating or speaking is answered, not lost: the tapped animal pokes with a tick and an uneaten snack wiggles, with no voice, food or miss. Each round's seed picks the scene: the meadow with a picnic blanket, an orchard with a falling apple and a trough, a kitchen garden with a tin tub, or the yard at dusk with a bowl; the rules and animals are the same everywhere. A small far barn stands on the horizon behind the fences, placed clear of every friend's tap circle (the orchard shows it only in wide views, where the tree leaves a gap).

**The pet's turn (2026-11-17, dive slice 9).** After the finale line, when she has a saved friend (the companion), the pet says "Now it's {pet}'s turn! Tap to give a treat." (`snack.petturn`) and one touch anywhere (on the pet itself too) sends an apple to the corner guide, which eats it in three bites and says "Yum! Thank you, says {pet}!" (`snack.petyum`); then the round ends as before. Never a need: no miss or hint, nothing saved or journaled, and with no touch in 5 seconds (`PET_TURN_WAIT`) the round ends with no treat. Skipped with no saved friend, in the how-to demonstration and in couch play (`petTurn` in `logic.ts`).

**Level 1, one big animal (2026-11-12).** Lap's only level before 2, so it changed in place (the `oneBig` plan, munch mode). One big animal stands in the middle (`BIG_SCALE` 1.1 against 0.6 for friends); a layer over the whole meadow under the animals has a touch anywhere feed the nearest animal, at any distance. A touch while one eats or a friend walks in squishes it with a tick and counts nothing. The second and third friends walk in from the right after the second and fourth snacks (`friendsAfter`), each with its call, and a touch then feeds the nearest; six snacks finish the round, and the layout settles after a walk-in so a resize mid-walk leaves no animal off-screen. The line `snack.big` ("Who is hungry? Touch anywhere to give a snack!") is device speech with no rendered clip; the six-second idle nudge is unchanged. The ghost finger still taps an animal, so `fingerdemo` was not rerun.

**Level 6, Who says moo? (2026-11-14, dive slice 5).** The first pre-K level, so `bands` gains `prek` and `LEVELS.prek` was 5–6 (the decided pre-K window is 5–8; it widens as 7–8 land, and is 5–7 with level 7 and 5–8 with level 8). The `sound` mode asks four questions; each deals three animals and their three snacks onto the blanket, plays one animal's sound (the speaker bubble replays it, free) and says `snack.givesound`, "Who says {sound}? Give it its snack!". She drags that animal its own snack. The animal is judged first: any other animal answers in its own voice ("That is the pig. Who says moo?") as a miss, even when the snack is its own favorite; the right animal with another food says `snack.notmine`, "No thank you! The cow eats hay." (a miss); its own snack is a yum. The second miss of a question glows the asked animal and the fourth glows its snack (both count as a hint). The same animal is never asked twice running and the bunny's hop is never the clue. The saved score is 2 and 1 in the play-through, as levels 3–5. The ghost finger drags the asked animal's snack to it (`snackTouch`, never a wrong animal), and the how-to card has a level 6 line.

**Level 7, Who eats it too? (2026-11-15, dive slice 6; the decided order puts the set of answers at 7 and Their own snacks at 8).** The `eats` mode asks four questions, never the same food twice running. A food shows in the bubble and `snack.eats` says "Who eats {food}? There are two!"; three animals stand on the meadow: two eaters (always including the animal whose favorite it is) and one definite non-eater. She taps both. Each eater found eats a piece of that food and keeps a green tick; only an animal's own favorite files a journal discovery. A found eater tapped again just answers in its own voice (no miss; `snack.eatsmore` after the first, `snack.eatsboth` when both are found). The non-eater says `snack.nothanks` ("No thank you! The cat eats fish. Who eats hay?"), a miss; the second miss glows an eater still to find (a hint). `EATS` (hay: cow, horse, sheep, bunny; fish: cat, bear; seeds: duck, hen, mouse; apple: pig, horse, bear; corn: hen, pig, cow, horse; oats: horse, sheep, hen) and `NOT_EATS` (the cat for the plant foods; cow, horse, sheep and bunny for fish) are in `logic.ts`, each animal's fact cited in its comment (Cornell Feline Health Center, NPS Big South Fork and Shenandoah, University of Kentucky dairy forages, UW–Madison corn silage, University of Missouri Extension G2807, UMN horse extension, Oregon State EC 1652 and PNW 477, Ontario OMAFRA sheep, House Rabbit Society Chicago, Audubon mallard, Nebraska/ICWDM house mouse, Iowa Pork Industry Center feedstuffs). The dog is never dealt at level 7 (no sourced definite fact). `LEVELS.prek` is 5–7. The three new lines are device speech with no rendered clip. Also fixed for every level: the hint glow was drawn from screen-space bounds onto the scaled stage, so on a phone it showed as a small square near the top-left; it now converts to stage coordinates and sits around the right animal on a 390×844 screenshot. The ghost finger taps each eater, and the how-to card has a level 7 line.

**Level 8, Their own snacks (2026-11-15, dive slice 7).** The `pairs` mode asks three pairs, each confirmed with the bell. Two animals with different favorites, never the same pair twice running, each want 1 to 4 and never the same number (a choice beyond the spec, so the sign's dot rows read apart). A sign shows each animal with its dots and `snack.pairs` says it ("Give the cow 2 and the bunny 3! Then ring the bell."); the food is never named. Each animal has a pile of five of its own food (`PAIR_PILE`), a row per animal's pile in a two-row spot (`ROW_STEP` 135) in all four scenes, with the animals' feet lifted on short views so the spot never covers them. Another animal's food says `snack.notmine` ("No thank you! The cow eats hay.") and slides home (a miss). The bell checks both: right goes on to the next round; wrong is a miss, the extras of every animal return to their own pile, and the line (`snack.pairsextra`, "The cow has 3 and wants 2. Here are the extras back.") names the first animal that sent extras, else the first animal that is off (`snack.pairsoff`, "The cow has 1 and wants 2."). At the second miss the glow lands on the animal still off and follows on as it is fed, or on the bell once both counts are right (a choice beyond the spec). `snack.pairsfull` ('Just right! Thank you, say the animals!') answers a right bell. The four new lines (`snack.pairs`, `pairsoff`, `pairsextra`, `pairsfull`) are device speech with no rendered clip, as level 7's were. `LEVELS.prek` is 5–8, the full pre-K window. The how-to card has a level 8 line.

**Levels 9 and 10, The menu board and The long menu (2026-11-16, dive slice 8).** They open the school window (`LEVELS.school` 8–10, `school` in `bands`). A wooden chalkboard replaces the speech bubble (`board: 'picture' | 'words'` on the plan; `boardLines`, `boardBox` and `boardWord` in `logic.ts`): each line is the animal's picture, a printed numeral and the food's short word ("hay", "fish", "apples", singular for one), and the whole line is a speaker, 104 units tall. A tap reads it (`snack.boardline`, "The cow wants 5 hay."), free, never a miss or a hint. Level 9 (`count` mode with `counts: [4, 9]`) shows one line with the food's picture and puts ten of the animal's food on the two-row blanket (`BOARD_SNACKS`); she gives that many and rings the bell, with the level 5 replies and glow. The instruction `snack.board` never names the number. Level 10 (`pairs` mode with `board: 'words'`) shows two lines, one per animal, with no food picture and the level 8 piles, replies and glow; its numbers are 1 to 4 and differ, as in level 8 (a choice beyond the dive's "4 hay" example, so the sign reads apart). Both ask three rounds. Upright on a phone the board stays clear of the bell and above the animals (checked at 390×844). The printed words and three new voice lines are device speech until clips are generated; no phonics claim is made.

**More animals (2026-11-14, dive slice 4).** Sheep (grass), horse (oats), hen (corn) and mouse (grain; decided 2026-10-23: grain, not cheese, since seeds are the duck's) are built once in the shared `src/art/critter.ts` (new parts wool, mane, comb, beak snout, big ears, tail and muzzle shade, all off for the first eight) and `src/audio/sfx.ts` (a baa, a neigh and a squeak; the hen reuses the cluck), so Peekaboo Barn's animals slice can reuse them. They join from level 3 up; levels 1 and 2 keep the first seven (`FIRST_ANIMALS`, `poolFor`). New snack art: oats in a red bucket, a corn cob, two ears of grain. Sheep and bunny both eat grass, so the dive's planned "a unique food across all eleven" did not hold: `dealAnimals` never deals two animals with one favorite and `eaterOf(food, present)` resolves among the animals present (rule-tested over 400 seeds per level). The sticker stays on the seven. The journal gains four `journal.snack.*` lines, forty observations in all. A new `FIRST_CAST` in `critter.ts` (the original eight, in order) pins Pet Kitchen, Hat Band, Little Conductor and Lemonade Stand, which dealt from `Object.keys(CRITTERS)`, so their seeds do not change. Checked on a phone portrait: a count round (sheep, horse, cow) and a kitchen-garden match round where grain, seeds and oats read apart. Level 7 *Who eats it too?* has its `EATS` table (below).
