## Design notes

*After Electroplankton.* Garden creatures sing when touched: each round seats a seeded five of six (bird, frog, bunny, turtle, bell, woodpecker) in a seeded order, and each sings its listening role (a high chirp, a low pitched ribbit, four quick drum beats for the bunny, two slow tocks for the turtle, the bell's tune, the woodpecker's knock), so the pictures have been heard before the questions; level 1 ends after twelve songs. Listening questions follow, with seeded sounds that differ between questions: high or low (bird or frog; the two tiles sing their notes as they appear), fast or slow (bunny or turtle; same note and span, twice the beats), does the tune go up or down (a 3–5 note tune, down being up reversed), then echoing a woodpecker's rhythm on a drum, judged forgivingly for wobble but not for the wrong rhythm. The background loop is silenced, so the garden's own sounds are the only music and a fast-or-slow question is not blurred by a tempo underneath.

Level 7 adds three pictured paths: rising steps, falling steps and a flat path. A seeded three- or four-note tune goes up, down or repeats one pitch; the instrument, duration and note times match across choices, and the same answer never appears three times running. Six supported answers finish; pre-K plays 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 9, 10. Wrong choices get a gentle retry and replay; two misses glow the right path and count one hint.

Level 5 draws a dot under the woodpecker at each knock, spaced in proportion to the heard gaps. The dots fade after the first drum tap and return on replay. Each round draws from twelve rhythms: two short patterns, one three-gap pattern and a four-gap final pattern. A good echo opens the sleepy tree's eyes and brings an answering knock flourish. After two misses, the drum lights for the first and each remaining tap, following the child's taps at the same relative gaps. Replaying is free; two misses offer one hint, and four echoed rhythms earn one sticker even with support. There is no absolute timing score or speed target. Ghost-finger demonstrations cover both levels.

Level 8, One big friend, is the lap entry before level 1 (lap order 8, 1; toddler stays 1–2). One large friend sings on touch-down anywhere in the view except the shell's top corner controls and bottom-left pet corner. Four taps bring a wave and the next friend walking in; quick taps during the handoff still sound. Twelve songs finish with no misses or hints. Each round features three friends from a seeded five-of-six cast; the cast varies across rounds. New taps replace any delayed beats of the previous call. Level 1's individual targets and tap cooldown remain unchanged. Entry ID 8 lands at the next band's easiest level on a birthday, without renumbering saved levels or pins.

Level 9, Sing Higher, adds a vertical flower stem with six pentatonic pitches (steps 2–7). Moving the bird auditions each snapped pitch; lifting commits one note. The bell, frog and bunny take turns joining on the step below. Twelve released notes finish the creative round with no misses or hints. A cancelled drag commits nothing. Preschool plays 2, 3, 4, 9; toddler remains 1–2.

Level 10, Copy the shape, plays two two-note contours (up, down), then four seeded three-note contours (rising, falling, arch and valley). The left bird sings and shows its path; tap it to replay freely. Drag the right singer and lift for each note. Any starting pitches and intervals with the same directions count. A complete wrong contour boings and replays; after two misses, one hint lights viable next pitches. A starting note with no room to continue gets a fresh try without a miss. Six shapes finish, including with support. Both levels keep the pet and corner controls clear and have drag demonstrations; iPad sound and child understanding remain unchecked.
