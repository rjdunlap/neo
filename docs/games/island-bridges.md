## Design notes

*grown-up, on the island by touch and on the couch · after Hashiwokakero (Bridges).* Numbered islands sit in a sea. Join them with plank bridges, one or two between the same pair, running straight along a row or column to the next island, so that every island ends with as many planks as its number, no bridge crosses another, and all the islands are one group. **By touch (the island):** the water between two islands that face each other shows faint dots; tap it to lay a plank, tap again for a second (a crossing or an island with no room bumps and costs nothing; on level 7 only, a third plank is laid and a fourth bumps). **Take off** is a switch: while it is on, a tap on a bridge lifts one plank (free); a hint to take a plank off turns it on for her, and any other hint turns it off. **Hint** and **Start over** are buttons. Every stretch of water is a cell at least 64 logical units across and every button at least 64: the sea sits beside its buttons, or above them, bigger, on a tall view. **School opening (about 9 and up, no gate; home stays Lantern Lake, Wonder Woods lists it by subject):** the `school` band plays levels 5 then 1 (order `[5, 1]`: the 5 by 5 sea, then the 7 by 7) with cells and buttons 100 units across (`touchLayout` in `logic.ts`; the grown-up layout is unchanged). Once a round at school, the first time an island turns red she hears `bridges.trouble`; the finale is `bridges.done-school`. Accounting is as built (only hints count). The accounting is the couch's (a plank beyond the answer is never refused or counted as a miss, decided 2026-10-23; a hint counts as help). On level 6 only, a **joined hint** lights the plank one island forces once no bridge may close a group off, rings the group that bridge would have closed off in dashed amber and dashes the closing bridge red (`bridges.hint-joined`, or `bridges.hint-joined-same` when the lit bridge itself can carry one plank, not two); other levels never give it. Known: when the forced island has two closing bridges the hint draws one, and the dashed ring shares the orange of the island rings; whether it reads clearly on an iPad is an open device check. Accounting is unchanged (only hints count). The how-to card has a level-6-only step, and its demonstration plays a sea with its last six planks left. **Controls on the couch:** the stick or D-pad moves the highlight to the nearest island in the direction pushed; the bottom button *arms* the island to lay planks, and then a push toward a neighbor lays one (a second push lays another, a third bumps); the left button arms it to take planks off instead (free); pressing the same button again lets go. A faint line shows each neighbor the island can reach. An island turns green when it has its planks, and its outline turns red when its number can no longer be reached, whichever bridges were laid (judged from the numbers and the room left, never from the answer).

**Score and help.** Every plank laid is an entry, and taking one off is free, so a wrong plank costs the one entry it took to lay it; a bridge that would cross another, a plank beyond the level's most (two, or three on level 7), or an island with no room bumps and costs nothing. The fewest is the planks in the answer, so the score is planks over par (a plank that is not in the answer is never refused or counted as a miss, decided 2026-10-23; only hints adapt the level). The pause menu has **Show a hint** (marks a challenge run as helped) and **Clear this sea** (planks already laid keep counting). A hint names a bridge that has more planks than the answer first; otherwise an edge one island decides by itself, and the spoken line says why (`bridges.hint-full`: the island has just enough room, so every open way takes a plank; `hint-last-way`: only one way is left; `hint-every-way`: one short of full, so every way takes at least one), chosen in that order from the island's number and the room it has on the board (`roomOf`, `forcedPlank` in `logic.ts`); otherwise any plank the answer needs, with the two islands ringed and the bridge lit.

**The rules** are in `logic.ts`. `layoutOf` finds the pairs of islands that face each other and which of those bridges would cross; `solve` settles each island's number against the room it has (a bridge that must carry planks forces what crosses it to none) and searches what is left, and a finished try must also join every island. `makePuzzle` grows a layout and its planks together (each new island joined to one already there, then extra bridges where two face each other across clear water), numbers each island with the planks it got, and keeps the puzzle only if `solve` finds exactly one answer; the two gentler levels also need `bycounting` (propagation alone finishes it, which is how an easy puzzle is told from one that needs guessing). Seven levels (grown-up order `[5, 1, 2, 6, 7, 3, 4]`, range 1–7, entry at 5): **level 5, a small 5 by 5 sea to start** (four to six islands, finished by counting, at least one island with a single neighbour; its cells are capped at 80 units like the other sizes, 64 or more upright and sideways), then 7 by 7 with seven to nine islands, 9 by 9 with eleven to fourteen, a busier 9 by 9 with fifteen to eighteen, and 11 by 11 with nineteen to twenty-four; **level 7, Up to three planks** (added 2026-11-03) is a 9 by 9 sea of 12–14 islands (the dive said 11–14; 12 keeps the levels-climb test true) where a bridge carries up to three planks and numbers go to 12: `Layout.max` (default 2, so levels 1–6 and the couch courses are unchanged) is read by `canAdd`, `trouble`, `solve` and `roomOf`, the picture and `parsePuzzle` encode 10–12 as base-36 letters, `every-way` hints any edge with room for two or more, and every sea uses at least one triple with a counting-only hint walk of all `forced` steps (the `joined` walk is off here). Triple planks are drawn at three thinner offsets and two-digit numbers smaller; voice `bridges.go-three` and `bridges.go-three-touch`; the how-to card has a level-7 step. A fourth plank bumps without a miss. The rule tests add a brute force over every 0..max assignment at two and three planks (shown to fail with the solver capped at two); **level 6, Keep them joined** (added 2026-11-03, grown-up max 6) is a 9 by 9 sea of twelve to fifteen islands where a bridge must not close a group off: `solve` takes an `isolation` flag (off by default, so levels 1–5, the couch trip and Island Hopping are judged exactly as before, and a test shows the rule never changes any of their answers) and the generator keeps a sea only if it is unique, not `bycounting`, needs this reasoning (`byreasoning`), and a hint walk from an empty sea includes at least one `joined` hint and no bare `next` hint. Rule tests assert that the solver agrees with a brute force over every plank assignment on thousands of random small layouts, that a two-answer layout is counted as two and refused as a puzzle, that every made puzzle has exactly one answer with numbers one to eight, that the highlight reaches every island, and that a player who follows every hint from an empty sea lays exactly the answer's planks.

**Couch.** A trip stop (face-off `twin`: each plays their own seeded sea, scored in planks over the answer) plays levels 1 to 3. Two puzzle-shelf courses of frozen seas. **Island Hopping** (five seas of 8, 13, 18, 20 and 22 islands; par 11 + 17 + 27 + 27 + 38 = 120); `course.ts` holds them with a `version`, and tests assert each has exactly one answer, every number one to eight, and that they climb. It opens in the last tier of trips and is on the shelf from the start. **Joined Seas** (id `joined`, version 1, listed right after Island Hopping; added 2026-11-04) is five frozen 9 by 9 seas of 12, 13, 14, 15 and 15 islands (par 12 + 15 + 16 + 17 + 24 = 84), each with exactly one answer, none finished by counting alone (`bycounting` false) and all by counting plus keeping the islands joined (`byreasoning`); its hint walk gives at least one `joined` hint, and the course is created at level 6, so hints ring the group a bridge would close off (`bridges.hint-joined`). Island Hopping stays at level 4. Tests also check the highlight reaches every island (`focusPath`). Its card reads "Not finished yet · par 84" before a run; voice `couch.course.joined`. Not built: a replay of the best way, a 13 by 13 size, hints that teach a named technique.
