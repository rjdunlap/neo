**Grows into:** twelve picture pairs to level 2, sixteen from level 3; varied animal subjects and acting lap flips; find the one named, find the opposite among three, match opposite pairs, spot the one pair among five (pre-K level 5)

## Design notes

*After concept board books and Sesame Street.* Sixteen concept pairs drawn in code (twelve through level 2, sixteen from level 3); the original eight start at level 1: big and small (a bear, duck or bunny), happy and sad (a cat, dog or pig), up and down (a balloon with arrows), open and closed (a box), full and empty (a glass), hot and cold (a steaming mug, an ice cube), day and night, fast and slow (a bunny, a snail). Level 2 adds tall/short flowers, an on/off lamp, an awake/asleep cat and a ball in/out of a basket. Level 3 adds a wet/dry sponge, a clean/dirty plate, a thick/thin book and a near/far ball (families wet, clean, size and position). Related concepts never compete in opposite-choice or pair boards: size, face, container and position families each supply at most one pair. Spoken words are unique, and the sticker pool is pinned independently. Seeded animal subjects stay the same on both sides of a pair, including the shown question and its answer. On the lap, tapping the picture flips it to its opposite while the word is spoken: big friends stomp, small friends tiptoe, the bunny zooms, the snail creeps, and the balloon rises or sinks. Motion stays inside the fixed card and pauses with the scene. Then find the one named, find the opposite among three, and match three opposite pairs. At pre-K level 5 (*Which two?*) five cards show one opposite pair and three single decoys from other families; she taps one, then its opposite, for three rounds. A wrong second tap is a miss naming both words ("not opposites"). Wrong picks are gentle misses naming the words; two glow the answer (at level 5, a card of the pair).

**The word and the praise never hold the next card (U7 fix batch 19, 2026-11-12).** At level 1 the flipped card's word starts with the act and the next card follows after the existing beat, its word cutting the last; at levels 2–3 the praise starts and the next question follows the beat, its instruction cutting the praise. The last card's word and the last question's praise are heard out.
