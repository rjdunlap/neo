## Design notes

*Levels 1–3 · after the weather and water study; [definition](../definitions/rain-garden.md).* A fat cloud hangs over a little garden on a slope. She taps it and four drops fall one at a time: on soil two tuck into the ground and two roll down the arrow into a pond where a friend (frog, duck or turtle) hops and sounds; on stone all four roll. Then she changes the ground and rains again. The action is comparing what happens under the same rain when one thing changes. A toy model: stone holds none, soil holds two, the rest runs downhill. The invented capacities say nothing about real soaking, which is slower and depends on how wet the ground already is.

Levels (toddler 1, preschool 1-2, pre-K 2-3, school 3–6): 1 "Rain on the garden": soil, rain, then rain again on fresh ground; a leaf ends the round (a tap during a shower is ignored). 2 "Two gardens, same rain": two boards side by side (also in portrait, whose logical view is 1024 wide, and upright on a phone, where the beds share the 768-wide area with ponds below and the tiles at the bottom), a ground tile under each, one wide cloud; flipping either tile resets both gardens, so the pair stays a fair comparison. 3 "Change it and try again": the first rain is kept on a bookmark card, the tiles are stone and soil, and the leaf waits for a rain on different ground, with a spoken comparison (including soil to stone, `rain.compare.back`). 4 "Two beds downhill" (school, its own scene `route.ts`): two beds on a terraced slope, the top bed's overflow running into the low bed and the low bed's into the pond; she taps each bed to make it stone or soil (four arrangements, the markings authored on the first board and seeded after), taps the cloud to try it, then the Check. The low bed (or, in the seeded variant, the marked bed) must store exactly two drops, shown by dashed drops on its sign, and the pond needs at least one; the Check passes only when both hold. Dry, ran past (too many for the bed) and pond-dry arrangements each get a spoken cause line (`rain.route.*`, `rain.miss.dry`, `rain.miss.pond`). Any arrangement that works counts, not one answer. Start from a failing board, with seeded friend and mirrored layout (mirrored about x=525 so the pond clears the pet; levels 1–3 keep their own layout).

Misses and hints: levels 1–3 have no misses; level 4 counts one miss per new failing arrangement at the Check (the same arrangement checked again counts nothing and flipping back to an already-judged arrangement re-checks free), and from the second miss the glow on the beds follows the arrangement and counts one hint once. Show me at level 4 flips one bed to a strictly closer arrangement (one hint) or, when the board already works, points at the Check (counted once); taps are allowed while the pet explains a miss; Start again keeps the round's misses and hints. Levels 1–3: Show me points at the next action (cloud, tile or leaf), one hint per newly pointed action. The leaf nudge "Tap the leaf when you're all done" comes after the third rain (level 1) or the second pair or comparison (levels 2-3) and counts nothing. Start again is the same board fresh, with the bookmark and trial counts cleared. Nothing is kept. Tinker Lab's 26th game; challenge `noticing`; music `STYLES.bubbles`; one title voice clip rendered.

Upright on a phone (levels 1–4): the layouts in `logic.ts` (`rainLayout`, level 4's `uprightRouteLayout`) stack cloud, bed(s) and pond down the 768-wide area, hung up from the bottom edge so a tall phone shows sky above; mirroring is ignored there; tests use upright views only (a non-upright 768 view never takes the branch).

Deviations from the definition: one generic hint line per action kind (`rain.hint.cloud`, `rain.hint.tile`); the pond friend reacts with its sound and a hop rather than a spoken `rain.friend` line; no idle-triggered demonstration (the shell's instruction repeat only); `rain.compare.back` added for soil to stone.

*Level 5 · "Three beds downhill" (school; [definition](../definitions/rain-garden.md#level-5-three-beds-downhill-definition), built 2026-11-23).* Level 4's interaction on a longer slope: three beds step down to the pond, the tree's bed and the stone path are fixed, and each of six authored boards (A to F) marks one or two beds with a leaf or flower sign and a four- or six-drop shower (shown as dashed drops on the cloud). Every soil bed keeps two drops, so the budget has to reach the marked beds and the pond. Level 4 now runs on the same board model (`RouteBoard`, `board4`) with unchanged behaviour and expected values. Miss and hint accounting are level 4's. Narration is chained from short clips (21 new voice lines). Layouts are the measured upright and landscape ones, with no mirroring. Departures from the definition: the level-4 "check" how-to step reads "each marked bed" and covers levels 4-5, and level 5 has its own shorter note (the card-length test caps cards at 840 characters); at level 5 the drops leave the cloud one after another, 0.45 s apart, at 0.7 pace, so six drops do not drag; a narration chain stops when she acts; the `raingarden` browser fixture uses a touch phone when `FROM_LEVEL` is 5 or more.

*Level 6 · rain again on wet ground (school; [definition](../definitions/rain-garden.md#level-6-rain-again-on-wet-ground-definition)).* Tap the same cloud twice. Water from the first rain stays in the beds, and the pond keeps one row per rain so the amounts are visible together. The first round starts with two soil beds and four drops. Later boards seed two or four drops, both bed surfaces and the pond friend. Between rains, either pictured prediction lifts and lights when tapped. A surprise counts as an experiment. Flipping a bed, tapping the rewind cloud or using Start again clears both showers and the prediction. After any complete pair of rains, the leaf stays available for the rest of the round. Show me points at the next cloud or leaf, counting one hint when it newly points. The leaf saves zero misses and awards the usual sticker with or without help.

The beds and runoff tray sit above and below the caption bubble on short upright phones. Sideways, the low bed sits to the right of the bubble. Twelve-second idle cues point at the cloud or leaf, and the second cue repeats the instruction without scoring help. Tapping the guide cuts off a prior narration chain so the repeated instruction can be heard. Co-play: one person picks a prediction picture and says why, then the other taps the cloud. Swap after the two rains.

*Level 7 · one garden for two rains (school; [definition](../definitions/rain-garden.md#level-7-one-garden-for-two-rains-definition)).* Two rain cards hang beside the three-bed slope of level 5: a light shower (four drops) and a big storm (six drops), each with its own signs (which beds keep two, and what the pond must get). Tapping a card previews that rain on her garden; the Check runs both rains, each on dry ground, and marks each card with a tick or a cross. A failed Check counts one miss (checking the same arrangement again counts nothing); from the second miss the bed to change glows, counting one hint the first time it lights. Show me flips the next useful bed or points at the Check. Previews, card picks, flips and Start again are free.

Where it departs from the definition: a passing Check ends the round directly after the pet's line, as levels 4 and 5 do, so there is no separate leaf button and the ghost finger ends on the Check. Picking the other card clears any settled drops from the board (the beds' ground stays) so one rain's drops are never read against the other card's signs; the definition had previews staying. The hold-sign's tick shows only once a whole rain has settled with at most two drops in the pond. Layout is measured with all three sockets marked (a sign position per bed), the card boxes at the definition's coordinates, and unit-tested at the eight views.

*Level 8 · the mystery patch (school; [definition](../definitions/rain-garden.md#level-8-the-mystery-patch-definition)).* A covered patch sits under the cloud and follows one of two pretend rules, shown as two idea cards: **empties between rains** or **stays full**. She builds a test from two slots (empty, a small rain of two drops, a big rain of four), taps the cloud and watches only the pond, which shows each rain's row with a badge for its size. She then lights every card that still fits the water she saw and taps the check. One rain looks the same under both rules, so lighting both cards is a right answer to it (no miss, but no leaf); only a second rain in a row separates the rules, and naming the one that fits lifts the cover, shows how full the patch ended and brings the leaf. A failed check counts one miss (the same cards on the same run again count nothing) and says why: both still fit, one idea does not fit, or the card does not fit. From the second miss each card shows the two small rows its rule would have left in the pond (rings for empties, squares for stays full), counting one hint the first time. Show me runs the whole deciding test (two big rains) as one hint, then lights the right cards, then points at the check and the leaf. Slot taps, runs, card toggles (even before any rain) and Start again are free. Rule code is in `mystery.ts` (`runPlan`, `fits`, `judge`, `mysteryHint`), its scene in `mystery-scene.ts`.

Where it departs from the definition: the new voice lines are spelled `rainmyst.*` instead of `rain.*` (the `rain` prefix needs a rendered clip for every line and a feature run does not render clips, so they use device speech until a Better voice pass); the pause sheet's Show me uses the definition's four worked steps but the check step is a point, not a tap; after the cover lifts she can still change the test, and the lifted patch then shows the latest run.
