## Design notes

Two guided levels show pale matching shape outlines; two copy levels use a separate small model; three mirror levels ask for matching wings, ending with two columns and six spots. Reusable stamps return to the tray. The final palette contains repeated shapes and colors, requiring both attributes to match. Drops outside a spot are exploration, not mistakes. The tokens come from a pool of twelve, drawn by seed, so a round's colors are distinct (plans of four or fewer also keep every shape and color different), and a copy board is never symmetric. On the second wrong drop the hint glows the matching spot and also rings the twin across the line (mirror levels, with the line "Look across the line at its friend.") or the same place on the little model bug (copy levels). On completion at levels 5–7, both wings and their stamps fold across the middle together, meet their twins and open again; a faint model wing stays visible. This uses the existing finish wait and does not change misses or hints. Level 8 (count mode) is a bare bug with six open places, three per wing, and a sign showing a numeral (2–5 by seed) with hollow dots that fill as stamps land; extras hang below the card. Any of the three tray stamps goes in any place and the count is said as each lands. A tap on a stamped place lifts the stamp off. Exactly the sign's number on a green check finishes. One stamp too many is never a miss ("That's four. We wanted three. Tap one to take it off."); a check with the wrong number is a miss that says what the bug has against what is wanted. One hint per round, on the second over-count or second wrong check, after which the glow shows the next useful move (the extra stamp, an open place and a tray stamp, or the check). Preschool meets it last (after levels 2–5) and pre-K between levels 5 and 6.
