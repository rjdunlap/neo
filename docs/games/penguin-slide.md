## Design notes

*After the ice-sliding puzzles in Pokémon and Zelda.* Tap the ice in a direction and the penguin slides until a snowy rock, the pond's edge or soft snow stops it, eating fish it passes. Puzzles are generated and kept only when a breadth-first search over slides finds the planned shortest solution (1–2 moves at first, up to 4–6 with two fish and soft snow). Levels 1–4 also reject any pond with a dead end (`hasDeadEnd` walks every reachable state), while level 5 and up keep theirs since undo is taught there; at level 1 a three-pond round holds at most one single-slide pond. Each touch pond draws its rocks as snowy rocks, igloos or snowmen (red scarf) and its fish orange, pink or teal, seeded; the stop rule is unchanged and couch courses keep the plain look. A tap shows a brief ice-blue scuff ring on the penguin's tapped side. The yellow arrow appears by itself after a few extra slides and counts as help. Sliding is exploration: there are no misses. Undo steps back one slide. Three slides beyond the shortest bring a hint arrow showing a best next slide; if no route is left, the undo button glows. **Level 6, Ice Blocks (school):** an ice block sits on the pond. Slide up to it, then slide into it again to push it: the block skates until it meets a rock, the pond's edge, another block, a fish (it never covers one) or soft snow, and the penguin stays where it is. A block that cannot move yet stops the penguin like a rock. Pushing counts as a slide; every block pond is generated so that it needs a push (with the blocks frozen like rocks the fish is out of reach or takes longer), and the solver proves the fewest slides over the penguin's place, the fish eaten and where each block is. A block pushed against the only way to the fish leaves nothing reachable, so undo glows. **Level 7, Two fish and a block (school):** two 6×5 ponds each hold two fish, one movable block and four rocks, with no soft snow. Their shortest routes take 4–6 slides, including a useful push; planning a stopping place must now account for both fish. Both ponds finish before one sticker. School extends to 2–9; younger windows and saved IDs/pins stay unchanged. **Level 8, block and snow:** two 6×5 ponds each combine both fish, one block, one soft-snow patch and four rocks. Shortest routes take 4–6 slides; generation requires both a useful push and a snow stop that changes the best route. The penguin and pushed block stop on snow, so planning must account for both kinds of stop. Its fixed ponds also make two couch challenge courses (Pond Practice, the Five Ponds and the Frozen Ponds, whose ponds hold ice blocks: five ponds, fewest slides 31, each with one shortest route). On the couch the round arrow is not drawn (the couch screen takes no pointer input); the left button undoes a slide and the pause menu's **Start this pond again** puts the penguin and every fish back, with the slides so far still counted. **Level 9, paired blocks:** two 6×5 ponds each have one fish, three rocks and two movable blocks, with no soft snow. Four authored ponds are drawn by seed and independently reflected across each axis (sixteen arrangements). Best routes take 6–9 slides; freezing either block makes the shortest route worse or impossible, so both pieces matter. Fewer fish and rocks leave room for planning the two stops. Undo, stuck-state help and the same solver remain available; both ponds finish before one sticker. Frozen couch courses are unchanged. **Level 10, Three fish (school):** two generated 7×5 ponds each hold three fish and five rocks, with no blocks or soft snow. The shortest route takes 5–8 slides, and generation rejects any pond where a single first slide from the start eats two fish, so she has to choose the order of the three. Fish are never lost, undo and the hint arrow work as before, and both ponds finish before one sticker. School extends to 2–10; younger windows stay unchanged and the bot uses the solver. Voice line `slide.three`; the how-to card has a level-10 step. **Level 11, Where will it stop? (preschool and pre-K, after level 2):** two 5×4 ponds with one fish and three rocks. A big yellow arrow shows a direction and she taps any cell where she thinks the penguin will stop; then it slides. Each pond shows three slides that all move the penguin, never straight back, passing the fish only on the third, and at least two of the three stops are further than the cell beside the penguin. A right guess sparkles and says `slide.predict.right`; a wrong one keeps her blue ring and bounces the stopping rock (`slide.predict.rock` or `slide.predict.edge`). Predictions are never misses or hints; there is no undo, hint arrow or stuck glow on these ponds, and tapping the penguin's own cell squeaks and is ignored. Keys and pad move a yellow ring and predict; the ghost finger and autoplay tap the stop cell; a restart goes back to the first shown slide. Preschool plays 1, 2, 11 in order and pre-K 1, 2, 11, 3, 4; school plays 2–10 then 12, and a level 11 carried into school lands on level 2 (`entryLevels`). **Level 12, A block onto the star (school, after 10):** two 6×5 ponds each hold one ice block, one star mark, three rocks and one soft-snow patch, with no fish; the shortest route is 3–6 slides and generation requires the snow to change it. The goal is the block stopping on the star, not the penguin reaching a fish: the shared `solved()` in `logic.ts` (every fish eaten and every star covered) drives the solver and the dead-end check, and a push that lands the block on the star wins with a sparkle. There are no misses; the hint arrow after three extra slides shows the next slide or push (one hint), and undo glows when the block can no longer reach the star, with a star-specific line (`slide.star.stuck`). Instruction `slide.star`, finale `slide.star.done`; the how-to card's goal now reads "Slide the penguin to the fish, or push an ice block onto a star." **Level 13, Two penguins (school, the top, after 12):** two 6×5 ponds each hold one fish, four rocks and a second penguin in a red scarf; the shortest route is 4–7 slides and a pond is kept only when freezing either penguin as a rock makes the fish unreachable or the route longer, so both are needed. She taps a penguin (a 100-unit cell) to choose it (it lifts over a blue ring), then taps the ice; the controller's action button switches penguin. Each penguin stops the other like a rock and penguins never push (`movePair`, `solvePair` and `alone` in `logic.ts`). There are no misses and choosing is not a slide; the yellow arrow (after three extra slides, one hint) stands by the penguin to move next; undo steps back either penguin's slide and re-chooses it, and glows when stuck. Voice line `slide.pals` (it mentions only tapping); how-to steps from level 13. The couch course for 13 is not built.
