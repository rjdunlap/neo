**Starts as:** tap a turtle or a rabbit pad and three friends on a bandstand play the same little tune slow or quick

**Grows into:** hear one whole tune at each pace and see which you chose (a pad tap changes the pace at the next beat, so fast is never the winner)

## Design notes

*toddler–pre-K (levels 1–4; toddlers play 1–2) · after Peter and the Wolf and Fantasia.* Three friends on a bandstand play one seeded four-beat pentatonic tune (one of six: hop, slide, wave, skip, rock or sway) over and over. A turtle pad plays it slowly (60 beats a minute) and a rabbit pad quickly (90); the notes and their order never change, only the pace. The first tap starts the band at once; after that a change waits for the next beat and shows a pending ring with a clock, and tapping the pad that is already playing calls the waiting change off. Four beat marks under the playing pad fill as the tune goes by. Level 1 lets her play at either pace. Level 2 adds a check beside each pad once she has heard its tune whole. Level 3 (preschool and pre-K) adds a fourth seeded friend, the listener, who holds a 200-unit sign showing the turtle or rabbit (the pads' picture and word) for the current half of the tune; the two requests are always one of each, in a seeded order, and the band holds silent between phrases. A pad tap is her pick and plays that half once at that pace (free, repeatable, never counted); Listen again replays the half at the picked pace; the green check submits. Check with nothing picked wiggles the pads and says to pick first (not counted). A wrong pick boings, bounces the sign, names the requested pace and counts one miss, and the pick stays. Two wrong Checks on one half glow the requested pad and the band previews that pace once, counted as one hint, at most once per half; the pause sheet's Show a hint does the same. A right pick plays the half at that pace while the listener nods, then the sign turns to the second request; after the second half the band and listener bow and the round ends with one sticker. Misses and hints at level 3 feed the adaptive level. The idle turtle pulse is off at level 3. The green Done is dim until the heard gate: at level 1 a whole phrase at the pace she last chose; at level 2 both paces heard whole, then the chosen one whole since she chose it; a waiting change closes the gate at once. Done before then blinks the beats still unlit and says to hear it whole (or to hear the other pace); it is never a miss. Listen again restarts the tune from its first note and keeps the heard marks. At the gate Done plays the tune once more with claps, the band bows and the round ends with one sticker. At levels 1–2 there are no misses and no hints, so a round finishes with both at 0. After six idle seconds before the first tap the turtle pad pulses once, which is not a hint. Fast is never the better pace. The band plays at half volume while the pet is speaking, and the background loop is silenced. At levels 1–2 the ghost finger taps turtle, then rabbit once the turtle tune has been heard whole, then Done; at level 3 it reads the sign, taps that pad, waits for the audition and taps Check. Nothing is saved. Not checked on a device: whether 60 and 90 sound clearly different from an iPad speaker, whether a two-year-old reads the pads and the pending ring, whether the drum masks the melody, and how device speech says turtle and rabbit.

Upright on a phone (levels 1–3): `conductorLayout` hangs the pads up from the bottom edge above the pet corner and marks; level 3's sign sits in a right-hand column beside a narrower bandstand (friends spacing 140) since 768 wide has no room under it. No rule or accounting change; the layout test covers four upright views, and `CONDUCTOR_UPRIGHT=1 BROWSER_SUITE=conductor` plays level 3 at 390 × 844.

Level 4, soft or loud (preschool and pre-K, built 2026-11-25): the same two pad slots hold a small drum (soft) and a big drum (loud), and both play at one steady 75 beats a minute, so only how strongly the band plays differs. `STRENGTH_GAIN` (gentle 0.45, strong 1, the gain every other level already plays at, so never louder than the device volume allows) multiplies every voice; `advance(…, 'strength')` stamps each beat with its `bpm` and `gain`, and a rule test proves pitch, order, bass, drum and timing are identical on both pads. The heard gate is level 2's (both heard whole, then the chosen one whole since she chose it), never a miss or a hint, and the friends sway in small or big bounces. No save change.

Level 5, two ways at once (preschool, pre-K and school, built 2026-11-25): two dials at once, the turtle and rabbit for pace and a small and a big drum for strength, as four pads in one row (130 units each) with a four-cell hearing mark under them, one cell for each of the four ways, each showing its animal and drum, a check once heard whole and four dots on the way playing now. Either dial changes on the next beat, and the two can wait together; the first tap on the strength dial starts the band on the turtle. `advance(…, 'dials')` stamps each beat with the pace's bpm and `STRENGTH_GAIN` for the strength, and a rule test proves all four ways play the same notes in the same order for all six tunes. The heard gate follows the latest change of either dial: the way she is in must be heard whole since the last change, and at least two ways (`COMPARE_AT_LEAST`) must have been heard whole, so there is always something to compare; hearing all four is never required, and nothing is a miss or a hint. This is the first level for the school band, which now lists the game with a one-level range. Ghost finger: the rabbit, then the soft drum, then Done.

Level 6, invite a friend (pre-K and school, built 2026-11-25): the three friends each play one part of the one accompaniment, the left friend the tune on the marimba, the middle one the plucked bass and the right one the drum, at one steady 75 beats a minute. A spotlight 160 units wide under each friend asks them in or out: the first tap starts the band with that friend at once, and every later tap (an invitation or a goodbye) waits for the next beat with the same clock ring as the pace pads, so each friend joins or leaves on a whole beat and never plays half a note; tapping again before the beat calls the change off. The clock never stops, even with nobody in, so the tune never skips or repeats and an empty stage just waits quietly (its first spotlight pulses every six idle seconds, an invitation and not a hint). A rule test proves every cue lands on a whole beat, that the notes and their order are the same whoever is in, and that each friend voices only their own part. Nothing is a miss or a hint. The heard gate is level 2's idea for groups: somebody is playing, that group has been heard whole since it last changed, and at least two different groups (`STAGES_AT_LEAST`) have been heard whole; an empty stage never finishes. Friends who are in sway and show a check and four beat dots; the others stand still. At Done the band plays the tune once more with claps and bows. Ghost finger: the tune friend, then the bass friend, then Done. The friends stand 190 apart over the spotlights, and the layout test covers the four landscape and four upright views. No save change. Not checked on a device: whether a young child hears "who is playing" as the part each friend takes.
