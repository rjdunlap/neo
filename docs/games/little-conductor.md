**Starts as:** tap a turtle or a rabbit pad and three friends on a bandstand play the same little tune slow or quick

**Grows into:** hear one whole tune at each pace and see which you chose (a pad tap changes the pace at the next beat, so fast is never the winner)

## Design notes

*toddler–pre-K (levels 1–3; toddlers play 1–2) · after Peter and the Wolf and Fantasia.* Three friends on a bandstand play one seeded four-beat pentatonic tune (a hop, a slide or a wave) over and over. A turtle pad plays it slowly (60 beats a minute) and a rabbit pad quickly (90); the notes and their order never change, only the pace. The first tap starts the band at once; after that a change waits for the next beat and shows a pending ring with a clock, and tapping the pad that is already playing calls the waiting change off. Four beat marks under the playing pad fill as the tune goes by. Level 1 lets her play at either pace. Level 2 adds a check beside each pad once she has heard its tune whole. Level 3 (preschool and pre-K) adds a fourth seeded friend, the listener, who holds a 200-unit sign showing the turtle or rabbit (the pads' picture and word) for the current half of the tune; the two requests are always one of each, in a seeded order, and the band holds silent between phrases. A pad tap is her pick and plays that half once at that pace (free, repeatable, never counted); Listen again replays the half at the picked pace; the green check submits. Check with nothing picked wiggles the pads and says to pick first (not counted). A wrong pick boings, bounces the sign, names the requested pace and counts one miss, and the pick stays. Two wrong Checks on one half glow the requested pad and the band previews that pace once, counted as one hint, at most once per half; the pause sheet's Show a hint does the same. A right pick plays the half at that pace while the listener nods, then the sign turns to the second request; after the second half the band and listener bow and the round ends with one sticker. Misses and hints at level 3 feed the adaptive level. The idle turtle pulse is off at level 3. The green Done is dim until the heard gate: at level 1 a whole phrase at the pace she last chose; at level 2 both paces heard whole, then the chosen one whole since she chose it; a waiting change closes the gate at once. Done before then blinks the beats still unlit and says to hear it whole (or to hear the other pace); it is never a miss. Listen again restarts the tune from its first note and keeps the heard marks. At the gate Done plays the tune once more with claps, the band bows and the round ends with one sticker. At levels 1–2 there are no misses and no hints, so a round finishes with both at 0. After six idle seconds before the first tap the turtle pad pulses once, which is not a hint. Fast is never the better pace. The band plays at half volume while the pet is speaking, and the background loop is silenced. At levels 1–2 the ghost finger taps turtle, then rabbit once the turtle tune has been heard whole, then Done; at level 3 it reads the sign, taps that pad, waits for the audition and taps Check. Nothing is saved. Not checked on a device: whether 60 and 90 sound clearly different from an iPad speaker, whether a two-year-old reads the pads and the pending ring, whether the drum masks the melody, and how device speech says turtle and rabbit.
