## Design notes

*After Neopets shops and pretend shops.* Tap shell coins worth 1, 2 and 5 onto the mat; tap one on the mat to take it back (the nearest coin to the finger); ring the bell. Price tags show the numeral and dots. Running totals are said aloud except where adding up is the puzzle (two items, giving change). Later levels pay two prices together, pay the same price a second, different way, give change from a customer's 10, then pay prices from 4 to 10 with the fewest shells. An exact but longer payment at that last step stays on the mat with an invitation to swap; repeating the invitation counts as a miss. Wrong amounts get a boing and a specific spoken explanation: coin values rather than coin count, one shell off, one of two prices, returning the price instead of change, or a coin bigger than the amount. Other totals get a too-much/too-little comparison. Two misses show faint coins; help counts once per order, even if both ways of paying need support. A full counter speaks a free invitation to swap coins. At levels 2 and 3 the first tap of a coin that is new at that level (the 2 at level 2, the 5 at level 3) has the pet name it instead of counting ("That is a two. It is worth two.") and the purse coin pulses; this happens once per game start and is neither a miss nor a hint. At levels 1–3 every coin (purse, mat and faint hint coins) also shows one ink pip per shell of value, five to a row, under a smaller numeral, on a Market Stall layer only, so the pips can be matched to the tag's dots; the hub icon, sticker and Lemonade Stand's coins are unchanged. At mix (3), ways (5) and fewest (7) the two-miss hint first says a strategy line ("Try the biggest coin that fits first, then fill up what is left.", or on a ways level's second payment "Try different coins: swap a big coin for smaller ones, or smaller ones for a big one.", since biggest-first could repeat the first way), then shows the faint coins as before; still one hint per order. Each round seeds a fruit, flower or balloon stall with its own awning and keeper. Flowers and balloons are named by color so two-item orders stay distinct; coins, tags, mat and payment rules are shared. The sticker keeps its original six-fruit recipe.
