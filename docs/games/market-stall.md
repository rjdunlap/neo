**Grows into:** naming the one, two and five-shell coins (level 14, between levels 2 and 3), ones and twos, fives, adding two prices, paying two different ways, giving change from 10 by counting up, paying with the fewest shells, telling how much is in a purse (level 8), paying 11 to 20 with a ten-shell coin (level 9), adding two prices past ten (level 10), making change from 20, deciding what a purse can buy and trading small coins for larger ones without changing their total

## Design notes

*After Neopets shops and pretend shops.* Tap shell coins worth 1, 2 and 5 onto the mat; tap one on the mat to take it back (the nearest coin to the finger); ring the bell. Price tags show the numeral and dots. Running totals are said aloud except where adding up is the puzzle (two items, giving change). At levels 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7, the levels where the voice counts on, the total of the coins on the counter is also drawn on a small white slate on the counter's edge beside the bell (`drawTotal()`; `showsTotal`, `totalShown` and `TOTAL_SLATE` in `logic.ts`): it appears with the first coin, follows every coin put down or taken back, is hidden while the counter is empty, never changes colour (the bell is still the check) and is not drawn at levels 4 and 6 (the ten strip already counts up the change). The same `showsTotal` rule decides the spoken total, so a coin named for the first time says its name and the slate shows the total. Later levels pay two prices together, pay the same price a second, different way, give change from a customer's 10, then pay prices from 4 to 10 with the fewest shells. An exact but longer payment at that last step stays on the mat with an invitation to swap; repeating the invitation counts as a miss. Wrong amounts get a boing and a specific spoken explanation: coin values rather than coin count, one shell off, one of two prices, returning the price instead of change, or a coin bigger than the amount. Other totals get a too-much/too-little comparison. Two misses show faint coins; help counts once per order, even if both ways of paying need support. A full counter speaks a free invitation to swap coins. At levels 2 and 3 the first tap of a coin that is new at that level (the 2 at level 2, the 5 at level 3) has the pet name it instead of counting ("That is a two. It is worth two.") and the purse coin pulses; this happens once per game start and is neither a miss nor a hint. At levels 1–3 every coin (purse, mat and faint hint coins) also shows one ink pip per shell of value, five to a row, under a smaller numeral, on a Market Stall layer only, so the pips can be matched to the tag's dots; the hub icon, sticker and Lemonade Stand's coins are unchanged. At mix (3), ways (5) and fewest (7) the two-miss hint first says a strategy line ("Try the biggest coin that fits first, then fill up what is left.", or on a ways level's second payment "Try different coins: swap a big coin for smaller ones, or smaller ones for a big one.", since biggest-first could repeat the first way), then shows the faint coins as before; still one hint per order. Each round seeds a fruit, flower or balloon stall with its own awning and keeper. Flowers and balloons are named by color so two-item orders stay distinct; coins, tags, mat and payment rules are shared. The sticker keeps its original six-fruit recipe.

Level 6 counts up the change: each change coin says its value, then the shopkeeper counts up from the price ("2. 7… 8, 9"; the count line is templated, so it uses device speech), and a ten strip under the price tag shows price cells green and change cells yellow, with a red edge when too much change is on the mat. The rules are `countsUp`, `countUpWords`, `countUpText`, `countUpSaid` and `tenStrip` in `logic.ts`.

**What the guide repeats (level 5).** In the `ways` mode, "Thank you! Now buy another one, and pay a different way!" (`shop.again`) is an instruction (`ctx.instruct`), because paying the same way again is a miss and the standing instruction does not say to change; the next order instructs its own line. `shop.fewer`, `shop.full`, coin names, counts and hints stay `say`.

**Level 8, How much is in the purse? (2026-11-21).** No bell and no total slate: three purses a round of 3–6 coins (totals 3–12, largest first, at least one coin above 1, never the same total twice running) lie beside a pouch, and she taps the total from three number cards (the total, the coin count and a neighbour differing from both). A coin tapped on the mat says its value, free. A wrong card is a miss that names its kind (`shop.purse.coins`: that is how many coins; `shop.purse.near`); two misses count the purse on aloud largest first with each coin hopping and light the right card, one hint per purse. The rules are `makePurse`, `purseCards`, `purseMiss` and `countOn` in `logic.ts`; the `shop.purse.*` lines are device speech, not rendered clips.

**Level 9, The ten-shell shell (2026-11-23).** A `mix` plan with coins 1, 2, 5 and 10 and prices 11–20, four orders a round; any exact payment buys, and the running total is said and shown on the slate as at levels 1, 2, 3, 5 and 7. `makeOrders` now caps targets at `TARGET_MAX` (20) instead of 10, which changes no earlier level's draws (their prices never passed 10). The tag from 11 shows the first ten as one bar of ten green cells and the rest as dots, five to a row, inside the same 112 × 88 tag (`tagMarks` in `logic.ts`; up to 10 it is unchanged). The first tap of the 10 names it ("That is a ten. It is worth ten."; `coinToName` now names any coin new at its level), and paying by coin count gets its own line, `shop.values.ten`, which says the price and so stays on device speech. On the mat every coin is drawn at `matCoinScale` (80/88) so the 88-wide 10 fits an 80-unit column and sizes still rank; levels without the 10 use full-size mat coins. Four purse coins run nearer the right edge (`purseFor`) so their tap boxes fit the 768-wide upright view; three-coin purses are where they were. The hint is level 3's: biggest coin first, then the fewest set, which always starts with the 10 (greedy is the true minimum for these coins, tested against a full search).

**Level 10, Two baskets to twenty (2026-11-23).** Three orders pair two goods with distinct prices from 4 to 10. The sum is 11–19 and changes from one order to the next. The shopkeeper says both prices and asks what they cost together. The price tags stay visible and the player works out what to pay using 1-, 2-, 5- and 10-shell coins. Any exact payment buys both goods. If she pays one price, the shopkeeper asks her to add both. Two misses show the fewest coins that pay the total, with one hint counted per order.

**Level 11, Change from twenty (2026-11-23).** A `change` plan like level 6 with coins 1, 2 and 5, prices 11–19 and a new optional plan field `paid` (20; `paidFor(plan)` defaults to 10 and level 6 now says `paid: 10`). The customer's 20 is drawn as two ten-shell coins (`paidCoins`), the ten strip becomes two rows of ten (`tenStrip(price, given, paid)`, `stripLayout(paid)`) and the count-up voice ends on 20. Any change that totals right is accepted, not only the fewest coins. Misses: giving back the price says `shop.price20`; too little or too much says `shop.change20.short` or `.over`; one-off and counted-coins misses are as before. After two misses the hint lays the fewest change, smallest coin first (`countUpHint`, `hintCountsUp`), and says `shop.hint.countup` ("Count up from the price to 20: 13… 15, 20"), one hint per order. The instruction is `shop.change20`. Departure from the dive row, which said two misses glow the next coin that counts up: it is built as faint hint coins in count-up order plus the spoken count-up, with no purse glow.

**Level 12, Can I buy it? (2026-11-23).** An `afford` plan, coins 1, 2, 5 and 10, four orders: two choose orders, then two spend orders. Choose: tap a good the purse covers; nothing is spent. The first too-dear tap is a miss, a later one is the nudge, which lights the cheapest affordable good (one hint per order). Spend: pick two goods, a free put-back, a third pick refused free; the bell with fewer than two picked is free; a wrong pair is a miss and stays picked; two misses light the right pair (one hint per order). Departures from the definition: the purse amount is folded into `shop.afford.ask` and `shop.spend.ask` (no `shop.afford.purse`), the shelf is shifted right by `SHELF.shift` (40) so the leftmost good stays inside the awning, the mat coins are drawn at scale 1.25, and `LEVELS.school.order` was added with level 13 and places Trade up before Can I buy it?. Rules and tests are in `logic.ts` and `logic.test.ts`; the placement is covered by `upright.test.ts`.

**Level 13, Trade up (2026-11-23).** The school ladder ends 11, 13, 12. Three orders begin with 10 ones, then two seeded heaps worth 6–12 shells with no repeat in adjacent orders. Tap coins to pick them, then tap Trade when they add up to a 2-, 5- or 10-shell coin. Undo restores the last heap in its original order. The slate keeps the starting total and the bell accepts any heap with the fewest coins. Early bells and unusable selections are free. The second early bell lights the coins for the first exchange on a shortest path or lights Undo when no exchange path reaches the fewest. Further bells can refresh the help, with one hint counted per order. The exchange rules are in `trade-logic.ts` and the heap and control handling are in `trade-counter.ts`. Coins have 100-unit cells in two rows of six on a centred mat. Undo, Trade and the bell sit 170 units apart along the foot. The short layout keeps the mat above the caption, and the awning clears Help. The four fixed feedback lines have Kokoro clips. The slotted instruction, exchange and completion lines use device speech.

**Level 15, Which costs more? (2026-11-28).** A `more` plan with no coins, played in pre-K only between levels 2 and 3, after *Which coin?* (pre-K 2, 14, 15, 3, 4; `entryLevels: [14, 15]`). Four orders each stand two goods on the afford level's outer shelf places (`MORE_SHELF`), tagged with two different prices 1 to 10 that are 1 to 6 apart (`makeMoreOrders`); the dearer good is on each side in exactly half the orders, and a pair never repeats running. "Which costs more, the apple or the pear?" (`shop.more`); she taps a good. The dearer one is a bell and a cheer ("10 is more than 5"). The other is a boing and a miss that lays both prices out as dots side by side on the counter mat, the matched dots green and the dearer row's extra dots yellow (`compareRows`), and asks which row is longer; the second miss of an order glows the dearer good (one hint per order). No bell, purse or slate. Incidental fix: `matCoinScale` returned −0 for a plan with no coins; it now returns 1.

**Level 14, Which coin? (2026-11-27).** A `which` plan with coins 1, 2 and 5, played between levels 2 and 3 (preschool 1, 2, 14; pre-K 2, 14, 3, 4; not in the school ladder, and `entryLevels: [14]` starts a school player carried there at level 3). Four orders ask for each coin once in a shuffled order, then one more that is not the last asked (`makeWhichOrders`). Each order shows one good whose tag is the asked worth, so a muted round still shows which coin, and the shopkeeper says "Give me the five-shell coin!" (`shop.which`). The coins carry pips as at levels 1–3 (`hasPips`). No bell and no slate: one tap on the asked coin hands it over, the shopkeeper counts its pips aloud ("1, 2, 3, 4, 5", `pipCount`) and takes it. Another coin is a boing and a miss that says what that coin is worth and stays in the purse; the second miss of an order glows the asked coin (one hint per order, `coinGlow`, which follows the coin when the phone turns). The coin's first-tap naming of levels 2, 3 and 9 does not apply here.

**Upright on a phone (2026-11-19).** `stallLayout` in `logic.ts` places the stall, and `purseFor`, `AWNING`, `COIN_HIT`, `BELL_HIT` and `MAT_BOX` live there too, tested over every level of every band and five upright views (plus 360 × 800) in `upright.test.ts`. Held upright the whole stall moves down the tall screen instead of sitting at the top over an empty lower half: the awning starts at 220 or lower (clear of Home), the counter sits at the larger of 580 and 45% of the height, capped so its front stays above the purse, and the awning is drawn from `awningTop`. Sideways keeps the old layout.
