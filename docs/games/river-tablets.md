## Design notes

*Levels 1–6 · [definition](../definitions/river-tablets.md) · ancient-river-civilizations slices AR2–AR3.* She presses a clay record of real things and lines it up against them one to one, the way people by the big rivers once did. Preschool plays levels 1–2, pre-K 2–3, school 3–5. Nothing is kept; the sticker is the round's only reward. Music is the calm `STYLES.paint`.

**1 One token each:** 3–5 sheep walk into the pen one at a time (child-paced: the next sets off when the last has its token). She taps the jar for each sheep; a tap while a sheep walks, or for a sheep that already has its token, only wobbles the jar. When every sheep has one the jar glows and a tap tips it: the tokens line up under the sheep one to one and the pet counts both aloud. Nothing is counted (0 misses, 0 hints); the jar glows after eight idle seconds, uncounted. **2 Marks on the envelope:** two clay balls of 3–6 tokens (they differ); tokens drop in while she watches and the ball closes. She presses a cell of the 8-cell face (2×4) for a mark and presses again to lift it (free), and taps the ball's rim to peek inside for two seconds (free, unlimited). Crack is the one commit: the tokens line up under the marks. **3 Which row?:** two tablets; a delivery of 4–6 goods (sheep and jars, both present, at most 4 of either, mixed order) arrives one at a time on a cart. She presses the row (the sheep row headed by a sheep picture, the jar row by a jar) and the mark lands in its next cell. Afterwards the storehouse opens, the goods line up under their rows and the pet reads the tablet ("Three sheep marks, three sheep..."). The second envelope's match, or the second tablet's read-back, finishes.

**4 A ten is a different mark (school):** two carts per round (12–29 grain; cart 1 packed with 2–9 loose, cart 2 spilled with 11–19 loose and 0–1 crates). A ten stamp and a one stamp press big and small marks onto two strips (caps 3 and 19); tapping a strip lifts its last mark, tapping the tablet's read band reads it aloud, tapping the yard makes the pet count it (crates by tens, then each basket) and tapping a crate opens it and counts its ten, all free. **5 Check the delivery (school):** two carts against farm tablets (12–29; the cart is the tablet plus or minus up to 3, at least one off). Three pictured verdict buttons, short, right and extra (spoken on touch); Right commits, short or extra open number buttons 1–3 and the number commits. **6 Two tablets disagree (school):** a farm tablet (left) and a storehouse tablet (right) are set against one cart, two cases per round. In one case the cart matches one tablet; in the other it matches neither, or it is empty ("the baskets were already shared out"), where *can't tell* is the only right answer. Tapping a tablet reads it aloud (free); a "this one" hand sits under each tablet, with *neither* and *can't tell* between them (110–120 units).

**Misses and help.** Level 1 has none. Level 2: a mismatch on Crack is one miss (unmatched tokens or marks wiggle, the pet names it, the ball closes with her marks kept); after the second miss on an envelope the fix glows (the next empty cell when there are too few marks, the last mark when too many) and counts one hint, once per envelope; the pet's Help is the same glow and the same single hint. Level 3: a wrong row is one miss (the mark bounces back and the pet says which row has the picture); on the second wrong press for one good the pet slides the mark into the right row (one hint per tablet) and from then the waiting good's heading glows for the rest of that tablet; Help glows the waiting good's row and is that tablet's one hint if it is not spent. Peeks, lifted marks and the read-back are free. Level 4: Hand in is the one commit. The shortest form matches; an equal longer reading (ten small marks for a ten) is accepted with no miss or hint and the pet shows "ten small marks can be one big mark"; otherwise one miss, the lineup shows the mismatch and the marks stay; after the second miss (or Help) a glow leads one step at a time toward the fewest marks, one hint per tablet. Pressing, lifting and the yard count are never counted. Level 5: a wrong verdict or number is one miss with the pet stating the tablet's count; after the second miss or Help the lineup plays and holds with mismatches glowing, one hint per cart. Level 6: each wrong pick is one miss with a spoken reason (a tablet that does not match, or that an empty cart cannot be checked against either); after the second miss on a case, or on Help, the cart lines up under each tablet in turn with leftovers glowing and held (an empty cart glows), one hint per case. Reading a tablet is never counted.

**History.** One line is spoken once at levels 2–3 and on the card: "Long ago, by the big rivers, people pressed marks in clay to remember how many."
