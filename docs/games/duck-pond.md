## Design notes

Duck Pond alone uses the prepared R22 illustrated duck, with frame-driven bob/squash and unchanged touch circles (other games retain their drawings). Count along as ducks hop in; then put N in, how many?, adding and taking away. The dots beside a number are there to count. Level 10 (make ten): 3–9 ducks swim in. "How many more to make 10?" is answered on lily pads. The missing ducks then swim in to fill the pond's ten places ("7 and 3 make 10!"). A wrong pad counts on aloud from the ducks already there. Level 11 (hiding ducks): the sign gives a whole from 5 to 10, some ducks paddle beneath a code-drawn bridge, and the child finds the hidden part on the lily pads. A wrong pad counts on from the visible ducks to the whole. A right answer brings the hidden ducks out one by one, counted aloud, into separate pond seats. At levels 8–11 the sign then shows the addition or subtraction sentence and the pet reads it; reading is not needed to answer. Level 12 (Bye-bye ducks, ID 12; lap and toddler entry, placed first in their order): five ducks swim on the pond, and tapping one makes it wave and swim off while the voice counts down ("Bye-bye! 4 left"). After the last, the mother duck calls and all five swim back, counted up. One song is one round; there are no questions, so no misses or hints are possible. A departing duck takes no further taps.

Levels 4–5 (put N in) make stopping her decision. The bank always holds one duck more than the sign's top number, every duck can go in, and each is counted aloud as it lands in the first free place. A green check beside the sign ends the round: with exactly the sign's number the ducks quack and the pet says "That's 5 ducks!"; with too few or too many it is a boing, the pond is counted together and the pet says "Oops, that's 6 ducks! We need 5." (one miss). A duck tapped on the pond hops back to its own place on the bank ("Back you go! 5 ducks in the pond.") and counts nothing. From the second miss on a question the duck to move glows (a waiting one while too few swim, a swimmer while too many), or the check once the number is there, and the question counts one hint. Level 3 still ends by itself at the number. The ghost finger brings ducks in up to the number, then taps the check.

Levels 6–7 (how many?) deal each round a dice face or two rows of five (level 7 also a scatter), counted back row first, so a number is not always the same picture. A pond may be empty once in a level (never first; the pads then read 0, 1, 2 and the voice says "None!"), and the pads carry their dots in fives under the numeral. **Mixed ponds (built 2026-11-24):** one round of level 6 and two of level 7 (never the first, never the empty pond) seat ducks and frogs on lily pads in the same layout and ask for just one kind ("How many frogs?" or "Count just the ducks"); both kinds are always there, so the answer is never the total, and the voice counts only the asked kind, on a wrong pad as well as a right one (`howManyRounds`, `mixedRound`, `mixedKinds`; frogs are `Duck`s of kind `frog` drawn with the shared `frogArt`). Each play also takes one of three backdrops (sunny, golden evening, a greener frog-pond sky and hills); the reeds and lily pads were already in the pond, and the night pond with fireflies is not built. The starting ponds of levels 8–9 are dealt the same way: level 8 seats its ducks in a dice face or two rows of five, level 9 adds a scatter. A taking-away story deals its layout for the ducks that start (a dice face included); an adding story deals one for every duck that will swim, with no dice, so the first ducks come in and the rest take the seats that follow. Once in a level 9 (never first) all the ducks swim away: the pads read 0, 1, 2, the voice says "None!" or "Zero! The pond is empty!" and the sign writes the sentence ("4 − 4 = 0").

Seating for the school levels still to come: 11 to 20 ducks sit in two ten-frames side by side on a widened pond (small ducks, the left ten filled first). Answer pads can reach 20, with dots kept at 10 or fewer. The footbridge split is used by level 14 and the two ten-frames by level 15.

Level 14, **Doubles**, appended after the lap openings (ids 12 and 13), so the school window is 8–15 with order `[8, 9, 10, 11, 14, 15]`. The footbridge splits the pond: three rounds of equal sides (1 + 1 up to 5 + 5), then two near doubles (one more on a side), never the same total twice running. The left side swims in from the left and the right from the right, each bobbing as the instruction names it ("1 duck" when it is one). She taps the pad with the total (pads 1–10). A wrong pad says "4 on this side, and 4 more" and counts on across the bridge; a right one is counted aloud and the sign writes "4 + 4 = 8". The finale ducks sit either side of the bridge. At every pad level (6–11, 14) the right pad glows from a question's second miss and the question counts one hint, however many more misses follow.

Level 13, **Friends**, is lap's first level (order `[13, 12, 1, 2]`; `entryLevels` `[12, 13]`). One big duck (2.4 times the size) is on the pond from the start and a layer over the whole screen takes every touch: the big duck quacks and cheers and a friend swims in from alternating sides, with the count spoken and shown on the sign with dots, up to five. Nothing holds the first touch (the instruction line is not awaited, and a touch during a swim-in brings the next); after the fifth a touch only quacks with a splash. After four quiet seconds the big duck hops, counting nothing. The round ends with a quack chorus, "Five friends…" and confetti, and no miss or hint can be reached. The ghost finger touches the water away from the duck. New lines `duck.friends` and `duck.friendsAll` have rendered clips. A touch in the bottom-left of the screen lands on the shell's pet corner, so the browser suite's touch spots avoid it.

Level 15, **Ten and some more**, is the school level after Doubles. A full ten pops onto the left ten-frame at once with one splash, then 1 to 9 ducks swim into a second ten-frame on the pond (ducks at 0.68). Rounds alternate: how many in all (pads 11 to 19), then the sign shows the total and she finds how many are on that side (pads 1 to 9); the ones never repeat twice running. A wrong pad counts on from ten ("Start at ten and count on!") or bobs the ten and counts just the ones; the glow comes from the second miss, one hint per question. A right answer writes "10 + 4 = 14" and the pet reads it. The finale narrows the pond back to five full-size ducks.

Level 16, **Every way**, tops the school window (order `[8, 9, 10, 11, 14, 15, 16]`; `rounds` counts totals: 3, 4, 5, 6 ducks, fourteen ways in all). All the ducks start on one side of the footbridge (the left in the first round, either side after). Tapping a duck sends it across to the nearest free seat; tapping it again brings it back; every tap is allowed. The sign reads the live split ("3 and 2"), and the green check keeps the split: a new one slides a card into a row of faint outlines (one fewer than the total, never counted aloud), a repeat pulses its card ("We found that one!"), a swapped one ("2 and 3" after "3 and 2") is a new card and the pet says the same ducks swapped sides, and a pond with a side empty is not a split yet (the check is dim and the pet asks her to send some across, once). The second repeat in a row glows one duck toward the nearest unfound split and counts the round's one hint; there are no misses. The round ends when every outline is filled. The ducks are 0.9 size at six so six fit a side; the card row is as wide as 120 per card allows, narrowing upright. Rules in `logic.ts` (`everyWay`, `keepVerdict`, `nextHintMove`, `everyMove`, `everySeats`), the card row in `every.ts`; the how-to card's demonstration shows the first two totals.

Level 17, **Count the peekers**, sits between levels 6 and 7 (preschool `[3, 4, 5, 6, 17, 7]`, pre-K `[5, 6, 17, 7, 8, 9]`; 17 is in `entryLevels` so a school save of it lands on school's first level). Four rounds of 4 to 10 ducks (the first at most 6) peek from 13 fixed hiding places on a taller pond; each place is reeds or a standing lily leaf, chosen at random and jittered by up to 5. Spots are at least 123 apart so jitter keeps ducks at least 108 apart; a tap area is a circle of radius 54 centred 60 above the duck's feet. Each round first asks "a few, or lots?" with two buttons, never a miss. Each tap on a duck rings and counts it; a ringed duck only quacks and is never counted twice. Pads are dotted, 1 to 10. A wrong pad is a miss: the rings are counted aloud, then the pet says how many are counted and asks who is still peeking (or, with every duck ringed, "{n} rings, so {n} ducks"). The second miss counts one hint and the uncounted ducks glow (the right pad if all are ringed). The right pad clears the rings and says "{n} ducks!". Mode `peek`; rules in `logic.ts`; the `duckpeek.` voice lines are outside the rendered clip list, so they stay on device speech.

Level 18, **Which side has more?**, sits between levels 7 and 8 on pre-K's ladder (`[5, 6, 17, 7, 18, 8, 9]`; 18 is in `entryLevels`). The footbridge splits a taller pond (half-height 190) into two sides with 1 to 10 smaller ducks each (scale 0.68, three across and four rows a side, mirror images). Five rounds: the first is four against six, lined up, and the ducks pair up before the question as a model; unequal sides differ by one to four; "the same" comes in at most one round (never the first; about three plays in five have one); no answer comes three times running; from the third round each duck starts on a random seat of its side, jittered by up to 8, so a longer-looking side is not always the bigger one. The targets are the two halves of the pond and a "the same" button (two matching triangles of dots) in the pad row, offered every round so its presence never gives the answer away. Any answer pairs the ducks up if they have not yet: each side lines up outward from the bridge, each pair bobs and gets a ring, and the ducks with no partner hop and glow. A wrong answer is a miss with a spoken hint; the second counts one hint and the right side (or "the same") pulses. The question is always "which has more" (or the same); "fewer" is left for later, and the pet never says how many more, which Picture Graph owns. Mode `compare`; rules in `logic.ts`; the `duckcmp.` voice lines stay on device speech like level 17's.
