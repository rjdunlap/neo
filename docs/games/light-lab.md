## Design notes

*After laser and mirror puzzles.* Mirrors on a grid turn a sunbeam a quarter; tap one to tilt it. Flowers wake when the light reaches them and let it pass on; colored glass tints the beam, and a pink flower only wakes in pink light. Puzzles are built backwards from a random beam path, with spare mirrors and rocks placed off it, and start unsolved and not nearly solved: no answer, spare mirrors included, is fewer than a floor of turns away (1 turn at levels 1–3, 2 at 4–5, 3 at 6–7), and at levels 4–5 the starting tilts wake no flower (rule-tested over 400 seeds a level, including that following hints always solves). A hint glows the first mirror that differs along the nearest answer (the fewest turns from where the mirrors are now), not the built path. From level 3 the sun is seeded on either side: sometimes in the last column shining left, with every tilt mirrored; levels 1–2 keep the left sun. Levels 1–2 show the beam live as mirrors turn (no misses possible); from level 3 the child plans and then taps the sun, which draws the beam cell by cell. A shine that misses is a miss, explained aloud (off the edge, a rock, the wrong color, one flower still asleep); two misses make the next wrong mirror on the known path. School level 7 adds three unscored predictions: the mirrors are locked, and the child taps one of three reachable rock or edge endpoints before the beam is replayed with an explanation. A fourth board returns to planning with the mirrors unlocked. **Levels 8–9, *Place a mirror* and *Two to place* (school only; the school range is 2–10, pre-K stays 1–4).** A wooden tray sits under the board (6×4 at level 8 with one fixed path mirror tilted wrong and one tray mirror, three puzzles; 7×4 at level 9 with two tray mirrors and a rock on the sun's row, two puzzles). She drags a mirror onto any free cell (it snaps to the nearest free cell and the pale cell under it shows where; a drop off the board floats back to the tray, free), taps a placed mirror to turn it, and taps the sun to shine. A sun tap with a mirror still in the tray is a spoken reminder, not a miss. A missed shine is a miss with the usual spoken reason; two misses glow the cell for the nearest answer's next mirror (one hint), moving to a mirror dropped there the wrong way, and once every answer cell is filled the glow shows a mirror to turn. The generator rejects any board solvable with fewer tray mirrors than it gives, and the sun can sit on either side. **Level 10, *Where's the mirror?* (school only).** A 6×4 board, three boards, with one mirror on the sun's row turning the beam up or down into a flower on the top or bottom edge (the sun on either side). A cloud covers every cell but the sun's and the flower's; the flower is already lit, and the beam shows only where it goes in and comes out (and in cells already uncovered). She taps the cloud where the mirror must be. The right cell puffs every cloud and the beam shines. A wrong cell is one miss, its cloud puffs away and a pale blue ghost beam shows where a mirror there would send the light; the second miss glows the sun's row and the flower's column (one hint, no second miss line). Lines `light.find`, `light.find.miss`, `light.find.hint`. Couch control() and autoplay move a ring over the clouds; the ghost finger taps the crossing. **Level 11, *Through, bounce or stop?* (pre-K and school; pre-K order 1, 2, 3, 4, 11, school order 2, 3, 4, 11, then 5–10).** A straight beam from the sun to a sleeping flower, with a thing on a stand in its path: a clear jar, a cloudy wax paper, a blocking thing or a shiny tin lid. Three free guesses first (through, bounce or stop picture cards for each of three things); a guess never counts as a miss, and the beam then shows what the thing really does (a clear thing lets it through, a cloudy one dims it so the flower wakes softly, a solid one throws a shadow wash, a shiny one bounces it to a flower). Then two judged choices from a tray of three things: wake the far flower, shade a sleepy owl or bounce the beam to the flower. A wrong pick is one miss with a teaching line that names the thing (`light.mat.*`, `{thing}` filled by device speech); two misses raise the glow on the right thing (one hint). Lines `light.materials` and `light.mat.*`. The couch has no spots at 11, so couch play is unchanged. **Level 12, *Split the sunbeam* (school only; school order 2, 3, 4, 11, 5–10, 12; pre-K unchanged).** A 7×5 board, two puzzles a round, with a half-mirror (drawn with a darker silver stripe) that sends the beam both straight on and turned (the bounced way, like a mirror). Both arms after the split are dim and still wake a flower. Each puzzle has one trunk turn, the half-mirror, an arm to each of two flowers (each arm needing 0–1 mirrors), two spare mirrors and one rock; the generator accepts a board only when the split is required (swap the half-mirror for a mirror, or remove it, and no answer exists) and the start is dark and at least two turns from an answer. She tilts mirrors and taps the sun; the half-mirror tilts too. Misses come only from shines, each with its own spoken reason (`light.miss.unsplit`, `light.miss.sleeping`, `light.miss.split`); two misses glow one tiltable thing along an answer (`light.hint.split` when it is the half-mirror), which always leads to an answer in the fewest turns. Lines `light.split` (the instruction) and `light.split.yay` on the first solve. Not in couch play or the Sunbeam Run course. Off-screen: shine a torch at a window pane at an angle; some light goes through and some bounces onto the wall. Couch play plays levels 4–6 (unchanged), and its puzzle shelf holds **Sunbeam Run**, a par course of five frozen boards (from two mirrors to turn to four, a sun on either side, two flowers, coloured glass; par from the solver's fewest turns, 2 + 2 + 3 + 4 + 4 = 15). Every mirror turn counts, even one turned back, and a shine that misses counts one more; two missed shines glow the next mirror and mark the run helped. Leaving or reloading puts that board's mirrors back and keeps its tries. Badges: Finished and Fewest turns.
