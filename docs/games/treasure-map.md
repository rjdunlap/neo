## Design notes

*After Battleship and treasure-hunt maps.* A parchment grid where row 1 is at the bottom, as on maps and graphs. Levels: picture rows and colored columns ("the apple row, the red column"; level 1 draws its row pictures from seven (apple, fish, star, flower, shell, a red heart, a balloon) and shuffles the column colours each round), letters and numbers ("B3"), putting a tree, house, boat or flag at a named square, following directions from where the pet stands ("2 left, then 3 up"; the pet then walks it, counting), and two landmark clues (for example, "the tree is 2 right" and "the boat is 1 up") whose lines meet at the treasure. Level 6 *Write the card* (school) turns the steps level around: she sets a two-line card beside the map, a count tile (taps cycle 1, 2, 3) and an arrow tile (right or left) for across, then a count and up or down, and footprints (two per square) trace the card on the map as she sets it; a line that would run off the map stops at the edge ("That is the edge of the map!"), not a miss. Go on an unfinished card asks for both lines (not a miss). Go walks the pet along the card, counting; ending on the chest finds it, otherwise it is a miss with an X where the pet stopped and a reason naming the line that was off ("Across was right, but up is one too many", "The down line was right. Across goes the wrong way", or both), the pet returns to the start and the card stays to mend. Two misses glow the across line from the pet and speak the level 4 step hint. Four finds a round, one chest showing at a time; tiles are 110 units and Go 220 by 94. A sign shows each request in pictures or letters. A wrong dig leaves a mark and says what was right ("right column, now look along the row"); two misses glow the target's column and row—or, for landmark clues, the two clue lines ("Follow the two glowing lines to where they meet"); on the steps level the hint lights the first leg from beside the pet to the turning square ("First go 2 left. Count along the glowing squares from the pet."), never the answer. Level 7 *How far?* (school) is a hidden chest on a 4 by 4 map, three finds a round, each a new chest; the sign shows a chest and "?". A dig the distances so far allow leaves a dug patch with a footprint stack (one print per step, toes up, two columns) and the number, and the pet says "{n} steps away"; it is never a miss. A dig the distances rule out is a miss (a boing, an X, and "That square is {got} from your dig at {square}, and that dig said {said}"). Digging a dug square again repeats its distance, and tapping a crossed-out square repeats its reason, both free. Two misses glow the squares that still fit and charge one hint. Any fitting order finds the chest within five digs. The bot digs A1, then the still-fitting square that best splits what still fits. Level 8 *Slide it* (school) puts a three-square L raft with a flag on a 5 by 5 map; the sign and pet say e.g. "Slide the raft 2 right and 1 up. Tap where the flag ends up!" (vectors of 1 to 3 each way, the raft on the map before and after). A wrong square is a miss with the column or row reason (`map.col`, `map.row`, or neither right). The right square keeps a flag, and the question moves to three outline cards (150-unit mini maps beside the map, the start raft faint): the raft slid, the raft turned over (mirrored across the flag's column, so the flag still matches), and one slid a square too far or short on one axis. A wrong card is a miss with a named reason ("That one turned over…", "That one slid 3 right and 1 up. Our raft slides 2 right and 1 up."); re-tapping a crossed-out card repeats it free, and a map tap during the cards is free. Two misses in either part count one hint: the flag's walk glows on the map, or in the cards part the right card glows. The right card slides the whole raft; three rafts a round. The bot taps the landing, then the right card. Level 9 *One line* (preschool entry; `order` 9, 1 first, then 2 for pre-K) is a 3 by 3 map with picture rows and shuffled colour columns, 120-unit squares (grid scaled 1.2). The pet asks "Dig in the red column!" or "Dig in the fish row!", the sign shows the one header with an arrow along its line, and any square in that line is right: the whole line shines, then a chest. Four finds a round, rows and columns alternating from a seeded first axis, no line asked twice. A wrong dig is a miss that names what she dug ("That is the blue column. Find the red column!"); after two misses on a find the line glows and one hint is counted ("Look at the glowing line. Dig anywhere on it!"). The bot digs the middle of a square in the line. Level 10 *Across the river* (school) is a 6 by 6 map with no headers, a river across between rows 3 and 4 and a road up between columns 3 and 4 (a bridge where they cross). The pet and sign say e.g. "Dig 2 squares right of the road and 1 square below the river"; the sign shows the road and river strips, a count tile and an arrow. One find per quadrant; the first is introduced by `map.riverintro`. A wrong dig is a miss with a boing and the first wrong part named (`riverReason`: wrong side of the river or road, or wrong count); two wrongs count one hint and light the target's column and row. Level 11 *Mirror twin* (school) stands a tree, house, boat or flag on a square and makes the mirror line shimmer; she taps where its twin goes (`map.mirrored` says the twin appears turned over: upside down across the river, flipped across the road). A same-side or wrong-square tap is a miss with `mirrorReason`; two wrongs count one hint and light the mirror path. School runs levels 1 to 11 (`order` 1 to 8, 10, 11); the sign sits beside the map in landscape and above it in portrait (when the view is at least 400 units taller than 600). Couch play ignores both modes.

Each round's parchment takes one of three tints and a corner doodle (compass, sun or ship), cosmetic only.

**On the couch.** Grown-ups play levels 2 to 5 (levels 6 to 11 are touch only) with a controller: the stick, D-pad or arrow keys move a teal ring one square at a time (up is the next row up, since row 1 is at the bottom), and the bottom button digs, or places the thing, on the ringed square; on the steps level the ring starts where the pet stands. Touch taps and the ring share one dig. The score is wrong digs (lower wins), so a face-off gives each player a fresh seeded map; a bot walks the ring across the columns, then the rows, and digs.
