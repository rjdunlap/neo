# Couch play

How to set up and play the grown-ups' couch route on a Mac with a TV, controllers or a keyboard. It runs in a desktop browser on the computer; the game does not run on the Switch console. How it works inside is in [DESIGN](DESIGN.md#couch-play); open questions for real play are in the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md#needs-a-person-or-a-device). Controller code can be exercised with simulated devices, but physical Switch controllers, Joy-Con pairing and TV latency still need your hands-on check.

## Pair your original Switch controllers

1. On the Mac, open **System Settings → Bluetooth**.
2. Hold **SYNC** until the player lights flash. On a **Pro Controller**, SYNC is beside the USB-C port. On a **Joy-Con**, detach it from the Switch and use the small SYNC button on its inner rail.
3. Select the controller in the Mac's Bluetooth list and connect. Pair each Joy-Con separately if trying the pair.
4. Open Puddle Island in an up-to-date desktop browser, focus the tab, and press a controller button. Use **Couch play** on the title screen (or the **C** key). A standard controller's bottom face button can also enter couch play after being released and pressed again.
5. Open **Controller setup** in couch play and check the detected count. The first standard controller is Player 1; the second is Player 2. If the browser reports an unmapped controller, use the keyboard or an already-owned Pro Controller for this prototype. Do not assume two Joy-Con halves are exposed as two usable standard controllers.

Mac controller customization, when available, is under **System Settings → Game Controllers**. If a controller keeps returning to the Switch, wake/pair it with the Mac again. To return it to the Switch later, use the Switch's **Controllers → Change Grip/Order** pairing screen (or the original Joy-Con rails).

References: [Apple's Bluetooth controller connection guide](https://support.apple.com/en-us/111099), [Nintendo Pro Controller diagram](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/27538/), [Nintendo Joy-Con diagram](https://en-americas-support.nintendo.com/app/answers/detail/a_id/22634/). The development Mac was running macOS 26.6.2 when these notes were made. Bluetooth pairing alone does not verify browser/game compatibility.

## Play tonight, including without controllers

**Couch play** is a separate grown-up route with its own save. A trip has six stops; each completed round lights one lantern and keeps one couch sticker, including rounds completed with help. Players take turns choosing from three games. The child profile, level pins, adaptive history, stories and sticker book are independent. Either player can operate the chooser; passing one controller is fine.

**Playing alone.** Choose **Just me** under *Who is playing?* on the start page (or on Settings). The start page becomes *Just for you*: a **Puzzle shelf**, and **Start a trip** for six games together (a face-off needs two, so it is not offered). Player 2, turn cards and the face-off tally are left out everywhere. The **Puzzle shelf** is the eight challenge courses (two in Penguin Slide, one in Bouncy Launch, two in Sudoku Garden, one in Lantern Lights, two in Picture Logic) with nothing to unlock: under each is your best and how far it is above par (the fewest tries the course can take), for example “21 slides · 3 above par (18)”, or “par: the fewest possible” for a perfect run. A course page has one card of marks, and **Play** or **Play again**. Once you have finished a Penguin Slide course (and nothing is half played), or right after a run ends, **Watch the best routes** replays the fewest slides for each pond, one at a time (pick Pond 1 to 5): it is a replay on the real pond, scored and saved nowhere, and it is not offered in the middle of a run, where it would be a hint you did not ask for. Switching back to two players brings the second set of marks back; nothing is lost either way, and a trip already under way finishes the way it began.

**Starting a trip.** Choose **Together** (shared lanterns, nobody wins or loses) or **Face-off** (each stop has a winner; both players still light every lantern). **Start another trip** after a finished one returns to this choice.

**How to play.** The first time a game is chosen, a screen shows its name, a spoken one-sentence goal, a drawn controller with the buttons it uses, and a window where a bot plays a real round with the same controls. **Play** (bottom button) starts; **Back** (right button or Esc) returns to the choices and leaves the game unchosen. After that, the game shows a short name card (press the bottom button to skip). **How to play** is always in the pause menu and returns to the pause menu.

**Earning games.** Three games are open at the start. Each finished trip, in either mode, opens the next group; the trip-complete screen names the games just opened and says how many more are still to come. Opened games are marked NEW until their how-to screen has been seen. Nothing locks again, nothing expires, and the choice of games never depends on how well anyone played. A free **Shuffle the choices** appears once more than three games are open.

**Settings.** **Settings** is a button on the couch start page, and a controller alone can reach it (D-pad down to the button, bottom button to open). Each change takes effect at once and is kept in the couch save and its backup; none of it touches the child's island or the grown-up panel behind its gate. The page holds:

- **Where I'm playing.** *Choose for me* (the default) shows controller pictures and button names when a controller is connected or the window is the size of a TV, and keyboard keys otherwise; the page says which it picked and why. *TV with a controller* and *Laptop with keyboard* are firm choices. It changes the how-to screens (a drawn controller or a drawn keyboard, button glyphs or key caps), the controls shown under a round, and the hint line under the menus. Games and controls themselves are the same either way: a controller and the keyboard both always work.
- **Sound.** Quieter and Louder (five steps, from off to full), and Music on or off. This is the couch's own volume; the island keeps its own and gets it back when you leave couch play.
- **Text size.** Normal, Large or Extra large, on top of the scaling to the window (about 1.4 times at 1080p). Extra large can make a page taller than a 1080p screen; it scrolls as the controller moves.
- **Who is playing.** *Two players* (how couch play began: trips together or face-off, a pair of marks on every course) or *Just me*, which is also on the start page. See **Playing alone** below.
- **Names.** Optional. Type a name for each player (a keyboard is needed once; W, S, space and backspace type letters there). Names replace Player 1 and Player 2 on every screen and in what is spoken. With *Just me* there is one name, which replaces “You” on your marks.
- **Couch backup** and **Controller setup** (pairing help), reached from here.

The island's own grown-up zone (her name, session length, levels, backup) is separate and has no button: on the island map, press and hold both top corners for three seconds. The Settings page says so at its foot.

The pause menu has a row of **Quieter**, **Louder**, **Music** and **Text** so sound and text size can change mid-round.

**The finale.** The sixth lantern ends a trip with a night sky: six lanterns rise and light, blue for what Player 1 took, pink for Player 2, gold when shared, with a scoreboard (face-off), the six stops, the games the trip opened, and, after the first finished trip, a Lantern Night keepsake that stays on the couch start page. Press the bottom button on **Start another trip** to go again.

**Challenges.** On the mode screen (or the start page) choose **Challenges**, then a course. It is a fixed run in one game, scored by how few tries it takes, and a try you undo or miss still counts. **Pond Practice** is five gentle Penguin Slide puzzles with no dead ends (stick or D-pad to slide, left face button to undo; the fewest possible is 18 slides), a warm-up or an easy game. **The Five Ponds** is five that take real planning (the fewest is 30 slides). **Cloud Hopper** is twelve small clouds in a row in Bouncy Launch (push down or right to squash the spring for more power, up or left for less, bottom button to launch; the fewest is 12 launches, one per cloud; two misses at a cloud bring a hint). Each player keeps their own best, separately for runs with and without a hint, and two badges per course. Leaving mid-pond or mid-cloud (or reloading) starts that part again with the tries you made still counted. In a pond, **Start this pond again** in the pause menu does the same without leaving: the penguin goes home, every fish comes back, and the slides so far still count. A run is one couch sticker.

**Sudoku Garden** is a grown-up puzzle that is on the couch only. Fill the bed so every row, column and box holds each number once. The stick or D-pad moves around the bed (hold to repeat); the bottom button on a square opens a **number tray** at the right (a tile for each number, with how many are left to place, and a pencil); in the tray the bottom button places the highlighted number, or, on the pencil, switches pencil marks on so it marks instead; the left button closes the tray, or takes a number (or its marks) back out of a square. A number that repeats in its row, column or box turns red; nothing is refused. Every number placed is one **entry** (a replaced number is another; taking one out and pencil marks are free), and the fewest possible is the bed's empty squares, which is par. The pause menu has **Show a hint** (a number you placed that cannot be right first, otherwise the next number the easy steps can place, with the reason spoken and written above the bed; a hint marks a challenge run as helped) and **Start this bed again** (entries already made still count). **Six Beds** is six 6 by 6 beds climbing from a few numbers to find to the only-place-left trick (the fewest is 124 entries); **Three Big Beds** is three 9 by 9 beds, an evening's puzzle (149). In a trip it plays 6 by 6 beds: in a face-off each player gets their own bed, and the one with fewer extra entries wins.

**Lantern Lights** is a second grown-up puzzle that is on the couch only. A pond of paper lanterns, each lit or dark: pressing one (bottom button) flips it and the four lanterns beside it, and the goal is every lantern lit. The stick or D-pad moves from lantern to lantern (hold to repeat) and a faint glow shows what a press would flip; the left button takes the last press back. Every press counts, even one you take back, and the fewest possible is the pond's par (worked out exactly by the game, so "par: the fewest possible" is a real thing to reach). The pause menu has **Show a hint** (a lantern in a fewest-press way; pressing it always brings the fewest down by one; a hint marks a challenge run as helped) and **Start this pond again** (presses already made still count). **Dusk on the Pond** is a 4 by 4 pond and four 5 by 5 ponds (the fewest is 36 presses). In a trip it climbs from 3 by 3 to a 5 by 5; in a face-off the one with fewer extra presses wins.

**Picture Logic** is a third grown-up puzzle that is on the couch only. A grid hides a picture: the numbers beside each row and above each column are the lengths of the runs of filled squares in that line, in order. The stick or D-pad moves (hold to repeat; your row and column are tinted); the bottom button fills a square (a filled one empties); the left button crosses a square out (a crossed one lifts), to mark where nothing goes; and **holding** either button while you move paints a whole run. A clue turns grey when its line matches and red when no way of filling the line fits what you have marked. When the filled squares are exactly the picture's, it takes its colors and says what it is. Every fill counts (a square emptied and filled again counts twice; crosses and emptying are free) and the fewest possible is the picture's filled squares. The pause menu has **Show a hint** (a mark that cannot be right first, otherwise a square one line decides, naming the line and its clue; a hint marks a challenge run as helped) and **Empty this picture** (fills already made still count). Every picture can be solved by line logic alone, with no guessing. **Pond Pictures** is a sailboat, a cat and a duck on 10 by 10 grids (the fewest is 182 fills); **The Big Pictures** is a lighthouse and a butterfly on 15 by 15 grids, an evening's puzzle (230). In a trip it plays 6 by 6 and 10 by 10 pictures; in a face-off the one with fewer extra fills wins.

**Face-off.** Penguin Slide, Bouncy Launch, Light Lab, Secret Code, Peg Garden, Sudoku Garden, Lantern Lights and Picture Logic give each player their own fresh board in turn (never the same hidden board twice), scored against that board's own best, so different boards compare fairly: slides over the best route; distance from the cloud centres; extra turns and missed shines; guesses; shots; entries over the empty squares; presses over the fewest; fills over the picture. Lower wins. Memory Match is one shared board where players alternate and a match earns another turn; most pairs wins. Bounce Back, Rhythm Neighbors and Bumper Garden are team stops: both players score. Ties score for both. A stop's first turn is saved, so leaving between the two turns changes nothing. One couch sticker is kept per stop, not per turn.

Games open in this order, one group per finished trip: Penguin Slide, Bouncy Launch and Bounce Back at the start; then Memory Match and Rhythm Neighbors; Light Lab and Secret Code; Peg Garden and Bumper Garden; Pattern Train and Egg Catch; Frog Hop and Sink or Float; then Robot Path; then Sudoku Garden, then Lantern Lights, then Picture Logic (all three always on the Puzzle shelf, as their courses).

Button prompts use **physical positions**, because Nintendo's printed A/B/X/Y letters can differ from other controllers' standard browser mapping. Single Joy-Con orientations and custom mappings are not implemented yet. A pet takes the pink paddle in Bounce Back until a second controller or W/S joins.

The controller reminder along the bottom of a round fades after ten seconds (it shows again when a pause ends, and **How to play** in the pause menu always has it). A lost window focus or controller disconnect pauses the round. Reconnect, or resume using the keyboard. Pause also offers how to play, instruction replay, a different game, and return to start. The browser may need one mouse click or keyboard press before it will play sound; controller input alone may not unlock audio.

Finished stops and sticker counts save immediately on this browser/device. Reloading or leaving an unfinished round restarts that round with the same seed. The next choices do not reroll unless you press Shuffle. Use **Couch backup** (on the Settings page) to download or restore the separate couch save (version 3; older saves are upgraded). It includes the keepsake, the challenge records and your settings. A restore replaces couch progress only. Child backup/reset does not include couch progress. Clearing site data removes both. Local couch storage is a versioned, bounded record: the trip's six stops, a pending first turn, one count/latest sticker seed per game, the games already explained, the completed-trip count (which is what opens games), the keepsake, and per course a run in progress, each player's two bests, their last ten runs and badges.

Each couch game has a bot used for its how-to demo. The browser suite also uses it to prove the controls can complete a round: `BROWSER_SUITE=couchgames` plays every game, `BROWSER_SUITE=couch` plays a full trip and a face-off trip, and `BROWSER_SUITE=couchcourse` plays the challenges with controller presses (the Five Ponds and Cloud Hopper twice each, one helped run and one perfect, and Pond Practice once), `BROWSER_SUITE=couchbeds` plays Sudoku Garden's Six Beds the same way, `BROWSER_SUITE=couchlanterns` plays Lantern Lights' Dusk on the Pond, and `BROWSER_SUITE=couchpictures` plays Picture Logic's Pond Pictures. Hold keys for at least ~120 ms when scripting key presses: the couch scene samples input once per frame, and a new couch scene ignores input for its first 0.35 s.

## Put it on the TV

Connect the Mac to the TV with an available display connection, move the browser to the TV, and enter browser full screen. Try the TV's Game Mode if input feels delayed. Keep the Mac close enough for Bluetooth. Couch pages scale with the window (about 1.4 times at 1080p; Settings has Large and Extra large), so judge whether text is comfortable from the couch, paddle feel and sound delay before deciding whether the pacing needs changes. If sound is off, the status line says so: a controller button alone may not wake the browser's audio, so press any key or click once.

The [live site](https://rjdunlap.github.io/neo/) has whatever is deployed from `main`. To try local changes, run `npm run dev` and visit **http://localhost:5173**.

Planned next for the couch (more puzzles for the shelf: par courses on the puzzles that exist, more grown-up puzzles beside Sudoku Garden, typing and word games, the daughter joining as a co-pilot, simultaneous face-off boards, Joy-Con support) is in the [roadmap](ROADMAP.md#grown-up-play-the-couch-route-for-one). There are no leaderboards across trips; a face-off compares only the two players in the same trip.
