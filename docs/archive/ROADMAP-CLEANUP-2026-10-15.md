## Next up as it stood

Scheduled work uses the [execution queue](../EXECUTION-QUEUE.md): one fresh project chat per slice, usage checks before dispatch, checkpoints between runs, and the same claim and verification rules as a human-started session. The queue selects from this roadmap; it does not change readiness or resolve developer decisions.

The [family experience review](../FAMILY-EXPERIENCE.md) adds a quality track alongside catalog growth: discovery, mode presentation, art and feedback, connected adventures and meaningful shared roles. Finish owned work first; then interleave bounded quality slices with content and ladder slices. Research is a complete docs-only task, and does not claim a game implementation.

Most sessions should build from this list; checks that need a person or a device never block it. Check effort follows the [verification budget](../../AGENTS.md#verification-budget). Roughly in order. Each chunk has a **status**, **one session's next slice**, the **check tier** it needs and where its **detail** lives:

- **ready**: the work is defined and nothing blocks it.
- **sketch**: a first-draft definition of ready exists; review it against the [planning rules](../ROADMAP.md#planning-rules), then build.
- **define**: an idea only; write the definition of ready first (the next slice is that write-up, a docs-only change).
- **decision**: waits on a call from the developer, listed under [Waiting on the developer](../WAITING-ON-DEVELOPER.md#waiting-on-the-developer).
- **person**: waits on someone playing it ([Needs a person or a device](../DEVICE-CHECKS.md#needs-a-person-or-a-device)).

## Lands as it stood

**Decided (the developer, 2026-10-09).** Now that a profile sets a person's age, the map no longer has to choose one. The island becomes **themed lands**, in the spirit of JumpStart's rooms and Neopets' lands, **plus an "all my games" home spot**. A land shows **the games for the person's age and younger**, with a **"for bigger kids" signpost** to that land's older games. The **couch puzzles come onto the island both ways**, by touch and by controller, a stage at a time, and couch play as a separate shell is retired at the end. Everything else here is a proposal until its stage is built.

**Why.** The age trail made the map the age selector: each place was one swipe path of every game for its band, 37 to 69 icons over 5 to 10 screens. A grown-up, who maps to `school`, landed in Wonder Woods, which leaves out 22 island games (Bumper Garden, Peg Garden, Fluffy Salon, Bounce Back, Duckling Parade among them), and the six couch-only puzzles were not on the island at all. The ten subject regions were given up for the trail on 2026-10-06 (commit `74f0e7c`) for two reasons that no longer hold: there were no profiles, so the map was where an age was chosen, and the region map's clouds hid what a band had no games for. Profiles now choose the age, and the signpost replaces the clouds without hiding anything.

**The shape**

- **The profile sets the age, the map is the world.** Every game has one home land and plays at the person's own levels. Nothing is gated.
- **Lands.** The ten subjects become ten lands on one map screen: Bubble Beach, Music Mountain, Paint Pier (the art subject, until now called Treehouse, which clashed with the pet's treehouse), Barnyard, Counting Cove, Cozy Village, Rainbow Meadow, Puzzle Peaks, Story Grove and Tinker Lab. - **The home spot.** One spot on the map is the person's home, drawn as their age place: Puddle Lagoon for lap up to Wonder Woods for early school. It opens today's swipe path (or subject cards) with every game for their age and younger. The age places live on as "your home", and a birthday changes the home spot; the trail between them is gone.

- **Levels.** `playBand` plays a game at the person's band when it has it, otherwise at the highest band below, otherwise at the lowest above (the rule the picnic already uses). `go.game` no longer refuses a band the game lacks.
- **Finding things.** A twinkle on unplayed games and on a land on the map that holds one; a host who suggests a game (one not played yet first, then the one played longest ago); the pet waiting at the last land visited; new games twinkling after a birthday. Favorites stay on the home spot's shelf for now.

6. **A tween band and place**, only when a game needs it.

**Settled later on 2026-10-09: a birth month and year, and a friend for grown-ups.** What follows from each:

- **Birth month and year is kept for every profile**, grown-ups included, on the device only. It is part of the backup file, so the backup page should say so. It is bounded twice, deliberately: **on load** only by shape (a month from 1 to 12, a whole year from 1900 to 2100), and **when a person types it** by the clock (a year no later than now and no earlier than 120 years ago). A clock-relative rule at load would let an iPad with a wrong clock drop a birth for good the next time the index is written; with the shape check a wrong clock only changes the start.
- **Old saves.** The current profile has only a band, so it keeps `profile.band` as its start until a birth month and year is entered once in the grown-ups' page; an old backup restores with none. Nothing changes for her until then.
- **A grown-up's friend needs a pet kind.** The saved pet is `{ name, color, hatched }` and `petSpec()` is Pip in a color, so choosing among the critters means adding a bounded `kind` (the seven existing designs; the *Choose your pet's kind* row in [IDEAS](../IDEAS.md#world-variety-animals-pets-and-environments), size M). Stage 1 can ship with Pip in a chosen color if the kinds are not ready, and gain kinds right after.
- **Voice.** The island's lines are written for a small child. A grown-up's friend wants a plainer set, with fewer exclamations and no "little one". The couch already has about 50 lines of its own, and `couchLine` swaps in a controller version where an original talks about touch, which is the precedent to extend.
- **No birthday at the top band**, since the band does not advance there: a profile aged 13 or more has nowhere further to climb. (This follows from the age, not from a kind of person; see below.)

#### Settled later on 2026-10-09: couch play is a way of playing, not a kind of person

The developer's answer on how the app knows someone is a grown-up: it should not. Couch play (a controller or keyboard on a bigger screen, the trips and face-offs) is **a form factor or a game mode a person can choose at any time, at any age**, and is not tied to being a grown-up. What follows:

- **A profile record has no child/grown-up flag and no mode field.** Age sets only the band and where levels start. Whether to hatch an egg or pick a friend is a choice in the add-a-player screen made from the age typed, and is never stored as a type.
- **Stage 1 has no `grownup` band** (stage 3 adds it), so ages 6 to 12 and 13 and over all map to `school`, the top band that exists. Nothing is lost: a person of any age can already open any place.
- **The mechanism was a proposal; the developer approved it and slice 1b built it. The constraint stays.** Until stage 2 the island scenes take no controller, so a controller user on the chooser must be able to reach the couch shell with the stick and the action button, and a controller confirm must never leave someone on an island they cannot drive. The leading proposal: the input used to pick a card picks the mode (a tap or click opens the island; a controller button or Enter opens the couch shell with that profile as Player 1), plus an explicit "play with a controller or keyboard" choice on each card for a mouse user who wants it. This replaces the earlier plan that a grown-up's card opens couch play.
- After stage 2 the same choice becomes "which input do I use", and the couch shell's trips and face-offs become Game night (stage 5), available to anyone.

#### Stage 1 definition of ready

**Slice 1a: the storage layer (built 2026-10-09, no visible change).** `src/progress/profiles.ts` (pure, tested) and the active-profile `Store` in `src/progress/store.ts`.

- **Storage.** `neo.save` stays the first profile in place. `neo.profiles` is a new index, `{ version: 1, active, profiles: [{ id, legacy, birth, startBand, addedAt }] }`, and a further profile's save is `neo.save.<id>`, each with its own `.previous` undo snapshot. Birth month and year and a *start here instead* band live **in the index entry and never in the save**: `migrate()` rebuilds a save from a whitelist, so an older cached build re-saving `neo.save` would strip any field added to it. The index is never empty (a missing or corrupt one becomes the one legacy entry, so today's app behaves as before), holds at most 8 profiles, and an index that could not be read is never written over.
- **Age to band.** `bandForYears` encodes the accepted boundaries, with 6 and over all `school` for now; a profile with no birth keeps its saved band; a *start here instead* band wins. A wrong clock only changes the start.
- **Switching.** `store` stays "the active profile's save" and its 17 importers do not change. `switchTo` reads the target before it swaps, flushes the pending write of the profile it leaves under that profile's own key, and never exposes default data.
- **Backup.** One file for every profile, `{ kind: 'puddle-island-backup', version: 3, … }`. Restoring **replaces a profile with the same id (keeping its own undo snapshot), adds an unknown one, and never deletes anyone**. A single-profile backup from today's build still restores, replacing the active profile, as it does now. Parsing is strict, so `{}`, an array or a couch file is refused instead of restoring a blank.
- **Accepted limits.** The layer has no screen until 1b. An older cached build restoring a *new* backup reads it as a blank profile (its lenient `migrate`), recoverable only by that build's one undo snapshot; the PWA updates only after the app is closed, so this is a short window.

**Slice 1b: the chooser and what a person sees (built 2026-10-09).** The build notes are in [the archive](COMPLETED-2026-10-09.md#profiles-stage-1-the-definition-of-ready-as-written-and-what-was-built), and the contracts in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md#persistence-and-grown-up-ui). It builds the leading proposal above as the developer approved it.

**Left out of stage 1 on purpose** (each waits for something that does not exist yet):

- **The comfort questions** (text size, pace, help, calm visuals, contrast) at add-a-player. The island has no pace, help, calm or contrast settings to bind them to; asking questions that change nothing would be worse than not asking. They arrive with the settings, as the [comfort options](../IDEAS.md#fun-and-arcade-review-2026-10-09).
- **A device-level sound setting.** Sound is still saved for each player and the grown-ups' page says so. Building it properly means a device record beside the index, seeded from the first profile's volume.
- **A friend's `kind`**: everyone has Pip in a chosen color (an older person skips the egg), until the pet `kind` exists.
- **Couch records are the slot's, not the person's.** Whoever enters couch play becomes Player 1 and shares that slot's course records. Linking records to people is stage 5.
- **The island does not take a controller yet** (stage 2), which is why Enter and a controller button open couch play.

## Grown-up play as it stood

*Direction changed 2026-10-09: this route folds into one map ([Profiles first](../ROADMAP.md#profiles-first-one-map-for-every-age)), and the map becomes themed lands with the couch puzzles on it ([Lands](../ROADMAP.md#lands-navigation-and-discovery)); new grown-up games are built for the map. The steps below still name the games and features to re-home.*

Couch play began as an evening for two grown-ups taking turns. This section turns it into something one person can pick up alone, and gives the developer's wife a reason to. Everything here is a **proposal** until built; the candidates, with their sources and controls, are in [IDEAS](../IDEAS.md#grown-up-play-candidates-couch-route), and the grown-up rules (no lives, streaks or dailies; a score is a personal best in its own unit; a timer never ends a round) are stated there.

**What the first playtest and the code say**

- **The fun was a fixed puzzle with an exact best number and one precise control:** slides over a known minimum, launches over twelve clouds. Every item below tries to be that.
- **The island already holds the cores of the classic puzzle genres.** Garden Rows is a Latin square (sudoku's cousin, uniquely solvable, up to 5×5), Pixel Pictures is nonograms that line logic alone can solve, Ferry Jam is Rush Hour with solver-checked harbors, Secret Code is Mastermind, and Light Lab, Robot Path and Penguin Slide each have a solver. Most of the new puzzles are scale, a controller `control()` and a par, not new engines.
- **One player is second-class today.** The start page and every record assume two people; Player 2's empty marks sit beside hers. Eight of the fourteen couch games are team stops, and five of those (Pattern Train, Egg Catch, Frog Hop, Sink or Float, Robot Path) were added as short, gentle games that map easily to a stick, so a six-stop trip alone mixes puzzles with toddler-pace games, and new games open one tier per trip, which paces a trip, not a puzzle. After a course there is no way to see what the best route was.
- **The island has none of the genres Neopets' own games lean on:** typing (Typing Terror), word chaining (Spell or Starve), match-three (Sutek's Tomb), snake-style steering (Meerca Chase), solitaire (Pyramids); and none of the number puzzles (sudoku and its kin, bridges, Lights Out).
- **She will mostly play on the TV with a controller, in several places** (the developer, 2026-10-07). So the couch route is controller-first with a keyboard fallback. Until step 1 the couch had no settings of its own: names were typed on Controller setup, volume and music changed only in the child's parent panel, which opens by holding both top corners of the island map for three seconds and has no visible button (so nobody had found it), and the text size followed the window. Where she plays, how loud it is and how big the text is now sit on one visible page; how many play joins it with step 2.
- **Letters, a timer and pressure are real design choices.** A typing game needs a keyboard, which the couch route reads only for a few keys today. Arcade games usually end in failure; here they end in a count (bonks, tries) and never a game-over.
