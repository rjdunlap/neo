## Direction

Puddle Island is an iPad learning game for the developer's daughter, who turns one in October 2026, in the spirit of JumpStart's playful learning, Neopets' companion and personal world, and the variety of classic arcade games. It should grow with her from lap play through elementary school, to about grade 5 (age eleven), on one familiar island where younger favorites never disappear. Four threads carry it:

1. **Many small, satisfying games with a clear learning purpose**, where the skill causes the fun: sharing fills plates, a pattern moves a train, a revised route reaches a friend. Familiar games grow deeper modes; a new game earns its place with a new kind of decision or interaction.
2. **A personal world**: her pet, its treehouse, things she made, and a discovery journal. Nothing decays, expires or waits for her.
3. **Short stories that connect games**: a friend needs help, two or three activities change the scene, and there is a complete ending. Free play stays one tap away.
4. **Games for grown-ups, on the same map** (decided 2026-10-09; the profiles front door, themed lands and Lantern Lake are built; touch support for four puzzles and the controller/Game night migration remain open: [Lands](#lands-navigation-and-discovery) stages B to E). Couch play is still its own shell, with its own save, trips, face-offs and challenge courses, and is now a way of playing any person can choose from **Who's playing?**. The direction is one app for every age: a landing screen of profiles, a person's age setting where the map and levels start, and no separate route. See [Profiles first](#profiles-first-one-map-for-every-age).

**How work gets judged right now.** She is not yet one and an iPad is not readily at hand, so the immediate feedback loop is grown-ups playing in a desktop browser. Device and child observations are listed below and never block building; equally, grown-up enjoyment is not evidence that a child is ready for something.

## Where we are

- **90 games** in eleven themed lands on the island map (Bubble Beach 4, Music Mountain 6, Paint Pier 4, Barnyard 6, Counting Cove 11, Cozy Village 12, Rainbow Meadow 7, Puzzle Peaks 11, Story Grove 9, Tinker Lab 12, Lantern Lake 8), each showing a person its games for their age and younger with a signpost to the bigger kids' ones. By band: lap 39, toddler 50, preschool 63, pre-K 76, early school (ages 6–12) 57 and grown-up (13 and up) 8 have levels of their own. The newest child's game is **Fruit Sums** (school, in Counting Cove; Number Trails, preschool and pre-K, is the one before); the newest grown-up one is **Gem Swap**, alongside seven others in **Lantern Lake**. Every game has rule tests, a grown-up how-to card and adaptive levels; the [README](../README.md#the-island-and-its-games) lists them.
- **The child's island:** pet hatching and customization, a sticker book, a gold twinkle on unplayed games, a favorites shelf of hearted games, **themed lands** with hosts who suggest a game and a signpost to bigger kids' games, a home spot (her age place) with every game for her age on one path or as subject cards, and a **how-to card every time a game opens**: its goal, how this level plays (held arrows beside "this level" step to another level in her band), how the round ends, and for **every one of the 90 games** a bot-played demonstration (a ghost finger, or a mouse pointer on a desktop). A grown-up can turn the cards off, and tapping "again" after a round skips one.
- **One story:** the Windy Picnic, with a journal, resumable steps and a keepsake.
- **The treehouse:** six free furnishings, a framed sticker, a picture board for a kept Stamp Studio picture, Rainbow Fingers painting or Pixel Pictures design, a tune plaque for a free Song Maker song, and a discovery journal of twenty-five observations from Sink or Float, Animal Snack, Photo Safari, Seesaw Balance, Bouncy Launch and Habitat Helpers.
- **Profiles (stage 1):** the front door is **Who's playing?**, a card for each person (their friend and name) and Add a player (name, birth month and year, friend). A tap opens the island map, with the home spot their age gives; Enter, a controller button or the card's own controller button opens couch play with that person as Player 1. The grown-ups' page edits any player (birth, a chosen start band, remove, one backup of everyone), and the map's top-left button comes back to the chooser. Nothing has been judged on an iPad or with real controllers.
- **Couch play:** twenty-three controller-ready games (fifteen from the island, and **Sudoku Garden**, **Lantern Lights**, **Picture Logic**, **Word Search**, **Island Bridges**, **Pond Conga**, **Tile Pairs** and **Gem Swap**, the eight grown-up games in Lantern Lake), six-stop trips in Together or Face-off mode, how-to screens with bot demos, a finale and keepsake, thirteen challenge courses with per-player records, a Settings page, and a Just me mode with a puzzle shelf. The player guide is [COUCH-PLAY.md](COUCH-PLAY.md).
- **Checked so far:** everything above has unit tests and recorded browser checks in headless Chrome. Nothing has been judged on an iPad, with real controllers on a TV, or with a child. What is deployed is whatever `origin/main` holds (`git log origin/main`).

### Additional languages

**Decided (the developer, 2026-10-11): additional languages are wanted.** Which languages and in what order is not decided; see [Waiting on the developer](#waiting-on-the-developer). The reference is JumpStart Spanish (1997, ages 3–6): ten clubhouse activities, each in two levels (hear the words, then reach a goal), plus folk songs, a calendar and puppet-show phrases; its reviewer found it rarely went beyond naming nouns, so the island's version should reach phrases, counting and songs ([IDEAS](IDEAS.md#jumpstart-title-by-title-2026-10-11)).

**What the code does today** (read, not changed):

- Every spoken line is an id in the flat `SCRIPT` in `src/content/voice-script.ts` (about 1,260 ids, each an array of English variants with `{name}`, `{pet}` and per-line placeholders). `LineId` is its key type.
- `src/audio/voice.ts` speaks with the device's own speech, and `pickVoice` keeps only voices whose language starts with `en`; its rate and pitch (0.95 and 1.15) were tuned on English voices.
- Some code reads the English text directly: the journal (`SCRIPT[entry.line][0]`), couch captions, and tests that check game data against a line. The how-to cards, the grown-ups' page and the couch pages are English strings outside the script.
- The save has no language field; settings are bounded and repaired in `src/progress/save.ts`. Parent-recorded voices are parked (the no-audio-files rule).

**What travels and what does not.**

| Kind | Examples | Plan |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Translates directly | Naming pictures (animals, colors, shapes, body parts, food), number words and counting, feelings, weather and clothes, days and months, public-domain songs, the pet's praise | Stage 1 and 2 |
| Needs its own design per language | Letter names and sounds (Word Monsters, Letter Trails), rhyme families (Rhyme Time), syllable counts (Clap the Syllables), reading tiles, opposites' picture pairs | Stage 3, each a deep dive of its own; a language's alphabet and sound rules can differ |
| Stays English for now | The grown-ups' page, how-to cards, couch captions | Until a grown-up asks |

**Stages** (each a docs-first definition, then its own build):

1. **The pet says the word.** A per-person setting in the grown-ups' page (profiles exist, so the language follows the person, as a bounded and repaired field in `save.ts`) makes the picture-naming lines add the second-language word after the English one: Peekaboo Barn, Animal Snack, Bubble Pop, Rainbow Fingers' color names, Duck Pond's and Frog Hop's number words. The second language is a table keyed by the same `LineId`s for only the lines it covers; a missing line falls back to English rather than going silent. A unit test asserts that every translated line has the same `{placeholders}` as its English line. The voice picks a device voice that matches the language tag and, if the device has none, the grown-ups' page says so and speech stays English. Stickers, levels and misses are unaffected by language.
2. **Instructions and praise in the language.** An option for older bands (or a family that speaks it): the whole voice switches, the journal and captions read through a `lineText(id)` helper instead of `SCRIPT[id][0]`, and the how-to cards stay English until translated.
3. **Language-specific play.** The small games JumpStart Spanish suggests, designed per language: hear-then-goal word rooms, a days-and-months calendar, folk songs, puppet-show phrases, and any literacy game that has to change (a deep dive each).

**Rules that hold.** No recorded audio files; English is the default and is always one tap away; nothing is gated by language; a language setting never changes difficulty or save keys; check actual device speech before claiming a pronunciation, as for phonics; and a native speaker reviews every line before it ships. Checks: stage definitions are docs only; stage 1 is the save and shell tier (typecheck, unit tests including placeholder parity, build, the suites of the games it touches).

## Family experience: quality and range

**Design review, 2026-10-12; proposals, not built.** The requested direction is a one-stop family play world in the spirit of Neopets and JumpStart. The [full review](FAMILY-EXPERIENCE.md) connects catalog gaps, primary research, navigation, levels, code-drawn visual design and game feel. It contains a matrix of experience families, concrete first-round sketches and a shortlist of research sessions. This does not change the existing game contracts, approved ladders or developer decisions.

**Design priorities.** Make discovery and presentation grow alongside the library. A player should be able to find a favorite, choose something unfamiliar, understand its input and mode, enjoy a readable response, and return to where they started. Strong candidates offer different reasons to play: cause-and-effect toys, pretend play, making and performing, exploration, serving and planning, construction, investigation, deduction, movement and genuine shared roles. A theme variant adds worthwhile content; a new identity needs a distinct action or decision.

**Recommended next sequence after owned work:** stage B’s known return/input-label gap; the [discovery action sequence](#discovery-actions) (D1a’s first build packet, direct Next game, then Little surprises); one Barnyard or Animal Snack feel pilot; a mode-gallery definition; then one connected episode and one role-swapped family activity. Interleave those bounded slices with the existing content and ladder work. This is a design recommendation, not a scheduler-policy change or a requirement to stop building until device research happens.

**Discovery research is written** in [DISCOVERY.md](DISCOVERY.md); chunk 19 now selects D1a’s precise screen/pool packet, followed by the bounded routing/mix builds, with optional support/automatic choices still open. **Research ready to undertake:** chunks 14 and 24 remain docs-only game studies, with their tracker and one-game claim workflow. Chunks 20–23 begin with separate system definitions; chunk 19’s [action table](#discovery-actions) states the next packet, dependencies and exits. Each study ends with a small complete round or flow, alternatives/mistakes/support, replay variety, a visual/feel example, relevant couplings and proportionate future checks. A functioning bot proves supported input and rules, not whether a person enjoys or understands the game.

**Range for older players.** Grow beyond deduction alone: an expressive tool, a cozy investigation, a comfortable arcade skill and shared planning all deserve space. Keep younger favorites accessible. Use the existing school and grown-up bands until a separate band decision is made; challenge, reading and motor precision are different demands. Keep the Harbor economy behind its existing person check, and retire couch play only after its replacements work.

**Quality exit evidence:** a person can find and return to an appealing activity; the action and consequence read with sound off; the next mode introduces a meaningful decision; a fresh arrangement still makes sense; both family roles contribute where offered; and a player chooses to replay, revise or show the result. Record actual observations, not session length or rewards collected. Device speech, reach and child fit stay separately open.

## Lands: navigation and discovery

**Decided (the developer, 2026-10-09).** Now that a profile sets a person's age, the map no longer has to choose one. The island becomes **themed lands**, in the spirit of JumpStart's rooms and Neopets' lands, **plus an "all my games" home spot**. A land shows **the games for the person's age and younger**, with a **"for bigger kids" signpost** to that land's older games. The **couch puzzles come onto the island both ways**, by touch and by controller, a stage at a time, and couch play as a separate shell is retired at the end. Everything else here is a proposal until its stage is built.

**Why.** The age trail made the map the age selector: each place was one swipe path of every game for its band, 37 to 69 icons over 5 to 10 screens. A grown-up, who maps to `school`, landed in Wonder Woods, which leaves out 22 island games (Bumper Garden, Peg Garden, Fluffy Salon, Bounce Back, Duckling Parade among them), and the six couch-only puzzles were not on the island at all. The ten subject regions were given up for the trail on 2026-10-06 (commit `74f0e7c`) for two reasons that no longer hold: there were no profiles, so the map was where an age was chosen, and the region map's clouds hid what a band had no games for. Profiles now choose the age, and the signpost replaces the clouds without hiding anything.

**The shape**

- **The profile sets the age, the map is the world.** Every game has one home land and plays at the person's own levels. Nothing is gated.
- **Lands.** The ten subjects become ten lands on one map screen: Bubble Beach, Music Mountain, Paint Pier (the art subject, until now called Treehouse, which clashed with the pet's treehouse), Barnyard, Counting Cove, Cozy Village, Rainbow Meadow, Puzzle Peaks, Story Grove and Tinker Lab. A land is one screen with a host (Juniper the gardener in Rainbow Meadow, Hazel the postkeeper in Cozy Village, a familiar critter elsewhere), its games and its own tune. A land has its own id, apart from the subject, so later lands (Lantern Lake, then the elementary zones below) can hold games from several subjects.
- **The home spot.** One spot on the map is the person's home, drawn as their age place: Puddle Lagoon for lap up to Wonder Woods for early school. It opens today's swipe path (or subject cards) with every game for their age and younger. The age places live on as "your home", and a birthday changes the home spot; the trail between them is gone.
- **What a land shows.** The games whose lowest band is at or below the person's band, so younger favorites never disappear and a grown-up sees all of them. A **signpost** shows the land's older games, played at their easiest band. A grown-up's *start here instead* still moves the whole start.
- **Levels.** `playBand` plays a game at the person's band when it has it, otherwise at the highest band below, otherwise at the lowest above (the rule the picnic already uses). `go.game` no longer refuses a band the game lacks.
- **Finding things.** A twinkle on unplayed games and on a land on the map that holds one; a host who suggests a game (one not played yet first, then the one played longest ago); the pet waiting at the last land visited; new games twinkling after a birthday. Favorites stay on the home spot's shelf for now.
- **Coming back.** A game remembers where it was opened (a land, the home spot, the journal or a story), so home and "again" go back there.

### Stage 1 definition of ready

**Slice 1a: the storage layer (built 2026-10-09, no visible change).** `src/progress/profiles.ts` (pure, tested) and the active-profile `Store` in `src/progress/store.ts`.

- **Storage.** `neo.save` stays the first profile in place. `neo.profiles` is a new index, `{ version: 1, active, profiles: [{ id, legacy, birth, startBand, addedAt }] }`, and a further profile's save is `neo.save.<id>`, each with its own `.previous` undo snapshot. Birth month and year and a *start here instead* band live **in the index entry and never in the save**: `migrate()` rebuilds a save from a whitelist, so an older cached build re-saving `neo.save` would strip any field added to it. The index is never empty (a missing or corrupt one becomes the one legacy entry, so today's app behaves as before), holds at most 8 profiles, and an index that could not be read is never written over.
- **Age to band.** `bandForYears` encodes the accepted boundaries, with 6 and over all `school` for now; a profile with no birth keeps its saved band; a *start here instead* band wins. A wrong clock only changes the start.
- **Switching.** `store` stays "the active profile's save" and its 17 importers do not change. `switchTo` reads the target before it swaps, flushes the pending write of the profile it leaves under that profile's own key, and never exposes default data.
- **Backup.** One file for every profile, `{ kind: 'puddle-island-backup', version: 3, … }`. Restoring **replaces a profile with the same id (keeping its own undo snapshot), adds an unknown one, and never deletes anyone**. A single-profile backup from today's build still restores, replacing the active profile, as it does now. Parsing is strict, so `{}`, an array or a couch file is refused instead of restoring a blank.
- **Accepted limits.** The layer has no screen until 1b. An older cached build restoring a *new* backup reads it as a blank profile (its lenient `migrate`), recoverable only by that build's one undo snapshot; the PWA updates only after the app is closed, so this is a short window.

## Older-child progression: a market week, not an island grind

The useful part of a child-sized economy is making a choice, seeing its consequence and revising a plan. The [Coolmath Lemonade Stand retrospective](https://www.coolmathgames.com/blog/optimization-games-to-turn-you-into-a-business-tycoon) describes the appeal as optimizing several small variables, especially price against weather; the original Apple game likewise made demand depend on weather, price and advertising ([contemporary Apple classroom guide](https://archive.computerhistory.org/resources/access/text/2019/03/102783879-05-01-acc.pdf)). That loop fits the elementary horizon's data tables, fair comparisons and multi-step projects.

The research supports an age- and purpose-specific boundary, not a blanket ban on points, scarcity or loss. A recent review of primary-school gamification found generally positive motivation and engagement but mixed learning outcomes ([systematic review](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0883035524001666)); a classic meta-analysis found that expected tangible rewards can reduce later free-choice interest, especially for children, when rewards control participation rather than communicate competence ([Deci, Koestner and Ryan](https://selfdeterminationtheory.org/wp-content/uploads/2014/04/1999_DeciKoestnerRyan_Meta.pdf)). By contrast, a controlled primary-school financial lesson improved taught knowledge and willingness to save, though transfer was limited ([Cash Quiz field experiment](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1111/joca.12241)), and an experimental token game found more functional use of saving and banking between ages nine and twelve ([saving-strategies study](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0167487005000541)). Use currency when budgeting, risk, delayed access or tradeoffs are the content; do not pay for every ordinary round merely to manufacture repetition.

Neopets' wheels supply a different pleasure: a named place, a ceremony, anticipation and a surprising little world event. The official Wheel of Excitement charges a persistent currency, allows a spin only every two hours and can leave the player worse off ([Neopedia entry](https://www.neopets.com/neopedia.phtml?neopedia_id=45)). Those mechanics are not inherently unsuitable for an older child: a visible fee creates opportunity cost, a bounded loss gives a prediction consequences, and rarity can make probability worth inspecting. The risk rises when random rewards are joined to purchases, hidden odds, rapid repeat buying, social pressure or limited-time availability. The UK government's loot-box review reports associations with problem gambling while emphasizing that the largely correlational evidence does not establish causation ([government response](https://www.gov.uk/government/calls-for-evidence/loot-boxes-in-video-games-call-for-evidence/outcome/government-response-to-the-call-for-evidence-on-loot-boxes-in-video-games)). Puddle Island has no purchases or online market, but should still make the odds, cost, alternatives and stopping point plain. UNICEF's RITEC work with children aged mainly 8–12 emphasizes autonomy, competence, creativity and meaningful choice; that remains the test, not whether a loop lengthens the session ([design toolbox](https://www.unicef.org/childrightsandbusiness/workstreams/responsible-technology/online-gaming/ritec-design-toolbox)).

### First slice: Lemonade Stand (built 2026-10-08)

**Built.** `src/games/lemonade-stand/`, school band (Wonder Woods), four levels: weather only (3 days), an event on the forecast (3 days), a price of 1, 2 or 3 shells (4 days), and a five-day market week where cups cost lemons and the purse carries over. Every rule in the original plan (in the archive) is in `logic.ts` and tested, and the game's own entry in [GAMES](GAMES.md#lemonade-stand) says what it does. **Where the build differs from the original plan (in the archive):** (1) a lemon makes two cups and costs one shell, so a batch of 4, 8 or 12 costs 2, 4 or 6 shells, because at one shell a cup even the best choice lost shells on a rainy day, and the plan says only overproduction should; the purse starts at 6, and the host tops it up to the smallest batch's cost when it falls below that; (2) the friends come one at a time under a SOLD OUT sign and the spare cups go to two friends on a picnic blanket, in place of SOLD and LEFT jars (the table carries the numbers); (3) help is a lightbulb button that says one true thing about today and circles the rows it is about (it is `helps()` in `logic.ts`, checked against the model), and asking counts as one hint for the day; (4) the pet's closing comparison is chosen by `insight()` from what the table can show; (5) there is no saved in-progress week and no "close the stand" button (the home button always works). *Not built:* a market-week chart in the journal, recipe quality and advertising, the couch, and everything in the two slices after this one.

The original plan, written before the build, is in [the archive](archive/COMPLETED-2026-10-09.md#lemonade-stand-the-plan-as-written-before-it-was-built).

### Second slice: a Maker Harbor fair board and tickets (proposal after the game is played)

This is the progression experiment, kept smaller than a global economy. A fair board presents named preparations such as **Make lemonade**, **Pack the dock crates** and **Route the ferry**. The Harbor awards tickets for completing distinct, visible work orders; completion with help counts, and an order says whether repeating it earns anything before play begins. Tickets do not replace stickers or adaptive levels and are not awarded for every arbitrary round. This makes their source meaningful and avoids teaching the child to farm the easiest game.

Begin with three useful places open. Let the child spend an early, attainable amount to choose which previewed booth or older-band game opens next; an unlock is permanent and never depends on speed, a high score or playing without help. Younger favorites and the existing lands never relock, and a grown-up can open every Harbor activity. That is genuine progression, but its purpose is anticipation, agency and a readable-sized Harbor—not a claim that the child proved mastery.

The fair may contain two **Harbor Fortune Wheels**, **Steady** and **Surprising**. Every segment is visible and spoken; before paying one ticket, the child predicts or compares distributions. For example, a ten-slice Steady wheel might have six small and four medium rewards, while Surprising has six common, two rare and two empty slices. A short history shows results as natural frequencies (“the lantern appeared 2 times in 10 spins”). Research with children around 9–11 supports teaching probability through concrete possible outcomes and frequencies rather than unexplained rarity ([probability intervention](https://www.nuffieldfoundation.org/project/childrens-understanding-of-probability-an-intervention-study), [natural-frequency study](https://www.frontiersin.org/journals/education/articles/10.3389/feduc.2020.00073/full)). Rare rewards are cosmetic, permanently available and previewable; a bounded collection guarantee or direct-price alternative prevents an endless chase. A spin may return nothing or less value than its fee because that risk was visible and optional. It never removes something already owned, sells a refill, hides its odds, expires a reward or uses a real-time cooldown.

The same tickets should offer guaranteed alternatives: decorate a booth, buy an item directly, save toward a new activity or take the optional chance. The learning value is in comparing those alternatives. If the child cannot name something worth saving for, the Harbor does not yet need persistent tickets.

### Third slice: Harbor Bank (proposal after tickets prove useful)

The bank is the deliberate exception to “no real-time waits,” because elapsed time, liquidity and interest are the learning content. It is always voluntary: ordinary play continues while tickets are deposited. Start with concrete terms, such as **deposit 10 tickets, receive 11 tomorrow**, then compare a liquid account with **11 tomorrow** against a certificate with **13 after three mornings**. Percentages, simple versus compound growth and early-withdrawal calculations belong to the later 9–11 modes, after the amounts themselves are understood.

Show the principal, maturity date, guaranteed return and early-withdrawal result before confirmation. Interest accrues and a matured certificate pays automatically while the child is away; there is no daily claim, streak, notification pressure or lost interest for missing a visit. At first, early withdrawal returns all principal and forfeits only the promised bonus. Label the accelerated island rates as a model, since realistic bank interest is too small and slow to see. Neopets' National Neopian Bank is a useful precedent for persistent savings and daily interest, but its manual collection is the part not to copy ([official Neopedia entry](https://www.neopets.com/neopedia.phtml?neopedia_id=73)).

### Progression decision

Do not add a universal point balance to the first Lemonade Stand build. Puddle Island already has several kinds of progress with distinct meanings: per-game levels, unplayed twinkles, favorites, stickers, creations, journal discoveries, story steps and named couch records. Converting all play into one spendable number would invite exchange-rate tuning and repetitive farming before it proves a useful choice.

For older players, combine **named project cards**, **child-chosen permanent unlocks** and a bounded Harbor balance. Prototype one reason to earn, one guaranteed reason to spend, one optional chance choice and one savings goal before widening the economy. Keep the amounts small enough to reason about; disclose every price and probability; let supported completion count; provide an everything-open grown-up switch; and never use real money, debt, upkeep, expiring offers, hidden odds or required grinding. Real elapsed time belongs only to a voluntary product whose stated lesson is waiting, such as the Harbor Bank certificate; ordinary games, rewards and projects remain available without waiting.

## Later horizons (elementary)

The long-term plan: a familiar island that grows from early play through roughly ages 5–11, by extending favorite games, adding new interactions where they help, and connecting them through places, characters and projects. These are development horizons, not dates or grade locks. Ages are rough planning guides; learning ranges overlap; and the horizons are not a proposal for one runtime band per school grade. Apart from Wonder Woods (the `school` band's place) and the steps marked built, everything in this section is a **proposal**.

| Horizon | What grows | Places and example experiences | A useful milestone |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |

| **E1 — Early elementary**, roughly ages 5–7 | Combine small quantities, follow and explain short sequences, use word and picture clues, measure and compare, editable routes and maps | **Wonder Woods**: help set up a woodland picnic, balance supplies, program a delivery route, follow a narrated clue | One zone with two deeper familiar games, one new activity and a small optional adventure |

The 6–8 pilot (the `school` band, built) bridges E1 and E2.

### E1: early elementary, roughly ages 5–7

Built so far: the `school` band and Wonder Woods (where many games play their top levels), Seesaw Balance 8–9 (take the same off both sides), Robot Path loops with step-through playback (7–10), Mail Carrier's picture map, two planned stops and shorter route (6–8), and Light Lab, Penguin Slide, Secret Code, Frog Hop, Market Stall and Clock Tower built to reach it.

- [ ] Wonder Woods as a thematic zone: a host, an errand and an identity separate from the band. [Lands](#lands-navigation-and-discovery) stage A gives every land a host, an identity and a launch and return context; what is left here is a woods land's own errand (below).
- [ ] A woodland errand: the Woodland Picnic, or a delivery for a friend, using Mail Carrier's map; every participating game stays independently playable.
- [ ] Check that a child can use hints, revise a route, leave partway, and return without losing progress or changing other games' levels; test loading and restoring old saves with appropriate defaults (levels, pins, stickers and home place preserved), new-band navigation and offline play.
- [ ] Let the child ask for a worked example or an easier mode; longer reasoning must never require faster fingers.
- [ ] Seesaw Balance with two different unknown weights.
- [ ] Evaluate optional narrated word and sentence practice separately from reading-free navigation; review language content and the actual speech output before treating a mode as ready.

Exit milestone: a complete early-elementary place offers deeper decisions and accessible help, and visiting it changes neither the child's global band nor the younger activities.

### E2: middle elementary

- [ ] Choose **Maker Harbor** or **Storybook Square** first, by the next desired skill area; build the other in a later expansion.
- [ ] Add the matching extensions from the [family table](#how-familiar-games-keep-growing): equal groups, parts and design, or word meaning and evidence in stories.
- [ ] One new interaction and one project per expansion, with several valid solutions where appropriate.
- [ ] Save a work in progress (a construction, program or story) and return to it directly from the journal.
- [ ] Decide whether branching game modes need separate progress records before one linear level number carries unrelated skills.

Exit milestone: she can choose a subject or project, make a plan, change it after feedback, and revisit a saved creation. Progress in reading never forces harder number work, or the reverse.

### E3: upper elementary

Built so far: **Habitat Helpers** (2026-10-09), the first small Discovery Marsh habitat model.

- [ ] Upper-elementary number, geometry and story-evidence tasks where they fit existing families.
- [ ] Pilot **Skywatch Isles** with one reusable robot routine, one blueprint mode and Light Lab's older modes, in code-drawn 2D scenes.
- [ ] Projects with several short sessions of optional work, saved at meaningful steps; she can stop after any step.
- [ ] Compare two results, pick a useful piece of evidence, or explain a choice through pictures, word choices or optional writing, with spoken help.

Exit milestone: a project supports planning, testing, revising and showing the result; different valid approaches reach the ending; and her evidence can be revisited beside the creation.

### How familiar games keep growing

**Games grow through families of modes.** A counting game becomes equal sharing, then grouping, then a recipe or packing problem; a route game gains loops, conditions and reusable subroutes. Keep the original activity recognizable, with a deeper decision at each step. Use the [experience matrix](FAMILY-EXPERIENCE.md#a-catalog-with-many-reasons-to-play) as well as subjects to keep expansions varied; making, investigating, moving and sharing roles should grow beside answer puzzles. A mode earns its place through play, not bigger numbers or quicker responses. Some favorites (bubble popping, dressing the pet, free painting) can stay simple toys for an older child; not every game needs to reach grade 5. Avoid making every game teach arithmetic: language, music, spatial thinking, inquiry, coordination, creativity and cooperation all have a place.

Everything in the three right-hand columns is proposed; steps already built are in [GAMES.md](GAMES.md). Choose modes by readiness and interest, never by advancing the whole library at once.

| Family | Early elementary | Middle elementary | Upper elementary |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Duck Pond, Monster Munch, Little Helpers, Scoop Shop, Lasso Loops** | Compose and decompose small totals; explain how many more are needed | Arrays, division with visible leftovers, equal pieces of a whole | Equivalent fractions, decimal amounts, scaling a small order or recipe with visual models |
| **Word Monsters, Letter Trails** | Reviewed sound and word combinations; short labels that matter in a scene | Prefixes and suffixes; choosing a word that fits a clue or sentence | Word parts and context for unfamiliar words; compose or revise a short message with spoken support |
| **Story Steps, Photo Safari** | Retell a sequence; match a spoken clue to an event | Infer a cause; choose an ending supported by clues; organize a short narrative | Compare two accounts; select supporting details; revise an explanation or create a branching story |
| **Robot Path, Egg Catch, Mail Carrier** | Repair one wrong command; compare two routes | Simple conditions, reusable route chunks, debugging a visible trace | Small functions or nested loops, several constraints, explaining a revision |
| **Size Parade, Bug Builder, Puzzle Pals, Rainbow Fingers, Block Tower** | Measuring with equal units, rotation, symmetry, composing shapes | Area and perimeter, tiling, equivalent shapes, following and changing a blueprint | Packing unit cubes for volume, coordinate designs, relating a drawing to a construction |
| **Sink or Float, Peg Garden, Bouncy Launch, Ramp Race, Chain Reaction** | Predict and observe; compare two trials with obvious differences | Change one variable, repeat a test, record outcomes in a picture table | Read graphs, compare evidence, notice a model's limits, improve an experiment |
| **Jelly Drums, Song Maker, Sound Garden, Beat Builder** | Rhythm conversations, phrases, musical turn-taking | Contrasting sections and layered rhythms; a response to a phrase | Compose for a story or event; revise a piece after listening; keep the pentatonic pitch rules |
| **Duckling Parade, Roundup, Dot Link, Bounce Back** | Follow patterns, sort by a stated rule, cooperate toward a visible goal | Combine attributes, plan an order of actions, explain a partner strategy | Optional constraint puzzles or cooperative remixes where the controls still feel good; otherwise relaxing favorites |
| **Weather Wardrobe, Feelings Faces, community errands** | Plan a short outing; consider what a friend might need | Read a map or simple timetable, coordinate roles, compare plausible perspectives | Plan a shared event with several needs and explain a compromise; feelings and family routines never have one correct answer |

The presentation can grow too: the pet becomes a companion in planning and investigating; older modes can use quieter feedback, richer tools and optional explanations in the same art style; a saved project shelf lets her choose what to continue or show a grown-up. Keep narration replayable, help easy to request, and celebrations at meaningful stopping points.

### Zone concepts

Names and boundaries are working proposals. Each zone starts as one small place with a host and a few activities; it needs no separate engine, economy or complete curriculum. A zone can hold several subjects and more than one learning range, and the same game identity can appear in several zones with an appropriate mode and a clear return route.

| Zone | Identity and recurring host | First small expansion | Later growth |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Wonder Woods** (place built) | A trail of hollow trees, streams and signs; Hazel, the squirrel postkeeper from Mail Carrier's map | Seesaw Balance 8–9, Robot Path loops and Mail Carrier's map (built); **the Woodland Picnic** uses all three | Older Peekaround Island modes, listening clues, short stories, gentle classification puzzles |
| **Maker Harbor** | Floating workshops and a friendly ferry crew; a beaver builder | Little Helpers' equal groups into dock loads; Mail Carrier into dock routes; Chain Reaction and Ferry Jam (built) as harbor activities | Shape Buddies, Wobble Works, area and volume blueprints; a **Ferry Fair** with earned tickets and child-chosen booth unlocks; later visible-odds wheels and a voluntary Harbor Bank |
| **Storybook Square** | A library, puppet stage and post office; an owl storyteller | Story Steps into clue-based endings; Word Monsters into meaningful word parts; **Silly Describer** | Story Theater, alternative viewpoints, an illustrated newspaper saved locally, **the Mixed-Up Invitations** mystery |
| **Discovery Marsh** | Boardwalks, ponds and an observation hut; a patient heron naturalist | Photo Safari into observation records; Sink or Float into repeatable trials; **Habitat Helpers** is built as the first small habitat model | Compare habitat needs, chart observations, test one variable, design a visitor garden with several valid solutions |
| **Skywatch Isles** | Hilltop observatories and model-building decks; a moth astronomer | A reusable Robot Path routine; a measured Bug Builder blueprint; Light Lab predictions and replays | Data stories, coordinate maps, a limited sun and shadow model, **the Lantern Observatory** project |

Every zone stays open. Neither birthdays, test results nor sticker counts become access gates, and returning to the original island stays easy. The treehouse and journal are shared destinations, so new zones enrich one personal world.

### What an expansion contains

A default scope, not a quota; a smaller release that deepens one favorite is useful too.

1. **Two extensions to familiar games**, each with a new decision or form of expression.
2. **At most one new interaction family**, such as cooperative shaping, beam routing or editable rules.
3. **One place, or a small addition to a place**, with a memorable host, clear landmarks and spoken navigation.
4. **One optional project** combining a few activities, with saved steps and a satisfying ending; free play stays directly available.
5. **One creation or known keepsake** to revisit in the treehouse or journal. Completed rounds keep the one-sticker reward; the project layer never duplicates it.
6. **Content review and verification**, including old-save behavior and regression checks for existing play.

Afterwards, decide what she wants to repeat, what confused her, and whether a fresh arrangement still makes sense. Expand the successful activity before filling out a whole zone.

### Educational reference points

The sequence borrows a few curriculum reference points, not a claim of alignment. Grade 3 math emphasizes multiplication and division, fractions, area and shape reasoning; grade 5 develops fraction and decimal operations and volume ([grade 3](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/3/introduction/), [grade 5](https://www.thecorestandards.org/Math/Content/5/introduction/)). For stories, using details from a text to support an explanation and understanding word parts are useful goals; listening comprehension and independent decoding are different things to observe ([grade 5 literature](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RL/5/), [foundational skills](https://www.thecorestandards.org/ELA-Literacy/RF/5/)). For investigations, plan fair comparisons, control relevant variables, and use observations as evidence; check the simulation's limits before presenting a game result as a fact about the world ([NGSS grades 3–5 engineering design](https://www.nextgenscience.org/topic-arrangement/3-5engineering-design)). Before calling a mode ready, review its facts, explanations, language, solvability, alternatives and behavior with help. Completion with hints is valuable play, not an assessment.

## Planning rules

**Ready to build** when you can state the **fun action**, the **specific skill**, the **smallest complete round**, the **starting support**, and **what deeper play changes**; which existing game could host it as a mode instead; and at least one example of a mistake or experiment and how the game explains it. Creative play is never forced into a single correct answer.

**Done** when authored or generated tasks are solvable and unambiguous (in rule tests), supported completion works, interruptions and resizing behave, prompts are spoken, rewards and saves are stable, the checks at the right tier of the [verification budget](../AGENTS.md#verification-budget) have run, and a fresh arrangement still makes sense beyond one memorized answer. Then update the README row, this file, [GAMES.md](GAMES.md) and the verification log. Assisted completion is worthwhile play but not evidence of mastery; content and device validation are separate from a passing build.

**Goals, scores and rewards** (carried over from the [play and progression review](archive/PLAY-AND-PROGRESSION.md)):

- Stickers stay predictable and repeatable: one per completed round, with help or without. Never delete duplicates or reinterpret old round history as an achievement.
- A unique achievement names a known condition, visible beforehand, and is earned once; repeating improves a record instead of adding copies.
- Personal bests come before any shared board, and scores keep their own units (slides, launches, guesses); never add them into one island ranking. Compare only the same course version and support category, with helped and unhelped runs kept apart.
- No daily resets, streaks, required returns or lost progress. Scores never go on creative or exploratory play, and optional challenges never withhold a story ending or the ordinary reward.
- The question for a grown-up prototype is whether the player would choose to play again because there is something to improve, discover or make; minutes played and stickers earned do not answer it.

## Parked or decided

- **Native Nintendo Switch port:** exploratory only. There is no browser on the Switch; a port needs Nintendo developer approval, development hardware and a rewrite of rendering, input, audio, speech and saves. Prove one controller-driven game on approved hardware before estimating more. Research: [Party and Switch](archive/PARTY-AND-SWITCH.md#running-a-game-on-the-original-switch).
- **A separate "Grown-up Play" launcher with its own adult save:** superseded by couch play, which has its own save and the challenge courses.
- **Leaderboards:** a local pass-and-play family board is a reasonable experiment once a second person wants to compare; public online rankings need a service, identity and score validation and are a later adult-only option.
- **More party structure:** a dice board, item economy, online matchmaking or controller support for every game is not needed. Simultaneous face-off boards and the daughter joining couch play as a co-pilot (any button cheers; a pet takes the other side) are proposals for later.
- **An opt-in "Keep playing" mode** for selected short games, with a brief acknowledgment and ordinary per-round rewards: still proposed, now scoped with [D4a](#discovery-actions). Cross-game Next and finite mixes (D1b/D2b) can be useful while retaining current celebration/intro behavior; automatic handoff (D6) and brief session support (D4b) are separate later decisions.
- **Combat, falling-speed and guess-and-lose games for grown-ups:** shooters and defense games (including Typing Terror's destroy-the-robots form; the friendly form is Pond Typing), Tetris-style falling blocks and Minesweeper are set aside for now; see the end of the [grown-up candidates](IDEAS.md#grown-up-play-candidates-couch-route) for what would bring each back.
- **Parent-recorded voice lines:** needs an explicit decision against the no-audio-files rule, plus offline storage and consent behavior, before any work.
- **A hold to enter grown-up games:** not needed (the developer, 2026-10-09). Deleting a person, resetting and restoring stay behind the gear's two-second hold, and each profile keeps a one-step undo.
