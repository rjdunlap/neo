# TotoLogic, reviewed for Puddle Island

*Reference review · first written 2026-11-10 from the website and store listing (`claude/totologic-research-d9185f`), rewritten 2026-11-11 after the app was played on an Android phone (`claude/totologic-played-review`). Documents only: nothing was built. Proposals here are proposals until the developer or a dispatch takes one.*

**What it is.** [TotoLogic](https://totologic.com/) ("Play. Learn. Grow.") is an Android app for children of about 3 to 8, published on [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.synthix.totologic) by Synthix Technologies, which appears to be a Nairobi developer (its terms are under Kenyan law, it takes M-Pesa, and its money game counts in Kenyan shillings). It is a suite of short activities in fourteen categories (Logic, Math, Shapes, Patterns, Visual, Memory, Painting, Reading, World Around Us, Mazes & Adventures, Animals & Nature, Quick Challenges, Brain Challenge, Words & Language), wrapped in a parent account with a child profile, a daily plan, learning sessions, a progress page and a paid plan. It is new: on 2026-11-10 the listing showed "100+" downloads, no rating and no reviews. There is no iOS version.

**A namesake.** Searching the name mostly finds an unrelated [Totologic blog](https://totologic.blogspot.com/) (Cédric Jules, 2013–2015) on game programming. It is covered in [one short section](#the-other-totologic) at the end.

**How this was checked.** In two passes.

- **2026-11-10, from outside.** The home page, the store listing and its four screenshots, the privacy policy, terms and contact pages, and the public [play-first sign-up page](https://totologic.com/smarter-screen-time/), where three demo puzzles were played and the page's script was read. The app was not installed.
- **2026-11-11, played.** Version 1.0.0 (14) on the developer's own Android phone (a Pixel 10, English (US), the app in landscape), on a trial TotoLogic's developer provided. The assistant drove the phone over USB (screenshots, taps and drags; [the tools](SESSION-GUIDE.md#playing-an-app-on-the-developers-android-phone) are kept) and played Today's plan and all fourteen categories on one age-3 profile at the "Just right" difficulty, with wrong answers on purpose; then Addition, Subtraction, Money, Patterns, Memory, Quick Challenges and Words & Language again with the difficulty fixed at "Ages 7-8"; two Reading sessions; Painting; the grown-ups' area and its progress pages; and one ten-minute learning session with a break.

What that play is and is not: **an adult design read, by an assistant, with the sound off.** The phone stayed on silent; speech was captured from the phone's audio and transcribed by software, so every quoted line is a machine transcript and the voice, the letter sounds and the music were not judged. No child played, and scripted taps say nothing about what a child enjoys or how a target feels under a finger. Not covered: sign-up, payment, emails, Focus Mode, the "Ages 5-6" setting, the later Reading stages, tablets, and a second child (adding one opens the plan chooser, so every age was read from one profile). No independent review, rating or press coverage was found. A separate note of bugs and usability feedback for TotoLogic's developer was written from the same play; it is the developer's to send and is not kept in this repository.

**Recommendation in one paragraph.** Played, TotoLogic is calm and kind, and thinner than its menu: fourteen categories come down to about a dozen activity types, and the island has a deeper game for every one. The first review's advice still stands where it rested on the website (a first minute that starts with play, a grown-up's view in plain words), but the app itself does not deliver the first of those: its first activity is a drag sort whose question is only spoken and whose drag is never shown. Play adds three things. **First, what it is like to be the muted player.** Almost every question is voice only, and with the sound off the assistant answered one wrongly because nothing on screen said whether to find the fruits or the vehicles. The island has the same gap in touch play, so [P3](#p3-the-question-on-screen-for-readers-a-definition-then-shell-m) moves to the front. **Second, a few small moments worth copying:** a spoken reason after a right answer ("The car doesn't belong. A cloud and a rainbow are up in the sky."), and a money total she can see ([P7](#p7-say-why-after-a-right-answer-two-dives-then-voice-lines-s-each), [P8](#p8-a-total-she-can-see-on-market-stalls-counter-a-dive-note-then-s)). **Third, cautions that bear on the island directly:** a first-try count that ignores wrong drags and then feeds a level and a "Proficient" label ([P9](#p9-a-read-through-of-how-drag-games-count-a-miss-docs-s)), labels that promise more than their contents, and decoration that competes with the idea being asked. The first review's P6 (tap to fill) was a misreading of a screenshot and is withdrawn. Its star counter, sessions, paid plan, emails and "brain" wording stay out, by the island's existing rules.

## Proposals

Each is a proposal, sized for one session unless it says otherwise, with the document it would change. P1 to P6 keep their numbers from the first review, each with what play changed; P7 to P9 are new. None is on [READY](READY.md): P1 to P3 each need a definition and a decision first. For the phone playtest the order of likely value is now P3, P1, P2, then the rest.

### P1. Play first, set up after (a definition; then shell and save, M)

The developer and their wife are the only players now, and anyone they hand a link to meets a form before a game. TotoLogic's web page shows the other order working at its smallest: play, then the questions, on the same card as the praise. For the island, define a first visit on a device with no players as **one picture choice and then a round**: three or so large age places (little one, bigger kid, grown-up), a tap on one starts the first card of that band's [first six](definitions/todays-six.md#u5-a-first-six-she-can-start-status-built-2026-11-10), and the round's result offers *Keep playing* and *Add me* (the existing form). The round is played on the blank profile that already exists and is already hidden from the chooser, so its sticker and history become the new player's when the form is filled; if it never is, the blank profile stays blank-looking and nothing is lost. The egg, the pet color and the pet name move to after the first round, where a pet is a reward for having played rather than a toll before it.

To settle in the definition: how many age places and which bands they stand for; whether an untouched guest round counts toward anything; that "Who's playing?" stays the first screen as soon as one named player exists (so this never changes a family device that is set up); and the save rule that adopting the blank profile is a rename, not a copy. This is shell, save and navigation work, so its tier is typecheck, unit tests, build, `profiles` and `phonefit`, and offline.

**After play:** unchanged, and still resting on the website. The app's sign-up was not seen, and what the app does once an account exists is the weaker example: its first activity asked for a drag nobody had shown. The island's first round must be one whose how-to card and ghost finger carry it with the sound off.

### P2. Add a player with taps (shell, S; can ship before P1)

Independent of P1, the form itself can ask less. TotoLogic's web form asks for a nickname ("No need for their real name") and one tap on an age. The island needs a birth month so a band moves on by itself, but on a phone the typed year and the month select are the slow part. Offer a row of age chips that fills the year (and leaves the month at the current one, editable on the grown-ups' page as now), keep the typed fields beneath for a grown-up who wants the exact month, and let the name be left empty (the pet's name or "Player 2" stands in until someone changes it). The same line that already says the name stays on this device can add that a nickname is fine. Tier: typecheck, the profile tests, `profiles`.

**After play:** unchanged. The app's add-a-child form could not be reached (it sits behind the plan chooser), and no way to change the child's name or age was found afterwards; the island's editable birth month is worth keeping visible.

### P3. The question on screen, for readers (a definition; then shell, M)

A phone is usually on silent, and both current players can read. The first review credited TotoLogic with printing every question; that is true of its website demo and **not of the app**, which speaks its questions as the island does. Playing it muted is the best evidence this page has: pictures on blobs with nothing to say what is wanted, a sum that exists only as sound, and one wrong answer given in good faith because "find the vehicles" and "find the fruits" share a screen. A grown-up handed the island on a silent phone is in the same place once the how-to card closes.

Define a caption for touch rounds: the current `ctx.instruct` line as one line of text near the pet, with a small speaker picture that repeats it (what tapping the pet already does). To settle: which players see it (the `grownup` band always; `school` perhaps; younger bands never, so nothing a child must read appears), whether it shows only when speech is off or muted, how slotted lines are filled (the roadmap's planned `lineText(id)` helper), and where it sits upright on a 390-wide screen without covering a target. Two things from play to add to the definition: the caption must change when the question changes within a round, and feedback that is only a sound (TotoLogic's wrong drop, the island's boing) needs something to see beside it; the island's glow after two misses already is that. This answers the UX review's first finding for the people who are actually playing. This review adds one clause to the Sound question on the [phone playtest list](DEVICE-CHECKS.md#what-the-phone-playtest-can-answer-now): with the sound off, could you tell what each game asked?

### P4. This week says what the games asked, and names one not tried (panel, S)

Each game already declares one of seven `challenge` kinds (numbers, words, logic, space, music, noticing, hands), used only to mix Today's six. Add to [This week](definitions/grownups-page.md#r5-this-week-opens-on-what-she-played), above the table, one plain sentence built from it: "This week: three counting games, two with words, one with music", and under it one line: "Not played yet: *Critter Crossing* (logic)", chosen from her band, a kind she has not played this week, first level. No percentages, no "mastered", no "recommended", no ranking: it names what the games asked and what is there, which is the wording rule. The pure function sits beside `weekRows` in `src/parent/`; the tier is typecheck and its unit tests. It needs a few lines added to the grown-ups' page definition first, with the kind names in plain words.

**After play:** TotoLogic's progress page is its most careful screen, and two of its habits fit this proposal. It **says when it cannot say** ("Not enough activity yet to say"), so the island's sentence should be absent, not padded, in a week with one or two rounds. And it **gives the reason beside the suggestion** ("Why this activity?"), so the "Not played yet" line can end with its reason in the same plain words ("nothing with music this week"). What to leave is clearer too: "Proficient" appeared after five odd-one-outs, on a first-try count that missed wrong drags, and the page's numbers disagreed with the end cards. A label is only as good as the count under it, and the island's page names what was asked and does not grade her.

### P5. A mix that survives a reload, as D5b's first slice (docs, then save, S–M)

Phone browsers drop a background tab freely, so on a phone "a reload forgets it" happens when someone answers a message, not only when they choose to leave. Today's six already survives this; a Little surprises mix and a Make my mix lineup do not. [D5b](ROADMAP.md#discovery-actions) is waiting to be defined as a saved, named lineup. A smaller first slice: remember only the mix in progress (a bounded list of game ids, the position, and the day), repaired on load, cleared when it ends or the day changes, with the unfinished round restarting fresh as the [navigation table](rework/HOME-NAVIGATION.md) already says. This is a note for D5b's definition, not a new item.

**After play:** TotoLogic does this at the same grain: a set left at its second item came back at "1 of 5 done", with the item itself starting again. Its Home did not keep up (the finished plan looked unplayed, and *Continue* listed categories with nothing to continue), which is the half to avoid: whatever is remembered must show as remembered.

### P6. Tap to fill: withdrawn

The first review read a store screenshot of Painting as a fill bucket beside a brush and asked the Rainbow Fingers dive to consider tap-to-fill. Played, there is no fill: the bucket pictures are palette pickers, a tap leaves a round dab and a drag paints a thick freehand stroke under the line art. The question may still be worth asking for a phone-width coloring page, but TotoLogic is not an example of it, and the [dive's row](deep-dives/rainbow-fingers.md#similar-games) is corrected. The tap alternative for drags that this proposal was cited for is [Pok Pok's P3](POK-POK.md#p3-a-tap-can-do-what-a-drag-does-a-question-now-a-definition-later), which stands on its own and gains the drag-only sort above as an example.

### P7. Say why after a right answer (two dives, then voice lines, S each)

The best teaching in TotoLogic is one sentence after a correct pick: "The car doesn't belong. A cloud and a rainbow are up in the sky." It turns a lucky tap into the rule, and it reaches the child who was right as well as the one who was wrong. The island says its rules mostly on a miss. Two games would gain most:

- **[Pattern Train](games/pattern-train.md).** When the odd car is found it hops off to a bell and the round ends on the shell's general praise. One line there, from slots the game already has, would say what the pattern was. The same goes for the last car of a fill round.
- **[Rhyme Time](games/rhyme-time.md).** "Yes! {word} does not rhyme." names the answer; its miss line already knows the rest ("{word} rhymes with {other}!"). The right answer can carry both halves.

Keep it to **one short line at the answer that ends the round**, never after every item: the [restaurant test](POK-POK.md#p2-the-restaurant-test-a-phone-playtest-question-docs-s) asks which line a player has tired of by the sixth game, and TotoLogic's own inconsistency (six of fifteen) suggests the lines are costly to write. Each is a note for its dive first, then an existing-game change: typecheck, the game's rule tests and one filtered play.

### P8. A total she can see on Market Stall's counter (a dive note, then S)

TotoLogic's Money shows the amount paid so far as a number that changes with every coin. [Market Stall](games/market-stall.md) counts on aloud ("{n}!") and draws only the coin values and the price, so a muted player adds the coins in their head and an early player loses the count when the voice is missed. Draw the running total on the counter in the modes where the game already counts on aloud, and not in the pair and change modes, where adding up is the puzzle and the game names each coin instead of the total. Leave TotoLogic's green total and lit *Pay* button: ringing the bell to find out is the island's check, and a button that lights when the amount is exact does the checking for her. The [dive](deep-dives/market-stall.md) decides which levels; tier as an existing-game change.

### P9. A read-through of how drag games count a miss (docs, S)

TotoLogic shows what an uncounted miss costs: its end card said "Every one right on the first try!" after wrong drops, and that count sat under a "Proficient" label, on a page that had already put a three-year-old's profile at "Level 5-6". The island shows no such count, but misses do more here: two smooth rounds step a level up. Each game does its own counting (four use `src/progress/coach.ts`), and no test looks across games. A code read on 2026-11-11 found wrong drops counted in the sorts it sampled (Critter Sort, Jar Sort, Shape Sorter, Sink or Float, Puzzle Pals, Timeline Trail, Tangram Town, Size Parade) and the never-a-miss games documented as deliberate, so **no bug is known**. The proposal is one table of the drag games (66 by that read's count) with each one's rule for a wrong drop, flagging any that neither counts it nor says it is free play; anything flagged becomes an ordinary bug fix with its rule test. Docs only until something is found.

### What not to take

A star per item and a counter in every corner; a first-try score on the child's card, and any card that grades a painting; "Proficient" and other badges on thin counts; session timers and Focus Mode, however gently ended; a second profile behind a bigger plan; accounts, emails, scheduled feedback requests and advert measurement; "Brain Challenge", "Big brain mode?" and wording about focus and attention; a parent's form about goals and strengths; a *Popular* row; sums and questions that exist only as sound; bins that swallow what was sorted; pictures that shrink as their number grows; one game filed under five names.

## Not established

- **What a child does with it.** No child played. Whether a three-year-old finds the drag, enjoys the praise or notices the stars is unknown, and the app has no reviews or published numbers.
- **Sound.** Every quoted line is a machine transcript of captured audio. The voice, the letter sounds in Reading and the music were not judged, and a transcript can be a word out.
- **How difficulty adapts.** The progress page named a level; reopening a finished set at "Just right" gave the same activities from the start. What "Just right" changes, and when, was not seen. "Ages 5-6" was not played.
- **The parts not reached:** sign-up and the set-up questions, payment, parent emails, Focus Mode, the Reading stages after Sounds, one of Memory's five tiles (Colors), most of Shapes' thirteen activities, a second child, and any tablet.
- **Whether the oddities are bugs or choices.** The unshuffled pairs (three games of three), the first-try count and the level were each seen a few times on one phone and one version, 1.0.0 (14).
- **Who makes it.** "Synthix Technologies" is the store name; the Nairobi reading rests on the Kenyan governing law, M-Pesa, the shilling prices and that name's other store listings, not on a stated address.

## The other Totologic

The name also belongs to [Totologic](https://totologic.blogspot.com/), a 2013–2015 programming blog by Cédric Jules, a teacher who worked in the game industry. It has nothing to do with the app. Three of its posts touch things the island builds, as seeds only:

- **[Triangulated circular maze generation](https://totologic.blogspot.com/2014/09/triangulated-circular-maze-generation.html).** Rings and spokes as the candidate walls, then a seeded depth-first walk keeps a tree of passages, with the middle opened up as a place worth reaching. A depth-first tree has exactly one route between any two points, which is the property [Mossy Path](deep-dives/mossy-path.md)'s mazes are already tested for, so a **round maze** (the pond in the middle, the turtle starting at the rim) would be a new board shape for the same rules rather than a new game. A note for that dive when it next plans levels.
- **[Tetris AI explained](https://totologic.blogspot.com/2013/03/tetris-ai-explained.html).** A placement search scored by holes (weighted twenty times the rest), height, roughness and slope, and a remark worth keeping: a computer player's *personality* comes from which inputs it is allowed, not from a worse score function. That is a tidy way to make a couch rival or a demo bot look like a beginner without making it play nonsense.
- **Chinese checkers, [its openings](https://totologic.blogspot.com/2013/06/chinese-checkers-best-opening-moves.html) and [a spherical board](https://totologic.blogspot.com/2013/10/chinese-checkers-on-spherical-board.html).** A reference if a hop-and-chain board game is ever considered for couch play; nothing is proposed.

Only the three linked summaries were read; no code was taken or looked at.
