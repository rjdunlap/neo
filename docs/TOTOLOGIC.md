# TotoLogic, reviewed for Puddle Island

*Reference review · 2026-11-10 · `claude/totologic-research-d9185f`. Documents only: nothing was built. Proposals here are proposals until the developer or a dispatch takes one.*

**What it is.** [TotoLogic](https://totologic.com/) ("Play. Learn. Grow.") is an Android app for children of about 3 to 8, published on [Google Play](https://play.google.com/store/apps/details?id=com.synthix.totologic) by Synthix Technologies, which appears to be a Nairobi developer (its terms are under Kenyan law and it takes M-Pesa). It is a suite of short tap-and-choose activities in eleven categories (Memory, Visual, Logic, Math, Shapes, Patterns, Painting, Animals, World Around Us, Language, Brain Challenge), wrapped in a parent account with child profiles, a daily plan, sessions, progress emails and a paid plan. It is new: the listing shows "100+" downloads, no rating and no reviews, was last updated 2026-11-01, and the site's "What parents say" section is empty. There is no iOS version.

**A namesake.** Searching the name mostly finds an unrelated [Totologic blog](https://totologic.blogspot.com/) (Cédric Jules, 2013–2015) on game programming. It is covered in [one short section](#the-other-totologic) at the end.

**How this was checked.** The app was **not installed or played**: it is Android only and needs a parent account, and a session does not create accounts. What was opened, on 2026-11-10 in the app's own browser pane: the home page, the Google Play listing and its four screenshots at full size, the privacy policy, terms and contact pages, and the public [play-first sign-up page](https://totologic.com/smarter-screen-time/), where three demo puzzles were played (one wrong answer on purpose) up to the form that asks for a nickname, which was left empty. That page's client script was read for the ten demo puzzles and the flow after them. Sound was not heard. No independent review, parent rating or press coverage was found, so everything about the app beyond its four screenshots is the developer's own description, and nothing here is evidence of what children do with it.

**Recommendation in one paragraph.** TotoLogic's games, as far as they can be seen, are ordinary: pick the matching picture, the odd one out, the next bead, count the ducks, color a lion. The island has deeper versions of every one of them, with spoken support, hints and a ladder. What TotoLogic has thought about, and the island mostly has not, is **the first minute and the grown-up's view**: a visitor is playing a puzzle before the page has finished loading, the home screen answers "what do I tap?" with one button, a parent is told in plain words what the child just did, and an interrupted sitting can be picked up. Those are the patterns worth taking, and they fit the current phone focus. Its timers, Focus Mode, star counter, paid worlds, parent emails, advert tracking and "brain" wording stay out, by the island's existing rules.

## Proposals

Each is a proposal, sized for one session unless it says otherwise, with the document it would change. None is on [READY](READY.md): P1 to P3 each need a definition and a decision first, and the order below is the order of likely value for the phone playtest.

### P1. Play first, set up after (a definition; then shell and save, M)

The developer and their wife are the only players now, and anyone they hand a link to meets a form before a game. TotoLogic's page shows the other order working at its smallest: play, then the questions, on the same card as the praise. For the island, define a first visit on a device with no players as **one picture choice and then a round**: three or so large age places (little one, bigger kid, grown-up), a tap on one starts the first card of that band's [first six](definitions/todays-six.md#u5-a-first-six-she-can-start-status-built-2026-11-10), and the round's result offers *Keep playing* and *Add me* (the existing form). The round is played on the blank profile that already exists and is already hidden from the chooser, so its sticker and history become the new player's when the form is filled; if it never is, the blank profile stays blank-looking and nothing is lost. The egg, the pet color and the pet name move to after the first round, where a pet is a reward for having played rather than a toll before it.

To settle in the definition: how many age places and which bands they stand for; whether an untouched guest round counts toward anything; that "Who's playing?" stays the first screen as soon as one named player exists (so this never changes a family device that is set up); and the save rule that adopting the blank profile is a rename, not a copy. This is shell, save and navigation work, so its tier is typecheck, unit tests, build, `profiles` and `phonefit`, and offline.

### P2. Add a player with taps (shell, S; can ship before P1)

Independent of P1, the form itself can ask less. TotoLogic asks for a nickname ("No need for their real name") and one tap on an age. The island needs a birth month so a band moves on by itself, but on a phone the typed year and the month select are the slow part. Offer a row of age chips that fills the year (and leaves the month at the current one, editable on the grown-ups' page as now), keep the typed fields beneath for a grown-up who wants the exact month, and let the name be left empty (the pet's name or "Player 2" stands in until someone changes it). The same line that already says the name stays on this device can add that a nickname is fine. Tier: typecheck, the profile tests, `profiles`.

### P3. The question on screen, for readers (a definition; then shell, M)

A phone is usually on silent, and both current players can read. TotoLogic prints every question and puts a speaker beside it; the island says the line and shows nothing, so a muted grown-up gets a how-to card and then silence. Define a caption for touch rounds: the current `ctx.instruct` line as one line of text near the pet, with a small speaker picture that repeats it (what tapping the pet already does). To settle: which players see it (the `grownup` band always; `school` perhaps; younger bands never, so nothing a child must read appears), whether it shows only when speech is off or muted, how slotted lines are filled (the roadmap's planned `lineText(id)` helper), and where it sits upright on a 390-wide screen without covering a target. This answers the UX review's first finding for the people who are actually playing, and it sits beside the sound question the [phone playtest list](DEVICE-CHECKS.md#what-the-phone-playtest-can-answer-now) already asks.

### P4. This week says what the games asked, and names one not tried (panel, S)

Each game already declares one of seven `challenge` kinds (numbers, words, logic, space, music, noticing, hands), used only to mix Today's six. Add to [This week](definitions/grownups-page.md#r5-this-week-opens-on-what-she-played), above the table, one plain sentence built from it: "This week: three counting games, two with words, one with music", and under it one line: "Not played yet: *Critter Crossing* (logic)", chosen from her band, a kind she has not played this week, first level. No percentages, no "mastered", no "recommended", no ranking: it names what the games asked and what is there, which is the wording rule, and it is the honest version of TotoLogic's milestone email with nothing sent anywhere. The pure function sits beside `weekRows` in `src/parent/`; the tier is typecheck and its unit tests. It needs a few lines added to the grown-ups' page definition first, with the kind names in plain words.

### P5. A mix that survives a reload, as D5b's first slice (docs, then save, S–M)

Phone browsers drop a background tab freely, so on a phone "a reload forgets it" happens when someone answers a message, not only when they choose to leave. Today's six already survives this; a Little surprises mix and a Make my mix lineup do not. [D5b](ROADMAP.md#discovery-actions) is waiting to be defined as a saved, named lineup. TotoLogic's "pick up interrupted sessions" suggests a smaller first slice: remember only the mix in progress (a bounded list of game ids, the position, and the day), repaired on load, cleared when it ends or the day changes, with the unfinished round restarting fresh as the [navigation table](rework/HOME-NAVIGATION.md) already says. This is a note for D5b's definition, not a new item.

### P6. Tap to fill, asked in the Rainbow Fingers dive (docs only, S)

On a phone, painting inside the lines of a small region with a fingertip is fiddly, and TotoLogic's coloring page gives a fill bucket beside the brush. The [Rainbow Fingers dive](deep-dives/rainbow-fingers.md) should decide whether its coloring-page levels take a tap-to-fill tool at phone width or at the younger bands: the learning (naming a color and choosing it for a thing) is unchanged, and the brush stays for free painting. The notebook's grown-up couch idea (*Color by Number*) is a different game and is not affected.

### What not to take

Session timers, Focus Mode and parent-set breaks; a star counter; paid worlds and a free trial; accounts, emails, scheduled feedback requests, testimonials and advert measurement; "Brain Challenge" and any wording about thinking skills or better screen time; a parent's form about goals and strengths; a *Popular* row; the lasting red mark on a wrong card. TotoLogic's eleven categories are a menu of quiz types; the island's lands and ladders already cover each one with a game where the action carries the idea.

## The other Totologic

The name also belongs to [Totologic](https://totologic.blogspot.com/), a 2013–2015 programming blog by Cédric Jules, a teacher who worked in the game industry. It has nothing to do with the app. Three of its posts touch things the island builds, as seeds only:

- **[Triangulated circular maze generation](https://totologic.blogspot.com/2014/09/triangulated-circular-maze-generation.html).** Rings and spokes as the candidate walls, then a seeded depth-first walk keeps a tree of passages, with the middle opened up as a place worth reaching. A depth-first tree has exactly one route between any two points, which is the property [Mossy Path](deep-dives/mossy-path.md)'s mazes are already tested for, so a **round maze** (the pond in the middle, the turtle starting at the rim) would be a new board shape for the same rules rather than a new game. A note for that dive when it next plans levels.
- **[Tetris AI explained](https://totologic.blogspot.com/2013/03/tetris-ai-explained.html).** A placement search scored by holes (weighted twenty times the rest), height, roughness and slope, and a remark worth keeping: a computer player's *personality* comes from which inputs it is allowed, not from a worse score function. That is a tidy way to make a couch rival or a demo bot look like a beginner without making it play nonsense.
- **Chinese checkers, [its openings](https://totologic.blogspot.com/2013/06/chinese-checkers-best-opening-moves.html) and [a spherical board](https://totologic.blogspot.com/2013/10/chinese-checkers-on-spherical-board.html).** A reference if a hop-and-chain board game is ever considered for couch play; nothing is proposed.

Only the three linked summaries were read; no code was taken or looked at.
